# Proposal for the critic: Night Clearance

**Fantasy:** Contract workers land a small ship on abandoned moons and carry scrap out of dark industrial buildings to meet their employer's money target before the deadline.
One-player test: The line still works with one player, but nobody is left on the ship to watch the map or open doors.

**Players:** 1–4, one crew. **A session:** one in-game day is one landing that ends at midnight (length in real minutes not found); a target cycle is three days, and a sitting holds several cycles until the crew fails one.

**Feelings the game is known for**
- "I was telling him about the thing behind him and his radio just cut off."
- "We had enough, and we went back in for one more load."
- "I heard you scream down the corridor and I ran the other way."
- "I was alone on the ship watching all three of your dots stop moving."

**The rules, one sentence each**
- The crew must sell scrap worth at least the target within three days, and the first target is 130 credits.
- Each time the target is met, a new three-day cycle starts with a higher target, with no upper limit and no ending.
- If the target is missed, the crew is fired and everything restarts from zero.
- Each day the crew picks one moon, lands, and has until midnight, when the ship leaves by itself and anyone not aboard dies.
- Scrap lies inside a building with generated rooms, and the building holds creatures and traps (gun turrets, floor mines).
- More creatures appear as the day goes on, and outdoor creatures start appearing in the afternoon.
- A player holds four items, some items need both hands, and carried weight slows the player and drains stamina faster.
- A scan pulse shows nearby scrap, its value, and the entrances.
- Scrap only counts once it is on the ship and then sold at the employer's own site, which costs a day to visit.
- The employer pays a lower share of the scrap's value on early days of the cycle and full value on the last day.
- A dead player watches the others until the day ends and comes back the next day.
- Each dead crew member costs the crew a share of its credits, less if the body is carried back.
- If the whole crew dies, all scrap stored on the ship is lost, but bought tools stay.
- A player at the ship's terminal sees a top-down map with crew, creatures and scrap, can open or close numbered doors and switch off a turret or mine for a short time, and buys tools there.
- The living can pull the ship's lever to leave early, and dead players can vote to make the ship leave early.

**How much there is:** about 11 moons plus the selling site (some cost credits to travel to); 2–3 hand-styled building kinds with generated layouts; 5 weather kinds; at least 17 creature kinds named in guides (full count not found); about a dozen buyable tools (lights, radio, shovel, stun items, ladder, jetpack and others); a few ship upgrades (a teleporter that pulls a player back, a loud horn, and others); full count of scrap kinds (not found).

**Voice:** Voice is heard by distance, and a bought hand radio carries it further; a blind outdoor beast hunts by sound, including voices and radio noise; the dead cannot be heard by the living.

**The choices**
- Go back in for one more load, or leave now while the crew is alive?
- Stay on the ship at the map, or go in and carry scrap?
- Spend credits on tools, or on travel to a richer and more dangerous moon?
- Carry a dead friend's body out, or carry scrap in that slot?
- Sell early at a lower rate, or hold everything to the last day and risk losing it in a wipe?

**What the other end decides in the same second**
- One more load: the player at the ship decides whether to wait or pull the lever.
- Ship or inside: the players inside decide whether to trust the ship player's directions or go by their own eyes.
- Tools or travel: nothing.
- Body or scrap: nothing.
- Sell early or hold: nothing.

**Scene, 4 friends**
- Three go into the building and one stays at the ship's map with a radio.
- The ship player sees a red dot moving toward one crew dot and says so on the radio.
- The warned player turns round, sees a tall thing that backs away when looked at, and keeps walking backwards while holding a heavy item.
- A second player, out of radio range, has a thing drop from the ceiling onto their head and shouts; the third hears it faintly through a wall.
- The ship player watches one dot stop, opens a numbered door for the other two, and calls the time as late evening.
- Two players reach the ship with half the scrap; a blind beast is standing by the ramp, so nobody talks.
- The ship player pulls the lever, and the crew counts what it has against the target.

**What experienced crews do:** One player stays on the ship with a radio, watches the map, calls out creatures, traps, the time and the exit, opens doors and disables turrets; the others buy two lights and a shovel first, drop scrap by the entrance as they go and haul it at the end, leave by mid-afternoon, leave at once after most of the crew dies, start on the free easy moons and move to paid richer moons after a few targets, and sell on the last day only, overselling a little to bank credits.
**What changes it from round to round:** The moon chosen, the weather on it, the generated building layout, which creatures appear and when, where the scrap and traps are, and which tools are on sale.
**What carries from one round to the next:** Within a run, credits, bought tools, ship upgrades and unsold scrap carry from day to day; after being fired, nothing carries.
**Can a new crew lose? Does it get harder?** Yes, a new crew can miss the first target or wipe on the first day; the target rises every cycle without limit, so every run ends in a failed target.
