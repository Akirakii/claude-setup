# game-scope-lessons

The user's lessons from dropping Dungeon Pets and Assemblia: prove the fun in days, start from a proven loop, plan ~3–4 months of scope.

A new game must be shippable in under a year.
The user weighs effort and risk against the return, not success for its own sake.
Plan ~3–4 months of scope: part-time work triples calendar time, and solo developers underestimate by 2–3x.
Start from a proven loop and prove the fun in days, before the design docs grow.

**Why:** Dungeon Pets and Assemblia both died while still exploring the design, with no proven fun core.
Research at the time: deep single-player hits take years (Balatro ~3 years solo, Slay the Spire 2 ~5 years).
The user then chose cheap online co-op (friendslop) anyway, knowing its risks (netcode ~5x the time, griefing, players not coming back).
Don't argue against co-op again; plan around it: ready-made Steam P2P like GodotSteam from day one, and a jam-sized scope.

**When:** a new game or a big feature is proposed.
Assemblia's earlier "default to single player" no longer holds, since co-op was chosen.
