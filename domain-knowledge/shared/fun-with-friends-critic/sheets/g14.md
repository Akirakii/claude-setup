# Proposal for the critic: Station Hop

**Fantasy:** Company workers ride an interstellar train between derelict space stations to haul out materials and sell them, because the employer replaces crews that miss their quota.
One-player test: The line still works alone, since one player can pick a station, gather and sell, but the vote and the warnings between players fall away.

**Players:** 1–4, one crew. **A session:** one mission has a three-day limit; length of one station visit and how many missions fit a sitting (not found).

**Feelings the game is known for**
- "I opened a door and the door ate me."
- "We voted for the harder station and regretted it in the first room."
- "I heard him shouting about a turret and then his voice just stopped."

**The rules, one sentence each**
- The crew uses a terminal on the train to pick a destination station.
- Each objective shows a complexity level, and the players vote on which one to take.
- A mission is either retrieval (bring materials back) or elimination (kill hostiles).
- Stations are generated, and hold materials, alien creatures and traps.
- Traps include doors that are disguised creatures, automatic turrets and landmines.
- Materials are carried back to the train and sold there.
- Selling enough completes the mission's quota, and anything sold beyond it is profit for the crew.
- Each mission must be finished within three days.
- Missing the quota fails the crew (what exactly is lost: not found).
- Players can use tools such as pistols, hacking devices and grapple guns.
- A large creature can down a player (how downed players are revived, and what death costs: not found).
- Higher complexity brings more disguised doors and traps.
- A bank, an antenna and a sanity meter exist (what each does: not found).
- Carrying limit, air or battery limits, and how a day ends (not found).

**How much there is:** number of creature kinds (not found); number of stations (not found), layouts generated; three trap kinds named; three tool kinds named out of "many"; character cosmetics; a cat on the train that can be petted; upgrades (not found).

**Voice:** Voice by distance between players; whether creatures react to it (not found).

**The choices**
- Vote for a low-complexity objective, or a high one with more traps?
- Grab the valuable materials near the entrance, or search every room with the time limit running?
- Open this door, or treat it as a disguised creature and go around?
- Sell only enough for the quota, or stay out longer for profit?
- Go back for a downed teammate, or carry the haul to the train?

**What the other end decides in the same second**
- Complexity vote: every other player casts a vote at the same terminal.
- Near or far: the others decide whether to split to other rooms or stay in earshot.
- Door: nothing (the door's behaviour is fixed).
- Quota or profit: nothing.
- Teammate or haul: (not found, since the downed state is not documented).

**Scene, 4 friends**
- Four players stand at the train terminal and vote, three to one, for a higher-complexity retrieval objective.
- They enter the station and spread through the first rooms, calling out materials by voice.
- One player walks ahead out of earshot and opens a door that turns out to be a creature.
- A second player hears the shout cut off and warns the others not to rush in.
- A turret opens fire in the next room and the crew backs out with what they carry.
- (Further second-by-second detail of a typical moment: not found.)

**What experienced crews do:** Play with others rather than alone, warn each other over the distance voice, stop and listen for sounds before entering a room, and take valuable items first instead of searching every corner (from one review's advice; no community guide for the current version found).
**What changes it from round to round:** The generated station layout, the objective type, and the complexity level voted for.
**What carries from one round to the next:** Profit from extra sales (what it buys: not found); character cosmetics; anything else (not found).
**Can a new crew lose? Does it get harder?** Yes, by missing the quota inside three days; it gets harder with the complexity level the crew votes for, and whether the quota itself grows (not found).
