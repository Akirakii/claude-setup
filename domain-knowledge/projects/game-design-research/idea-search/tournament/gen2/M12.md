## Kept ideas

### M12-1 Double Bounce
- Rule (one sentence): The crew bounces on one big trampoline. Whoever lands right beside a friend as that friend pushes off flings them twice as high, and two friends landing side by side at the same instant kill each other's bounce.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the trampoline sheet.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the lanterns that count drift above normal jump height, so every catch takes two people. A launcher lands on purpose beside the flyer and gives up their own height. The flyer pushes off at that instant and steers in the air to the lantern. If Ana is coming down beside me, I either push off to ride her landing or step away so we don't land together. At 6, a flyer coming down from a high launch can launch someone else even higher.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): festival acrobats on the town square's big trampoline, catching paper lanterns the wind tore loose before they drift onto the thatched roofs.
- Look (one line): a night square, a sagging canvas sheet, glowing lanterns drifting at different heights over thatch.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, a small sheet, where at any moment one player is launching, one is flying and one is getting back into rhythm. At 6, a wide sheet with more lanterns at once. Side-by-side landings happen by accident more often, and two launches in a row reach the highest lanterns.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a lantern settles on a roof and the thatch catches. Jo landed next to Max at the same instant, both sank into the sheet and neither rose. Everyone saw the two of them flop.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): a simple wind simulation sends lanterns on new paths and at new heights each round. The highest ones need launch chains the crew invents, and the crew's bounce rhythm never lines up the same way twice.

### M12-2 Toolbox Drift
- Rule (one sentence): In a ship with no power and no gravity, the crew can only change course by throwing and catching one heavy tool chest: whoever throws it drifts backward, and whoever catches it drifts on with it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the tool chest (its flight) and everyone's drift through the hold.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): nobody can steer in open space. To send Kim toward the lifeboat hatch, Leo throws the chest from behind her. He drifts off the other way, so now someone must throw it to him head-on to stop him. A catcher chooses: grab it and ride, or let it fly past to someone beyond. A player touching a wall can push off, so throws are how the crew crosses the open middle. Every throw moves two people in opposite directions, so each throw depends on who is drifting where right now. The thrower picks the target and force; the catcher picks catch or let pass.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a mining-ship crew whose power died, crossing the tumbling cargo hold to the lifeboat before the air runs out.
- Look (one line): a huge dark hold, drifting crates and spinning cargo chains, helmet lights, one orange tool chest.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one chest and a triangle of throws. At 6, two chests in a bigger hold. The chests can meet mid-air, and a player who catches both shoots off fast.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Kim drifts out through the open loading door because Leo threw the chest to her too hard. She caught it and sailed past the last handrail. Everyone saw the throw.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the hold layout, the drifting crates and the spinning debris are generated and run on physics. Every throw changes two drifts at once, so the crew's paths never repeat.

### M12-3 Bed Board Tunnel
- Rule (one sentence): The crew crawls an escape tunnel one person wide whose only props are the boards already holding up its roof, so every board moved to a newly cracking spot leaves bare roof over somebody else.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the boards along the tunnel roof (which body is under which board).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): sand starts trickling over Sam. The nearest board is over Dana, two bodies back. Dana must crawl clear before it comes down, but nobody can pass anybody, so the players ahead must shuffle forward too. Then the board goes hand to hand to Sam. The digger at the front needs boards to push forward. The last board at the back comes free only once the last player has crawled past it. The back player decides when to pull, the middle players decide whether to keep or pass, and the front decides when to dig.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): prisoners of war digging out under the fence before dawn roll call, shoring the tunnel with boards from their bunks.
- Look (one line): side view of a sandy tunnel lit by fat lamps, bed boards over crawling bodies, sand trickling down.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, 3 players, 4 boards and a short tunnel. At 6, 6 players, 7 boards and a longer tunnel. Cracks open at a rate tied to crew size, and every seat sits under a board someone else may need.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (a crack trickles sand for 3 seconds before it falls).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the roof falls on Ben two seconds after Ana pulled the board above him to pass it forward. Everyone saw the sand pour where the board had been.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the soil is generated in bands: soft sand cracks often, clay rarely, and roots slow the digger. Cracks open at random spots, so the boards shuffle differently every round.

### M12-4 Ranger's Sack
- Rule (one sentence): The ranger stuffs every raccoon she catches into her sack and gets slower with each one she carries, and any sacked raccoon can wriggle out at any moment, landing right at her feet.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the ranger (one chase rule: she walks toward the last noise and runs at whoever she sees) and her sack.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a ranger with an empty sack outruns any raccoon, so someone gets caught on purpose to slow her. A sacked raccoon rides around the camp and picks where to drop out (by the cooler, by the fence hole), but lands in her sight. At that moment a free friend must clang a pot or show themselves to turn her away. Every drop-out speeds her up toward whoever she is chasing. Free raccoons run, grab and make noise; sacked raccoons ride and pick the moment.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): raccoons raiding a campsite's food at night.
- Look (one line): a moonlit campsite, tents and coolers, a lantern-swinging ranger with a wriggling sack.
- At 3 / at 6: how much each sacked raccoon slows her depends on crew size. At 3, one in the sack brings her down to about raccoon speed; at 6, it takes two. If the whole crew is in the sack, she walks back to her cabin and the night is lost.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (she is always visible and her sight cone is drawn).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Mo wriggled out while the ranger was chasing Lia. She sped up and bagged Lia with the last sandwich. Everyone saw Mo drop out.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the camp layout (tents, cars, coolers) is generated, and she reacts only to what the raccoons do. Who rides and when they drop out is the crew's call, so no two chases match.

### M12-5 Floe Haul
- Rule (one sentence): Each player rides their own drifting ice floe, and any two can throw a rope across and haul their floes together, which also drags every floe roped to either of them.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the floes on the current and the web of ropes between them.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): I want to pull Ana's floe out of an iceberg's path, but she is still roped to Cal. My haul drags Cal too, unless Cal cuts loose or hauls the other way at the same moment. A thrown rope needs a catcher with free hands. The actions are throw, catch, haul, cut, and hop across when two floes touch.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a polar camp whose ice broke up overnight, hauling itself back together and over to the ship before the current carries everyone past it.
- Look (one line): white floes on black water, red ropes snapping taut, a ship's lights far off.
- At 3 / at 6: each player carries one rope, so there are 3 ropes at 3 and 6 at 6. At 6, the web is big enough that one haul can tug three people.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Dee's floe hit the iceberg. Cal hauled toward Ben while Dee was hauling away, and the rope between them snapped tight and dragged her sideways. Everyone saw the haul.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the floe field, the currents and the cracks are generated, floes split mid-round, and the rope web is physics, so every round is a new tangle.

### M12-6 Bull Relay
- Rule (one sentence): The bull charges the nearest player it can see, so the crew passes it from one to another by stepping out of cover just as the last one ducks behind it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the bulls (one charge rule).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a bull outruns a person in the open, so whoever has its eye can only keep it a few seconds. The next player must step out where the bull will see them the moment the current one ducks, standing so the new charge heads toward the pen. If two step out, the bull takes the nearer one, maybe not the planned one. A charging bull smashes the cover it hits. The ducker picks when; the next player picks where.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): villagers herding bulls that broke loose on market day back into the pen.
- Look (one line): a whitewashed street, fruit carts and stalls, dust clouds, red scarves.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one bull, then two. At 6, two bulls, then four. A new bull breaks loose every 40 s until that cap. With two bulls loose, two players can end up holding both bulls' eyes at once.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (each bull's view cone is shown).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the bull bowled Pia over because Tom ducked behind the cart before she stepped out, and it turned on her at close range. Or Tom stepped out early, and the bull broke away toward him, away from the pen.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the market is generated, and the bulls smash carts and stalls as they charge, so the cover changes all round long.

### M12-7 Diver's Eyes
- Rule (one sentence): The key log holding the jam looks like every other log from above, so someone must dive under the jam to find it, and they can only come back up where friends have levered a gap open.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the floating driftwood jam (one physics pile).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the diver sees the key log from below, is running out of air, and picks a gap to come up through. The players above must lever that gap open, and that rolls the logs their friends are standing on. Once the diver is up and says which log is the key, the levers must shove it from where they stand. The diver picks where; the levers pick how and when.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a fishing village clearing a storm's driftwood jam from the harbour mouth before the fleet comes home in the dark.
- Look (one line): a grey harbour, a tangle of bleached driftwood, a diver's bubbles rising between the logs.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one diver, two levers and one key log. At 6, two key logs and two divers, with four levers splitting between two gaps.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": which log is the key. It is uncovered only by diving under. The jam hides it; no player does.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ola runs out of air under the jam because Pip levered the log next to her gap and rolled it shut. Or the fleet arrives because the levers shoved the log beside the one Ola called.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each jam is a generated physics pile, and the key log is a different log every time.

### M12-8 Ore Carts
- Rule (one sentence): Ore carts roll down from the quarry with no brakes and coast farther the more people are riding them, so the crew stops each cart at its own chute by jumping on and off carts as they roll.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the fan of tracks below the quarry and the carts rolling on it.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Raf hops off cart A so it stops short at chute 2, and lands on cart B beside it, so B now rolls farther. But Kim is riding B and needs it to stop short at chute 3. She must hop off at the same moment, or B rams the carts ahead. A rider who hops off near a switch can throw it for the next cart, so every hop both brakes a cart and sets a route. My hop changes the cart a friend is riding.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a night crew at a mountain quarry, sorting ore carts into their crusher chutes before the morning trains.
- Look (one line): floodlit tracks on a slope, carts with painted chute numbers, sparks and dust.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, a new cart every 8 seconds, so two are usually rolling. At 6, a new cart every 4 seconds, with three or four rolling side by side.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (chute numbers are painted on the carts).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a cart slams into the carts at chute 4 and tips its ore because Raf jumped onto it a second before Kim jumped off. Everyone saw him land.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the cart order, chute numbers and loads (full or empty) are random each shift. Each chute's line fills up during the shift, so the stopping spots keep moving.
