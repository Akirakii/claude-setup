# Proposal for the critic: Linked Climb

**Fantasy:** A group of people chained to each other start at the bottom of an underworld and want to climb out of it to the top.
One-player test: The game can be played alone, but then there is no chain partner, so the line about being chained to each other no longer holds.

**Players:** 1–4, one crew (a separate race mode sets two pairs against each other). **A session:** one climb of the whole tower, reported at about 3 to 8 hours for a crew, with autosave so it can be spread over several sittings.

**Feelings the game is known for** (2–4, each as a line a player says to a friend)
- (not found in the sources as player quotes; the lines below restate what the rules produce.)
- "You jumped before I was ready and took all four of us down."
- "I was hanging off the edge on the chain and you dragged me back up."
- "We lost half an hour of climbing to one bad step."

**The rules, one sentence each** (every rule a player must know; as many as the game really has, 8–14)
- All players are joined by one chain of fixed length, so nobody can move further from a neighbour than the chain allows.
- The chain is a simulated physical object that pulls on players, carries their weight and wraps around platforms and edges.
- The crew climbs one tall hand-made obstacle course by running and jumping from platform to platform.
- A player who falls hangs on the chain and drags on the others, and if the others cannot hold, the whole crew falls.
- In the standard mode there are no checkpoints, so the crew keeps whatever height it lands at, as low as the start.
- In the beginner mode there are checkpoints, and after a fall the crew can teleport from the pause menu back to the highest point it has reached.
- In the lava mode the rules are the same as standard, and lava also rises slowly from below the whole time.
- The host chooses how the chain is set up: as a line, where the front player leads, or as a closed loop, where every player moves freely but all must coordinate.
- Some sections have launchers that throw players across gaps or up to higher platforms.
- Ten wing pickups are scattered through the course; one source says they let players recover after a fall, and exactly how is (not found).
- A timer counts up during the climb and there is no time limit outside the lava mode.
- Rankings and achievements count only in the standard and lava modes.
- Progress saves by itself in the beginner and standard modes, and it can be resumed only in the same mode with the same team.
- Play is online or local on one screen with up to four input devices.

**How much there is:** One hand-made tower made of stacked themed zones (one guide names four zones over about 3,600 units of height; another names three checkpoint zones); four modes (beginner, standard, lava, two-against-two race); no enemies; ten wing pickups; no tools or upgrades; whether more maps were added later is (not found).

**Voice:** Whether the game has its own voice chat is (not found); guides tell crews to talk by voice the whole time to time jumps and call a stop.

**The choices** (3–5 lines a player really faces, "do X, or do Y?")
- Jump now, or wait until every teammate says ready?
- Go first across the gap and anchor the others, or let a steadier player go first?
- Try to haul a hanging teammate back up, or let go of the ledge and fall together to a safer platform?
- Take the short risky jump, or the longer safe way round?
- Play with checkpoints, or without them so the run counts for the rankings?

**What the other end decides in the same second** (per choice, or "nothing")
- Jump timing: every other player decides whether to jump on the same count or brace.
- Going first: the others decide where to stand so the chain has slack.
- Hanging teammate: the hanging player can do little; the ones on the ledge decide whether to pull or hold.
- Route: the rest must take the same route, so they agree or argue.
- Mode: decided once by the crew before the climb.

**Scene, 4 friends** (5–7 bullets, one real moment the game is known for, second by second)
- Four players stand in a row on a narrow beam, an hour above their last safe landing.
- The front player counts down for a jump to the next platform.
- Two jump on the count, one jumps late, and one says "not ready" and does not jump.
- The chain goes tight in the air and pulls the two jumpers back short of the platform.
- They hang below the beam, and their weight drags the late jumper off as well.
- The last player is pulled over the edge and all four fall, landing far below.
- (Assembled from the listed rules and guide warnings, not copied from one recorded match.)

**What experienced crews do:** They count down before every jump and treat one "not ready" as a full stop, wrap the chain around a solid platform to anchor the team, swing or drag a teammate across a gap that a plain jump cannot cross, practise the course alone first, and memorise the layout of each area.
**What changes it from round to round:** Nothing in the course, which is fixed; only the chosen mode, the chain setup, and the number of players change.
**What carries from one round to the next:** The saved height in the beginner and standard modes, the wings collected, achievements and ranking times; no strength or items carry over.
**Can a new crew lose? Does it get harder?** There is no fail state in the beginner and standard modes, only lost height; in the lava mode the lava reaching the crew ends the attempt (the exact result is (not found)); the store text says each layer is tougher than the one below it.
