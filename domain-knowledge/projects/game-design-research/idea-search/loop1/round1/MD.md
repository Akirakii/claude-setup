# Method D: shared goal, private reason (round 1)

## Method log

**Steps run**
1. Read `checks.md` only. No other research notes, no web while generating.
2. Listed 22 private stakes. Each is one rule from a short list, dealt or shown during a round:
   treasure (a gem spot only you see) · ward (keep friend X safe) · dare (do snack/act Y to X once) ·
   debt (you owe X two favours) · grudge/payback (X gets hit once) · pact (you and X both finish) ·
   glory/credit (you make the final move) · avoid (never hold the bad thing) · flavour (catch your own kind) ·
   bet (guess who finishes last) · crush (carry X longest) · shadow (stay near X) · keepsake (don't break
   your trinket) · spotlight (saves on camera) · hot curse (don't end holding it) · bounty (X gets dunked) ·
   secret shortcut · gift (hand X a gift) · hat (end wearing X's hat) · rival (end with more than X) ·
   thirst (X ends with the most) · fear (avoid one hazard type).
3. Built a co-op core for each stake where players act straight on each other's bodies: hook, shove,
   feed, tag, throw, catch, climb on, relight, drink. Each stake had to change which friend you act on
   next. 27 raw ideas.
4. Kept 8 with 8 different core rules, ranked P first, then formula, then R.
5. Novelty step: one web search per kept idea, plus one per swap-in. 11 searches. The 12th hit the
   session's web-search limit, so MD-8 is **unchecked** for novelty.

**Raw count:** 27.

**Gates the raw ideas failed most**
- Same core rule as another raw idea (6). Many stakes landed on the same verb. Example: the human bridge and
  Mud Backs both use a friend's body as your floor, and that friend can drop you.
- P2, no answer within seconds (4). Carried, bubbled or caught players mostly wait.
- R1, a plan card wins (2). One shield ring or one relight rotation covers everyone.
- N, already exists (3, all found in the novelty step).
- P3, nothing to hurt (1). Losing a hat costs nothing.

**Swaps (novelty step)**
1. Butterfingers Bakery (you can't run; a friend's shove moves you to catch falling cakes) → a Steam game
   (app 4789340) has the same core: you can't move yourself, only a friend moves you. Swapped in Fingertips.
2. Fingertips Rooftop (grab a falling friend's hand, pull them up or swing them down) → Heave Ho: grab
   friends' hands and swing them to safety. Same core. Swapped in Switcheroo.
3. Switcheroo (swap places with any friend you see, loot moves with you) → Suitchi: a 2-player co-op game
   about switching places. Same core. Swapped in Blood Bank. Its search hit the limit, so it's unchecked.

**Near-misses kept, scored ~ for N**
- MD-1: Skyhook, a local co-op game where players fire grappling hooks at each other. Brace or let go
  wasn't found.
- MD-3: SUPPERTIME, a jam game where you feed teammates, with no powers. Super Free Play: eat to transform.
- MD-5: buddy throw is common (BattleBlock Theater, Super Dungeon Bros). A catcher on the far side wasn't
  found.
- MD-6: a Roblox bomb-tag game. A holder who can't move, plus guards that follow the holder, wasn't found.
- MD-7: Spotlight Fantasy (board game, share lanterns), Torchr Runners (torches burn out). "Only a friend
  relights you" wasn't found.

**Good**
- Stakes that name a friend (ward, dare, debt, payback, thirst) pick who you act on next. Both P3
  (help or hurt) and R3 (pushback) pass without an extra rule.
- When the hub action is also the way to peek, F6 adds no rule. Feeding shows the dare, hooking shows
  the gem, drinking shows the card.
- A stake counts only if the team wins. That keeps the bending a bend, never a betrayal, so no
  hidden-role game creeps in.

**Bad**
- Most cores fall into one family: "only a friend can do X for you". P is strong, but scorers may read
  the 8 as one pattern.
- Treasure stakes only work when movement runs through friends (hook, throw). Elsewhere they turn solo.
- Credit stakes make everyone want the same thing ("pass to me!") instead of pulling people apart.
- Moves like brace or let go within 1 s, touch-pass and catches all need one machine to rule who
  touched first. Online lag isn't checked.

## Kept ideas

### MD-1 Hookline
- Rule (one sentence): Your hook only sticks to friends, and the friend you hook has one second to pick: brace (you fly to them) or let go (they fly to you).
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Thieves fleeing up a crumbling clock tower as floodwater rises, slinging themselves off each other.
- Aesthetic (one line): Chunky cartoon climbers with hook guns, grey stone ledges, a bright water line creeping up; snap-and-whoosh sounds.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I hook Ben. If he braces, I zip to him and fly a body-length past his ledge, which is the only way to gain height. If he lets go, he zips to me. A higher friend who hooks a lower one is rescuing them, and the lower one should let go. But if the lower one braces, the rescuer gets dragged down. So every hook is a one-second deal between two named people, and either side can help or hurt.
- Extra rules: (1) a ledge cracks 3 s after a second person lands on it; (2) each climb, every player gets one gem spot on the wall that only they can see. Flying through it banks a private point, which counts only if the whole crew tops out.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The ledge layout is reshuffled each climb from a few kits. Gem spots pull each person toward a different friend's line, so "lowest hooks highest" breaks the moment someone hooks sideways. Cracking ledges change who can safely brace for whom every few seconds.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the hook between two friends.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, each hook has only 2 targets, and one bad let-go strands someone by the water. At 6, more lines; ledges crowd and crack, and side trips multiply.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): Gem spots. The friend you're hooked to sees your gem glow during the pull. You hide them because friends won't brace for side trips.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Dana hooks Eli sideways to reach her gem. Eli braces, his ledge now holds two and cracks, and Eli drops into the flood. Everyone saw the sideways hook: Dana.
- Online lag: the hooked player's machine decides brace or let go; both see a short wind-up. Not checked.

### MD-2 Cowbell
- Rule (one sentence): The bull only charges whoever wears the bell, and any touch between two clowns moves the bell to the other one.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Rodeo clowns luring a mad bull into pens, slapping the bell from one clown to the next.
- Aesthetic (one line): Dusty arena, loud clown suits, a huge snorting red bull, banjo stings on every pass.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I tag Lu and the bell jumps to her. The bull swings toward her within a second, and she must run it toward a pen or tag the next clown. I can save her by touching her to take the bell back, or dump it on her when a barrel has her pinned.
- Extra rules: (1) the bull speeds up each time the bell changes hands (reset per pen), so passing a lot stops working; (2) each pen, each clown gets one card from a short list: Glory (you wear the bell when the bull enters the pen), Shield X (X never gets hit), Payback X (X gets hit once). Cards count only if all 3 pens fill before sunset. A hit clown is out 8 s; 3 hits lose the show.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Pens open at new spots each round. The bull's speed-up turns "pass early, pass often" against the team. The same pass means something different each round: Glory clowns hog the bell, Shield clowns dive in, and Payback clowns dump it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the bell.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, every pass goes to one of 2 friends, and a hog stands out. At 6, the arena is crowded and a careless brush passes the bell by accident.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): Cards. When a clown is hit, the card of whoever last passed them the bell is shown to all. You hide yours because nobody stands near a known Payback.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Kim holds Glory and won't give up the bell, then runs into a corner. Jay dives in to take it and gets gored: that's the third hit. Everyone saw Kim hog it.
- Online lag: touch-pass needs one host deciding who touched first. Not checked.

### MD-3 Spoon-Fed
- Rule (one sentence): You can't eat what you pick up; you stuff it into a friend's mouth, and they get its power for 5 seconds.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Goblins raiding a sleeping giant's pantry, hand-feeding each other magic snacks to break out before he wakes.
- Aesthetic (one line): A giant kitchen seen from mouse height, goblins with puffed cheeks, one bright colour per power.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I hold a chili to Tom's mouth, and he has a second to bite or turn his head. If he bites, he breathes fire for 5 s wherever he faces: the web ahead, or the hay behind with Sue in it. Other snacks: spinach (lift heavy things), gum (float up) and bean (a burp blast that shoves backward). The wrong snack for the wrong friend wastes it or hurts them: gum under the pepper shelf means a sneeze and noise.
- Extra rules: (1) snacks drop from the shelves at random in each room, and noise fills the giant's wake meter; (2) each room you get a private dare, "feed [friend] a [snack]". A done dare is a private point that counts only if the crew escapes.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Which snacks drop, and where, changes who should eat what. Dares push you to feed the wrong friend, who sees it and decides in the moment to bite or refuse.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): feeding a friend.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, each goblin feeds two friends and every power runs on a short clock. At 6, there are more mouths, more misfeeds and more noise.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): Dares. Feeding a friend shows you their dare for 2 s. You hide yours because friends won't bite from a known darer.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ana feeds Bo gum because it's her dare, while Bo stands under the pepper shelf. Bo floats up and sneezes, and the giant wakes. Everyone saw the pink gum leave Ana's hand.

### MD-4 Mud Backs
- Rule (one sentence): Everyone sinks slowly in the swamp, and you can climb onto a friend's back to get out, which makes them sink twice as fast until they shrug you off.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Lost explorers crossing a sinking swamp at dusk, using each other as stepping stones.
- Aesthetic (one line): Green-brown mud, fireflies, glugging bubbles, comic gasps as heads go under.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I climb onto Pip. I'm out of the mud, and from his back I can leap three times farther, but he sinks faster. Pip decides right away: carry me a few steps, or shrug me off into a deep patch.
- Extra rules: (1) a friend sunk to the neck is stuck until someone hauls them out (2 s, and the hauler sinks while hauling); (2) each crossing, every player gets a public debt, "let X climb you twice". Paying it banks a private point if the whole crew reaches the bank before dark.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Fog hides deep patches and stumps until you're close. Who is deepest changes every few seconds, so who climbs whom keeps changing. Debts pull people toward one named friend at bad moments.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): a friend's back.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, there's one back at a time, and two people stuck means nobody is left to haul. At 6, there are many backs, but more people are sinking too.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): None (debts are public; fog only hides the ground).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ivy shrugs Sam into a deep patch to make room for Rob, the friend her debt names. Sam goes neck-deep, and dark falls before anyone hauls him. Everyone saw the shrug: Ivy.

### MD-5 Big Top Toss
- Rule (one sentence): Nobody can jump the long gaps; a friend picks you up and throws you, and a friend on the far side must catch you or you drop to the net.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Circus acrobats escaping a burning big top, flinging each other between platforms.
- Aesthetic (one line): A red-and-gold tent, swinging spotlights, smoke, a drumroll on every throw.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I lift Zoe; she can wriggle free within a second. I throw her where I aim, and she steers a little in the air. Max, on the far platform, must raise his arms at the right moment. A bad aim or a skipped catch drops her to the net, sending her back to the start for 6 s. A friend alone on a platform can't leave by a long gap until someone is thrown over to them, so the crew must never strand anyone.
- Extra rules: (1) platforms catch fire one by one, and each round the next one to burn is shown 5 s ahead; (2) each show, you secretly bet on which friend gets out last. A right bet is a private point if everyone gets out.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Fire order and platform layout are new each show. Who is stranded where shifts after every throw. Bets make you throw others first and arrive "late" for one friend's catch.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the throw and the catch.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, every throw needs all three people, so one fumble strands someone. At 6, two throws fly at once, and paths cross.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): Bets. A dropped catch shows the catcher's bet to all. You hide yours because friends won't fly to you.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Cal bet that Ana would get out last, so twice his arms come up late for her. Ana is still on the platform when it burns. Everyone saw the late arms: Cal.

### MD-6 Hot Hands
- Rule (one sentence): Whoever holds the lit bomb can't move, so the crew gets it to the vault door only by throwing it friend to friend, and a dropped bomb blows in two seconds.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Bank robbers in striped shirts running a sputtering bomb down a guarded corridor to the vault.
- Aesthetic (one line): Cartoon noir: a round black bomb with a sparking fuse, flashlight cones, jazzy drums.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I throw to Rae, and she has a second to catch. Then she's frozen while she holds it, and she must throw on before the guards reach her. A throw to a friend with a guard behind her, or with no clear lane, hurts her.
- Extra rules: (1) guards always walk toward whoever holds the bomb, so the threat follows the team's passing; (2) each vault, you get a private card, "you plant it" or "[X] plants it". It counts only if the crew cracks 3 vaults. A bomb that blows anywhere but the door is one of 3 alarms.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The corridor layout and guard starts are new each vault. Guards drift to wherever the bomb is, so the safe lanes keep moving. Cards make people call for the bomb or steer it to one named friend.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the bomb.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, there are always just two targets, and guards close in fast. At 6, there are more lanes but more guards, and more voices calling.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): Cards. Catching from someone shows you their card. You hide yours because nobody passes to a hog.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Max's card says he plants it, so he holds a beat too long waiting for the door lane. A guard tackles him, and the bomb drops and blows. Everyone saw him freeze: Max.
- Online lag: catch timing is lag-sensitive. Not checked.

### MD-7 Last Light
- Rule (one sentence): Your lantern dies 12 seconds after it's lit, and only touching a friend's lit lantern relights it, which takes 3 seconds off theirs.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A night watch on a haunted island, lighting beacons while ghost hands grab anyone whose light goes out.
- Aesthetic (one line): A pitch-black island, warm pools of orange light, blue ghost hands in the grass, crickets.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): Joe has 2 s left and runs at me. I let him touch, losing 3 s, or I step away. Then I'm the low one and must find someone. Help is relighting; hurt is stepping away.
- Extra rules: (1) a lit beacon refills anyone touching it, and the next beacon's spot shows only once the last one is lit; (2) each night you're secretly given one friend to keep lit. It counts only if all 5 beacons are lit by dawn. A dark player is dragged back to the last beacon, out for 10 s; 4 drags lose the night.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Nobody knows where the next beacon is until it appears, so who runs ahead changes. The low friend changes every few seconds. Wards pull light toward one person.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): lantern-to-lantern touch.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, there are only two other flames, so one step away is deadly. At 6, there are more flames, but more people drawing on them.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): Wards. A relight shows the giver's ward to the receiver. You hide yours because others would drain your ward.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Kai relights only his ward, Mo. Lea runs to Kai with 1 s left, and he steps away. Lea goes dark: the fourth drag. Everyone saw Kai step back.
- Weak spot: "move in pairs" may harden into a plan card (R ~).

### MD-8 Blood Bank
- Rule (one sentence): Hold a friend for one second to move one drop of blood between you in the direction you choose, and they can twist free before it lands.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A vampire family racing through their castle to the crypt before sunrise, sharing and stealing blood to cross the sunbeams.
- Aesthetic (one line): A gothic castle in purple and gold, dusty sunbeams through tall windows, caped cartoon vampires, a sizzle on every burn.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): A sunbeam burns one drop per second. Vic needs 4 drops to cross the next one, so he grabs me to drink, and I have a second to twist free or let him. I can push drops into a friend about to cross, or drain one who is hoarding.
- Extra rules: (1) shutters open at random as the sun rises, a new beam every 20 s, and vampires regain blood slowly in shadow; (2) each night you get a private card, "end with more blood than X" or "X ends with the most". It counts only if every vampire reaches the crypt.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Beams open at random, so who needs blood, and where, changes. Drops shift between people every few seconds. Cards bend who you feed and who you drain.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the blood transfer.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, each vampire has 2 partners and blood runs thin. At 6, there are more donors but more beams to cross.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): Cards. Drinking from someone shows you their card. You hide yours because people dodge a known drainer.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ida's card says she must end with more blood than Vic, so she drains him just before his beam. Vic burns to ash mid-crossing. Everyone saw Ida's grab.
- Novelty: unchecked (the session hit its web-search limit).
