# Anchors (earlier ideas with known scores, re-scored blind)

## Kept ideas

### Manchor-1 Bull Relay
- Rule (one sentence): The bull charges the nearest player it can see, so the crew passes it from one to another by stepping out of cover just as the last one ducks behind it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the bulls (one charge rule).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a bull outruns a person in the open, so whoever has its eye can only keep it a few seconds. The next player must step out where the bull will see them the moment the current one ducks, standing so the new charge heads toward the pen. If two step out, the bull takes the nearer one, maybe not the planned one. A charging bull smashes the cover it hits. The ducker picks when; the next player picks where.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): villagers herding bulls that broke loose on market day back into the pen.
- Look (one line): a whitewashed street, fruit carts and stalls, dust clouds, red scarves.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one bull, then two. At 6, two bulls, then four. A new bull breaks loose every 40 s until that cap. With two bulls loose, two players can end up holding both bulls' eyes at once.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (each bull's view cone is shown).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the bull bowled Pia over because Tom ducked behind the cart before she stepped out, and it turned on her at close range. Or Tom stepped out early, and the bull broke away toward him, away from the pen.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the market is generated, and the bulls smash carts and stalls as they charge, so the cover changes all round long.

### Manchor-2 Striking Gang
- Rule (one sentence): The holder moves a glowing bar on the anvil while strikers swing heavy hammers that land one second after they start, and the bar must be beaten to the target shape before it cools, because a blow on cold metal cracks it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the hot bar on the anvil (its shape and its glow).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a striker commits a blow at a spot. The holder sees the swing start and slides or turns the bar so the right part lands under it, or pulls it clear. The other striker must swing on the off-beat (two hammers at once bounce off each other) and aim where the holder is moving the bar. The holder decides when to go back to the forge to reheat, which stops every striker. Holder = place, turn, reheat; strikers = where and when to commit.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a village forge crew rushing to make an anchor before the ship sails.
- Look (one line): a dark smithy, a bar fading from orange to red to grey, a shower of sparks with every blow.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = one holder and two alternating strikers. 6 = a long anchor shank held at each end by two holders who must move it together, and four strikers in two pairs on either side. Target shapes come from a list of ≤ 20, and everyone sees the target drawn on the wall.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the bar cracks with a loud snap right where a striker hit it after it turned grey. Or the bar flies off the anvil because the holder slid it into a falling hammer. Either way, one person is seen at the spot.

### Manchor-3 Megalith Haul
- Rule (one sentence): A huge stone slides only over logs, and each log that rolls out behind it must be carried round and laid in front before the stone reaches bare ground.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the stone on its row of rollers.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Rope players pull the stone and set its pace. Runners grab the log freed at the back, run it forward and lay it, and the angle they lay it at turns the stone. As a runner I lay my log to fix or continue the turn the last runner made. As a rope player I slow down when no runner is coming with a log. On a downhill the stone rolls by itself, and rope players must switch to the back ropes and hold it, so the jobs flip with the ground. 2 extra rules: rope pull, log angle steers.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a stone-age village moving a standing stone to the henge before the solstice sunrise.
- Look (one line): pale chalk hills at dawn, a grey slab riding on pale logs, hide ropes, people in wool.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = 1 on the rope, 2 runners, slow stone, short path. 6 = 3 rope, 3 runners, faster stone, longer path. Anyone can drop a rope and become a runner.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the stone's front edge passes the last log and digs in, or tips off a skewed log into a ditch. The gap is where one runner was due, still holding their log, or the rope player who kept pulling; everyone sees it.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): generated ground each haul (slopes, mud where logs sink, boulders and ditches to steer round). Logs split after a few passes, so supply varies. A skewed log turns the stone, so no two hauls take the same line.

### Manchor-4 Blind Spot
- Rule (one sentence): Each player sees the traps around every teammate but never the traps around themselves.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the trap field (one generated floor).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): everyone fetches parts of a machine scattered across the field, and the parts need two people to lift. To warn a friend you must look at them, so you stop watching your own route. Who I watch depends on who just got close to a trap, and my safe path depends on who is watching me. (Honest note: the jobs are mostly the same for everyone.)
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): treasure hunters in a trapped temple who can see their friends' doom but not their own.
- Look (one line): stone temple tiles, traps glowing red only on other players' screens, a soft beam showing who watches whom.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 each player has two watchers; at 6 there are more eyes but more voices talking over each other, and the field grows.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": the traps near you. They are uncovered only when a friend tells you or you step on one. No player has a reason to hide them; the game hides them.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ben walked onto spikes while Zoe, the only one looking his way, turned to lift her part. The watch beams show she left him.

### Manchor-5 Shove Crew
- Rule (one sentence): No one can walk; you can only shove a friend away from you, so the crew crosses the board by pushing each other.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the players' bodies on one board.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): where I can go depends entirely on who stands behind me and when they shove. My best shove depends on where the last shove just left each friend. At any moment one player lines up, one shoves, and one is the one being moved.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): clumsy penguins escaping melting ice by shoving each other across it.
- Look (one line): flat ice floes from above, cracks spreading, and round penguins skidding in a line.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 every shove matters and someone always ends up stuck behind. At 6 it is a crowded, chaotic billiards game, with more angles and more people in the way.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a penguin skids into the water. The one who shoved is seen doing it, at the angle they chose.

### Manchor-6 Cage Eyes
- Rule (one sentence): The troll in the yard is invisible to free players and visible only to players it has caught, who watch from the cage and talk the free players past it, but kicking the can frees everyone and leaves the crew blind again.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the troll (one simple chase rule: walks toward noise, chases whoever it sees).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): free players see the yard and the eggs but not the troll; caged players see only the troll and the players, not the eggs. Runners grab eggs on the cage's calls; caged players throw pebbles to lure the troll away. Every rescue takes away the crew's eyes, so the crew must decide who stays caged, and at the start someone has to get caught on purpose.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): village kids stealing eggs from a troll's yard before dawn.
- Look (one line): moonlit yard, a cage hanging from a pole, big footprints appearing in the mud.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 one caged and two running is the sweet spot, and a second capture forces a blind rescue; at 6 the crew picks its own mix of eyes and runners.
- Hidden: where the troll is; uncovered only by being caught; the troll hides by itself, and the crew hides from it by sneaking.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): dawn comes with 4 of 5 eggs because Ana kicked the can early, freed the only eyes and got caught; or Tom ran left when the cage shouted right.
