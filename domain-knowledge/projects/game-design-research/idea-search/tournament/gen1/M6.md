# M6 "systems analogy": generator output (gen1, 2026-11-10)

## Method log
- Steps taken: read formula.md and `~/.claude/design-anti-patterns.md` (user rule 11; it holds no game ideas).
  Listed 34 coupling structures from biology, physics, engineering and economics. Wrote one line on how
  each one's parts depend on each other, mapped the parts onto 3–6 players, then added a fantasy and a
  look last. No web search, and nothing else under research_notes/ or reports/ was read.
- Raw count: 34 structures, giving 34 candidates. I merged two pairs that shared a rule (ant trails into
  bat sonar, and antagonist muscles into insect gait), then kept 8 with 8 different core rules.
- Most common failure was **F3 (13 of the 24 dropped)**. Many structures boil down to one shared sum
  (gears, hydraulics, seesaw, public goods). Others are a pipeline where the downstream part only waits
  (relay, clotting cascade, convoy, canal locks, keystone). The second most common was **N (11 dropped, some only ~)**.
  Famous structures already have games: a series circuit is a hand-holding chain, lock and key is
  Hole in the Wall, a supply chain is the Beer Game, a crossed control loop is Spaceteam, and mimicry is
  Among Us.
- Strengths: physical structures give low content almost for free (F1: one physics rule plus generated
  terrain). Blame is local and visible, because when a part fails, it is one body in one place. Mapping
  "players = parts" gives a crew size that scales on its own (one leg, rope, bar or flock per player).
- Weaknesses: the method produces one-rule physics toys. It rarely produces hidden information or
  semi-co-op stakes (only 1 of the 8 kept ideas has a traitor). The fantasy gets bolted on after the
  logic, which risks the user's triad. And the strongest analogies are the ones games have already used.

## Kept ideas

### M6-1 Stone Walker
- Rule (one sentence): The crew walks one giant creature with one leg per player, and the creature topples if its body leaves the area under its planted legs.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the creature's body (where its weight sits over the planted feet).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a planted leg pushes the body, and a lifted leg swings to a new foothold. I may lift only after my neighbours have planted and shifted the weight away from me. The moment a friend plants, I am free to swing. At any instant some players push while one or two swing, which are different actions that fail without each other.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): shrine keepers who have climbed inside an old stone spider, carrying the island's last egg off as the sea rises.
- Look (one line): side view of a mossy stone creature, each leg painted a player's colour, waves climbing up behind it.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is a three-legged beast that can never stand on two legs, so it must lurch: two players lean the body forward while the third swings fast (hard, and new crews can fall). At 6 it is a six-legged insect that can walk in two alternating sets of three, but six voices must agree on the rhythm.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the creature tips toward one leg and slides into the water. Everyone sees that the red leg lifted while the body was still over it.

### M6-2 Parade Balloon
- Rule (one sentence): Each player holds one tether of a giant helium balloon, and the balloon drifts wherever the taut ropes pull it as the crew walks it down a windy street.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the balloon, meaning its position and which ropes are taut.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): when a lamppost, sign or overpass nears my side, I must step around it or let my rope go slack. The players opposite must take up the pull at that same moment, or the balloon swings into the post. When a gust hits, the windward players dig in while the others run to re-angle their ropes. Who pulls and who gives way changes every few seconds with the street.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): underpaid parade handlers wrangling a giant inflatable duck through a storm because the show must go on.
- Look (one line): three-quarter view of a confetti-strewn city street, a huge glossy cartoon balloon, and tiny handlers in matching jackets.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is a triangle, so one slack rope makes the balloon lurch at once (tense, losable). At 6 it is a ring with more margin, but obstacles hit three or four ropes at once and the calls overlap.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the balloon pops on a lamppost on Sam's side. Sam let go early, or Kim yanked from the far side while Sam was stepping around. Both moves are seen in the moment.

### M6-3 Burning Big Top
- Rule (one sentence): The crew crosses a row of trapezes by leapfrog: the player at the back lets go and must be caught and swung on by each friend in turn, swinging to meet them at the right moment.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the pendulum swings (one physics rule: pumping in time adds height).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the flyer chooses when to let go, and the catcher must pump so that their swing meets the flyer at that same instant. Both decide at once. A catcher holding a flyer chooses when to throw them on, and the next catcher has to read that throw. Players not in this catch pump to get into phase for the next one.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a disgraced circus troupe escaping their own burning tent mid-show.
- Look (one line): side view of a candy-striped big top, spotlights and creeping flames, silhouettes in sequinned colours.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 each flyer is caught twice per leapfrog and every catch is critical. At 6 the chain is longer, so two flyers can be in the air at once and catchers must juggle phases.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Mia falls into the flames. The replay of the moment shows that either she let go early or Tom's swing was still on its way back.

### M6-4 Bloodstream Medics
- Rule (one sentence): Each player sees only one kind of germ, and a germ can be destroyed only by a different player than the one who tagged it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the tags on cells (who tagged what, visible to all).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): I tag what only I can see, and I can only kill what others have tagged. So I tag near whoever is free to shoot, and I shoot the tags whose germs are closest to the heart. Each kill needs two different players doing two different actions, and every tag pulls a friend's aim.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): microscopic medics racing through a patient's veins to stop an infection reaching the heart.
- Look (one line): soft glowing pinks and reds, translucent cells, and each player's germ kind tinted through their own coloured visor.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 there are three germ kinds and no traitor. Every kill needs one of the other two, so it is tight and losable. At 6 there are six kinds and one secret traitor (from 4 players up).
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": from 4 players up, one player is secretly working for the infection. The traitor tags healthy cells and goes quiet about their own germ kind. They are uncovered two ways: a healthy cell that gets shot bleeds white and shows the name of whoever tagged it, and the vital-signs bar shows which germ kind is spreading. The traitor hides because they win if the heart stops.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the heart stops. Blue germs got through untagged, and blue is Ben's kind. Or three white bleeds all carried Ana's tag.

### M6-5 Circle of Herds
- Rule (one sentence): Each player herds one flock that can only live by grazing on the next player's flock in a closed ring, and every flock must survive the season.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the meadow and the flocks on it (one flocking-and-eating rule: touching your prey flock eats it, touching your predator's flock loses you animals).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): I steer my flock toward my prey flock to feed, and away from my predator's flock. The owner of my prey decides whether to offer a few stragglers or hide, and my predator's owner decides whether to hold back. Each pair is a live two-way deal, and every player is predator to one friend and prey to another.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): shepherd spirits of a valley keeping the cycle of life turning through one hard winter.
- Look (one line): top-down watercolour meadow, each flock a coloured swarm, and frost creeping in from the edges.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is a rock-paper-scissors ring, where one greedy flock starves the next within seconds. At 6 the ring has six links, but all six flocks share one shrinking meadow, so contacts nobody planned happen constantly.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the green flock dies out and the ring collapses. Everyone saw purple's flock sit on green for ten seconds, or green's owner never let it graze.

### M6-6 Salt Flat Crossing
- Rule (one sentence): Each player is immune to one kind of storm and casts a shelter behind them against it, so the crew survives each incoming storm only by getting the right friend in front.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the storm fronts crossing the plain (one weather rule) and the shelter shadows they cast.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): when a fire front shows on the horizon, the fireproof player runs to face it and the others tuck in behind. When a frost front comes from the other side a few seconds later, the frost-proof player turns the other way and someone must switch shelters. My spot depends on who is covering which side at that instant. Each shelter covers only two people, so somebody always has to choose whom to cover.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): elemental spirits escorting each other across a cursed salt flat to the last oasis.
- Look (one line): blinding white salt flat, coloured storm walls rolling in, and short sharp shadows behind each player.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 there are three storm kinds, and each shelter exactly covers the other two, so being one step late kills. At 6 there are six kinds and storms arrive two at a time, so the crew must split into two huddles facing opposite ways.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Lee freezes. The frost-proof player was standing on the wrong side, and their shadow is visibly pointing away from Lee.

### M6-7 Heavy Boots
- Rule (one sentence): In a tumbling zero-gravity station any player can turn heavy, which anchors them in place and pulls every light player toward them.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the pull between players (who is heavy right now).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): to cross a gap, the friend ahead goes heavy and anchors while I go light and drift to them. Then I anchor so they can let go and push off. If two players go heavy at once, the light ones are pulled between them, maybe into spinning debris. So the heavy call depends on who is drifting where right now, and the anchor and the drifter do opposite things at the same moment.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a salvage crew whose gravity boots got wired together, escaping a station that is breaking apart.
- Look (one line): dark station interior, slow-spinning debris, and heavy players glowing with a pull ring.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is a three-person leapfrog where every swap is critical. At 6, pulls from several heavy players add up, so the crew needs a rhythm to stop yanking each other off course.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ana is pulled into a spinning beam because Joe went heavy while she was mid-drift toward Kim. His glow lit up at that exact moment.

### M6-8 Blind Lamps
- Rule (one sentence): Each player's lamp lights the dark for everyone except its holder, and the cave creatures freeze only while lit.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the light cones.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): I never see what my own beam shows, so friends must call where to point it ("left, there's one on Kai"). I see my own path only in their beams. When someone swings their lamp away to cover a friend, the ground under me goes dark, so I aim my lamp to cover the direction they just dropped. Everyone aims for others and calls for others at once.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): miners trapped after a collapse, feeling their way out of a cave where things move in the dark.
- Look (one line): pitch-black screen with sharp cones of warm lamplight, where you see every beam but your own.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 each player sees by only two beams, so every turn of a head matters (losable). At 6 there are five beams to see by, but more creatures come from more sides and the calls overlap.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": what your own beam shows is hidden from you. You uncover it only through friends' calls. Nobody hides it on purpose (no motive), so F6 is at best ~.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a creature reaches Kai from the north. Lee's beam had been holding the north and visibly swung away without a word.
