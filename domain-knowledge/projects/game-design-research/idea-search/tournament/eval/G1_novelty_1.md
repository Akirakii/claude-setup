# Novelty batch 1 (24 ideas)

### G101 Lone Scout
- Rule (one sentence): The fake floor tiles show as holes only on the screen of a player with no friend within 15 m, and one player may secretly want the crew to fail.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the temple floor, generated each run, where fake tiles look solid to anyone near a friend. A player alone slowly freezes, so scouts must come back.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): The group can't see the fake tiles while together, so someone steps away to see them and calls them out. The group picks the route while the scout warns, and the scout must stand where the group is heading. As the cold drains the scout, another player takes over.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): tomb raiders in a temple whose traps reveal themselves only to someone alone in the dark.
- Look (one line): a torch-lit stone hall, the group in warm light, the scout in cold blue light where the holes glow.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is one scout and two walkers, and a traitor appears only in some runs, since one traitor is a third of the crew. At 6 two scouts can stand on different sides and check each other's calls.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": which tiles are fake (uncovered by going alone, or by throwing a torch onto a tile); who the traitor is (uncovered by sending a second scout and comparing calls). The traitor hides because they win if the crew falls short.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Tom calls "third tile on the left is safe" and Mia drops through it. Tom is blamed in the moment; whether it was a lie or a mistake is the argument after.

### G104 Sunbeam Relay
- Rule (one sentence): The only light in a cave is a sunbeam from the entrance, and the crew carries it deeper by bouncing it from one player's hand mirror to the next. Anyone outside the light slowly freezes.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the beam. Extra rule: the sun moves, so the angle of the beam coming in keeps changing.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): my mirror angle depends on where the player before me sends the beam and where the player after me is standing. When anyone moves, everyone after them must re-aim. The first player follows the moving sun, the middle players relay, and the last player burns through cobwebs to open the way deeper.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): explorers carrying daylight into a frozen cave.
- Look (one line): one hard gold beam zigzagging through blue-black rock, frost growing on whoever is left in the dark.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, the beam bounces three times and the caves are shallow, with sharp turns. At 6, a long zigzag chain where one slip leaves many players in the dark.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": the cave ahead is dark and is uncovered by swinging the beam forward. Nobody gains by hiding it.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the beam stops at one player's mirror because they turned to look at a noise, and the three players after them freeze. The blame is plain: the beam visibly ends at that mirror.

### G116 Hump Yard
- Rule (one sentence): Freight cars roll one at a time down a hill into a fan of tracks, and the crew must cut each car loose, throw its switches and ride and brake it, so it meets the cars on its chalked track gently before the shift ends.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the track fan below the hill (its switches and the rolling cars).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the cutter's release timing sets the gap between cars. If two cars bound for different tracks run too close, the switch player can't throw the switch between them and shouts "hold!". Riders walk back up after each car, so the cutter releases only when a rider is ready. A rider brakes according to how far down the track the last car stopped. Cutter = when, switch player = where, riders = how fast.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a night crew in a 1950s railway yard, sorting a long freight train before the morning trains leave.
- Look (one line): a floodlit yard from above, chalk numbers on the car sides, sparks from the brake shoes.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = cutter, one switch player, one rider (the cutter must pace the train to the lone rider's walk). 6 = cutter, two switch players (upper and lower switches), three riders, and a longer train. One yard; the car order and destinations are random each shift.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (each car's track number is chalked on its side).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a boxcar slams into the cars ahead and jumps the rails, spilling crates, and its rider is seen braking late. Or a car runs down the wrong track while the switch player is still pulling the lever, a beat too late.

### G167 Fence of Light
- Rule (one sentence): A beam links each player to the next in a fixed ring, and the animals can't cross a beam.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the ring of beams, which snaps if two neighbours are more than 12 m apart. Sheep flee the nearest player (one simple flocking rule), and they must be penned before night.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): My spot only means something next to my two neighbours. To let sheep into the pen, one pair must open their gap at the right moment while the rest push from behind, and a stray needs a flank runner whose neighbours must stretch to cover. Roles come from where you stand and change every moment.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): shepherds with light-staffs bringing a glowing flock home before dark.
- Look (one line): green hills at dusk, light beams between staffs, fluffy sheep.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 the fence is a small triangle that must take the flock in bites. At 6 it is a hexagon that can sweep the field, but six people must keep in step, and the ring order puts friends who can't see each other side by side.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Eva runs ahead to cut off one stray, her beam to Jon snaps, and twelve sheep pour through. The snapped beam glows red at Eva's end.

### G170 Purse Seine
- Rule (one sentence): One boat lays a net in a circle around a fleeing fish school while the skiff holds the net's other end, and the winch player pulls the net's bottom shut, which must come after the circle closes and before the school dives out underneath.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the net (a floating ring between the two boats, open at the bottom until it's pulled shut).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the school flees boats and noise by one simple flocking rule. The seine driver chooses how wide and fast to circle. The skiff fights the current and chooses when to swing in to meet the seiner. The winch player watches how deep the school is (how dark the shoal looks) and how close the two boats are, then chooses when to close the bottom. At 4–6, chase boats drive the school toward the net or block its escape, but their noise also makes it dive.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a sardine purse-seine crew at dawn, filling the hold before the boat has to head in.
- Look (one line): a blue sea seen from above, the cork line drawn across it in a ring, a silver flashing shoal.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = seine boat, skiff, winch. 6 = those three plus three chase boats, against bigger and faster schools. One sea; the schools are flocks, not hand-made.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (the shoal's depth shows in how dark it looks).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the shoal pours out of the gap beside the skiff because the skiff swung in late, or dives under because the winch closed too late. The leak happens right beside one person's boat.
