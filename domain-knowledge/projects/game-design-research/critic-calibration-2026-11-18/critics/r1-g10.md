# Critic round 1: Hot Bags (g10)

## The problem underneath
The fantasy does not need a friend: the proposal's own one-player test says the line still works alone.
The only thing a friend adds is reading orders out from the train.
No rule says when orders arrive or how long an order is, so nothing written stops a courier reading the terminal before leaving.
"That's the wrong dish, run" is caused by a rule (wrong food makes the customer attack).
"Something is on the only path" is caused by a rule (creatures roam and appear suddenly).
"Deliver what you're holding" is caused by the timer plus the lost-day rule.
"I can barely hear you" has no written rule behind it; radio fade is (not found), so it is unknown, not counted.

## The scene, second by second
0:00 the reader reads two orders; three couriers leave, and nobody decides anything about a friend.
Couriers open bags apart from each other: three people doing the same job side by side.
One courier walks back and cooks alone; no friend is in that act.
The wrong dish is handed over: this is the first moment a friend's act (a half-heard order) reaches another player.
The customer attacks one courier; the others can only listen.
The creature on the path: no rule lets a friend clear it, lure it or pull the courier out.
The last delivery is a lone gamble by one courier.

## Fewest and most players
At 1 there is no reader and no talk, and the game still runs (the proposal says very hard, not impossible).
At 2 a reader on the train leaves two hands outside, so both read the terminal and both walk: the train seat is dead.
At 7 six couriers ask one reader on a radio with no push-to-talk, so the reader's queue is the game and the couriers wait.
Nothing written scales the target or the orders with crew size, so six carriers against one target is unpriced.

## The plans
The strategy that always wins: the reader says each order, the courier says it back, and nobody delivers before the read-back.
By the fifth session: one friend on the train with a card (order, ingredients, cooked or not), couriers in fixed lanes with one order each.
A perfect crew's round: no wrong dish, no chase, food that needs no cooking picked first; the wrong-dish chase only returns in the last-minute gamble.
The timer does force that gamble from outside the crew, so the core act is not purely a mistake.

## Findings
S1 (severe, only this design): the one coupling is a relay of text any player can read alone; the need for the friend rests on an unwritten rule (when orders appear).
S2 (severe, missing baseline part): no help with a price in the moment; the reader pays once, at day start, by not carrying.
M1 (medium, only this design): couriers never act on each other or on one shared thing; foraging, cooking and delivering are parallel solo jobs.
M2 (medium, only this design): the train seat is dead at 2 and a jammed queue at 7; no rule scales with crew size.
M3 (medium, only this design): the train holds the orders, cameras, radar, cooking table and shop, so one seat can take every decision and the couriers become hands.
B1 (the baseline has this too, row 1): "walk back to check the order" is answered by asking the reader.
B2 (the baseline has this too, row 3): flashlight and batteries lower the need for a guiding voice.
B3 (the baseline has this too, row 4): "cook or find ready food" and "sell or keep" are crew sums with no friend; the proposal writes "Nothing" for both.
B4 (the baseline has this too, row 6): a friend who stays on the train keeps the whole-crew death from happening, if the train is safe (unwritten).
B5 (the baseline has this too, row 7): fixed roles and a read-back card by session five; the chase only happens in mistakes.

## Co-op check
1 Coupled: fail, a courier who read the order delivers without anyone.
2 Split: pass, orders live on the train and the finds live in the field, and anyone can sit down.
3 Talk: pass, the day timer and the radio make people ask.
4 Blame: pass, the wrong dish gets one visible courier chased.
5 Not easy: pass, new crews die fast and the target rises.
6 Not solo: fail, one player can play and finish, and one carrier clears an order alone.
7 Two-way: pass, reader and courier each decide in the same second on choices 1 to 3; the reader as the one who knows everything is noted under M3.
Lone survivor: not stuck, since every job is a one-player job.
Dying on purpose: no gain found.

## The four baseline parts
Priced help on good nights: missing, the only help is the relay and its price is a seat, not a cost per help.
Rising pressure that breaks the plan: present, the rising target pushes crews to two orders at once and pulls the reader off the train.
Two threats at once: present, a one-hit creature needs watching while the timer and an angry customer punish waiting.
Carry-over: present, money, gear and bought map types change the next day.

## Could not break
1. The wrong-dish attack: one rule turns a talking mistake into a visible, physical chase.
2. Orders on the train and food in the field: a split by position, as long as orders change during the day.
3. The rising money target with cash and gear carried over.
4. Fresh days: map, orders, food spots and creatures all change without new content.
5. One item per hand: no super-carrier, and cooking forces trips back.
6. The end-of-day gamble: the timer makes "deliver what you hold" the right call some days.
