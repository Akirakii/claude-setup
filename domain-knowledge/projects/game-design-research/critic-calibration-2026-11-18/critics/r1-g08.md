# Critic round 1: g08, Deep Disposal

## The problem underneath
The fantasy does not need a friend: the proposal's own one-player test says the line works alone.
The need for a friend comes from one rule only: the submarine has no window, so the wheel is blind without the sonar reader.
That rule lives on the trip; at the site all swim out and "nothing found" for anyone left in the submarine.
So the dive (7–8 minutes, where the timer, the air and the deaths are) has no rule that makes one friend's act need another's.
Feelings 1 and 2 are caused by the windowless rule; feeling 3 by the two-item cap plus the alarm; feeling 4 by "a dead body is evidence".
Feelings 1, 3 and 4 are mistakes a good crew avoids; only feeling 2 survives good play.

## The scene, second by second
Trip: the reader calls, the pilot turns, the throttle player holds a lever: one seat knows, two obey.
The blip closes in, the answer is "slow down", and the engine goes silent: the same answer the police ask for.
Dive: four swim out, two push a cart, one baits the mutant, each watches their own air.
Alarm: one sum (items held against stars), worked out by each player alone.
Death near the exit: a friend drops one item and drags the body; the dead player then waits for the dive to end.

## Fewest and most players
1 player: the reader and pilot are one head, so the split information is gone and the trip is walking between three stations.
8 players: three stations and one wrench make four jobs; the other four have nothing written for them on the trip (trip length not found).
Whether the required evidence scales with crew size is not written; eight pairs of hands against one timer is unchecked.

## The plans
Strategy that always wins: run slow and silent the whole trip.
Nothing written prices slow: "reach the site sooner" has no clock, fuel or cost behind it, and slow also answers the closing blip.
By the fifth session: fixed reader, pilot and throttle; a cart pair, a bait, air trips on a rhythm, out before the alarm, snacks for the stars.
A perfect crew's round: silent trip, no hit, no leak, no death, no star; the wanted level never moves and nothing else rises by itself.

## Findings
1. SEVERE, only this design: the one choice with two ends (fast or silent) has a free option, since slow costs nothing written.
2. Medium, only this design: the dive is carrying in parallel; the split information that makes the trip co-op stops at the airlock.
3. Medium, only this design: the pressure rises only from the crew's own failures, falls with snacks, and going deeper is optional (required-amount growth is unknown, so not severe).
4. Medium, only this design: a dead player has no job until the dive ends, up to 7 minutes.
5. Medium, only this design: the rule doesn't say what a friend's body pays in the shredder; if it pays, a death next to the airlock is a score machine.
6. Medium, only this design: at 1 the core coupling is gone and at 8 half the crew is cargo on the trip.
7. The baseline has this too (a tool that shows the answer): the wave-matching station tells what a blip is.
8. The baseline has this too (bought tools lower the need for friends): cart, body-part detector, see-through vision.
9. The baseline has this too (one sum for the whole crew): one more body, air now, deeper or stay, the friend's body.
10. The baseline has this too (one end only stands): the throttle player and the pilot follow the reader's call.
11. The baseline has this too (fixed roles with a card of answers, fear only in mistakes): blips learned by signal, three feelings are mistakes.

## Co-op check
1. Coupled: pass, on the trip only (a blind wheel fails without the reader); the dive fails it.
2. Split: pass (stations split the information by position, and anyone can sit at any).
3. Talk: pass (a closing blip and the alarm force speech).
4. Blame: pass (a hull hit, a leak and a body to drag are physical and seen).
5. Not easy: pass (a new crew was near a full wanted level after two dives; stars add police).
6. Not solo: fail (1 player is offered, and one player may hold several jobs).
7. Two-way: fail (four of five choices have nothing at the other end; in the fifth the pilot follows).
Lone survivor: not stuck, but three bodies against two hands means stars; dying on purpose: see finding 5.

## The four baseline parts
Priced help on good nights: present; dragging a dead friend costs a hand slot, air and time, and the one-hit stalker isn't the crew's mistake.
Rising pressure that breaks the plan: missing; stars rise only on failure and depth is the crew's choice.
Two threats at once: present; a leak pulls a hand off a station while a blip closes, and noise against collision on the trip.
Carry-over: present; credits, tools, upgrades and the wanted level change the next trip and site.

## Could not break
1. The windowless hull: real split information, real time pressure, an ambush the crew can't schedule.
2. A dead friend is a load with a price, not only a loss.
3. The wanted level changes the next trip (police on the map), not only a score.
4. A leak during a chase: three stations plus a wrench is more jobs than a small crew has hands.
