# M7 "contradictions" (after TRIZ), gen1

## Method log
- **Steps.** I wrote 24 contradictions from the formula's tensions, each in the form "players must X and must not X" (listed as C1–C24 in the tags below). I split each one by time, space, condition, or part and whole, with 1–3 splits per contradiction, which gave 44 raw rules. I scored each against F1–N roughly in my head. Then I kept the 8 with no clear ✗, each with a different core rule. Besides `formula.md` I read only my own anti-pattern list (`~/.claude/design-anti-patterns.md`). No web search, nothing else under research_notes/ or reports/.
- **Raw count 44.** Most common failures:
  - **N (~17):** the split landed on a known core rule. Examples: rope-tied players, one shared body, Among Us, Keep Talking, Hex-A-Gone, Ultimate Chicken Horse, volleyball, the playground parachute game.
  - **F3 (~9):** pooled bars, pressure plates, turn-taking, side-by-side carrying.
  - **F5 (~3):** idle seats, almost always from splits in time.
- **Split by condition worked best.** "You can X only while a friend does Y" builds a two-way link between players right into the rule. Splits in time mostly gave turn-taking, so someone waits. Splits by part and whole mostly gave split-information puzzles, which already exist.
- **Good at:** core rules that fit in one sentence, tie players to each other, and need no hand-made content.
- **Bad at:** it says nothing about what works against the players (the threat, task or timer), so every kept idea needed a separate step to add one. The results cluster on limits to seeing and speaking (5 of 8 kept). Novelty is often ~ because the method twists a known mechanic instead of finding a new core.

## Kept ideas

### M7-1 Curse of Silence
- Rule (one sentence): Your screen goes black while you talk, and each falling rock is visible to everyone except the player it will hit.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the rockfall over the shared canyon path. One simple system drops rocks at random spots, each aimed at one player, while the path crumbles from behind. The game reads each player's own mic level.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): I can't see my own rock, so a friend must call it, and calling blinds them for as long as they speak. If B is already mid-warning (blind), C has to take my warning, and short calls ("Mia, left!") beat long ones. Each moment splits players into warners, who go blind, and dodgers, who move on someone's word.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a cursed expedition through a canyon where the mountain punishes speech with blindness.
- Look (one line): dusty low-poly canyon, chunky rocks, and a black screen with a speaking icon while you talk.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, each player has only two possible callers, and if both are talking the rock lands. At 6 there are more eyes but more rocks and more people talking over each other, so every warning blinds one more person.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": your own incoming rock, uncovered only by a friend saying it. Nobody hides it on purpose; the rule hides it.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ben is flattened. Eye icons show that Ana and Kai both saw it, but Ana was mid-sentence warning Kai, and Kai stayed quiet to keep his sight. Kai is blamed.

### M7-2 Light Bridge
- Rule (one sentence): The floor exists only where another player's flashlight shines; your own light holds up only your friends.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the dark void and the crew's flashlight cones, each in its owner's colour. A wall of dark pushes from behind, and generated pillars block light.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Where I can step depends on where a friend aims right now, and where I aim decides where others can step. Swinging my light to catch Tom's jump drops the floor under Sam. Each player walks on others' light and aims for others at the same time, so both ends decide.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): lamplighters crossing a bottomless dark on bridges made of each other's light.
- Look (one line): black void, warm coloured cones that turn into glowing planks where they hit.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, each light has to hold up two walkers or switch between them fast. At 6 there are more lights but more bodies to hold up, and crossing cones make it unclear whose light is holding whom.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Sam falls because the plank under him was Lea's colour and Lea swung away to save Tom. Lea is blamed.

### M7-3 Swapped Souls
- Rule (one sentence): You see through your own character's eyes, but your controls steer the next player's character in a ring.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the steering ring (who steers whom), fixed for each run and shown by hats in the driver's colour, on an obstacle course of physics hazards (swinging logs, rising water).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): I describe what my body faces to whoever drives me, while driving a body I see only through its owner's words. What I say decides my driver's next move, and what my passenger just said decides mine. Everyone is a describer and a driver at once.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a party whose souls slipped one body over and must get through the course before the water rises.
- Look (one line): bright toy-like obstacle course; each body wears a hat in its driver's colour.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, each player talks to one friend and listens to another. At 6 there are six crossing conversations, and remembering who drives you is half the game. Odd sizes work, because a ring has no pairs.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (the ring is shown).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ana's body walks into the water. The hat shows Kai was driving; the replay shows Ana said "right" and Kai went left. Kai is blamed.

### M7-4 Flying Hands
- Rule (one sentence): You can grab or hit things only while flying through the air, and only a friend can throw you.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the throw (pick up a friend, aim, release) and physics. Fireworks crates fall from random windows of a burning circus tower and blow up if they hit the ground.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): The thrower picks aim and power, and the flier steers a little in mid-air and picks which crate to grab. A third player can catch a flying friend and throw them on. Where I should stand depends on where my friend will land.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): circus acrobats saving the show by catching falling fireworks in mid-air.
- Look (one line): striped tent colours, squash-and-stretch acrobats, sparks.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, one throws, one flies and one runs to catch or throw again, and the roles swap every landing. At 6 there are two or three throw chains at once, and they get in each other's arcs.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): A crate bursts on the sand. The arc shows Max threw Zoe short, or Zoe grabbed the near crate instead of the falling one. Max or Zoe is blamed, and the arc shows which.

### M7-5 Wall of Ammo
- Rule (one sentence): The only ammo is the blocks you pull out of the wall you are hiding behind.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): one physics wall of stacked stone blocks. One simple enemy type (archers walking up) shoots any player it can see through a hole.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Pulling a block opens a hole that exposes whoever stands behind it, and it can topple the stack a friend relies on. So I pull where nobody stands and call it out, friends shift, and someone may push a block back in to plug a hole instead of throwing it. Which block is safe to pull changes with every pull a friend makes.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): villagers under siege with nothing to throw but their own wall.
- Look (one line): chunky grey blocks, arrows thudding into stone, holes of daylight.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is a short wall and few holes to watch. At 6 the wall is longer, but there are more bodies behind it, so nearly every pull exposes someone.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Rob pulls a low block on the left, the stack slumps, and archers shoot Ina through the gap. Rob's pull is the visible cause.

### M7-6 Loudest Voice
- Rule (one sentence): The beast always walks toward whichever player is talking loudest right now.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the blind beast, which follows the loudest mic (the game reads each player's own mic level), in a generated dark house with keys to collect before the candle burns out.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): To sneak to a key, I need a friend to be louder than me. That pulls the beast to them, so they run while another friend takes over being loud. Players split into decoys (shout, run) and fetchers (whisper, sneak), and my best volume depends on who is loud this second.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): kids in a dark farmhouse hiding from a blind giant that hunts by ear.
- Look (one line): dark wood house, a huge stooped shape, each player's voice drawn as rings.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is one decoy, one fetcher, and one spare who takes over the shouting when the decoy is cornered. At 6 the loudest player keeps changing, and one laugh sends the beast across the house.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Pia laughs at Leo's whisper and suddenly is the loudest. The beast turns from the decoy and takes her by the door. Her voice rings show it was her.

### M7-7 Fence of Light
- Rule (one sentence): A beam links each player to the next in a fixed ring, and the animals can't cross a beam.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the ring of beams, which snaps if two neighbours are more than 12 m apart. Sheep flee the nearest player (one simple flocking rule), and they must be penned before night.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): My spot only means something next to my two neighbours. To let sheep into the pen, one pair must open their gap at the right moment while the rest push from behind, and a stray needs a flank runner whose neighbours must stretch to cover. Roles come from where you stand and change every moment.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): shepherds with light-staffs bringing a glowing flock home before dark.
- Look (one line): green hills at dusk, light beams between staffs, fluffy sheep.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 the fence is a small triangle that must take the flock in bites. At 6 it is a hexagon that can sweep the field, but six people must keep in step, and the ring order puts friends who can't see each other side by side.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Eva runs ahead to cut off one stray, her beam to Jon snaps, and twelve sheep pour through. The snapped beam glows red at Eva's end.

### M7-8 Lone Scout
- Rule (one sentence): The fake floor tiles show as holes only on the screen of a player with no friend within 15 m, and one player may secretly want the crew to fail.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the temple floor, generated each run, where fake tiles look solid to anyone near a friend. A player alone slowly freezes, so scouts must come back.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): The group can't see the fake tiles while together, so someone steps away to see them and calls them out. The group picks the route while the scout warns, and the scout must stand where the group is heading. As the cold drains the scout, another player takes over.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): tomb raiders in a temple whose traps reveal themselves only to someone alone in the dark.
- Look (one line): a torch-lit stone hall, the group in warm light, the scout in cold blue light where the holes glow.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is one scout and two walkers, and a traitor appears only in some runs, since one traitor is a third of the crew. At 6 two scouts can stand on different sides and check each other's calls.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": which tiles are fake (uncovered by going alone, or by throwing a torch onto a tile); who the traitor is (uncovered by sending a second scout and comparing calls). The traitor hides because they win if the crew falls short.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Tom calls "third tile on the left is safe" and Mia drops through it. Tom is blamed in the moment; whether it was a lie or a mistake is the argument after.
