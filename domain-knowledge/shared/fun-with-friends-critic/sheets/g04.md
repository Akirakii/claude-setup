# Proposal for the critic: Clip Hunters

**Fantasy:** A film crew rides a capsule down into monster-filled ruins to record scary footage, because the views on their video channel are what keep them employed.
One-player test: The line still works alone, since one player can hold the camera and film, but nobody is left to be filmed, to lure a monster or to carry the camera out.

**Players:** 1–4, one crew. **A session:** one dive lasts at most about 8 minutes 20 seconds of air; a run is three in-game days with one dive per day; how many runs fit a sitting (not found).

**Feelings the game is known for**
- "It was chasing me and I kept filming instead of running."
- "We watched our own clip afterwards and heard ourselves screaming."
- "He died holding the camera and the whole day's footage went with him."

**The rules, one sentence each**
- The crew has three days to reach a view target, and missing it ends the run.
- Each day allows one dive into the ruins, and sleeping in the bedroom ends the day.
- The crew gets one camera per dive, and it holds about 90 seconds of film with no upgrade for more.
- Air drains at a fixed rate for the whole dive, pauses inside the capsule, and a player with no air dies slowly.
- Views depend on how dangerous the filmed creature is, how much of the screen it fills, how long it is held in shot, and what it does to the players.
- The score for one subject flattens after about 9 seconds of well-framed footage.
- Faces, emotes done in front of the lens, filmed deaths, bones, and old objects held while filmed all add views.
- Player voices are recorded onto the footage.
- The footage only counts once the camera is back on the surface and uploaded at the home's viewing station, and each recording uploads once.
- A camera left below can be picked up on the next dive with its footage, but not after day three, when the map resets.
- If the whole crew dies or the camera stays below, that day earns no views.
- Views pay ad money, which buys lights, healing gear, filming gadgets and emotes at a kiosk.
- Each player carries three items, and items left in the ruins are gone.
- Dead players come back on the surface for a hospital bill that grows with the number of deaths that dive.

**How much there is:** 32 kinds of creature; two underground maps with generated rooms and a capsule landing spot that varies; five item categories at the kiosk (one guide counts seven filming gadgets); no camera upgrade; cosmetic hats and home decorations.

**Voice:** Voice by distance between players; it is recorded onto the footage, and at least one creature reacts to loud players.

**The choices**
- Keep filming the creature for a few more seconds, or run now?
- Spend the last film on this creature, or save it for a more dangerous one deeper in?
- Go back for the dropped camera, or go back for the downed teammate?
- Leave while there is air to walk out, or push further for a better shot?
- Buy a light and healing gear, or buy a gadget that raises views?

**What the other end decides in the same second**
- Keep filming or run: the non-camera players decide whether to step in front of the creature to draw it off the camera holder.
- Spend or save film: nothing.
- Camera or teammate: the downed player decides, before going down, whether to throw the camera toward a friend or the capsule.
- Leave or push: each other player decides whether to follow or head to the capsule alone.
- Light or gadget: nothing.

**Scene, 4 friends**
- The crew steps out of the capsule and one player starts recording with about 90 seconds of film.
- A large fast creature comes round a corner and the camera holder turns the lens on it instead of fleeing.
- A second player runs past the creature shouting, and it switches to that player.
- The creature grabs the second player while the camera holder films the grab at close range.
- The camera holder is hit next and throws the camera toward a third player before going down.
- The third player stops the recording, picks the camera up and runs for the capsule with air running low.
- On the surface the survivors upload the clip and watch it together, voices included, as the view count ticks up.

**What experienced crews do:** One player holds the camera for the whole dive, the others lure creatures and act for the lens, shots are held about 9 seconds at close range and then cut, the crew sets a fixed point to leave, and the camera is saved before a body.
**What changes it from round to round:** The landing spot, the generated rooms, which creatures appear, and optional sponsor side-tasks.
**What carries from one round to the next:** Within a run, money and bought items carry between days; after a failed target, money and equipment are lost and the target resets; a second currency from sponsor tasks buys hats and home decorations (whether that survives a failed run: not found).
**Can a new crew lose? Does it get harder?** Yes, by missing the target on day three; the target rises after each one met (exact numbers: guides disagree), and more dangerous creatures appear more often after the first two targets.
