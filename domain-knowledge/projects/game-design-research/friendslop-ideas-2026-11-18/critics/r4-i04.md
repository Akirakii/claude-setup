## The numbers the rules give (worked)

- Moonlight: +1% fur per second. Shade: −0.5% per second. Beam: +4% per second.
- A guide must be at 50% or more, a picker under 50%. Fur does not carry between nights (rule: only the mark carries), so each night starts with every friend at 0.
- Nobody can see a bloom for the first 50 seconds of a night (or 12.5 seconds standing in a beam): someone has to grow to half first.
- A guide has 50 seconds of plain moonlight between "useful" and "wolf", and is never more than 12.5 beam-seconds from turning. No plan removes this; the rule forces it.
- Every second in the light costs two seconds in the shade to undo. In a steady state each friend can be in the light one third of the time. Over a 600-second night that is about 200–230 seconds of light per friend.
- With 4 friends, on average 1.3 are in the light; a guide-and-picker pair can be out about two thirds of the night. With 3 friends, half of it.
- Hold: the held friend's fur stops, the holder gains 1% per second, the pair moves at half speed. A guide who is d seconds of running from shade needs 2d seconds held, so the holder gains 2d%. A clean picker (under 50%) who holds does not turn unless shade is more than 25 seconds of running away or a beam sits on them.
- Catching a wolf: two friends, 5 seconds, each gains 10% (double speed in its light). The wolf comes back at 50%.
- A bite: +50%. A bitten friend at 50% or more turns at once.

## The best move for a crew that only wants the mark, and the plan by the fifth night

The plan a crew settles into (worked, from the numbers above):
1. One friend stands in the light for 50 seconds to become the guide.
2. Guide and picker go out in short shifts. The guide turns back at a fixed cap (about 80%), chosen so that one surprise beam of a couple of seconds does not turn them. The picker turns back before 50%.
3. Everyone else stands in the shade shrinking fur; one of them calls beams from the doorway.
4. Trips never go further from shade than the guide's remaining fur in seconds, so the hold is never needed.
5. If a beam turns someone anyway, the wolf steers loops over ground already picked and comes side-on past two friends waiting at the shade's edge; or the crew waits for a cloud.
6. When the mark is met, everybody stands in the dark until dawn.

Two stronger moves the written rules allow, which a mark-chasing crew would find by night five:
- **Turn on purpose.** Shade takes 100 seconds to bring a guide from 100% to 50%. Turning next to two waiting friends does it in 5 seconds for 10% each. The guide never has to rest. The cost is the trampled ground (steered over picked beds) and the risk of a head-on misjudge.
- **Carry the picker.** The rule fills the holder's hands, not the held friend's. A guide who holds the picker keeps the picker's fur at zero all night while the picker picks; only guides rotate. If two friends may hold each other, both freeze, even in a beam. (unwritten whether a held friend can pick, and whether a mutual hold is allowed)

Which night the cap plan stops reaching the mark cannot be worked out: the mark's numbers, the number of blooms, whether they regrow, how many a picker carries and where the cauldron is are all unwritten.
So I cannot say that the fifth night forces the deep walk; the sheet's last line claims it and no number backs it.

## 1. The seven traits

1. **Yes.** The bloom shows only within three steps of the half-furred friend (rule), so guide and picker must move as one; the hold needs two bodies (rule); the wolf needs two catchers and the wolf's own steering (rule). All five listed choices change what a second friend must do at once.
2. **Yes.** At full fur you play the wolf and steer it; caught, you return at half fur as a guide; a bite adds fur and nothing else (rules). No death, no spectator state. Stronger than any sheet on the ladder.
3. **No.** "Voice: none in the game" (rule).
4. **Yes, with a limit.** Each trip is "one more bloom or back to shade" and the loss is shared: a wolf at dawn fails the night for all, and the wolf tramples and bites (rules). The limit: nothing is gained past the mark, so the call stops the moment the mark is met, and a fixed cap turns it into a habit.
5. **No.** Blooms near a furry friend show to everyone as written; beams cross "where everyone can see them". The guide is a lantern, not a holder of information. (It would be partly if only you could read your own fur; the rule says you see it on your own arms and does not say others can't.)
6. **Partly.** Beam times and places are random by rule. Bloom positions and clouds are named only in the "what changes" line, not in a rule. One mansion, one bloom, no creatures, nothing carried.
7. **Yes.** "The mark rises every night, and a night that fails ends the run" (rule). Whether the end is the crew's doing or plain arithmetic (not enough blooms or seconds exist) cannot be told without numbers.

## 2. The two closest sheets

**g01** (tier A): a rising target, "one more load", the whole crew pays.
**g03** (tier A): a personal bar that fills until you drop, a teammate's body stops the timer (the carry there, the hold here), a moving threat that forces the call.

Has that they lack:
- The failed player becomes the hazard and keeps full agency (g01's dead watch; g03's passed-out can't move or be heard).

Lacks that they have:

- Anything to spend or argue over between nights (g01: credits, tools, which moon).
- A role with information the others lack (g01's ship map).
- A use for surplus (g01 banks oversold credits) and a way to leave early (g01's lever).

## 3. Is it fun with friends?

**maybe.**
The tether, the wolf you still steer and the beam shout are rule-caused and as strong as anything in tier A, but by the numbers each friend spends about two thirds of every night standing in shade, and the cheapest way to meet the mark makes turning a drill and the hold unnecessary.
Three of the four listed feelings depend on the mark forcing mistakes, and the sheet has no numbers for the mark, the blooms, the beams or the clouds, so that is a claim, not a rule.

Sits between **g05** (above) and **g06** (below): a physical bond between friends as strong as g05's chain, with g06's thin variety and single threat.

## 4. Holes of this design alone

1. **Two thirds of the night is waiting in the dark.** Shade shrinks fur at half the speed light grows it (rule), so the best move is to stand still twice as long as you worked. Tier A: not shared (g03's ledge rest is short; g01's ship player has a map and doors).
2. **Turning on purpose is the fastest way to lose fur.** 100% to 50% in 5 seconds for 10% on each of two catchers, against 100 seconds of shade. Best move: turn at the shade's edge beside two friends, steer over picked ground. The fantasy's fear becomes a tool. Tier A: partly shared (g03's crews stop risking the group for a fallen scout because the statue brings them back), but there failure is not made profitable.
3. **The hold rule has two gaps.** Only the holder's hands are full, so a guide can carry a picker whose fur then never grows; and nothing forbids two friends holding each other, which would freeze both even in a beam. Best move: carry the picker all night. Tier A: not shared.
4. **Nothing to do once the mark is met.** Surplus is worth nothing, only the mark carries, and dawn can't be called early (unwritten). Best move: everyone stands in shade to the end. Tier A: not shared (g01 has the lever and banked credits; g02 turns surplus into money).
5. **The numbers that decide everything are unwritten:** the mark per night, bloom count and regrowth, carry size, cauldron place, beam width and frequency, what a cloud does to fur and blooms and how often it comes. With one mansion, one bloom and nothing carried, a sitting's later nights differ only by beam timing. Tier A: not shared (g05, tier B, has a fixed course and no variation).

Unwritten or contradictory, said plainly:
- "The wolf … turns back by itself at the edge of the dark" can mean it turns around or it turns human. If human, a wolf fixes itself by steering into the dark and the two-friend catch is pointless. I read it as "turns around".
- Scene: "Ben passes half fur and can't pick any more tonight" contradicts the shade rule (100 seconds of shade puts him under half again).
- Scene: Ana goes from three quarters to nine tenths in two seconds in a beam; at 4% per second that takes 3.75 seconds.
- Scene: Ben turns while holding, from just over half; that needs about 11 seconds inside a beam that crosses at running speed. Either beams are very wide (then "stand in it four seconds to pick" is not a choice, you are in it longer anyway) or the scene overstates.
- Picking time (the "four seconds" in a choice line) is in no rule.
- Whether a furry friend can hold, whether others can see your fur, and what happens with two wolves in a crew of three (the last human cannot catch alone, so the night is lost) are unwritten.

## 5. What I could not break

- The guide must be half wolf to be useful. No plan keeps the guide safe from a beam.
- The three-step tether: two friends have to move as one, and each stop or step of one is a demand on the other.
- The turned friend keeps playing and is the one who must make the catch possible by steering.
- The catch costs the catchers their own fur, so a rescue can chain into a second wolf.
- The beam call from the friend who is resting.

## 6. The smallest change (my opinion)

A caught wolf comes back at nine tenths fur, not half.
This closes the turn-on-purpose reset, keeps the rescued friend in danger for the rest of the night, and makes the hold the thing that friend needs next, which brings the "hold me" line back into the settled plan.
It does not fix the waiting in shade; that needs the shade rule's number changed.
