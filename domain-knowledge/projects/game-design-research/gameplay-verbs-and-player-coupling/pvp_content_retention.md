# PvP / players-as-content vs PvE content cost, and retention by verb type

Tag legend. **[source](link)** = the linked page says it; "(page read)" = I fetched and read the page, "(summary only)" = I saw only a search-engine summary of it, so the wording is not checked. **[read]** = I pulled the raw numbers myself (SteamCharts monthly tables, fetched 2026-11-09) and did the arithmetic. **[memory]** = from my training data, not checked this session (treat as assumed). **[opinion]** = my inference. Research date: 2026-11-09.

Terms used below. **CCU** = concurrent players at one moment (SteamCharts reports a monthly average and a monthly peak). **Dn retention** = share of a day-0 cohort that plays again on day n. **"Max-avg month"** = the month with the highest monthly average CCU on SteamCharts; percentages like "+12m 4%" mean "the monthly average 12 months later was 4% of that max". CCU decay is not cohort retention: CCU mixes old players, new buyers, sales, free weekends and update spikes.

## Q1. Which designers have said other players are the (cheapest) content, and in what context?

### Takeaway
The "players are the content" idea is real and on record, but it comes almost entirely from MMO and platform people (CCP/EVE, SOE, Raph Koster, Valve), and their argument is about cost and longevity of persistent worlds, not small session games. I found no direct quote from Riot, Valve's CS team, Bartle, or a party/friendslop developer saying it.

### Cited Findings
- EVE Online: Game Developer (Christian Nutt, 2016-04-01) says EVE stays vibrant because "its players are the content"; subtitle: "In content-driven MMOs, players burn through handcrafted content and then abandon the game." It quotes former EVE senior producer Jon Lander (2012) calling CCP "relatively hands-off janitors of the virtual world" and the game "about player-created stories." — [source (page read)](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-eve-online-i-s-big-battle-proves-players-i-are-i-the-content-in-a-sandbox-mmo)
- The same article points to a 2012 talk by former CCP designer Matthew Woodward on sandbox design, but does not summarize it; the phrase "content is expensive" attributed to Woodward in one search summary does NOT appear in the article. — [source (page read)](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-eve-online-i-s-big-battle-proves-players-i-are-i-the-content-in-a-sandbox-mmo)
- EVE players push back on the phrase: forum threads argue "CCP is setting me up to be other people's content" (i.e. being someone else's content is not fun for the victim). — [source (summary only)](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/players-as-content-and-the-victim-mindset/373239)
- Sony Online Entertainment (Clint Worley, 2014-05-21): "A chunk of content that takes us four years to make gets consumed by the players in less than a year"; "we're chasing our tail"; even "a dedicated team of say, a hundred developers cranking out an expansion... the players consume it so fast"; players "can just go to a different game any time they want." His answer was player-made content (EverQuest Next / Landmark). — [source (page read)](https://www.gamewatcher.com/2014-21-05-sony-content-consumed-too-fast-in-mmos-player-generated-content-is-future)
- SOE CEO John Smedley, same period: headline "content-driven MMORPGs are 'unsustainable'" (I could not read the body). — [source (headline only)](https://pcgamer.com/john-smedley-content-sandbox-mmorpg)
- Raph Koster (MMORPG.com, Morgan Ramsay, 2015-07-15): "Quests are easily an enormous amount of the cost of a modern MMO." On PvP-focused Crowfall: "By not doing a PvE extravaganza, they're able to instead spend those dollars on other things" and "Crowfall is able to rewind time on things like costs, hugely." — [source (page read)](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=30091)
- Koster (GamesRadar, around Ultima Online's 28th birthday, so ~Sept 2025): friendships are the top predictor of whether people stay in an MMO; players return "to a place to enjoy with friends even after exhausting the content"; headline quote "Content does not last forever." — [source (summary only; body did not load)](https://gamesradar.com/games/world-of-warcraft/as-ultima-online-approaches-its-28th-birthday-former-creative-lead-reveals-what-it-takes-for-an-mmo-to-last-for-decades-and-why-they-sometimes-fail-content-does-not-last-forever); same position in GameStar, headline says MMO longevity "is not about content" — [source (summary only)](https://www.gamestar.de/artikel/mmo-ueber-jahrzehnte-angesagt-geht-nicht-um-content,3429088.html)
- Koster also says sandbox MMOs work best when they combine content (raids, dungeons) with social/sandbox elements (farming, housing), i.e. he does not say content is unnecessary. — [source (summary only)](https://mein-mmo.de/en/mmorpg-legend-reveals-baking-and-planting-flowers-is-more-important-to-players-than-killing,513961)
- Gabe Newell (Valve, 2013): "even at this early stage we cannot compete with our customers in the production of content" (UT Austin talk, as reported by PC Gamer) — [source (summary only)](https://pcgamer.com/top-tf2-item-makers-making-500k-a-year-we-cannot-compete-with-our-customers); DICE 2013 keynote: "Our customers have defeated us... not by a little, but by a lot" — [source (summary only)](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2013/02/07/watch-gabe-newell-39-s-dice-keynote-now). Note: this is about user-made items/maps (UGC), not opponents as content.
- Koster's Theory of Fun (book, 2004/2013) ties fun to pattern-mastery: tic-tac-toe is boring once solved; chess stays because humans cannot exhaust its patterns. Only secondary paraphrases seen. — [source (summary only)](https://bumblingthroughdungeons.com/theory-fun-game-design-raph-koster/)

### Inferences
- [opinion] Two different claims hide under "players are the content": (1) **opponents as content** (other minds keep the pattern unsolved: chess, CS, poker, social deduction) and (2) **players as producers** (UGC, player stories, player economies: EVE, Valve Workshop, Garry's Mod). The cost argument (Worley, Koster, Newell) is mostly about (2) and about persistent worlds. Only Koster's Theory of Fun line backs (1), and it is about depth (unsolvable patterns), not about cost.
- [opinion] Every on-record source is from persistent online worlds with strangers in them. None speaks to a closed friend group of 2–8 people, which is the friendslop case. Transfer to friendslop is an assumption.
- [opinion] The EVE "victim" pushback is a warning for the user's "high coupling between players" formula: when player A's fun is player B's cost, B leaves; PvP-as-content needs the losing side to still have fun (social deduction solves this with laughter and short rounds).

### Gaps
- No direct quote found from Riot, Valve's CS team, Richard Bartle, or a party/friendslop dev (Innersloth, Landfall, semiwork, Zeekerss) saying opponents are cheap content. Search did not surface one; may exist in GDC Vault talks behind a paywall.
- Woodward's 2012 CCP talk contents not checked.
- Koster's PC Gamer interview (Stars Reach) with MMO budget figures (UO ~$2M 1997, SWG $10–12M 2003, WoW ~$63M 2004, SWTOR >$200M) seen only as search summary; page body did not load: [link](https://pcgamer.com/games/mmo/ultima-online-and-star-wars-galaxies-vet-tells-me-the-theme-park-assembly-line-mmo-just-isnt-viable-anymore-especially-as-dev-costs-spike-we-hit-the-wall). Treat figures as unchecked.

## Q2. What does PvE content cost to keep a live game going (the content treadmill)?

### Takeaway
On-record developers agree that players eat handmade PvE content far faster than teams can make it (SOE: 4 years of work gone in under a year); big live games answer by removing content (Destiny Content Vault), timegating, or handing old games to other studios (Deep Rock Galactic). Friendslop avoids the treadmill by design, at the price of ~3% D30.

### Cited Findings
- SOE: 4 years of content consumed in under a year; ~100-dev expansion teams can't keep up (2014-05-21). — [source (page read)](https://www.gamewatcher.com/2014-21-05-sony-content-consumed-too-fast-in-mmos-player-generated-content-is-future)
- Koster: quests are "easily an enormous amount of the cost of a modern MMO"; going PvP instead of "a PvE extravaganza" cuts cost "hugely" (2015). — [source (page read)](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=30091)
- BioWare, SWTOR (GDC 2013): the team expected players to take 3–5 months to reach the level cap and was caught off guard by how fast they got there. — [source (summary only)](https://pcgamer.com/bioware-gdc-panel-star-wars-the-old-republic)
- Bungie, Destiny 2 (2020-06-09): "Destiny 2 is too large to efficiently update and maintain," and its size and complexity cause "more bugs and less innovation"; answer was the Content Vault, which removed Mars, Io, Titan, Mercury, Leviathan and their activities in Nov 2020. — [source: Bungie post (summary only; page did not load)](https://www.bungie.net/7/en/News/Article/49189); [vault contents (summary only)](https://pcgamer.com/can-removing-half-of-destiny-2-really-be-good-for-the-game). In Aug 2022 Bungie said it would stop vaulting expansions from Lightfall on, but seasonal content still goes away. — [source (summary only)](https://ggrecon.com/guides/destiny-2-content-vault-explained)
- Destiny 2 Steam CCU: max-avg month Oct 2019 (Steam launch) 165,308; Oct 2026 avg 24,742 = 15% of that, with big swings around each expansion (e.g. June 2024 avg 123,023 at The Final Shape, then 36,169 by Oct 2024). — [read] checked (SteamCharts app 1085660, 2026-11-09)
- Deep Rock Galactic (co-op PvE): seasons usually ~6–7 months apart; Season 5 slipped to ~a year after Season 4 because the spin-off Rogue Core took the team; Season 6 was handed to outside studio Invisible Walls so Ghost Ship could focus on Rogue Core (delayed to ~Q2 2026). — [source (summary only)](https://www.gamesmarket.global/deep-rock-galactics-future-and-why-ghost-ship-partners-with-invisible-walls/); [PC Gamer (summary only)](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/fps/deep-rock-galactics-roguelite-spin-off-is-delayed-but-in-the-meantime-its-getting-another-season-of-new-stuff-including-a-tomb-like-biome)
- WoW: Blizzard's Mike Morhaime said players have become much faster at consuming content (MCV). — [source (summary only, low confidence)](https://mcvuk.com/?p=107936)
- Deconstructor of Fun (2016): in content-driven F2P, "creating more and more content becomes vital for your business," which locks studios into the treadmill. — [source (summary only)](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog//2016/09/managing-and-avoiding-content-treadmills.html)
- AppMagic (2026-03-06) on friendslop: session-based progression "is cheaper to build and balance," so teams ship fast and move on if a game doesn't land; meta-progression "barely exists"; games aim to fill "a handful of evenings rather than build habits"; content-heavy co-op aims to build habits, friendslop aims to create moments. — [source (page read)](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025)
- Party games have their own treadmill: Jackbox ships a new numbered Party Pack roughly yearly instead of updating old ones. — [memory, not checked]. Jackbox Party Pack 3 Steam CCU: max-avg 1,403 (Apr 2020, lockdown), Oct 2026 133 = 9.5%. — [read] checked (SteamCharts app 434170, 2026-11-09)

### Inferences
- [opinion] The treadmill problem is a ratio: dev-hours per player-hour of content. Handmade PvE has a ratio near "years of work → weeks of play" (SOE). Opponents, procedural levels and physics chaos lower the ratio; the user's "low content" formula is a bet on a low ratio, not on zero content.
- [opinion] Destiny's Vault is evidence that content also has a **carrying cost**, not only a making cost: every old raid/map is code that new features must stay coupled to. This matches the user's "low coupling between features" rule: the Vault was Bungie cutting feature coupling after the fact.
- [opinion] DRG shows the small-studio version: one live PvE game's seasons competed with the studio's next game for the same team, and the studio chose the next game. For a solo dev the same choice comes sooner.

### Gaps
- No hard cost figures (dollars or dev-months per raid/season/map) found for Destiny, WoW, DRG or any co-op game. Bungie/Blizzard do not publish them; only qualitative statements exist.
- Bungie's own post and the SWTOR GDC article bodies were not read (only summaries); exact wording unverified.

## Q3. Is there data comparing retention or CCU decay by game type among short-dev co-op/party games (2024–2026)?

### Takeaway
Cohort retention by sub-type does not exist publicly: AppMagic gives D1–D30 for four titles only (friendslop average D30 ~3%; Content Warning D1 19.8% → D30 ~3%). My own SteamCharts pull shows short-dev social-deduction/bluff games decay just as fast as physics/co-op-vs-environment games (most fall to 2–8% of their peak month within 12–24 months); the spread inside each type is bigger than the gap between types. The long-lived PvP games (CS, TF2, Rust, Dead by Daylight) are not low-content.

### Inferences
- [opinion] No support in this sample for "social deduction / PvP-social retains better than co-op vs environment" among short-dev games. Oct 2026 share of max month (arithmetic on the [read] numbers above): social-deduction set 1.7–5.6%, median ~3.4% (Among Us 4.0, GGD 2.4, Dread Hunger 1.7, Liar's Bar 2.7, Lockdown 5.6, Guilty as Sock 4.3); co-op-vs-environment set 2.5–8.1%, median ~5.9% (Lethal 2.5, Content Warning 4.8, Chained 7.1, Pico Park 2 8.1, Schedule I 6.1, YAPYAP 5.6). Ages differ (Among Us is 6 years past its spike, YAPYAP 8 months), so this is a rough cut, not a test. The two that hold best at 12+ months (R.E.P.O. 16.6%, PEAK 21.4%) are physics/extraction co-op that kept shipping updates. The PvP outlier (Mage Arena) is the worst in the whole set at 0.4%.
- [opinion] The evergreen PvP games are not "low content": CS, TF2 and Rust run long update/cosmetic/map pipelines and economies; DbD (best D30 at 11.3%) is PvP **plus** a heavy DLC treadmill. What they share is not low content but (a) **public matchmaking with strangers**, so the game does not end when one friend group stops, (b) a **skill ceiling and ranked/status**, and (c) a **creator or modding layer** (Workshop, GMod, community servers). Friendslop drops all three by design.
- [opinion] A plausible frame for the user: "players as content" yields **longevity** only when the player pool is open (strangers fill seats) and skill can deepen; inside a closed friend group it yields **novelty per session** (laughs, clips), which sells copies but does not retain. The data fits "friendslop is a sales-spike model," as AppMagic says, more than a retention model.
- [opinion] PvP small games carry a risk PvE co-op doesn't: **liquidity**. Once average CCU drops to ~20 (Mage Arena), public lobbies can't fill; a co-op-vs-environment game still works for 2 friends at any population. For a friends-only game this matters less, because the lobby is the friend group.
- [opinion] Update cadence is a confounder: the 2025 hits that held up (R.E.P.O., PEAK, RV There Yet?) got updates; the ones that crashed hardest mostly got few. So "content" in small doses still moves the curve, even in friendslop.

### Gaps
- No public cohort retention (D30/D90) per sub-type (social deduction vs physics co-op vs hide-and-seek). AppMagic's full list of 45 friendslop titles and any per-type split are not in the free article; Gamalytic and GameDiscoverCo Plus data are paywalled and were not checked. GameDiscoverCo free posts found cover discovery, not retention.
- No pure hide-and-seek Steam title with clear data was checked (prop hunt lives inside Garry's Mod, so GMod's curve is not a hide-and-seek curve).
- SteamCharts is Steam-only: Among Us and Fall Guys were huge on mobile/console/Epic, Sea of Thieves on Xbox/Game Pass, so Steam CCU understates them. Causes of late spikes (Valheim, PEAK, Overcooked! 2, Golf With Your Friends, Dead by Daylight in 2026) not checked (likely updates, sales, free weekends or sequels).
- CCU is not retention: a game with steady new sales can show flat CCU while each cohort still churns fast.
- No data found on chess, poker or tabletop Werewolf player-base longevity in this session; the "chess lasts centuries" claim stands as common knowledge, not measured here.

## Q4. Do social games also decay when the meta gets solved or the friend group burns out?

### Takeaway
Yes. Among Us, Goose Goose Duck, Fall Guys, Liar's Bar and Dread Hunger all lost over 90% of their peak-month Steam players; adding modes/roles did not bring Among Us back. The data cannot separate "meta solved" from "friend group moved on" from "trend over", and I found no study that measures meta-solving in social games directly.

### Cited Findings
- Among Us Steam monthly peak CCU fell 132,293 (Mar 2021) → 97,271 (Apr) → 28,568 (May 2021); July 2021 19,530. — [source (summary only, GameCharts)](https://gamecharts.org/steam/among-us); consistent with my pull: avg 176,454 (Oct 2020) → 11% six months later → 4% a year later → 4.0% in Oct 2026. — [read] checked (SteamCharts app 945360, 2026-11-09)
- Game World Observer (2021-03-11): Among Us Steam MAU dropped to 24K (headline; body not read). — [source (headline only)](https://gameworldobserver.com/2021/03/11/among-us-mau-drops-to-24k-players-on-steam)
- Sportskeeda attributes the drop to "the absence of new content" and the arrival of other games like Valheim (low-quality source, opinion). — [source (summary only)](https://opera.sportskeeda.com/among-us/the-quick-rise-even-quicker-fall-among-us-what-really-happened)
- Innersloth answered with a June 2021 roadmap: hide-and-seek mode, new roles (Sheriff/Scientist reported), new map, 15-player lobbies. — [source (summary only)](https://www.pcgamer.com/among-us-hide-and-seek-sheriff-new-map). Steam CCU kept falling after it (+12m 4%, +24m 3%). — [read] checked (SteamCharts, 2026-11-09)
- Fall Guys (party PvP, Steam): avg 124,479 (Aug 2020 launch) → 54% next month → 10% at +3m → 7% at +6m; Oct 2026 avg 391 = 0.3%. — [read] checked (SteamCharts app 1097150, 2026-11-09). Fall Guys went free-to-play on Epic/consoles in June 2022 and stopped selling on Steam, so the Steam tail understates total players. — [memory, not checked]
- Goose Goose Duck (social deduction) peaked 20 months after its Steam launch (Jan 2023, all-time peak 701,898) and fell to 7% of that month's average within 6 months. — [read] checked (SteamCharts app 1568590, 2026-11-09)
- Liar's Bar (bluff PvP) avg 19,758 at launch (Oct 2024), 44% next month, 5% at +6m. — [read] checked (SteamCharts app 3097560, 2026-11-09)
- AppMagic's explanation for friendslop churn is not meta-solving but design intent: players "have a great time for a couple of weeks, then move on"; viral clips and free access make spikes that fade "once the initial wave passes." — [source (page read)](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025)
- The ICIS 2025 finding (friend teams play less unless schedules match) points at coordination cost as a churn driver in friend-group play. — [source (summary only)](https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis2025/hti/hti/33)
- Koster's pattern-mastery view: a game gets boring once its patterns are mastered; human opponents delay that only if the space is deep (chess vs tic-tac-toe). — [source (summary only)](https://bumblingthroughdungeons.com/theory-fun-game-design-raph-koster/)

### Inferences
- [opinion] Three decay causes overlap and the public data can't split them: (1) **trend decay** (streamer/viral wave passes; hits every 2020–2026 spike game, social or not), (2) **friend-group decay** (the group's schedule or interest ends; one person leaving ends the game for 3–7 others), (3) **solve decay** (social-deduction tells get learned: who lies how, which map spots are safe). (1) and (2) clearly apply to friendslop; (3) is plausible but unmeasured.
- [opinion] Social deduction looks vulnerable to (3) inside a closed group: the same 5 friends learn each other's bluffs fast, while with strangers (Among Us public lobbies, poker rooms) the opponent pool stays fresh. Chess, poker and Werewolf last as *formats* across generations of strangers, not as one group's habit.
- [opinion] Coupling cuts both ways: "high coupling between players" makes each session vivid, but it also couples churn. When one node (player) leaves, every loop that needs N players breaks for the rest. A game that also works at 2 players, or with strangers, decouples churn.
- [opinion] Among Us adding modes and roles in 2021–22 without recovering its Steam curve suggests content added after a social game's trend wave passes does not restart it; the update-supported 2025 hits (R.E.P.O., PEAK) updated while still on the wave.

### Gaps
- No source measures "meta solved" in a social-deduction game (e.g. impostor win-rate drift or lobby churn by group age). Innersloth has not published retention.
- No direct data on friend-group burnout (how long a fixed group keeps playing one party game). The ICIS paper is the closest, and only its abstract summary was seen.
- Fall Guys and Among Us cross-platform totals not checked; the 3.8M concurrent Among Us figure some sites cite is likely all-platform and unverified.
