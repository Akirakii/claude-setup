# Proposal for the critic: Front Page

**Fantasy:** A team of news photographers walks into a big city overrun by invading creatures, and wants to take pictures dramatic enough for the front page and get out alive.
One-player test: The line still works with one player, but nobody can rescue the player's camera, free them from a trap, or appear in their shots.

**Players:** 1–8, one crew. **A session:** one round is one trip into the city followed by a photo review at the office; the length of a trip is (not found); how many trips a sitting holds is (not found).

**Feelings the game is known for** (2–4, each as a line a player says to a friend)
"Don't help him yet, I need the shot first."
"Turn around, turn around, it's right behind you, smile."
"I died but please grab my camera, the good one is on it."
"Let's see what made the paper."

**The rules, one sentence each** (every rule a player must know; as many as the game really has, 8–14)
The crew starts each round in an office and walks through a door into the city streets, buildings and alleys.
Every player carries a camera with a limited number of shots (7 to 15 depending on the camera).
Photos score points for what is in the frame: creatures, destruction, gore, deaths and teammates.
More things in one frame score more, and each extra body part in frame adds a rising amount.
A board next to the mission door lists the bonus labels in play, such as one for battle scenes.
A teammate in the frame doubles a creature shot, and a teammate being killed in the frame is worth double.
A selfie mode turns the camera on the player, and a creature chasing the player in a selfie earns its own bonus label.
Rare creature variants (a different colour or shape, faster and more dangerous) and higher difficulty score more.
Players must escape the city alive with their cameras; how the escape works and whether there is a time limit is (not found).
A dead player's camera stays in the city, and a living teammate can carry it out with its photos.
Back at the office the photos are scored, and group poses at hand-in add points.
The crew must reach a point target, and the target rises every three trips.
The crew starts with 3 trip passes, each trip uses one, and a photo that makes the front page earns more.
Money earned buys gear between trips: other cameras, a trap, boots for more health, a radio for more speed and stamina, a backpack.

**The choices** (3–5 lines a player really faces, "do X, or do Y?")
Free the teammate caught in the hanging threads, or photograph them first?
Spend a shot now on a small scene, or save it for something bigger later?
Lead a creature toward the others for a crowded frame, or keep it away from them?
Buy the camera with the most shots, or the one with a score bonus and fewer shots?
Go back for a dead teammate's camera, or leave with the photos already taken?

**What the other end decides in the same second** (per choice, or "nothing")
The trapped teammate decides nothing; they wait and talk.
The teammates decide whether to spend their own shots on the same scene.
The teammates being led toward decide to pose, run, or take the shot themselves.
Nothing.
The dead teammate decides nothing; they watch.

**Scene, 3 friends** (5–7 bullets, one real moment the game is known for, second by second)
- One player lures a chasing creature down the street toward a drain cover.
- A second player stands on the far side of the cover and starts a dance pose.
- The third player walks into red threads hanging in a doorway and is held there, calling for help.
- The creature steps on the cover and the thing under the street grabs it.
- The first player flips to selfie mode and shoots the grab with the dancing teammate in frame.
- The second player turns, photographs the trapped teammate, and only then walks over to free them.
- Soldiers fire a rocket into the street and all three shoot the blast before running.

**What experienced crews do:** Guides say to read the bonus board before leaving and aim shots at those labels.
Guides say to pack the frame with as many things as possible and not to look for one clean subject.
Guides say to buy the highest-capacity camera first.
Guides say to bring a partner so a camera can be rescued and so a teammate can stand in the frame.
Guides say to lure soldiers toward creatures, and creatures onto drain covers, to stage a scene, and to drag bodies and body parts to the spot first.
Guides say to use the cheap trap to pin a small or medium creature and pose next to it.
**What changes it from round to round:** The bonus labels on the board, which creatures appear, and whether rare variants, soldiers and building-size creatures show up; whether the map layout changes is (not found).
**What carries from one round to the next:** Money, bought gear and the count of trip passes; what a dead player loses is (not found) beyond the camera left in the city.
**Can a new crew lose? Does it get harder?** Yes, the crew runs out of trip passes if no photo makes the front page; the point target rises every three trips, and higher difficulty adds more creatures, more kinds, soldiers and building-size creatures.
