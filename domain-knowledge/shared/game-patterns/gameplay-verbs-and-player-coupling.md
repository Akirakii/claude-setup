# Hidden human choices replace authored content

Hide-and-seek, bluffing and social deduction stay fresh with almost no content for one reason: the thing each player acts on is another human who picks a counter and cannot see everything, so no fixed best move exists and every round comes out new without anyone authoring it [opinion]. Carrying, throwing at enemies, shooting enemies and building act on the environment, and the environment's answer does not change with your plan. The best move gets found once, so the game has to keep buying newness with levels, enemies and items, or with tightly connected systems such as physics, AI directors and generated maps [opinion]. Game theory backs the first half: search games, where the target's location is hidden, have no safe fixed plan ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.09538.pdf)). The content a verb needs depends on who is on the other side, not on the verb itself. Seeking human hiders in MECCHA CHAMELEON shipped after about two months of work and reached about 340,000 players online at once ([source](https://www.3djuegos.com/pc/noticias/se-hizo-dos-meses-se-lanzo-hace-unos-dias-tiene-240-000-jugadores-asi-ultimo-exito-steam-que-nadie-vio-venir/amp); [source](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/06/meccha-chameleon-is-a-hide-and-seek-body-painting-game-taking-over-steam/)). Seeking an AI ghost in Phasmophobia runs on 14 maps and 30 ghost types ([source](https://gonintendo.com/contents/57161-phasmophobia-content-roadmap-for-2026-revealed); [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phasmophobia_(video_game))). In the ranked table below, all ten top verbs act on another player's mind, and the bottom four (race, shoot enemies, collect, build) act on the environment [opinion]. A co-op game has no enemy team, so the only other mind is a teammate, and mind-to-mind coupling has to be built by stopping teammates from acting as one mind. The known ways are: split what each player sees, narrow how they can talk, give them stakes in one shared node that pull apart, or add time pressure [opinion, from the designers cited below]. The honest limit is that low content bought the launch spike, not the long tail: friendslop games keep about 3% of new players after 30 days ([source](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025)), and social-deduction hits kept no more of their peak than co-op-versus-environment hits ([read: SteamCharts, 2026-11-09]). The games that held up got updates while still on the wave, strangers in public lobbies, player-made maps, or generated layouts [opinion, from the data below].

Content = authored material players use up (maps, enemies, items, prompts, levels). Coupling between players = how much one player's action changes another player's result and next decision. Hub, nodes and loops follow the user's graph model: the hub is the core action players share, nodes are the systems a player touches, and a loop is one round of playtest-and-change.*

## A fixed world gets solved once; a human picking the counter never settles

Every round of a game has to get its newness from somewhere. There are only three places to get it: authored content, which is used up as it is played; connected systems, where a few rules combine into situations nobody wrote; and other players' choices [opinion]. Raph Koster, whose theory of fun is about learning patterns, names the third source directly: **"Sometimes the puzzle is provided by a computer, sometimes by another player"** ([source](https://www.raphkoster.com/2012/01/24/an-atomic-theory-of-fun-game-design/)). Jesper Juul draws the same line between two kinds of game. Games of emergence are "a small number of rules that combine and yield large numbers of game variations", and they "tend to be replayable". Games of progression are a designer-set sequence of challenges, and their replay value is "very low". He adds that in multiplayer "the actual game played has input from a lot of different sources in addition to the game designers" ([source](https://www.jesperjuul.net/text/openandtheclosed.html)). The deciding question is whether the thing a verb acts on answers back. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy separates acting on a passive world from acting on one that reacts, and says of a natural obstacle that **"the environment is not interested in your plans"** ([source](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/game-theory/)). Chris Crawford makes the same cut: dodging a falling branch "is not interactivity, it's reactivity" ([source](https://ludism.org/gamedesign/Interactivity)).

Game theory shows why hide-and-seek never settles. **Chase games where both sides see everything are solved by one fixed plan. Search games, where the target's location is hidden, need what game theorists call a mixed strategy: choosing among options by set odds instead of always making the same choice** ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.09538.pdf)). The Gal and Casas model shows how this works. A hider picks one of several spots, and the seeker can check only some of them. If the seeker can check enough spots, the hider always uses the single safest one. Below that line, the hider spreads choices so that every spot gets used, risky ones included. When the seeker keeps searching after a miss, "the hider should choose his location more randomly" ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.09538.pdf)). In content terms, ten hiding spots against a human seeker are ten pieces of content that get chosen again every round for a new reason. Ten spots in a puzzle are used up once the right one is known [opinion].

Real players don't play those odds evenly, and that is where the depth comes from. Groups playing rock-paper-scissors fall into cycles that contradict the textbook answer: winners tend to repeat their move and losers shift to the next one ([source, summary only](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.5199); [source, summary only](https://m.phys.org/news/2014-05-reveals-chances-rock-paper-scissors.html)). David Sirlin argues that a game's strategic depth "should be defined almost entirely on its ability to support and reward yomi". Yomi is his word (Japanese for "reading") for reading the opponent's mind. The chain of "I know that you know" wraps back to the start after three layers, so it never resolves ([source](https://www.sirlin.net/ptw-book/7-spies-of-the-mind)). He calls hidden, simultaneous choices "a very useful tool in fighting against solvability". He also says fast real-time games already count as simultaneous "because of the speed of the gameplay" ([source](https://www.sirlin.net/articles/designing-yomi)). That explains why tag works with nothing hidden: when reacting takes longer than committing, the direction of a dodge is a hidden choice [opinion]. Each player's habits become the content the other player studies, and nobody pays to author them [opinion].

**Bluffing is the same structure applied to information.** Kuhn showed that bluffing, which looks like psychology, is part of mathematically best play in simplified poker ([source, summary only](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.3365)). In von Neumann's model the best player bets with the strongest and the weakest hands, so no bet gives the hand away ([source, summary only](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~tom/papers/poker2.pdf)). Björk and Holopainen's pattern catalogue gives bluffing two requirements: players must know different things, and there must be a way to pass hints indirectly. It also says bluffing clashes with equal information and with luck ([source](https://math.vu.nl/~eliens/media/pattern-bluffing.html)). Stakes decide whether lying exists at all. In a three-player version of Kuhn's poker, bluffing drops out of best play when the pot is 2 or less ([source, summary only](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.08124)). Chris Hecker built SpyParty on this idea: "When you force a human to act like a computer, you get a game" ([source](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/hecker-visits-uc-berkeley-to-talk-i-spyparty-i-future-of-emotion-in-games)).

Carrying, throwing at enemies, shooting enemies and building point at the environment. Their best move doesn't depend on anyone's choice, so it gets found and then has to be replaced [opinion]. Koster warns that a core mechanic "is probably interesting only for a limited amount of time" ([source](https://www.raphkoster.com/2012/01/24/an-atomic-theory-of-fun-game-design/)). Sony Online Entertainment put the cost bluntly: **"A chunk of content that takes us four years to make gets consumed by the players in less than a year"** ([source](https://www.gamewatcher.com/2014-21-05-sony-content-consumed-too-fast-in-mmos-player-generated-content-is-future)). Co-op games against the environment show both ways to pay:

- **Content plus generated maps.** Deep Rock Galactic's CEO calls their method "procedural level design", a "mix between hand-crafted components and code", and admits "we will need to add a lot of different things that you can encounter" ([source](https://gamingbolt.com/deep-rock-galactic-interview-heigh-ho-off-to-plunder-we-go/amp)).
- **A system that reacts to players.** Valve built Left 4 Dead's AI Director so "the action changes each time it's played" ([source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-discomfort-zone-the-hidden-potential-of-valve-s-ai-director)).
- **What happens when the content slows.** Helldivers 2 players tired of the same content when updates slowed ([source](https://game8.co/articles/latest/helldivers-2-escalation-of-freedom-update-doubles-player-count-after-downward-spiral)).
- **Levels.** Overcooked 2, a carry-and-throw game, shipped 36 to 45 base levels depending on who counts ([source](https://twinfinite.net/guides/overcooked-2-levels-long-many-how/)). Its designers' stated dream for their next game is that "every single level introduces something new" ([source](https://www.nintendolife.com/features/interview-were-not-making-a-party-game-now-its-more-of-an-evening-thing-ghost-town-is-moving-on-from-overcooked)).

In the user's graph model, an environment verb has to buy newness with content (more content loops) or with connected systems (more connections between nodes). Those are exactly the two things the formula wants low [opinion]. **Physics is the one partial exception.** A physics engine is a connected system the developer does not write, and every feature uses it without changing it [opinion]. Human: Fall Flat, made by one developer at its 2016 launch, has sold 55 million copies ([source](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/curve-games-human-fall-flat-has-sold-55-million-units)).

**Content need follows whoever picks the counter, not the verb.** The same verb flips from cheap to expensive when the other side changes from a human to the environment [opinion].

- **Seek.** MECCHA CHAMELEON's whole rule set is "The Seekers win if they find everyone within the time limit" ([read](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4704690/)). Phasmophobia needs 14 maps and 30 ghost types because its target is AI ([source](https://gonintendo.com/contents/57161-phasmophobia-content-roadmap-for-2026-revealed); [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phasmophobia_(video_game))).
- **Hide.** Prop Hunt needed no new maps. TF2's maps already had lots of clutter, and flat cartoon lighting made a disguised player hard to tell from a real prop ([source](https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2017/05/from-modders-to-mimics-peoples-history.html)).
- **Deduce.** Mafia's original rules had no special roles at all ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia_(party_game))). Among Us launched with one map ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Among_Us)).
- **Bluff.** Liar's Bar has two tables and four characters ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar%27s_Bar)).
- **Shoot.** The versus-AI shooters sit on content pipelines. Deep Rock Galactic moved to free seasonal content and sold over 8 million copies ([source](https://80.lv/articles/deep-rock-galactic-sales-surpassed-5-5-million-copies/)).

**The top verbs come in pairs where one verb is the other's counter:** bluff and call, hide and seek, disguise and spot, sabotage and accuse. One rule therefore produces both sides of the game [opinion]. The social-deduction rule sets back this up, because each has a single secret action and every other verb exists to expose it [opinion]:

- Werewolf's night kill ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia_(party_game)))
- The Resistance's secret mission card ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Resistance_(game)))
- Prop Hunt's disguise ([source](https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2017/05/from-modders-to-mimics-peoples-history.html))
- MECCHA CHAMELEON's body paint ([read](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4704690/))

**Uneven sides bring content back.** Dead by Daylight's chase is the same verb as tag, but the killer side needs about 42 distinct killers and the game keeps shipping chapters ([source](https://www.gfinityesports.com/misc/dead-by-daylight-best-killers-tier-list-dbd-dowsey/)). Even-sided Gorilla Tag launched small [from memory]. It grew to over $100M in revenue, and its team grew to over 100 people only after the hit ([source](https://roadtovr.com/gorilla-tag-revenue-vr-success-another-axiom/)). When one side's kit is unique, someone has to author it [opinion].

**Environment verbs climb the table when one shared object ties the players together.** R.E.P.O.'s crews carry fragile valuables whose value drops when damaged ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.P.O.)), and Chained Together links players with a chain ([source](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/06/25/chained-together-85k-concurrent-players-launch-steam)). A lab study tested combinations of co-op patterns and found that only one pairing raised engagement: **players working on the same object plus a limited shared resource** ([source](https://auc.edu.au/2017/11/combining-cooperative-design-patterns-to-improve-player-experience/)). That is the R.E.P.O. and Lethal Company recipe of fragile loot and a shared quota [opinion]. A DiGRA paper on co-op patterns warns that forcing several players to move one object feels artificial if the world gives no reason for it ([source](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/639/639)). This route gives coupling through bodies at medium content, not coupling through minds [opinion].

**On the user's hunch that hide-and-seek couples players most:** it sits in the top group, but bluffing and deducing tie with it. Its coupling also runs across the line between the two teams. MECCHA CHAMELEON's store page describes each hider painting their own body, and nothing in the rules it lists ties one hider to another ([read](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4704690/)), so inside the hider team players act side by side [opinion]. The ratings are judgment calls. No published study rates verbs by content need. The closest anchor is Zagal, Rick and Hsi's warning that a co-op game's experience must differ each time ([source, blog summary](https://grdphil.blogspot.com/2010/11/review-collaborative-games-lessons.html)).

## Co-op without an enemy team has to stop teammates acting as one mind

With no opposing team, the only other mind a player meets is a teammate. If teammates see everything, want the same thing and have time to plan, they act as one mind, and every surprise has to come from the environment [opinion]. Designers call this failure the **quarterback problem: one player works out the best moves and directs the rest**. A playtester asked Justin Gary, "What is my presence here actually doing to change the game compared to just one person manually running an extra position?" Gary's honest answer for most co-op games was "not much" ([source](https://justingarydesign.substack.com/p/the-quarterback-problem)). Zagal, Rick and Hsi named it in 2006: without "sufficient rationale for collaboration", a game degenerates into "one player making the decisions for the team". Their first lesson is the fix in one line: "introduce a tension between perceived individual utility and team utility" ([source, blog summary](https://grdphil.blogspot.com/2010/11/review-collaborative-games-lessons.html)). The standard co-op patterns (complementary abilities, abilities that combine, abilities usable only on others, shared goals) mostly give players jobs and bodies that help each other ([source](https://www.sbgames.org/sbgames2018/files/papers/ArtesDesignFull/187736.pdf)). One person could still run every seat [opinion]. That is the same failure the user's Deathly Reality Show v1 minigames hit, judged "too simple and easy and requires no cooperation at all" ([source: ~/.claude/design-anti-patterns.md, item 24]).

Known designs break the one mind in four ways.

**Split the information.** In Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes the defuser sees the bomb but not the manual, and the experts hold the manual but cannot see the bomb ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_Talking_and_Nobody_Explodes)). Its designer Ben Kane says "The fun really lives between players". He says each side must hold information worth trading, and that the rules must be "totally unambiguous", so all the friction sits in the talk ([source](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/finding-the-fun-in-bomb-defusal-with-i-keep-talking-and-nobody-explodes-i-)). In Spaceteam each player gets a different control panel, and orders arrive on your screen for controls on someone else's. The shouting was never designed: on hard levels "you basically have to talk over each other otherwise the timer runs out" ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceteam)). Online games get split information almost free because every screen is private. Jackbox's phone-only design works because each player can receive private information ([source](https://builtin.com/articles/jackbox-games-design-party-pack)).

**Narrow the channel.** In The Mind, players must play numbered cards in rising order without talking. Its designer says some people enjoy playing with no communication "except the passing of time" ([source](https://opinionatedgamers.com/2018/06/11/dale-yu-interview-with-wolfgang-warsch/)). Lethal Company limits talk to proximity voice (voice chat only nearby players can hear), plus walkie-talkies for range ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company)). Among Us allows discussion only during meetings [from memory].

**Split the goals around one shared node.** In Gamble With Your Friends, friends share one bank account and one debt to a loan shark ([source](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3892270)), so "one reckless player can destroy the run" ([source](https://www.whisperofthehouse.com/gamble-with-your-friends/beginner-guide)). In Dead of Winter, each player also has a secret personal objective: you win only if both the group goal and your own are met, and some objectives make you a secret betrayer ([source, summary only](https://hellnotes.com/dead-of-winter-board-game-review)). Battlestar Galactica's board game deals a second loyalty card midway, which can turn a loyal player into the traitor ([source, summary only](https://en.battlestarwiki.org/Board_game)).

**Split the time.** Real-time pressure means nobody has time to plan for everyone. Overcooked's designers fought what they called "First to Fun", where one player takes charge and leaves the others behind ([source](https://www.nintendolife.com/features/interview-were-not-making-a-party-game-now-its-more-of-an-evening-thing-ghost-town-is-moving-on-from-overcooked)). Of the four, time pressure is the hardest to get around, because even a player who knows everything cannot direct others fast enough [opinion].

**A co-op game can also borrow the enemy team's verbs without having an enemy team.** Lethal Company's v45 update added the Masked, which can turn a player into a monster that looks like a crewmate ([source, summary only](https://esports.gg/news/gaming/lethal-company-releases-first-major-update-v45-patch-notes)). A popular mod makes monsters replay recorded player voices ([source](https://primagames.com/tips/are-there-skinwalkers-in-lethal-company-answered)). Phasmophobia's ghost and equipment react to spoken key words ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phasmophobia_(video_game))). Each of these puts suspicion or hidden information into co-op without a human traitor, at the cost of one monster's worth of content [opinion].

**Pure coordination couples teammates' minds too, but it settles.** A focal point is the answer people pick by default when they cannot talk. Schelling's students, asked to meet a stranger in New York, mostly chose noon at the Grand Central information booth ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focal_point_(game_theory))). The two-person meeting problem becomes trivial as soon as one person is told to wait ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendezvous_problem)). Once a group finds a shared answer, nobody has a reason to leave it [opinion]. A Spyfall reviewer saw play converge on "local optima" as players learned its 24 locations, sometimes within one evening ([source](https://www.meeplelikeus.co.uk/spyfall-2014/)). Keep Talking generates each bomb by code, so the expert's lookups change every round ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_Talking_and_Nobody_Explodes)). To keep coordination fresh, reshuffle who knows what each round, cut the channel again, or add a threat that punishes predictable meeting spots, which brings hide-and-seek back inside the team [opinion]. These findings match points 2 (split), 3 (talk) and 7 (two-way) of the user's co-op gate in `~/.claude/design-anti-patterns.md` [opinion].

## Low content bought the spike; the long tail came from somewhere else

The retention numbers are blunt. D30 is the share of new players still playing 30 days after their first session. AppMagic puts friendslop's average D30 at **about 3%**. Content Warning fell from 19.8% on day 1 to 4.4% on day 7 and about 3% on day 30. For comparison, Phasmophobia holds 5.3% and Dead by Daylight 11.3%. AppMagic describes friendslop as aiming at "a handful of evenings rather than build habits" ([source](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025)). The low-content mind-verb hits fell the same way:

- **Liar's Bar.** Its monthly average went from 19,758 players at launch to 8,789, then 4,734, then 542 in October 2026 ([read](https://steamcharts.com/app/3097560)).
- **MECCHA CHAMELEON.** It peaked at 340,534 players online at once ([source](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/06/meccha-chameleon-is-a-hide-and-seek-body-painting-game-taking-over-steam/)) and had 3,395 online on 9 November 2026 ([read](https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamUserStats/GetNumberOfCurrentPlayers/v1/?appid=4704690)). It still sold a reported 20 million copies by mid-August ([source](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/meccha-chameleon-hits-20m-sales-with-22k-daily-players)), so the spike paid even though the tail did not hold.
- **Among Us.** It now averages 4.0% of its best month ([read](https://steamcharts.com/app/945360)).

**Mind verbs did not hold players better than environment verbs.** The October 2026 monthly average, as a share of each game's best month, compares the two groups [read: SteamCharts monthly tables, 2026-11-09; medians are arithmetic, opinion]:

| Group | Range | Median | Games |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short-dev social deduction and bluffing | 1.7–5.6% | about 3.4% | Among Us, Goose Goose Duck, Dread Hunger, Liar's Bar, LOCKDOWN Protocol, Guilty as Sock! |
| Short-dev co-op against the environment | 2.5–8.1% | about 5.9% | Lethal Company, Content Warning, Chained Together, PICO PARK 2, Schedule I, YAPYAP |

The games differ in age, so this is a rough cut, not a test [opinion]. The worst game in the whole sample is Mage Arena, a player-versus-player game with spells cast by voice, at 0.4% [read: SteamCharts, 2026-11-09]. Low-content mind verbs also failed at launch: ROUNDS peaked at about 2,269 players despite about 95% positive reviews ([source](https://steamdb.info/app/1557740/charts)), and LOCKDOWN Protocol at about 9,400 ([source](https://steambase.io/apps/lockdown-protocol)). The verb alone does not explain the hits. The big ones also had a funny moment you could read in a second: the roulette gun, the body paint, the ragdoll fall, the cut-off scream on proximity voice [opinion].

The games that held their players did so through one of five routes.

1. **Updates during the wave.** R.E.P.O. holds 16.6% and PEAK 21.4% of their best months a year or more later, and both kept shipping updates ([read](https://steamcharts.com/app/3241660); [read: SteamCharts, 2026-11-09]). R.E.P.O.'s D30 is "above average" for friendslop ([source](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-friendslop-games-in-2025)). Among Us added a hide-and-seek mode, new roles and a new map in its 2021 roadmap, after the wave had passed ([source, summary only](https://www.pcgamer.com/among-us-hide-and-seek-sheriff-new-map)). Its Steam players kept falling, to 4% of the best month a year later ([read](https://steamcharts.com/app/945360)).
2. **Heavy content.** Phasmophobia, the most content-heavy game in the set, holds 23.8% six years on ([read](https://steamcharts.com/app/739630)).
3. **Strangers, skill and mods.** The long-lived player-versus-player games hold the most: Counter-Strike 71%, Team Fortress 2 63%, Dead by Daylight 67%, Rust 60% ([read: SteamCharts, 2026-11-09]). None of them is low content. What they share is public matchmaking with strangers, a skill ladder, and a modding or creator layer [opinion].
4. **Player-made content.** Garry's Mod averaged 22,382 players in October 2026, close to its 25,704 in October 2015 ([read](https://steamcharts.com/app/4000)), and Prop Hunt has lived inside it as one mode among many ([source](https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2017/05/from-modders-to-mimics-peoples-history.html)). MECCHA CHAMELEON supports Steam Workshop maps, meaning player-made maps shared through Steam ([read](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4704690/)). Valve's Gabe Newell said "we cannot compete with our customers in the production of content" ([source, summary only](https://pcgamer.com/top-tf2-item-makers-making-500k-a-year-we-cannot-compete-with-our-customers)).
5. **Generated maps.** R.E.P.O.'s levels are randomized from modular pieces ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.P.O.)), and Valheim's five-person team ([source](https://embed.gamereactor.eu/valheim-was-developed-by-only-five-people/)) relied on a generated world [from memory].

Outside video games, Mafia has lasted about 39 years with no owner and no updates. It spread through classrooms, summer camps and conferences ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia_(party_game))), and Codenames sold over 16 million copies in ten years ([source](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/this-czech-board-game-has-outsold-kingdom-come-and-turns-10-with-a-new-edition)). Those games live as habits that pass between strangers, not as one friend group's game [opinion].

**"Players are the content" is true for strangers, and unproven for a closed friend group.** The on-record statements come from persistent worlds full of strangers. Game Developer says EVE Online stays alive because "its players are the content" ([source](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-eve-online-i-s-big-battle-proves-players-i-are-i-the-content-in-a-sandbox-mmo)). Koster, speaking about MMOs, says friendships are the top predictor of staying and "Content does not last forever" ([source, summary only](https://gamesradar.com/games/world-of-warcraft/as-ultima-online-approaches-its-28th-birthday-former-creative-lead-reveals-what-it-takes-for-an-mmo-to-last-for-decades-and-why-they-sometimes-fail-content-does-not-last-forever)). Carrying these claims over to a group of 2–8 friends is an assumption [opinion]. Inside one group, the same five friends learn each other's bluffs, while a pool of strangers stays fresh. That is plausible but unmeasured [opinion].

Coupling between players also couples quitting: when one friend stops, the game stops for the rest [opinion]. One study of a large online game found that friend teams played less unless their schedules closely matched ([source, summary only](https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis2025/hti/hti/33)). Being someone else's content is not fun for the loser, and EVE players push back on exactly that ([source, summary only](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/players-as-content-and-the-victim-mindset/373239)). The losing side needs short rounds and something to laugh at [opinion].

Public lobbies bring their own costs. MECCHA CHAMELEON's public servers vanish when the host leaves ([source](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/06/meccha-chameleon-is-a-hide-and-seek-body-painting-game-taking-over-steam/)). Liar's Bar players reported cheaters in public lobbies ([source, user reports](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3097560/discussions/0/4625853071149253012)). A human on the other side also did not free a live-service game from content: Knockout City, a player-versus-player dodgeball game, shut down citing "the amount of content required to sustain an audience" ([source](https://www.pcgamer.com/two-years-after-launch-live-service-dodgeball-game-knockout-city-is-shutting-down)).

## What the formula needs: one hidden choice inside the shared hub

The formula has three parts: low content, low coupling between features, and high coupling between players. Only verbs whose newness comes from other players meet all three at once [opinion]. Environment verbs force a choice between more content, which breaks the first part, and more connected systems, which breaks the second. Physics is the one connected system that comes free [opinion]. The earlier research found that the co-op hits already couple players through one shared node in the hub: the chain, the RV, the bank, the camera, the fragile valuable ([source: research.md, 2026-11-09 entries]). That gives coupling through bodies. The cheapest way to add coupling through minds is **one hidden thing per player that makes teammates read each other** [opinion]. Concretely, that is one of the following:

- a split view set by where each player stands, plus a lossy way to share it, such as proximity voice;
- a private stake in the shared node, like Dead of Winter's personal objective on top of a shared bank;
- a timer that leaves no time for one player to direct the others;
- a monster that borrows the hide or mimic verb.

All of these are rules about who sees what, when, and who owns which node. None of them needs new levels [opinion]. Zagal's third lesson guards the shared node: "Players must be able to trace payoffs back to their decisions" ([source, blog summary](https://grdphil.blogspot.com/2010/11/review-collaborative-games-lessons.html)). If the bank drops, everyone has to see whose bet did it.

The research gives five concrete checks [opinion unless tagged]:

1. **Per node.** Ask whether a player's best choice changes depending on what a teammate or opponent just chose. If not, that node is a puzzle: it will be solved and then used up.
2. **Search budget.** Keep the seeker's budget below "check every spot", or hiders fall back to one safe spot ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.09538.pdf)).
3. **Stakes.** Make lying win more than it risks, or players stop lying ([source, summary only](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.08124)).
4. **Where randomness goes.** Put it in the setup (who knows what), not in the outcome, because luck crowds out bluffing ([source](https://math.vu.nl/~eliens/media/pattern-bluffing.html)).
5. **Cost of a wrong guess.** It should cost a round, not the match. Caleb Compton argues "any combo is escapable" ([source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/know-thy-enemy-designing-for-yomi-in-games)).

**Plan where the long tail comes from before launch, because the verb will not supply it** [opinion]. The evidence offers five sources:

- **Strangers in public lobbies.** These need host migration and enough players to fill lobbies.
- **Player-made stages.** A hide or mimic verb turns any map into content, as Prop Hunt did with TF2's clutter.
- **Generated layouts**, as in R.E.P.O.
- **Small updates while the game is still on its wave.** R.E.P.O. and PEAK did this; Among Us updated after its wave and did not recover.
- **A short life, priced for it.** MECCHA CHAMELEON sells at $5.99 ([read](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4704690/)) and PEAK launched at $7.99 ([source](https://80.lv/articles/climbing-sim-peak-has-sold-4-5-million-copies-in-less-than-a-month)).

Two more risks follow from a small rule set [opinion]. First, it cannot be owned. Hasbro judged Mafia "unprotectable" ([source](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/werewolf)), and Prop Hunt moved from engine to engine ([source](https://www.blog.radiator.debacle.us/2017/05/from-modders-to-mimics-peoples-history.html)), so expect clones within weeks. Second, a game that also works with two players keeps one friend's absence from ending everyone's session.

## Conclusion

The verb is the wrong unit to rank. What sets a game's content bill is what sits on the other side of the verb. Seek, throw, shoot and defend each flip from nearly free to content-hungry when that side changes from a human to the environment. Hide-and-seek is not special in itself; it is the cheapest way to put a human on the other side with something hidden. So the useful question for each node in a design is "who picks the counter here, and what can't they see?", not "which verb should this be?" [opinion]. For a co-op game, the answer has to come from teammates who are kept from being one mind, since there is no enemy team to supply it.

Low content solves newness within each round, not the reasons to come back. The evidence says newness per round sells copies, and coming back depends on a pool that renews itself: strangers, player-made maps, generated layouts or updates [opinion]. The user's formula therefore needs a fourth part the three-part version leaves out: which renewable pool the game plugs into, or a deliberate choice to price for a short life [opinion]. What remains unmeasured: no study rates verbs by content need, no public retention data splits friendslop by verb type, and nobody has measured how fast a closed friend group wears out a bluffing or hiding game.
