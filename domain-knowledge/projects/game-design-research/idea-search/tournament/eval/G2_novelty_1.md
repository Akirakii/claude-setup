# Novelty batch 1 (18 ideas)

### G201 Diver's Eyes
- Rule (one sentence): The key log holding the jam looks like every other log from above, so someone must dive under the jam to find it, and they can only come back up where friends have levered a gap open.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the floating driftwood jam (one physics pile).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the diver sees the key log from below, is running out of air, and picks a gap to come up through. The players above must lever that gap open, and that rolls the logs their friends are standing on. Once the diver is up and says which log is the key, the levers must shove it from where they stand. The diver picks where; the levers pick how and when.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a fishing village clearing a storm's driftwood jam from the harbour mouth before the fleet comes home in the dark.
- Look (one line): a grey harbour, a tangle of bleached driftwood, a diver's bubbles rising between the logs.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one diver, two levers and one key log. At 6, two key logs and two divers, with four levers splitting between two gaps.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": which log is the key. It is uncovered only by diving under. The jam hides it; no player does.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ola runs out of air under the jam because Pip levered the log next to her gap and rolled it shut. Or the fleet arrives because the levers shoved the log beside the one Ola called.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each jam is a generated physics pile, and the key log is a different log every time.

### G204 Threshers
- Rule (one sentence): Two players slam a pair of long poles together to crush the beetles swarming the floor, and the others dart between the poles to pull out grain before the next slam.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the pole pair and the strip of floor between the poles.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the slammers want to slam often because beetles pile up, while the gatherers need the gap open.
  - Both decide at the same moment, by voice and by watching.
  - The two slammers must close together. If one end closes first, the poles skew and sweep a diagonal, a third danger.
  - Roles swap after each harvest.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): farmhands threshing a cursed harvest crawling with beetles.
- Look (one line): a sunlit barn floor, two long bamboo poles, gold grain under a black swarm.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, two slammers and one gatherer. At 6, two pole pairs cross in a plus (four slammers, two gatherers), so each slam also endangers the other pair's gatherers. At 4 and 5, one pair plus 2 or 3 gatherers.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a gatherer is clapped and drops their armful, or the beetles overflow the floor. The blame goes to the slammer who closed early or skewed, or the gatherer who lingered, seen in the moment.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): beetles spread by one simple rule, grain lands at random, pole layouts vary (1–3 pairs at angles), skewed poles follow physics, and roles rotate.

### G207 Bell Pour
- Rule (one sentence): One player tilts a crucible of molten bronze into a clay mould whose
  channels branch to several vents, and each vent must be plugged by another player at the instant
  metal reaches its mouth. Too early traps air and the bell cracks there; too late spills metal.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the mould (a tree of channels and its vents), fed by
  the crucible.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The pourer controls the flow rate but cannot see the vents.
  - Each plugger sees only the vents near them and runs between them.
  - A surge from the pourer changes which vent fills next.
  - The pourer slows or speeds up on the pluggers' calls ("two filling at once, slow!").
  - The pourer's job rotates with every bell.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a village foundry crew casting the town's bells
  before the furnace dies.
- Look (one line): a dark shed, glowing orange streams, and sparks spitting from the vents.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: one player pours and two run to cover about 4 vents.
  - At 6: two crucibles pour into one large mould from two gates, and four pluggers cover about
    8 vents. The two metal fronts must meet while still hot.
- Hidden: none. The channels are drawn on the mould, and metal glows through the clay.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a vent overflows beside the plugger who was late.
  Or the bell is rung at the end and cracks at the vent someone plugged early, and the crack shows
  whose vent it was. The clock is cooling metal: if the stream stops for ~2 s, the channels freeze
  (a cold seam the pourer caused), and the crucible cools over ~40 s.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each mould is a
  random branching channel tree from one small generator (vent count, branch lengths, heights), and
  bells grow larger through a run. Round 50 has a channel layout no one has poured before.

- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.

- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the deck tilts by physics and bends every arc, leaks spawn by one rule with random strengths, and the throwing web between players keeps changing.

### G213 Weave

- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): if my rope's next crossing must be "under", I need a friend whose next is "over", and we must meet over the giant at the same moment, one jumping and one ducking. - Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Lilliputian sailors tying down a washed-up giant before he wakes.
- Look (one line): tiny figures racing across a huge sleeping body on a beach, ropes criss-crossing.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, three ropes make a braid. At 6, they make a net. Odd sizes are fine, because who needs over or under shuffles with every crossing.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the giant sits up and the ropes slide off. The slipped crossing lights up and names the player who went the wrong way.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the giant thrashes by physics and tears different crossings each time; the weave pattern is drawn by the players' own paths; giant poses come from a short list.

### G216 Ice Whip

- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):

- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): someone is flung onto thin ice, or the pivot falls through. The blame goes to the tail who let go late or the pivot who aimed wrong, seen in the moment.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each landing sets the next pivot, so the crew makes its own route. Floe and crack layouts are generated, and the line moves by physics.

### G222 Rivet Gang
- Rule (one sentence): Every joint on the steel frame needs a rivet thrown up red-hot from the forge, caught, and hammered while a second player holds the other side of the same hole, all before it cools.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the frame's open rivet holes (each hot rivet carries a cooling clock).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): The heater times each rivet in the fire (too short, not hot; too long, burnt) and throws it to whichever pair is ready. The catcher must be where the arc lands and set the rivet in a hole of their choice. The second player must reach the far side of that same hole before it cools. The heater picks a target by who is free and in reach. The catcher moves by the throw. The far-side player moves by the catcher's chosen hole. 2 extra rules: the crane swings in the next beam on its own clock, and wind sways the beams and the throws.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): 1930s ironworkers raising a skyscraper on bare girders.
- Look (one line): rust-red steel over a hazy skyline, glowing rivets arcing through dusk.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = heater plus one pair on the beam; every rivet needs all three. 6 = heater plus two or three pairs on different beams; the heater chooses who gets each hot rivet, and pairs reform on the fly.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a beam still on its temporary bolts tears loose when the crane lets go, because too few rivets went in. Each lost rivet was seen falling glowing to the street: a bad throw (heater), a missed catch (catcher), or cold because the far side was empty (that player).
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): a generated frame each floor (beam layout, hole positions, which girders connect for walking). Wind gusts. The building grows, so throws get longer until someone carries the forge up a floor, which leaves a gap with no heater.

### G225 Fling Rope
- **Rule (one sentence):** Nobody can climb on their own; an anchored friend reels you in so hard that you fly up past them, and you must grab a hold at the top of your flight.
- **Hub:** the ropes between players, a single physics rule: a fast reel flings, a slow reel drags. Extra rule: a used hold crumbles a few seconds after you let go.
- **Coupling:**
  - The anchor picks reel speed and timing.
  - The flyer picks the lean and which hold to grab, and shouts which hold they want.
  - Once they grab, the flyer becomes the anchor for the next person. The anchor's flight is already wasted if the flyer is late.
  - Each player is roped to two others, so who can fling whom changes as the crew climbs.
- **Fantasy:** a mountain rescue team climbing a collapsing cliff before the tide comes in.
- **Look:** bright ropes zig-zagging up a dark, crumbling sea cliff.
- **At 3 / at 6:**
  - At 3 it is a chain of three: every fling uses one of only two ropes.
  - At 6 the ropes form a web, giving more routes, but tangles block flings.
- **Hidden:** none.
- **A failed run:** Ana reels too hard and Leo overshoots into an overhang, falls, and drags the chain down. His flight arc showed Ana's reel speed.
- **Tail:** the wall is generated from rock chunks, holds crumble after use, and the order of the rope web changes every run.
- **Novelty:** ~. *Tangled Together* has reeling but both players walk; *Mountain Climbers* has launching.

### G228 Blind Spot

- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the trap field (one generated floor).

To warn a friend you must look at them, so you stop watching your own route. Who I watch depends on who just got close to a trap, and my safe path depends on who is watching me. (Honest note: the jobs are mostly the same for everyone.)

### G231 Looping Mill
- Rule (one sentence): Glowing steel rods snake through a mill of roller stands, and whenever a rod's head pops out of a stand a player must catch it with tongs and feed it into another stand before it whips loose, while the hot loop left between the two stands sweeps across the floor.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the mill floor and the rods on it.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Each rod needs a set number of passes through any stands. Feeding into a far stand makes a long loop that cuts across the floor. My path to the next head runs across loops my friends just made. The stand I pick decides whose path my loop blocks and where the rod's head pops out next. Several rods run at once with heads due at different moments, so who takes which head is called out live. 1 extra rule: touching a loop burns you and you drop your tongs.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): catchers in an 1890s rod mill.
- Look (one line): a dark iron hall, orange rods looping across black floor plates, sparks.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = 4 stands, 1–2 rods at a time. 6 = 7 stands, 3–4 rods, and the floor fills with loops.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a head isn't caught in time and the rod cobbles, whipping across the floor. It's seen who called that head and didn't get there, or whose long loop cut off their path. Three cobbles and the mill shuts down.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): loop physics (rope sim). Stands are placed at random on a grid each shift. The rod schedule speeds up. The floor pattern is never the same twice.
