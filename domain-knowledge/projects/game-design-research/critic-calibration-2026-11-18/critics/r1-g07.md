# Critic r1, g07: Front Page

## The problem underneath
The fantasy does not need a friend: the proposal's own one-player test says the line still works alone.
A friend is a score multiplier (doubles a creature shot) and insurance (camera rescue, freeing from threads), never a requirement.
The rules do cause the four listed feelings: each line has a rule that pays for it (friend in frame, selfie bonus, camera stays in the city, scored review).

## The scene, second by second
0–5 s: one lures, one poses; both ends decide, and the poser stands next to danger.
5–8 s: the third walks into threads; this is an ambush the crew didn't schedule.
8–12 s: the selfie shot lands; the shooter spends 1 of 7–15 shots, which is the only price written.
12–15 s: the second photographs the trapped friend, then frees them; no rule written makes the wait cost either of them anything.
15 s+: the rocket; all three press the same button at the same thing.

## Fewest and most players
1 player: plays and finishes; selfie mode puts a body in the frame, so even the "teammate" half has a solo stand-in.
8 players: eight cameras shoot one staged scene side by side; nothing written scales the target with crew size or stops eight copies of one shot from each scoring.

## The three plans
Always wins: read the board, drag bodies to a drain cover, lure a creature on, put a friend in the frame, shoot the packed frame.
By session five: highest-capacity camera first, cheap trap pins a creature, pose beside it, one board row per shot.
A perfect crew's round: a built photo set; the chase and the trapped friend happen only when someone slips.

## Findings
1. SEVERE, only this design: a friend multiplies the score but nothing fails without one, and one player alone clears it (co-op points 1 and 6).
2. MEDIUM, only this design: "free them or photograph first" has one answer, photograph first, since the wait has no written price.
3. MEDIUM, only this design: "which camera" has one answer (guides: highest capacity first) and the other end decides nothing.
4. MEDIUM, only this design: at 8 players everyone does the same job side by side on one scene, and no rule scales the target (B22, B24).
5. The baseline has this too (row "One end of a choice only stands"): the trapped friend only waits and talks.
6. The baseline has this too (same row): the dead friend only watches.
7. The baseline has this too (row "Bought tools lower the need for friends"): the trap replaces the friend who lures, boots and radio replace a rescuer.
8. The baseline has this too (row "A choice is one sum for the whole crew"): "spend a shot now or save it" is a count of shots left against the board.
9. The baseline has this too (row "fixed roles with a card of answers, fear only in mistakes"): lurer, poser, shooter, and the bonus board as the card.
Not counted: a friend's death in frame pays double, which would make a planned death the best last shot, but what a dead player loses is (not found).

## The four baseline parts
Priced help on good nights: present; standing in a friend's frame next to a creature is the help, the price is the poser's own body, and the best plan does it every trip.
Rising pressure that breaks the plan: present; the target rises every three trips while shots stay at 7–15, so each shot must be worth more and safe scenes stop paying.
Two threats at once: present; the threads hold one friend while a creature chases another, with the drain thing and soldiers on top.
Carry-over: present; money, gear and trip passes change the next trip.

## Could not break
1. Every listed feeling has a rule behind it.
2. A friend's failure is seen as it happens and again at the review.
3. The shot limit makes each press a commitment.
4. The camera rescue: a dead friend's work rides on a living one, and going back is priced by danger.
5. The crew can lose: passes run out, and the target rises.
6. The lure: both ends decide in the same second (pose, run, or shoot).
