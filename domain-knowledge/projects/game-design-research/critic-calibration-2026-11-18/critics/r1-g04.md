# Critic round 1: g04 (Clip Hunters)

## The problem underneath
- The fantasy does not need a friend: the proposal's own one-player test says one player can hold the camera and film.
- A friend adds a subject for the lens, a lure and a second pair of hands for the camera, all extras on top of a solo act.
- Feeling 1 (kept filming while chased) is caused by the rules: views pay for screen fill and hold time up to about 9 seconds.
- Feeling 2 (heard ourselves screaming) is caused by the rules: voices are recorded and the clip is watched at upload.
- Feeling 3 (footage died with him) is only true on day three: on days one and two the camera and its footage can be fetched on the next dive.

## The scene, second by second
- 0 s: the crew leaves the capsule, air drains, 90 s of film.
- The creature comes round a corner: the holder films, the second player lures, both decide in the same second.
- The lure is grabbed, so the lure failed as a help and paid as footage.
- The holder is hit and throws the camera: the third player picks it up and runs.
- The fourth player does nothing in the whole scene.

## Rounds
- Fewest (1): film one creature at close range for 9 s, cut, walk out; no lure, no catcher, so one hit loses the day, but the round can be played and finished.
- Most (4): one films, one lures, two stand in shot or follow; one camera and one creature leave two players without a job.
- Strategy that always wins: hold 9 s at close range, cut, save the camera before the body, leave at a fixed air mark.
- Fifth session: exactly the plan the proposal writes under "what experienced crews do".
- Perfect crew's round: about ten 9-second shots, lure each time, out at the mark; the core act (filming while chased) still happens, so the idea survives perfect play.

## Findings
1. SEVERE, missing baseline part: no rule puts two threats on the crew at once; air is one clock for everyone and one creature is covered by one lure.
2. Medium, only this design: one player alone can play and finish (co-op point 6); whether the target scales with crew size is not written.
3. Medium, only this design: "camera or teammate" has one answer, since the dead come back on the surface for a bill and the footage does not; the proposal admits it.
4. Medium, only this design: a filmed death adds views and costs only money, and the target is counted in views, so dying on purpose in front of the lens may be the cheapest shot; what happens when the bill can't be paid is not written.
5. Medium, only this design: faces, emotes, bones and held objects add views with no danger and no number, so a safe farm next to the capsule can't be ruled out ("depends on the numbers" is a fail).
6. The baseline has this too (row 7, fixed roles and a card of answers): one holder, lures, 9 s, a fixed leave point.
7. The baseline has this too (row 4, one sum for the crew): "leave or push" is settled by a fixed air mark, and "light or gadget" has nothing at the other end.
8. The baseline has this too (row 4, no friend in the choice): "spend or save film" is the holder's call alone, as the proposal says.
9. The baseline has this too (row 5, one end only stands): players who emote in front of the lens, and players who follow the holder out.
10. The baseline has this too (row 3, bought tools lower the need for friends): lights, healing gear and gadgets that raise views.
- Not counted: what a dead player does for the rest of the dive is not written.

## Co-op check
1. Coupled: pass; the lure is worth nothing without the camera, and the camera reaches the surface past a hit only through a friend's hands.
2. Split: pass; holder, lure and subject are split by position and the camera can change hands.
3. Talk: pass; air, 90 s of film and a chase make people shout, and the shouting is on the clip.
4. Blame: pass; the holder goes down with the camera in front of everyone, and on film.
5. Not easy: pass; a new crew, or one player, can miss the target, and the target and the creatures rise.
6. Not solo: fail; finding 2.
7. Two-way: pass; lure and filmer decide together, thrower and catcher decide together; the choices with "nothing" at the other end are crew sums, not couplings.
- Lone survivor: not stuck, they can pick the camera up and walk out.
- Dying on purpose: may make it easier, finding 4.

## The four baseline parts
- Priced help on good nights: present; stepping in front of the creature risks the grab and a growing bill, and it is planned play, not a mistake.
- Rising pressure: present; the target rises while film stays at 90 s and one subject flattens at 9 s, so last run's shot list stops paying.
- Two threats at once: missing; finding 1.
- Carry-over: present; money, items, the gap to the target and a camera left below all change the next dive.

## Could not break
1. Keep filming or run: a price that slides each second, a 9 s cap, and a friend deciding at the other end.
2. The camera thrown before going down: the dying player still decides.
3. Footage counts only once the one camera is uploaded on the surface: the whole crew's stake is one object that can be dropped.
4. Watching the clip together with voices: the payoff is made by a rule, not by a hope.
5. The perfect crew's round still contains the core act.
