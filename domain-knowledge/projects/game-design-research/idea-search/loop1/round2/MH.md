# MH: searching threat (round 2)

## Method log
- Listed threat senses first (sound, light, bubbles, heat, smell, hull shake), then paired each with an act one
  friend does to another, so the help itself is the clue. 20 raw pairs.
- One searcher rule for all six: it goes to the **place** of the last clue it noticed (not to a person), sweeps
  outward from there, and patrols places where clues piled up earlier in the run (pushback on habits). A director
  only paces it (sends it nearer or lets it drift off). No "hunts whoever holds X" rule; no hiding spot it skips.
- Grouped the raw list by core family (sight given by a friend, refill only from a friend, movement off a friend,
  muffling a friend, passing items). Refill had 7 near-copies; kept one (Tin Toys). Dropped Head-lamp Ring as the
  same family as Sonar.
- Tested each against P4 ("at the moment of the act, what does each end choose?") and R5 by trying the lines
  "pair up for good", "go slow", "help only when it's far", "always help the nearest". Cut ideas where one held.
- Known risk: refill and muffle ideas pull toward fixed buddy pairs. Counter used: one-way acts (you can't wind and
  be wound at once), goals spread across the map, odd crews, and the searcher's memory of busy spots.

## Raw candidates

6. Wrong Hands: each thief's tool works only in someone else's hands; the watchman goes to dropped-tool clatters. (kept MH-6)
7. Head-lamp Ring: you see only through one friend's head-cam, so they must look at you; the stalker sees bodies lit by lamps. (dropped: same family as Sonar)
8. Buddy Breath: divers breathe only from a friend's spare hose; the eel swims to bubble plumes. (dropped: same core as Tin Toys)
9. Last Match: a dying candle relights only from a friend's flame, and relights flare; moth-things fly to flares. (dropped: refill copy)
10. Huddle: warmth only from hugging a friend; a heat-sighted stalker sees hugs. (dropped: refill copy, hugs pool up)
11. Trade Places: two who reach for each other at once swap spots, both spots pop; the butler walks to pops. (dropped: a rescue drops the rescuer next to it; weak loop)
12. Bell Pairs: your bell is silent only while you hold a friend's hand; the giant wakes to rings. (dropped: settles into fixed pairs where one leads)
13. Trust Fall: you get down a shaft only by dropping into a friend's catch; crashes carry. (dropped: "first one climbs down slow, rotate" beats it)
14. Ventriloquist Ring: your footsteps sound where your linked friend stands. (dropped: only hurts; "move when it's far from your link" beats it)
15. Hide Me: only a friend can shut you in a cupboard, lids creak, it opens creaked lids. (dropped: the hidden one only waits)
16. Thorn Pull: only a friend can pull your curse thorns, and you scream. (dropped: refill copy)
17. Hot Torches: a torch burns your hand after 2 s, so you toss it on; dropped torches hiss. (dropped: too close to round 1's hunted holder)
18. Pickpocket Crew: lift coins from friends' pockets, jingles call the guard. (dropped: no shared co-op core)
19. Sleepwalkers: left untouched 20 s you sleepwalk; a friend's shake wakes you, loudly. (dropped: the sleeper only waits)
20. Camouflage: only a friend's spray paint hides you, and spraying hisses. (dropped: refill copy)

## Kept ideas

### MH-1 Sonar Deep
- Rule (one sentence): Your ping shows one friend their surroundings for a few seconds (never you), and the blind
  thing in the trench swims toward wherever a ping came from.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): salvage divers in a pitch-black trench, pulling lost cargo out before
  your air runs out, while something huge and blind hunts by ear.
- Aesthetic (one line): black screen; green sonar rings draw wire-frame rocks around a friend and fade in 3 s; the
  thing is only ever a shape caught in someone else's ring.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I aim at Mira and hold the
  button: a short hold gives her a small, quiet ring; a long hold gives her a wide, long view but carries far from
  me. She now sees cargo, cliffs and maybe the thing, and decides: grab cargo, shout what she sees near me, or ping
  me back so I can see to swim away from my own noise. Blind after pinging, I need her answer.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): the thing goes to the last ping's
  spot and sweeps out; where it is is known only from what a friend's ring showed. Who should ping whom, and how
  loud, changes with who is near cargo, who just pinged (and is now the clue), and where the thing was last seen.
  A pair that ping-pongs from one spot builds up a busy spot it then patrols. No safe spot: open water, it sweeps
  everywhere; staying silent leaves you blind.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the ping (aim at a friend, hold for loudness).
  Lag: none critical; pings are one-sided.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 each diver has only two friends who can give sight, so every ping is a hard call. At 6 more
  givers but more pings, so the thing is busier and the crew splits into groups around scattered cargo.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): where the thing is; uncovered only when it falls inside a ring a friend
  gave you; you hide by not pinging, which costs your friends their sight.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Tom gives Mira a big ping near the cargo while the thing is close to
  Tom; it comes to his spot. Mira, seeing, grabs cargo instead of pinging Tom back; Tom swims blind into it. The
  ping lines (who pinged whom) show for everyone, so all saw Mira not answer.

### MH-2 Dead Air
- Rule (one sentence): In zero gravity you can grab a wall to stop but can only move by pushing off a friend,
  which sends you both flying apart, and every push clangs through the hull to the thing crawling on it.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): salvagers in a dead space station, floating cells back to the airlock
  while something on the outer hull feels for every bump.
- Aesthetic (one line): slow-tumbling silhouettes in red emergency light; each push sends a visible ripple running
  along the walls.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I grab Jo and we both press push;
  we fly apart along the line between us, at a speed set by how hard we both push (hard = fast and loud). Jo
  chooses the angle with me (she picks where she ends up too) and how hard; a soft push is quiet but slow. Then
  someone drifting ahead must catch me (a quiet grab) to give me my next push.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): to send me toward the cell, my
  partner must fly the opposite way, maybe toward the thing's last clang. Each push picks who goes where and who
  takes the bad direction. The thing crawls to the latest clang and sweeps nearby; the director paces it. Rooms
  and cell spots change per round from a short list. No safe spot: grabbing a wall stops you but you can't leave
  without a friend, so a stuck pair is a sitting target.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the push between two players.
  Lag: both must press within a short window; check online.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 each push leaves the third player as the only catcher, so it's always a triangle. At 6 it plays
  like billiards: more catchers, more clangs.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): the thing's spot on the hull; you hear its scrape and see dents through
  the wall; you hide by drifting still, which leaves you unable to move without a friend.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ana pushes hard off Ben to reach a cell; Ben shoots toward the last
  clang and nobody is placed to catch him; he drifts into the thing. The push angle is visible: Ana's call.

### MH-3 Tin Toys
- Rule (one sentence): Your key turns only in a friend's hand, each turn gives you two seconds of walking and
  makes one click, and the house cat comes to clicks.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): wind-up toys crossing a dark nursery at night to gather the music
  box's lost pins before the child wakes.
- Aesthetic (one line): tin-plate toys among giant furniture in moonlight; each click a small "tk" ripple; the cat
  only two eyes in the dark until it's close.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I take Pip's key and turn: each
  turn is 2 s of walking for Pip and one click. Pip decides when to walk off (fewer turns but sooner gone); I decide
  how many turns, and whether Pip or someone else gets them. While winding I stand still and my own spring runs
  down, so I need someone to wind me next.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): a toy next to a pin needs 3
  turns; a toy crossing the room needs 10, a loud cluster. The cat walks to the last click cluster and pats around;
  where it is and where the pins lie decide who gets wound and how much. Toys that run down freeze where they are,
  so someone must make a rescue run. Winding can't happen both ways at once, and pins are spread, so fixed pairs
  don't hold; busy rooms get patrolled. No safe spot: the cat paws under beds and bats still toys it finds.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the key on each toy's back.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 a triangle where each toy lives on two friends. At 6 more winders, but more clicks for the cat
  to choose from.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): where the cat is; seen only when its eyes face you or by its purr up
  close; you hide by not winding, which leaves friends frozen.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Kit gives Pip 10 turns beside the bed while the cat is under it; the
  cat pounces on the clicks; Pip runs, but Kit, wound down, freezes and is batted off the table. Kit's loud winding
  is the visible cause.

### MH-4 Frog Legs
- Rule (one sentence): A frog can hop far only by leaping off a friend's back, and every big landing slaps loud
  enough for the half-blind chef to hear.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): frogs escaping a restaurant kitchen during the dinner rush, counter to
  counter, while the chef, without his glasses, chops at every slap.
- Aesthetic (one line): steamy copper kitchen; "SPLAT" rings on landing; a huge squinting chef sweeping his hands
  over the counters.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I crouch; Bo runs up my back and
  leaps. I choose where I face (where Bo lands) and when I kick up (a well-timed kick sends Bo farther and louder);
  Bo chooses whether to go and can twist a little in the air. Bo lands and crouches for the next frog. A solo hop is
  quiet but tiny and can't cross gaps.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): the chef goes to the last slap
  and feels around; the director paces his steps. Counter layouts and pot spots change per round from a short list.
  Which frog leaps off which back, and how hard, depends on where the chef's hands are, which gap is next and who is
  stranded. He sweeps counters where slaps piled up. No safe spot: every counter is in reach; staying put means the
  run ends at closing time.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): a friend's back (crouch).
  Lag: the leap and the kick are a timed pair; check online.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 one leapfrog chain where everyone is back and jumper in turn. At 6 two or three chains, or one
  long one, and more slaps.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Bo faces the sink and kicks hard; Lu lands with a huge slap right
  under the chef's hand. Bo's facing and kick are visible.
- Note vs the round-1 hook: the hook pulls two together and one end stays; here one frog launches off another and
  the back chooses the direction.

### MH-5 Dust Sneeze
- Rule (one sentence): Moving through tomb dust builds a sneeze that bursts out unless a friend pinches your nose
  in time, and the mummy goes to every sneeze it hears.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): tomb robbers carrying gold out of a dusty crypt while its mummy
  shuffles the halls listening.
- Aesthetic (one line): torch-lit dust drifting in beams; a twitching red nose shows a sneeze coming; "ACHOO" rings
  fill the hall, a pinched one is a small squeak.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): Mira's nose twitches; I pinch it
  in the one-second window and her sneeze becomes a squeak (heard only close by), but we both stop for a moment.
  Mira decides where to steer before it comes (toward me or toward someone else) and can hold it in a little
  longer, risking a bigger sneeze; I decide whether to drop my gold run for her.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): sneezes build from how each
  player moved (running and carrying gold kick up more dust), so who needs a pinch next shifts all the time. The
  mummy walks to the last sneeze and sweeps; the director paces it. Gold is spread and the tomb's sand is rising,
  so going slow loses and fixed pairs are too slow. Halls with many sneezes get patrolled. No safe spot: dust
  everywhere; standing still clears your nose but the sand keeps rising.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the pinch.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 two possible pinchers each, so a sneeze often comes with nobody near. At 6 pinchers are close
  but sneezes stack up.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): where the mummy is; heard by its shuffle, seen when torchlight catches
  it; you hide by standing still, which costs time against the sand.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ana's nose twitches at the coffin; Ben, carrying gold, keeps going;
  ACHOO; the mummy comes. Ana's red nose was visible, so Ben passing by is seen, and Ana's sprint built it.

### MH-6 Wrong Hands
- Rule (one sentence): Each thief's tool works only in someone else's hands, so tools are handed or tossed, and a
  tool that hits the floor clatters for the watchman.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a crew of cursed burglars in a sleeping museum, where the curse makes
  your own tool fail you.
- Aesthetic (one line): marble halls in blue night light; tools spin through the air between thieves; a clatter
  sends a ring through the hall and the watchman's lamp turns.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I toss my crowbar to Ed. He must
  turn and catch it on time (silent) or let it clatter at his feet. You hold one tool at a time, so to catch it he
  must first toss away the lockpick he holds, to someone who can use it, or drop it (clatter). Handing it over by
  hand is silent but slow.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): which cases need which tool
  (pick, crowbar, glass cutter, saw) is dealt per round, so who should hold what changes. The watchman walks to the
  last clatter and sweeps with his lamp cone; the director paces him. Rooms with many clatters get patrolled. No
  safe spot: he walks every room; standing still with a tool you can't use wastes it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the tools (4 kinds, doubled at 6).
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 each thief carries more tools than hands are free, so tosses chain. At 6 two copies of each
  tool and more tosses in the air.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): the watchman's spot; seen by his lamp cone; you hide by not tossing,
  which slows the crew.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Jo tosses the cutter to Ed while he is still holding the pick; he
  fumbles both, a double clatter, and the watchman finds the crew at the case. Jo's toss and Ed's fumble are both
  visible.
- Weak point: catching is usually the obvious right answer, so the receiving end decides less than in MH-1 to MH-5.
