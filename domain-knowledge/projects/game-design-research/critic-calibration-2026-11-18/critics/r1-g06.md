# Critic r1: g06 Siren Ward

Note: the read tool returned the whole of critic-anti-patterns, last section included; nothing from that section is used here.

## The problem underneath
- The fantasy does not need a friend: the proposal's own one-player test says one player can search, hide and escape.
- The only thing a friend adds is drawing the creature away, and a thrown brick or glass does the same job.
- Feeling 1 (laugh into the mic) is caused by the mic rule, but it is a mistake a good crew stops making.
- Feeling 2 (under the table) is caused by the hide rule, and it is a feeling for one player with no friend in it.
- Feeling 3 (saw the planks) is caused by the loud-work rule and is the one feeling the rules force on a good night.

## The scene, second by second
- 0 s: three creep with two planks; nothing presses them, no clock is written.
- Whisper about the saw: how loud a whisper the creature hears is not written, so the choice has no price I can check.
- Sawing starts: the sawer only keeps sawing; the creature comes from the floor below.
- The second player runs upstairs loud, then crouches into a stall: the cover rule makes the end of the run safe.
- The third player unlocks a door alone: a job done side by side, not together.
- Nobody is hurt, no brick is thrown, no kit is used: the written scene is the plan working with no price paid.

## Fewest and most players
- 1 player: waits until the creature sounds far, does the loud work, hides, repeats; waiting costs nothing because no clock is written.
- 5 players: one creature can chase one player, so one draws it and four are safe to search in parallel.
- No rule scales items, exits or the creature with crew size, so more friends make the run easier.

## Always-wins, fifth session, perfect crew
- Always wins: everyone silent, one player parks the creature on another floor, the rest collect and build.
- The proposal names this plan itself under "what experienced crews do".
- Fifth session: a fixed route per map and exit (about six maps times two exits), a quick check of the few random spots, same roles.
- Perfect crew: nobody speaks, nobody is chased except on purpose, no brick or kit is spent; the mic feeling never happens.

## Findings: only this design
- SEVERE 1, missing part: no pressure rises; the proposal says it gets harder only by a chosen setting, so last run's plan is never broken.
- SEVERE 2, missing part: one creature per map, so one player covers the only threat and everyone else stands in a safe state.
- SEVERE 3, base idea: one player can finish alone, and the friend's one job is replaced by a found brick.
- MEDIUM 1: the only thing that makes people speak is punished, so the best move is to stop talking; with known maps there is nothing left to say.
- MEDIUM 2, missing part: the help (drawing the creature) has no written price; its risk ends for free in a stall, and no rule says what the helper loses.
- MEDIUM 3: crew size is ignored, 5 is easier than 2 and 1 still finishes.
- MEDIUM 4: rules not written: hearing ranges, how long loud work takes, run speed against creature speed, and "kills players it catches" against "heal a hurt player".

## Findings the baseline has too
- Fixed roles and a route card by the second run, fear only in mistakes: baseline row "the crew settles into fixed roles with a card of answers".
- "Which exit" is one sum for the whole crew, the other end decides nothing: baseline row "a choice is one sum for the whole crew".
- "Stand guard" during loud work has no verb, the guard only stands: baseline row "one end of a choice only stands".
- Bricks and glass do the decoy's job without the decoy: baseline row "bought tools lower the need for friends" (found here, not bought).

## The four baseline parts
- Priced help on good nights: missing; the help happens on good nights, but no price is written and cover ends the risk.
- Rising pressure: missing; no rule, stated by the proposal.
- Two threats at once: missing; one creature per map.
- Carry-over: present; money unlocks new maps, though costumes and documents change nothing inside a run.

## Could not break
- Loud work is required by every exit, so the crew must call the threat to itself at least a few times a run.
- The draw-away coupling is two-way: the worker picks the moment, the runner picks the floor and when to double back.
- Blame is plain: the one who laughed or sawed is heard, and the creature walks to that spot.
- The lone survivor is never stuck, and dying on purpose gains nothing I can find.
