# Novelty batch 2 (18 ideas)

### G202 Bed Board Tunnel
- Rule (one sentence): The crew crawls an escape tunnel one person wide whose only props are the boards already holding up its roof, so every board moved to a newly cracking spot leaves bare roof over somebody else.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the boards along the tunnel roof (which body is under which board).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): sand starts trickling over Sam. The nearest board is over Dana, two bodies back. Dana must crawl clear before it comes down, but nobody can pass anybody, so the players ahead must shuffle forward too. Then the board goes hand to hand to Sam. The digger at the front needs boards to push forward. The last board at the back comes free only once the last player has crawled past it. The back player decides when to pull, the middle players decide whether to keep or pass, and the front decides when to dig.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): prisoners of war digging out under the fence before dawn roll call, shoring the tunnel with boards from their bunks.
- Look (one line): side view of a sandy tunnel lit by fat lamps, bed boards over crawling bodies, sand trickling down.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, 3 players, 4 boards and a short tunnel. At 6, 6 players, 7 boards and a longer tunnel. Cracks open at a rate tied to crew size, and every seat sits under a board someone else may need.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (a crack trickles sand for 3 seconds before it falls).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the roof falls on Ben two seconds after Ana pulled the board above him to pass it forward. Everyone saw the sand pour where the board had been.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the soil is generated in bands: soft sand cracks often, clay rarely, and roots slow the digger. Cracks open at random spots, so the boards shuffle differently every round.

### G205 Ring Remote

- **Hub:** the control ring (who drives whom), shuffled when someone dies. Extra rule: an obstacle course built from a short list of trap types.
- **Coupling:**
  - I steer Bo from Bo's spoken description of what Bo sees, while Ann steers me from mine. Everyone talks and drives at once.
  - My best move depends on what the player steering me just did to me, because it changes what I can report to the one I'm steering.
- **Fantasy:** a cursed crew whose bodies got swapped around a circle, trying to get out of a trap-filled temple.
- **Look:** each player has a coloured thread from their hands to the body they drive.
- **At 3 / at 6:** at 3 a ring of 3, odd sizes fine; at 6 a ring of 6, with longer delays in what gets passed on.
- **Hidden:** what the player you steer can see. You uncover it by asking them. Nobody hides it on purpose, so F6 is ~.
- **A failed run:** Kai walks Mo into a spike pit, and Mo's thread runs straight to Kai.
- **Tail:** the course is generated from the short list of traps, and the ring reshuffles after each death, so the order is always new.
- **Novelty:** no ring-swap control game found.

### G208 Ore Carts
- Rule (one sentence): Ore carts roll down from the quarry with no brakes and coast farther the more people are riding them, so the crew stops each cart at its own chute by jumping on and off carts as they roll.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the fan of tracks below the quarry and the carts rolling on it.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Raf hops off cart A so it stops short at chute 2, and lands on cart B beside it, so B now rolls farther. But Kim is riding B and needs it to stop short at chute 3. She must hop off at the same moment, or B rams the carts ahead. A rider who hops off near a switch can throw it for the next cart, so every hop both brakes a cart and sets a route. My hop changes the cart a friend is riding.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a night crew at a mountain quarry, sorting ore carts into their crusher chutes before the morning trains.
- Look (one line): floodlit tracks on a slope, carts with painted chute numbers, sparks and dust.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, a new cart every 8 seconds, so two are usually rolling. At 6, a new cart every 4 seconds, with three or four rolling side by side.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (chute numbers are painted on the carts).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a cart slams into the carts at chute 4 and tips its ore because Raf jumped onto it a second before Kim jumped off. Everyone saw him land.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the cart order, chute numbers and loads (full or empty) are random each shift. Each chute's line fills up during the shift, so the stopping spots keep moving.

### G211 Stone on Rollers
- Rule (one sentence): A huge stone rides log rollers down a tidal slope. Each log that pops out
  the back must be carried to the front and slid under the nose in the same instant the rope-holder
  lets the stone roll forward onto it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the stone and its logs.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The catcher grabs the log popping out the back before it rolls into the sea.
  - The slider places it under the nose. The angle they choose steers the stone, which moves
    where the catcher must stand next.
  - The rope-holder decides when to let go and how far to let it roll.
  - If the rope-holder lets go early, the nose digs into the sand or traps the slider's hands. If
    they hold too long, the tide gains.
  - Jobs rotate by one with every log.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): stone-age builders hauling a standing stone across
  the sands to a holy island before the tide closes the crossing.
- Look (one line): a grey stone on wet sand that mirrors the sunset, with the sea creeping in.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: one catches, one slides and one holds the rope, rotating.
  - At 6: a wider stone on two lanes of logs (left and right), with a team of three on each.
    When the lanes get out of step the stone turns, so the two teams must talk.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the nose drops off the front log and buries itself.
  Either the slider standing there was late, or the slack rope shows the rope-holder let go early.
  Or the tide covers the stone.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): a generated flat
  (slope, soft patches, tidal channels to cross, rocks to steer around) and tide speed. Steering
  through it by log angle is new each round.

### G214 Set Glue

- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - I hold a piece and line up its edges (they glow when they match) while the statue sways.
  - A friend paints the glue at the moment I'm lined up, and I must stay lined up for four seconds.
  - Meanwhile a third player bracing their own piece makes the whole statue sway.
  - Then the jobs swap, so everyone both holds and paints. The painter waits on the holder's
    alignment, and the holder fights the sway the others make.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): museum restorers rebuilding a giant statue between
  earthquake aftershocks before opening time.
- Look (one line): white marble pieces joined by gold glue lines, each line tinted with its holder's
  colour.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: one holds, one paints, one steadies the base or holds the next piece.
  - At 6: three joints in progress at once on one wobbling statue.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): an arm sets crooked, so the next pieces won't fit and
  the statue tips. The crooked gold line carries the holder's colour, and the bump that moved them is
  seen as it happens.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):
  - The statues are broken by a fracture routine (random cuts through a few base shapes), so the
    pieces differ every round.
  - A crooked joint carries forward and makes the later fits harder.
- Search: Porcelaine (2 players rebuild porcelain) and Stuck Together (one glues crates, one moves the

- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):

  amount of smoke between teammates was not found.

### G223 Stage Fight
- Rule (one sentence): In a staged brawl every attack is wound up in plain view toward one body zone, and its target must show the matching block before it lands; a blow that lands is a real injury.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the stage, watched by one simple audience that leaves when the fight goes slow or repeats.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): The attacker picks a target, a zone and a wind-up speed. A fast wind-up thrills the audience but is harder to read, and repeating a move bores them. The defender reads the wind-up and blocks, or sells it with a dramatic fall that thrills but leaves them down for 2 seconds. My best attack depends on who is free (not already blocking someone), who is down, and which moves were just used. Two attacks on one defender at once is the main danger, so attackers must watch each other. 1 extra rule: the audience.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): stunt actors on opening night of a swashbuckling play, faking a tavern brawl.
- Look (one line): footlights, a painted tavern set, wooden swords, a dark crowd that cheers or starts to leave.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = a triangle; everyone attacks and defends, and two attacks on one person are easy to spot. 6 = crossing pairs and chains, more ways to double up on one defender, and more moves to keep fresh.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (the wind-up shows the zone).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a blow lands for real; the actor yelps and the prop sword cracks. It's seen whether the wind-up was unreadable (attacker) or the wrong zone was blocked (defender). Three injuries or an empty house ends the show.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the audience punishes repeats, so the crew must keep inventing new sequences; the players are the tail. Set props come from a short list (stairs, tables, a chandelier rope), placed at random, and change which zones can be reached.

### G226 Fire Fresh
- Rule (one sentence): Whoever left the campfire more recently beats whoever has been out longer (players and wolves alike), so a friend far out can only be saved by someone who leaves the fire *after* the wolf chasing them left its den.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the campfire, and the "who left home last" order that every body carries, shown as frost creeping up each body. There are 2 extra rules:
  - wood must be fetched to keep the fire alive;
  - wolves leave their dens on a simple timer and chase the coldest player they are fresher than.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): I time my exit off yours and off the wolf's.
  - If I leave too early, the wolf that left after me beats me too.
  - If I leave too late, you get bitten.

  Gatherers go deep for wood and turn cold. Escorts go out fresh to drive wolves off. The gatherer
  can't fight and the escort brings no wood, so each fails without the other.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a winter camp crew foraging firewood in a night forest full of wolves.
- Look (one line): dark snow, one orange fire, frost climbing each silhouette, wolves' eyes in the trees.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one gatherer and one escort are out while one waits at the fire for the right moment. At 6, two or three sorties overlap, and the exit order becomes a busy, shouted relay.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none. Frost shows how long everyone, wolves too, has been out.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the fire dies, or a gatherer is dragged off. The blame goes to the escort who stepped out before the wolf (the frost order shows it on screen), or to the gatherer who went too deep.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): wolf exits come from den timers mixed with the crew's own exits, so the order of who left last is new every night. The forest is generated (wood and den spots), and the crew's timing habits are the content.

### G229 Hoist Relay

- Look (one line): beams swinging on ropes and a tower of scaffold leaning in the wind.

- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a load slips past the catcher's hands and smashes

- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): generated load
  lengths (a longer beam swings more slowly), gust patterns and floor heights.

### G247 Shove Crew

- Look (one line): lamp light, wooden doors on ropes, and grey smoke rolling along the roof.

- Hidden: none.
