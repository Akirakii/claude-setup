# MF2: people handling people (round 2)

## Method log
- Listed 26 real activities where one person handles another under time pressure. I skipped the brief's own examples (medics, judo, lifts, lifeguards, rugby, circus, cheer, carrying the injured, bouncers). For each I wrote who handles whom and what the handled person decides back.
- Boiled each down to what passes between the two bodies: height (pulley), bounce, speed, momentum, a stop-or-shed choice, a wake-up moment. I cut any idea where, by round 5, the handled end only holds, waits, obeys a call or is moved like a puck.
- For R, each kept idea gets a mid-round reveal that no player controls and that does not follow a player (a random tent waking, rock lanes, stars peeling loose, gaps opening in the ice, a debris line). Each also gets a private stake as pushback, and the shared goal still decides whether the crew wins. No threat hunts whoever holds, did or is most X.
- I checked for existing games from memory only, since web search was not allowed. Ideas too close to Heave Ho, Chained Together, PICO PARK or Gang Beasts were cut or marked as weak.
- What is checked and what is assumed: the gate fit is my own guess, nothing was played, and the "known game" claims come from memory.

## Raw candidates
1. Counterweight Shaft (simul-rappel, pulley): the rope you haul in comes out of your partner's side. Kept.
2. Bedtime Bounce (trampoline double bounce): landing right after a friend steals their bounce. Kept.
3. Slap Relay (speed-skating relay push): a slap hands over all your speed, and you stop dead on cracking ice. Kept.
4. Push-Off (formation skydiving, zero-G): both press, and both fly apart at the same speed in opposite directions. Kept.
5. Possum Toss (stunt throw, playing dead): limp players get thrown and choose when to wake up mid-air. Kept.
6. Strap Grab (yanking a friend back from traffic by the backpack): the grabbed one stops, or shrugs the pack off into your hands. Kept.
7. Whip Release (roller derby whip): two linked players spin, and the first to let go flies. Cut: too close to the hook and to Heave Ho.
8. Three-Person Slingshot (water-balloon launcher): two hold a band, a third backs in and launches. Cut: the holders only aim, and at 3 the last two can't launch each other.
9. Shield Ring (bodyguards, testudo): your shield covers only the player in front of you. Cut: it settles into a conga line, which is one plan card.
10. Lion Costume (lion dance): the front player sees, the back player jumps. Cut: the back player obeys, and pairs break odd crews.
11. Human Tower Lookout (castells): stack up to see or reach higher. Cut: same core as PICO PARK.
12. Rope Team (glacier travel): a fall drags your neighbours. Cut: same core as Chained Together.
13. Buddy Breathing (cave diving): air comes only from a teammate's mouthpiece. Cut: one lookup line wins ("whoever has the most air gives").
14. Blanket Toss Scout (Inupiaq blanket toss): the holders toss a scout above the fog. Cut: the holders only hold and wait for "now".
15. Trapeze Chain (catch and throw onward): each catcher swings the flier on to the next catcher. Cut: it becomes a call-and-obey rhythm.
16. Rueda Pinball (salsa circle on a tilting ballroom): spin and release a dancer to the next hand. Cut: same family as the whip, and the flier is a puck while in the air.
17. Chimney Sweeps (back-to-back chimney climbing): a pair pushes against each other to climb. Cut: it is matching rhythm, with almost no decision.
18. Piggyback Rest: being carried refills your stamina and drains the carrier's twice as fast. Cut: "rotate" beats it.
19. Kayak T-Rescue: a capsized player rights themselves by grabbing a teammate's bow. Cut: the capsized end only waits.
20. Log Birling: your steps roll the log under your logmates. Cut: it is reflex balancing, and three on one log is a pool.
21. Freefall Formation: gripping skydivers drift toward the sum of their leans. Cut: at 6, the summed lean is a pool.
22. Smoke Divers (hand on shoulder): holding someone shares their sense. Cut: leader and follower, so one end obeys.
23. Quicksand Pull: pulling you out sinks me. Cut: the sinking end only waits, and "rotate" beats it.
24. Crowd-Surf Delivery: the surfer is passed over a lava floor. Cut: the surfer is a puck.
25. Sidecar Monkey (sidecar racing passenger): the passenger throws their weight around in turns. Cut: race tracks are content, and it only works in pairs.
26. Life Net: two players hold a net, and a jumper aims for it. Cut: the holders only hold.

## Kept ideas

### MF2-1 Strap Grab
- Rule (one sentence): Grab a teammate's pack strap and they jerk to a dead stop, unless in that same instant they shrug the pack off. Then the pack and its loot stay in your hands, and they run on lighter.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Tomb raiders sprint out of a collapsing temple with packs full of idols. Each raider sees a different set of the old traps.
- Aesthetic (one line): Torchlit stone halls, dust pouring from the ceiling, gold glinting through canvas packs.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): A loaded runner skids about 2 m before stopping, and only a strap grab stops you on the spot. I yank your strap. Within a beat you choose. You can stop and trust me (I may see a dart plate you can't). Or you can shrug and keep your speed, losing your pack to me (maybe I'm lying to rob you). Then you answer back: shout, grab my strap later, or bargain for the pack.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The traps are new every round, and each one shows to different players, so who must stop whom changes at every hall. Every grab is either a save or a theft. "Always trust" gets you robbed, and "never trust" gets you darted. How greedy each player is shifts with the scores.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the pack strap.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, each trap shows to one player, so every run needs both others. At 6, each trap shows to two players, so a lie can be called out ("I see nothing there"), but two liars can team up.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): Which traps you see. A trap is uncovered when someone steps on it, or when you toss one of your idols onto a tile to spring it (this costs you loot). The reason to hide: a fake "trap!" grab steals a pack. Extra rules on the hub: (1) loot still in your pack at the exit is yours; (2) the crew scores only if all but one get out before the collapse behind them catches up.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Mina yanks Tom's strap and yells "plate!". Tom remembers her lie in the last hall, shrugs and runs on, straight onto a dart plate. Blame: Tom for not trusting, Mina for crying wolf, and both acts were seen.
- Lag: the grab and the shrug land in one short window (about 0.3 s), so the server must decide which came first.

### MF2-2 Counterweight Shaft
- Rule (one sentence): Each rope hangs over a pulley with a player on each end, so the rope you haul in comes out of your partner's side and you rise exactly as far as they drop.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Gold miners climb out of a flooding shaft on old pulley ropes and grab gold from the walls on the way up.
- Aesthetic (one line): Lantern light on wet rock, creaking wooden wheels, black water rising below.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I haul up to reach a gold seam. That drops my partner 3 m, maybe into a gas layer or a rock lane. Within seconds they answer. They can haul back, and then we stall in a tug. Or they can let go and swing to another rope, which leaves my end empty and drops me fast.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Rock falls (2 s of rumble first) and gas layers appear at random heights during the round. Gold seams move each round. Every climb sends someone down, so who goes up next is argued each time. Each miner's own gold tempts them to haul for themselves.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the rope over the wheel. Extra rules: (1) a player alone on a rope against a sandbag rises at half speed; (2) the crew wins if all but one reach the top cage before the water. Gold is private score.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, one miner is always on a sandbag rope and keeps hopping to steal a human partner. At 6, three pairs keep reshuffling as players hop between ropes.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): none. One note: each lantern lights only your own column, so you learn your partner's danger only from their voice.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ben hauls for a gold seam while Ana shouts "rocks in my lane, stop!". She drops into the rockfall and is knocked into the water. Blame: Ben.

### MF2-3 Possum Toss
- Rule (one sentence): Any possum can go limp, and a limp possum can be picked up and thrown by a teammate. The thrown one picks the moment it comes alive in the air.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A gang of possums raids a sleeping campsite for marshmallows and hot dogs, playing dead whenever a flashlight comes on.
- Aesthetic (one line): A night campsite with a dying fire, glowing tents, flashlight cones and crickets.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I throw limp you over the lit picnic table. You choose when to wake. Wake early to bite the chip bag as you pass. Wake late to stay "dead" through the beam and land in the shadow under the car. Then you answer: throw me next, or go limp where I can reach you. Possums waddle slowly, and a throw is the only fast way across a lit area.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): A random tent wakes. Its zipper sounds 2 s before its beam sweeps the way the tent faces, often in the middle of a throw. The snack layout changes every round. The thrower must be in the dark to throw, and the flier decides on the spot.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the limp body. Extra rules: (1) a beam spots any possum that is awake and moving, while limp ones are ignored even in flight; (2) the gang must drag a set number of snacks to the den under the RV before dawn, and three spots wake the camp and end the run. What each possum eats is private score.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, every throw leaves only one possum free to watch the tents. At 6, chains of throws cross the camp.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Jo wakes mid-flight to bite the marshmallow bag, right inside Dad's beam, and she is spotted. Blame: Jo for waking in the light, or Sam for throwing her past a tent that was zipping.
- Known games: from memory, Gang Beasts and Human: Fall Flat let you grab and throw friends, but neither has limp-by-choice plus a wake moment, so N is likely weak.

### MF2-4 Bedtime Bounce
- Rule (one sentence): Everyone bounces on one big bed. If you land right after a teammate lands next to you, you shoot up twice as high and their bounce dies.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Siblings after lights-out bounce to grab glow stars off the ceiling before a parent opens the door.
- Aesthetic (one line): A dark bedroom, glow-in-the-dark stars, moonlight through the curtains, flying pillows.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I land next to you on purpose to give you height, and I go flat. You choose to take it by timing your landing, or to let it pass. Or you land right after me without asking and steal the height I wanted. Both ends decide every time.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Stars peel loose at random spots and heights for about 4 s. Who is near a star and who still has bounce changes second to second. Footsteps come at random times. Each kid wants their own jar full, so every gift costs the giver.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the bed. Extra rules: (1) footsteps give 3 s of warning, and anyone not lying down when the door opens is caught, with three catches ending the run; (2) the kids need a total number of stars by morning, and each kid's jar is private score.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, there is room to choose partners. At 6, the bed is crowded and steals happen by accident. The bed size scales with the crew.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Lily gives Max a double bounce just as the footsteps start, and Max is at the ceiling when the door opens. Blame: Lily's late gift or Max's greedy take, and both are seen.
- Lag: the double-bounce window is a same-moment action between two players, so lag risk is high.

### MF2-5 Slap Relay
- Rule (one sentence): Slap a teammate's hand and they take all your speed on top of their own, while you stop dead.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Village ice couriers skate parcels across a lake that is breaking up in the spring thaw.
- Aesthetic (one line): White ice, black water, cracks racing outward, red parcels on scarves.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I slap you up to jumping speed and stop dead on ice that starts cracking under me. You choose to jump the open gap now, or curve back and slap me moving again, which gives me speed and costs you the jump.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Gaps open at random during the round, so who should jump changes every time. Where each giver stopped decides who is in danger next. Each courier's own parcels pull them away from the slower crew.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the hand slap. Extra rules: (1) ice under anyone standing still breaks after 2 s, and they crawl out slowly at the nearest edge; (2) the crew needs the one heavy post bag across, and each courier's own parcels are private score.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, a wide gap needs both others to give, which leaves two people stopped on cracking ice. At 6, long relay chains form.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Kai gives Rina his speed on a thin patch. She jumps the gap and goes for her own parcel instead of turning back, and Kai's ice breaks. Blame: Rina, or Kai's choice of spot.

### MF2-6 Push-Off
- Rule (one sentence): With dead jetpacks, two crew members who both press kick off each other and fly apart at the same speed in opposite directions, and nothing else changes your course.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A salvage crew drifts after their station blew up, hauling cargo to the last escape pod.
- Aesthetic (one line): Silent orbit, Earth glow, slow tumbles, frost inside helmets.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I line up with you and press. If you press too, I shoot toward the crate and you shoot the other way. So you choose: agree now, wait a beat while we turn so you fly toward the pod instead, or refuse. Whoever flies off needs someone placed to catch them.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Everyone's position is the result of earlier kicks, so the best partner for each kick is new each time. A debris line sweeps through on a random path with 5 s of warning. Each salvager's own crates tempt them to kick for themselves.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the kick between two bodies. Extra rules: (1) catching a passing teammate makes you drift together at your average speed; (2) wreck hulls are slick, so you can stop on one but not push off it.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, every kick sends one of only three crew away, and a far drifter needs the other two to kick toward them. At 6, chains of catches and kicks form.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Dee and Ola kick apart to reach a crate. Nobody is placed to catch Ola, and she drifts into the debris line. Blame: Dee for asking, Ola for agreeing.
- Known games: from memory, Heavenly Bodies is a zero-G co-op game where you push off surfaces, so N is likely weak.
