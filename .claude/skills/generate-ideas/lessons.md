# Lessons from earlier idea searches (newest first)

Full logs: `~/Playground/game-design-research/research.md` and `research_notes/Idea search/`.

## Summary (rewritten 2026-11-11 after 26 pool cycles, about 400 ideas; details in the cycle sections below)

**Why ideas fail, most common first**
1. **A plan wins every round:** rotation, "share evenly", "the nearest helps", "hand it to whoever is farthest", a
   decoy, a threshold, a lookup ("the least furry stirs"), a role split (help that costs only time), one queue or slot.
2. **By round 20 the crew avoids the coupling or scripts it.** Avoiding: a solo way out, a huddle, moving as one block.
   Scripting: one planner who sees everything, a memorized order, a growth ladder, a loop of two. Every core that was
   played to round 20 needed at least one fix here.
3. **One player soaks up or collects everything** (a sickness dump, a super-welder).
4. **A safe spot or a solo exit in the setting** (a wall to push off, the bow outside every boom, furniture to hold).
5. **A dice roll hidden in a word** ("risk", "may", "trips").
6. **A goal that rules out one side of the choice, or can't be met** ("all twelve jewels", "every star").
7. **Side by side on one object, a pile everyone adds to, or a rope tie** between two players.
8. **A place to reach** (a level, so content), including leaving the arena.
9. **A taken family or theme** (rope-tied climbers, Pico Park seesaws, grave robbing, friendly fire is a known family).

**Why ideas pass**
1. A pressure grows on its own, and only a specific friend relieves it; the help costs the helper's body or spreads
   the harm onto them.
2. **Space:** every move frees, blocks, pushes, swaps or sweeps a specific friend (the top-ranked cores are spatial).
3. The harm is also the power (furrier arms stir faster, rage hits harder), so the best helper is the one at risk.
4. Something dealt changes which plan is best, and split information stops a planner (Harbor Jam's fog).
5. Caps and rising costs stop dumps and harvest loops (one sickness at a time; a longer daze each pin).
6. The opposition is the players themselves: a cursed friend, or a friend's own work (friendly fire from a tool).
7. The job pulls the danger in, so more progress means more danger. Recheck this after every fix.

**Where new cores come from:** a new relation between two friends ("my X does Y to you": my gaze freezes you, my voice
pushes you, my steps turn your floor) or a new friend-verb; not new harms, folklore, hit-game verbs or new domains,
which give skins. At best about 1 new core per cycle.

**Most productive steps:** the sweeps (every old lesson re-run on every core), scenes at 3 and 6 players, and long
scenes at rounds 1, 5 and 20 with a second look after each fix.

## The goal must fit the fantasy (user, 2026-11-10)
- **Format that landed:** [job] [struggle] [goal] as natural sentences ("this is way much beter").
- **Vague verbs hide holes:** "fight it" in Sin Eaters meant nothing defined; the user couldn't picture it.
- **The second pass finds design holes, not just wording:** making the lines concrete changed 7 of 16 ideas
  (Coven's named witch acted alone; the sleeper decided nothing; Burning Set could roll itself out).
- User on Sin Eaters: "i know the preassure and the obstacles and how to work together with friends.. but the
  goal doesnt fit the theme". 12 of 16 passing ideas used a generic "last until dawn" timer.
- The goal should be what these people want in the story, ideally a job that pulls the pressure in (sin-eaters
  *want* to eat the dead man's sins). Then the crew sets its own pace: more progress means more danger.
- An idea also needs **the job**: what you do when nobody is in trouble. Pressure plus struggle alone only
  describes the emergency.

## Cycle 6 (2026-11-11, 00:44–00:45)
- **New cores are about one per cycle whatever the method:** memory (c4: 1 of 6), researched sources (c5: 0 of 4),
  friend-verbs (c6: 1 of 6). The checks pass roughly 1 new fantasy in 6, and most of the rest die on the same killers.
- **Most friend-verbs come back as "take the harm":** remember for, give years and resize all became core 1 or core 3,
  because the cheapest way to make a verb cost the helper is to move the harm onto them. Speak for and disguise as died
  on "whoever is farthest" or a lookup, because a sound or a face that draws the threat is a decoy.
- **Real animal rituals are a good source:** the hermit crab vacancy chain is a real behavior. When a big shell turns up,
  crabs wait beside it, line up by size and all move up one shell at once
  ([Discover](https://discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/hermit-crabs-line-up-by-size-to-exchange-shells),
  [AskNature](https://asknature.org/strategy/social-networking-aids-housing-search); research by Randi Rotjan,
  Behavioral Ecology 2010, not read directly) [read]. It gave a new choice shape, C: one friend's move frees the next.
  A card game uses the same behavior ([Hermit Hustle](https://eric-dittmore.itch.io/hermit-hustle)), and Shell Beach is

## Cycle 1 (2026-11-11, pool: `research_notes/Idea search/pool.md`)
- **Fixes work when they target the one check that failed, and they usually change the setup, not the wording:**

  Hypnotist's Show swapped mini-games for one word everyone obeys. 4 of 4 failures fixed (Claude's own scoring).
- **A threshold gets solved unless something moves it.** Research: in simple push-your-luck setups a fixed
  cutoff is provably best ([Fighting Fantasy study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.10172),
  [Guts Poker](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.06556)); when the best move depends on changing state, the best
  strategy can't easily be memorised. The design answer [opinion, from the search summary]: make the cost
  depend on changing state, add hidden information, or make the payoff depend on other players' actions.
  For co-op, the last one is the user's formula itself: what a friend just did should move the threshold.
- **Two ways to help, each costing something different** is a new choice shape (Grave Robbers: pull your friend
  free and risk being grabbed, or slam the lid and crush their hand). It avoids "push your luck or pass it".

  Gallery: whoever isn't carrying blocks).
