# M8 tabletop and playground translation (gen1, 2026-11-10)

## Method log
- Steps taken: listed 40 co-op or semi-co-op games from memory (board, card, party, parlour, playground), wrote the one rule that makes players depend on each other for each, then translated each rule into a real-time physical online version that changes the core through bodies, physics or space. No web, no earlier notes read. Raw count: 40 candidates (2 merged into others, so 38 distinct).
- Checks failed most often: **F3** (board games about hidden cards became talk-only rules where one player directs or the others only obey; stacking and carrying games became everyone doing the same job side by side; tilt and balance games became one pooled sum), then **N** (playground body games such as leapfrog, human pyramid or rope-linked crews already exist as physics co-op video games: Pico Park, Heave Ho, Chained Together), then **F5** (pair games such as the three-legged race or wheelbarrow race break at odd crew sizes).
- Good at: playground and parlour games already have physical rules (holding a rope, catching, being caught, being watched), so the translation came naturally and stayed low content. Semi-co-op and traitor parlour games (wink murder, lifeboat) gave hidden things with a real reason to hide.
- Bad at: novelty tops out at ~ for most ideas, because the source game shares the core rule by design. The method gives a rule but no goal or fantasy, so those had to be added by hand, and the added goal is where most extra rules crept in.
- Kept 8 with 8 different core rules: throw-only carrying, a held elastic fence, steering the next player's body, a tower that is both footing and material, a sinking boat with secret cargo, a hunter only the caught can see, a saboteur who can only act unwatched, and a colour you can't see on yourself.

## Raw candidates
1. Hanabi: you see everyone's cards but your own → you can't see your own paint colour, beams knock out matching colours, friends repaint you with balloons. **Kept M8-8.**
2. Heads Up / Who Am I (parlour): same rule as Hanabi → merged into 1.
3. The Mind: play in order by timing alone → crew must cross a one-person rope bridge in an order no one is told. Fails F3 (players wait), voice chat breaks the rule.
4. Magic Maze: each player moves all pawns in one direction only → each player is one thruster on a shared raft. Fails N (split-control vehicles exist), F3 pooled push.
5. Pandemic: cards are given only when in the same city → one copy of each tool, handed over only by touch. Fails N (Overcooked-style handing), F1 needs hand-made tasks.
6. Forbidden Island: tiles sink, players shore them up → tiles sink under weight, crew must keep spreading out. Fails F3 (side by side).
7. Flash Point: fire spreads, carrying a victim slows you → physics firefighting. Fails N.
8. Escape: Curse of the Temple: a cursed player needs a friend in the same room → frozen player thaws when a friend crouches by them. A revive feature, not a core; fails N.
9. Freeze tag / Stuck in the mud: frozen players freed by teammates → frozen players become solid blocks friends climb on. Fails F1 (needs puzzle levels), N ~ Pico Park.
10. Kick the can: one kick frees everyone caught → only caught players can see the hunter. **Kept M8-6.**
11. Chain tag / Octopus: only the two ends of the chain have free hands → a linked line where only the ends can grab. Fails F3 (middle links only obey), N ~ Chained Together.
12. Chinese elastics / parachute games: the held band is the play space → whole crew holds one elastic rope and closes it around a herd. **Kept M8-2.**
13. Long rope (double dutch): turners set the rhythm, jumpers read it → two players swing a giant chain that powers a mill while others work inside its sweep. Fails F2 (fuzzy goal), F3 (turners keep a fixed beat).
14. Three-legged race: two players share a leg → pairs share a leg. Fails F5 (odd sizes).
15. Wheelbarrow race / chicken fight: one is the legs, one is the hands → rider and mount. Fails F5, N.
16. Human pyramid / leapfrog: bodies are platforms → stack to reach ledges. Fails N (Pico Park, Heave Ho).
17. Human knot: hands tangled, untangle without letting go → tethered crew must untangle to fit through gates. Fails G (weak time pressure), low drama.
18. Hot potato / egg toss: keep passing, the holder is at risk → the holder can't move and the floor under them breaks, so the flame moves only by throws. **Kept M8-1.**
19. Keepy-uppy / hacky sack circle: no one touches twice in a row → keep an orb in the air across a map. F3 ~ (same job), N ~ volleyball, same family as 18.
20. Telephone: message passed down a chain → pose your body to show the next player a shape. Fails F3 (one-way chain), N ~ Gartic Phone.
21. Charades: one acts, others guess → ragdoll charades. Fails N, not co-op at its core.
22. Sardines: finders squeeze into the hiding spot → crew must cram into a shrinking safe spot. Fails F3 (side by side), N ~ hole-in-the-wall shows.
23. Mirror game / trust walk: one moves, the other follows → you steer your left neighbour's body but see only around your own. **Kept M8-3.**
24. Blind man's buff / Marco Polo: blind seeker guided by voice → one blind strong player guided by sighted ones. Fails F3 (obeys, one directs).
25. Jenga played for a group height: every pull changes the tower for the next player → climb the tower you are taking apart. **Kept M8-4.**
26. Junk Art / Rhino Hero stacking: each placement shifts stability → build a tower above rising water. Fails F3 (everyone fetches and stacks); merged into 25.
27. Lifeboat / Dead of Winter: shared survival plus a secret stake → overloaded physics boat, throw cargo out, each secretly owns one crate. **Kept M8-5.**
28. Wink murder: the killer acts only when the detective isn't looking → a hidden saboteur can act only while no player's camera faces them. **Kept M8-7.**
29. Grandmother's footsteps: move only while the watcher looks away → carry a swinging load, any swing when the giant turns sends you back. Fails F3 (carriers side by side), N ~ red light green light.
30. Space Alert: plan, then all actions play out together → each round replays earlier rounds as solid ghosts. Fails F1/F2 (heavy build), N ~ Quantum League.
31. Codenames Duet: each player sees half the key → each player sees a different set of deadly tiles. F3 ~ (same job), close to 1.
32. Mysterium: a mute ghost gives picture clues → a mute player arranges objects to show the others. Fails F3 (one directs).
33. Wavelength: one player knows the target, the team turns the dial → one knows the spot, others drag a heavy dial. Fails F3 (one directs).
34. Just One: identical clues cancel → two players touching one object at once shatters it. Fails F2 (no goal of its own).
35. Sky Team: each pilot's dice tilt the plane → players' weight on each wing tilts a glider. Fails F3 (pooled sum).
36. Exquisite corpse / Consequences: each adds a part seeing only the fold → each player lays track for a rolling boulder in their own zone, seeing only the hand-off. Fails F5 (idle while the boulder is far).
37. Captain Sonar: stations on one submarine → crew at ship stations. Fails N (station co-op exists), source is team vs team.
38. Co-op musical chairs (lap version): chairs go, nobody is out, sit on laps → shrinking platform, bodies stack. Fails F3 (side by side), N ~.

## Kept ideas

### M8-1 Last Ember
- Rule (one sentence): Whoever holds the ember can't move and the ground under them starts to crack after 3 seconds, so the crew carries it across the ruins only by throwing it to each other.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the ember (one physics object: thrown in an arc, caught inside a small radius, lost if it falls into a gap).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the holder picks the throw from where the runners just went; runners must spread to different safe spots ahead (two on the same spot wastes the throw) and the old holder must sprint ahead the moment they let go. Thrower aims, catcher picks the spot; a throw fails without someone standing in the right place.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): temple keepers carrying the last flame out of a sky temple that is falling apart.
- Look (one line): broken stone pillars over clouds, one bright ember arcing between small dark figures.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 the holder has two targets and every throw forces a sprint; at 6 there are many targets, people block each other's lanes and the holder must pick fast.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the ember drops into a gap; everyone saw whether Tom threw short or Ana stood on the wrong pillar.

### M8-2 Rubber Fence
- Rule (one sentence): The whole crew holds one long elastic rope and must close it into a loop around a panicking herd and drag them into the pen, and the rope snaps wherever two neighbours stretch it too far.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the rope (a physics rope with each player holding one point, in a fixed order, two players holding the ends).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the two end players are the gate and must meet to close the loop; the middle players shape the wall and block runaways. When Ana dashes to cut off a runaway, both her neighbours must move with her or the rope snaps between them. Gate and wall fail without each other.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): farmhands rounding up a stampede of sheep before the storm hits.
- Look (one line): green hills, one bright red rope zig-zagging around fluffy white blobs.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 the rope is short and makes a small triangle, so the crew brings in a few animals per trip; at 6 the rope can circle the whole herd, but six people must move as one shape.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ken sprints ahead, the rope snaps between him and Mia, and the herd pours out through their gap in front of everyone.

### M8-3 One Seat Over
- Rule (one sentence): Each player steers the body of the player on their left but sees only around their own body, so you walk a friend by their voice while your own legs belong to someone else.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the ring of control (A drives B, B drives C, the last player drives A).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): how I steer Ben depends on what Ben just told me he sees; at the same moment I must tell Cara, who steers me, what is around me. Everyone is the eyes for one friend and the hands for another; no one sees the whole beach, so no one can direct the rest.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a cursed pirate crew whose souls slipped one seat over, loading treasure into the boat before the tide comes in.
- Look (one line): foggy beach at dusk, each screen showing a small circle of light around its own body.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 a tight triangle of talk; at 6 a long ring where one mix-up travels around; odd sizes are fine because it is a ring.
- Hidden: what is around each body (only its owner sees it); uncovered by asking and describing; nobody has a reason to hide it.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ben's body walks off the pier with the chest; everyone knows Ali was driving Ben, and Ben knows what he told Ali.

### M8-4 Pull Tower
- Rule (one sentence): The crew stands on a tower of loose stone blocks in rising water and must build a bridge to the next tower using only blocks pulled out of the tower they stand on.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the tower (a physics stack that is both everyone's footing and the only building material).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): blocks are too heavy for one: one player pushes a block out, another must catch it on the far side. Before the push, the others must move their weight to the side that keeps the tower standing, so where I stand depends on which block a friend just started on. Pushers, catchers, counterweights and the bridge layer do different jobs.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): flood survivors hopping from ruin to ruin.
- Look (one line): grey stone stacks in brown floodwater, small figures leaning out as counterweights.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 one pair works while the third balances and lays the bridge; at 6 two pairs work and two balance, and the extra weight makes the tower touchier.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the tower tips when Ken pulls a corner block while Mia stands on that corner; everyone saw both moves.

### M8-5 Overboard
- Rule (one sentence): The crew must keep an overloaded lifeboat afloat until shore by throwing cargo overboard, while each player secretly owns one crate that pays only them if it reaches shore.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the boat (float and tilt physics).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): crates take two to lift, so every throw needs a partner who can refuse; when a crate goes off the left side the boat rolls and someone must step right at once; one player holds the rudder into the waves while others bail and throw.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): shipwrecked smugglers in a storm, each with a private stash on board.
- Look (one line): small wooden boat, huge dark waves, crates with painted symbols.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 the boat is lighter and every lift uses two of the three; at 6 the people themselves are heavy enough that someone may have to jump out and swim alongside, holding the rope.
- Hidden: which crate is whose; uncovered by watching who refuses to lift what, or by prying a crate open for 2 seconds to read its tag; hidden because others will throw yours first.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the boat flips when Mia and Sam lift a crate from the same side; or it sinks because Ken kept guarding his heavy crate and nobody would throw it.

### M8-6 Cage Eyes
- Rule (one sentence): The troll in the yard is invisible to free players and visible only to players it has caught, who watch from the cage and talk the free players past it, but kicking the can frees everyone and leaves the crew blind again.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the troll (one simple chase rule: walks toward noise, chases whoever it sees).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): free players see the yard and the eggs but not the troll; caged players see only the troll and the players, not the eggs. Runners grab eggs on the cage's calls; caged players throw pebbles to lure the troll away. Every rescue takes away the crew's eyes, so the crew must decide who stays caged, and at the start someone has to get caught on purpose.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): village kids stealing eggs from a troll's yard before dawn.
- Look (one line): moonlit yard, a cage hanging from a pole, big footprints appearing in the mud.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 one caged and two running is the sweet spot, and a second capture forces a blind rescue; at 6 the crew picks its own mix of eyes and runners.
- Hidden: where the troll is; uncovered only by being caught; the troll hides by itself, and the crew hides from it by sneaking.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): dawn comes with 4 of 5 eggs because Ana kicked the can early, freed the only eyes and got caught; or Tom ran left when the cage shouted right.

### M8-7 Look Away
- Rule (one sentence): One hidden saboteur can break things only while no other player's camera is pointed at them, so the crew must keep eyes on each other while building a raft before the flood.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): where each player is looking (the game knows who is watched by whom).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): logs take two to carry and lashing needs both hands, so when I turn to work I stop watching Ben and must say so, and someone else must take over watching him; whom I watch depends on who just turned away. Workers and watchers swap all the time. Any two players can tie a suspect to a post, losing a pair of hands.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): castaways racing a flood, one of whom is secretly paid to make sure nobody leaves.
- Look (one line): jungle riverbank, wet logs, each player trailing a faint cone of sight.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 two honest players can watch both others but then nobody works, so the timer forces gaps; at 6 there are more hands but five people to keep track of.
- Hidden: the saboteur; uncovered by noting who was unwatched when a rope snapped (the snap is loud and instant); hidden because the saboteur wins if the raft fails.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the lashings snap as the flood hits; the snap came when Ana and Ken both turned to carry a log and left Mia unwatched, so Mia is suspected and Ana and Ken are blamed for looking away.

### M8-8 Wet Paint
- Rule (one sentence): Each player is coated in a paint colour that everyone else can see but they can't, coloured beams sweep the floor knocking out anyone of the matching colour, and the only way to change a friend's colour is to hit them with a paint balloon.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the paint (beams of one colour sweep the floor; each player carries one colour).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): I can't see my own colour, so my dodge depends on what a friend just shouted; when Tom sees a red beam coming for red Ana he can shout or repaint her blue, but must first check no blue beam is near. The repainter and the dodger do different things, while everyone pushes parts carts to the machine before the shift ends.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): workers in a cartoon paint factory gone haywire.
- Look (one line): white factory floor, bright sweeping colour bands, splattered workers.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 each player is watched by two friends; at 6 many eyes but loud, crossing calls.
- Hidden: your own colour; uncovered by asking or glancing into a rare mirror puddle; nobody has a reason to hide it.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ana steps into a blue beam right after Tom's balloon turned her blue; everyone saw the balloon.
