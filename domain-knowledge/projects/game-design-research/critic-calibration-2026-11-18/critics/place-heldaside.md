# Comparing critic, round 2: held-aside designs g13, g11, g14, g12

Read: the brief, `ladder.md`, sheets g01–g10, and the four designs (Sources lines skipped, no links, no web).
Each design is judged alone against the ten reference sheets, never against the other three.
A place is a guess from the written rules only. "(not found)" is treated as unknown: no fault, no merit.
Trait 1 is marked by counting the design's own "other end" lines: a line counts when it names a decision a teammate makes at that moment.

## g13 (Cellar Run)

### 1. The seven traits
1. **Partly.** 2 firm lines of 5: the silent friend (the real friend must speak at once), talk or go silent (the others stop too). Split or stay is "each decides which way", the same kind of line g09 has. Sanity food and coins: nothing. Rules behind the firm ones: the copy "cannot answer over the microphone"; devices "check whether the players stay quiet".
2. **Partly.** Rule: "a dead player can be brought back by a crewmate using a doll item". What the dead player does while waiting is (not found), so the kept-involved half has no rule.
3. **Yes.** Voice by distance; the copy is caught by its lack of voice; noise-checking devices; a creature reacts to loud noise.
4. **No.** No rule. The only goal is the exit, there is no haul and no leave-early call.
5. **Partly.** The real friend is the only one who can prove which body is real, by voice (copy rule). Hints are on walls anyone can read, so "remember unused hints" is crew memory, not one player's hidden information.
6. **Partly.** Rule: some solutions are drawn fresh (maze directions) and problems are picked at random. The maps, the path ("progress follows one fixed path") and the door order are hand-made and fixed.
7. **No.** No rule. Maps are played in order and then the game is finished.

### 2. Closest two sheets
**g06 (Siren Ward)** and **g05 (Linked Climb)**.
- g06 is the same skeleton: a hand-made building, items plus small puzzles open the way out, a creature listens to the microphone.
  - g13 has, g06 lacks: a revive (the doll; g06's death is permanent), voice by distance, voice as an identity test (the copy), a sanity meter that drains with no creature near, several creature kinds per map.
  - g13 lacks, g06 has: loud work the crew must do (sawing, prying) that calls the creature, so noise is a price paid on purpose; the decoy job (lead it to another floor); two exits that change the creature; written hiding rules.
- g05 is the same content shape: one hand-made course walked from start to end, nothing rising, nothing new on a second visit.
  - g13 has, g05 lacks: creatures, voice rules, a revive, random solutions.
  - g13 lacks, g05 has: a rule that ties every player to every other for the whole session (the chain), and a failure that costs the whole crew something written (height).

### 3. Place on the ladder
**Tier C, between g05 and g06.**
It is g06's design with the permanent-death hole closed and one new voice rule (the copy), so it sits at the top of C.
It stays under g05 because no rule binds the players to each other outside the copy moment, and the content is spent in about one evening.

### 4. Holes of this design alone
1. **The puzzle is one brain's job.** Best move: the best puzzler solves, the rest follow and carry. No rule gives pieces of a puzzle to different players. Tier A shares it: no (g01 splits the map from the walkers, g02 splits the carry, g03 has pull and boost).
2. **One evening of content.** Best move: finish the 2–4 maps once; on a second visit the crew knows where the hints and locks are, and a fresh maze direction changes one answer, not the round. Tier A shares it: no (g01, g02, g03 all generate). g05 (tier B) shares it.
3. **The copy is beaten by one habit.** Best move: ask every crewmate met to speak, back off on silence (the guide's own line). The ask costs nothing except near a noise device, and no rule puts the two together. Tier A shares it: no.
4. **Staying together is always right.** Best move: never split; the doll then always has a carrier nearby. The only written price is sanity draining over time, and three foods restore it. Tier A shares it: g03 (its crews also "stay together"), so this one is not held against g13.
5. **Two of five choices are private** (sanity food, coins). Tier A shares it: g01 (three of five "nothing"). Not held against g13.

### 5. What I could not break
- The copy plus voice by distance plus the noise devices: a friend can be silent because they are the copy, because they are far, or because they are near a device. Three written rules give one silence three meanings.
- A hint found before its lock forces talk later ("who read the wall?"), and the rule is written.
- Creatures keep chasing after the way out is found, so the end of a map is not a walk.

### 6. Smallest change to move up one tier (opinion)
Draw the door order and the hint-to-lock pairing fresh each run, and place each hint where only the player standing there can read it while the lock is elsewhere.
That turns trait 6 to yes, trait 5 to yes, and gives the puzzle a second end.

## g11 (Spirit Survey)

### 1. The seven traits
1. **Yes.** 3 lines of 5: stay or go back (the vehicle player reads the sanity monitor and calls them out), the cursed object (the others place themselves before it is used, a moment early), hide or circle (everyone else goes silent and switches electronics off). Ward and smoke, and the guess: nothing. Rules: the vehicle holds the monitors; the hunt is drawn to microphones and switched-on electronics.
2. **No.** The only written rule is a dead player as a spectator who cannot speak to the living (and that line is the researcher's own, not confirmed). A silent wait.
3. **Yes.** Voice by distance, a radio for the whole crew, the ghost is drawn to talking during a hunt, tools and the ghost react to words said aloud.
4. **Partly.** Rule: the hunt can start when the crew's average sanity falls to about half, so each extra minute in the dark spends a meter everyone shares, and "guess now and leave, or stay" repeats inside a job. But the written loss is one job's pay and the dead player's own equipment; there is no run and no shared haul to lose.
5. **Yes.** Rule: the vehicle holds the map, a monitor of each player's sanity and a monitor of ghost activity; the players inside cannot see them.
6. **Yes.** Rule: a randomly picked type out of about 30, its room, the cursed object and the side objectives change each job. The 17 buildings are hand-made.
7. **No.** Written: "the game does not get harder by itself, the host picks the difficulty".

### 2. Closest two sheets
**g01 (Night Clearance)** and **g06 (Siren Ward)**.
- g01: one player sits outside at screens the others can't see and talks them through a dark building where something hunts by sound.
  - g11 has, g01 lacks: a goal won by knowing (name the type from evidence and quirks) instead of by carrying; a danger line set by the crew's average, so one player's risk starts everybody's hunt; spoken words as an input to tools.
  - g11 lacks, g01 has: a target that rises until the crew fails; a run that can be lost whole; a dead body that is worth carrying; generated layouts; a hard clock (midnight).
- g06: a creature that hears the microphone in a hand-made building, hiding, difficulty by setting only.
  - g11 has, g06 lacks: the outside seat with hidden information, a shared meter that decides when the danger starts, a different deduction each job.
  - g11 lacks, g06 has: loud work the crew must do with the creature near (in g11 the answer to a hunt is silence only).

### 3. Place on the ladder
**Tier A, between g03 and g04** (the bottom of A).
It has g01's outside seat and voice rules plus a deduction that differs each job, and like g03 it rises only by the tier the crew picks.
It sits under g01 and g02 because nothing is lost across jobs and the dead have no part; the two holes it has there are both shared by a tier A sheet.

### 4. Holes of this design alone
1. **The safety items can stop the danger from ever starting.** Best move: ward near the ghost, pills to hold the average above half, smoke stick in hand; as written, a normal hunt then does not start and the crew collects evidence in peace. No limit on these items is written. Tier A shares it: no.
2. **The deduction is one brain's job.** Best move: the player who knows the thirty types reads the evidence and names it; the others carry tools. One shared notebook, no rule splits the evidence between players. Tier A shares it: no.
3. **The dead wait in silence**, up to the rest of a 10–30 minute job. Best move: none. Tier A shares it: g01 (the dead watch until the day ends and cannot be heard). Not held against g11.
4. **Nothing rises and nothing is lost between jobs.** Best move: stay on a comfortable setting. Tier A shares it: g03 (easy setting and tiers are the crew's pick). Not held against g11.
5. **Hand-made buildings get learned.** Best move: memorise two hiding spots and one furniture loop per building; the guides already say so. Tier A shares it: no (g01, g02, g03 generate layouts).

### 5. What I could not break
- The crew-average sanity line: the danger for all four is started by what any one of them does, and only the vehicle player can see how close it is.
- The hunt cutting off the crew's only coordination tool (talking), written as a rule, with a radio that turns to static.
- Quirks that contradict the evidence, and fewer evidence kinds on harder settings: the last two types can't be ruled out from a safe place.
- The cursed object: a 1-second grace hunt that smoke cannot stop, chosen by the crew.

### 6. Smallest change to move up one tier (opinion)
It is already in the top tier by my reading; the change that would move it up inside the tier is a job for the dead.
For example the dead player's voice comes out of the talk-to-the-ghost tool, so the living must tell the friend from the ghost. That turns trait 2 to yes with one rule.

## g14 (Station Hop)

About half of this sheet is (not found). Those lines are unknown and are not counted either way; the place rests on the rules that are written.

### 1. The seven traits
1. **Partly.** 1 firm line of 5: the vote (everyone votes at the terminal), which is a menu moment. Near or far is "split or stay in earshot". Door: nothing. Quota or profit: nothing. Teammate or haul: (not found).
2. **No.** No rule. A creature can down a player; reviving and what death costs are (not found).
3. **Yes.** Voice by distance, the only written voice rule. Whether creatures react is (not found).
4. **Partly.** Rule: selling enough meets the quota, anything beyond is profit, and missing the quota fails the whole crew. No written rule says what staying longer can lose.
5. **No.** No rule. The terminal picks the destination and nothing more.
6. **Yes.** Rule: stations are generated; the objective type and the voted complexity change.
7. **No.** No rule. Difficulty comes from the vote; whether the quota grows is (not found).

### 2. Closest two sheets
**g01 (Night Clearance)** and **g09 (Dome Salvage)**.
- g01: workers for an employer, a quota inside three days, materials hauled out of generated buildings with turrets and mines and sold.
  - g14 has, g01 lacks: a crew vote on a shown complexity level per objective; a second mission kind (kill hostiles); doors that are creatures; pistols, hacking devices and grapple guns as tools.
  - g14 lacks, g01 has: the terminal seat with the map, doors and turret switch (trait 5); a beast that hunts by voice; the midnight departure; the pay rate that changes over the cycle; the cost of a dead crewmate and the body carry; the rising target (g14: not found).
- g09: a haul-for-a-target loop in generated facilities where the players act side by side, and the sheet has many unknowns.
  - g14 has, g09 lacks: the vote, the door-creature, voice by distance written.
  - g14 lacks, g09 has: a body that is worth carrying back (fed to the creature), a light that runs out, coded doors.

### 3. Place on the ladder
**Tier D, between g08 and g09** (the top of D).
Every rule it writes down is in g01 with a second end attached there; its own additions (the vote, the door) have nothing written at the other end.
It is above g09 because voice by distance and the shared quota are written, and below the tier C sheets because each of those has one rule that makes two players need each other (g06 the decoy, g07 the teammate in frame, g08 sonar and wheel).

### 4. Holes of this design alone
1. **No rule makes one player need another.** Best move: four players loot four rooms, the same as four solo players in earshot. No shared carry, no outside seat, no split information. Tier A shares it: no.
2. **The vote has one right answer.** Best move: always vote the lowest complexity; the written rule gives more doors and traps for a higher level and writes no reward. Tier A shares it: no (g01's richer moons pay more; g03's tiers at least give a sash).
3. **The door is a private coin flip.** Best move: go around every door that can be gone around, or let one player open while the rest stand back; "the door's behaviour is fixed" and nobody else can see or do anything about it. Tier A shares it: no (g01's turrets and mines can be switched off from the terminal, which gives them a second end).
4. **Guns and kill missions turn threats into targets.** Best move: carry pistols and shoot first. Tier A shares it: g02 has bought weapons, but its sheet makes fighting the last resort; g14 makes killing a mission type, so not shared.

### 5. What I could not break
- Voice by distance plus a lethal door: a shout that stops is a warning made by two written rules.
- The vote leaves a name on the blame ("we voted for the harder station"), which a host-picked setting does not.

### 6. Smallest change to move up one tier (opinion)
Seat one player at the train terminal with a scan that shows which doors are creatures and where turrets are, readable only there, passed on by voice over distance.
That gives the door choice a second end and turns trait 5 to yes.

## g12 (Long Way Home)

### 1. The seven traits
1. **Yes.** 3 firm lines of 5: the slope (those outside tell the driver go, turn or stop), the anchor (the remote player decides when to pull, the driver whether to add throttle), getting out (the driver waits or rolls). Scarce items and the flipped van are an argument, not a second end. Rules: one shared van with one driver; the hook is carried on foot and the cable is pulled with a remote kept in the van.
2. **Partly.** A tipped van is a failure the whole crew works on together (right it with the winch). A downed player has an injector or comes back at a tombstone at the next checkpoint; what they do meanwhile is not written. Reloading the checkpoint wipes the failure instead of telling it.
3. **Yes.** Voice by distance, and the spotter outside and the winch call depend on it.
4. **Partly.** Rule: one van, body damage, saves only at checkpoints, so "drive it straight, or stop and winch" is a repeated risk call with a loss everyone shares. It is not a leave-now call and there is no haul.
5. **Partly.** The spotter outside sees the wheels and the tilt, the hook carrier sees the anchor, the remote is in the van. No written rule says the driver can't see these; the split comes from where people stand.
6. **No.** "The map is fixed; some items are placed at random."
7. **No.** No rule. No difficulty settings; whether later parts are harder is (not found).

### 2. Closest two sheets
**g05 (Linked Climb)** and **g03 (Summit Scouts)**.
- g05: one physical object the whole crew shares on a fixed hand-made course, where one player's mistake costs everybody's progress.
  - g12 has, g05 lacks: four different jobs on the one object (driver, hook carrier, remote, spotter); voice by distance written; animals, scarce items, repair.
  - g12 lacks, g05 has: every player tied in every second (in g12 the tie holds only on hard ground); a standard mode with no checkpoints, where a fall costs up to everything; rankings; a lava mode that adds a clock.
- g03: a journey out with stages ending at a save point, afflictions cured by found items, a downed friend who returns at the next stage marker.
  - g12 has, g03 lacks: one vehicle that forces one pace and one shared loss.
  - g12 lacks, g03 has: a mountain replaced every 24 hours; eight harder tiers; the fog behind the crew; every player acting the whole time; the passed-out friend who must be carried.

### 3. Place on the ladder
**Tier B, between g05 and g06** (the bottom of B).
The winch rule puts three or four hands on one physical outcome with voice as the only link, which is g05's cause of shouting with real roles added.
It sits under g05 because the tie is off on easy ground and a checkpoint reload undoes the loss; it is above tier C because that tie is the core loop, not one moment.

### 4. Holes of this design alone
1. **No clock, so the safe way is always right.** Best move: winch every slope in short bursts and never drive it straight; the written cost is only time, and nothing chases the van (thirst, stamina and oil are written without a rate). Tier A shares it: no (g01 midnight, g02 rising danger, g03 fog).
2. **On easy ground three players are passengers.** Best move: stay inside the van, where the snake and the eagle can't reach. Tier A shares it: no.
3. **Reload replaces the rescue.** Best move: when the van flips near a fuel station, reload instead of winching it upright; the story is deleted. Tier A shares it: no as written (g03 saves at campfires, but no reload-on-failure is written there).
4. **One run of content.** Best move: none needed; the second drive meets the same slopes and the same anchors. Tier A shares it: no. g05 (tier B) shares it.
5. **One seat holds most of the play.** Best move: the best driver drives the whole run. Tier A shares it: g01 (one player stays on the ship for the whole landing). Not held against g12.

### 5. What I could not break
- The winch triangle: the hook carrier picks the anchor, the remote player picks the moment, the driver picks the throttle, and a pull started without a word tips the van. All from written rules.
- The single van as the shared loss: any one player's mistake costs all four the same thing at once.
- The eagle rule (safe when clipped to the cable or caught by a teammate): a threat whose answer is another player's hands.

### 6. Smallest change to move up one tier (opinion)
Put something behind the van that makes slow cost something, the way g03's fog does: for example the engine oil burns per minute the engine runs, winch pulls included.
Then "drive it straight or winch" becomes a real call each slope. A drawn-fresh route would be the second step, and a bigger one.
