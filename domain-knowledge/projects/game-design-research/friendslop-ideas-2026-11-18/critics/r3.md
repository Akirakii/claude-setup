Read: the brief (r2), ladder.md, g01–g10, and the four sheets. Nothing else. No web.
Each design is judged alone against the ten reference sheets.
Tags used: (rule) = a written rule says it; (unwritten) = no rule says it; (scene-derived) = a number I worked out from a scene line, which is not evidence, only the best guess the sheet allows.
"Plan" = what a crew that only wants to meet the target does by the fifth night.

## i04 — Moonsick

### 0. Novelty (kept apart from the verdict)
Core act: pick moonlit flowers that only a half-turned friend can reveal, pass your own turning onto whoever holds you, and catch the friend who turned.
Not the core act of any ladder sheet. No sheet has a player who becomes the crew's only threat.
web: not searched this round.
Mark: **new**.

### 1. The seven traits
1. **Yes.** Bloom shows only within three steps of a friend at half fur or more, and only a friend under half fur can pick (rule), so guide and picker move as a pair; a hold moves all growth onto the holder at double speed (rule); the wolf is caught only by two friends from the side or behind (rule). 4 of the 5 listed choices have a written other end.
2. **Yes.** At full fur the player plays the wolf and steers it (rule); two friends hold it to bring it back (rule). No death, no spectator state.
3. **No.** The sheet says voice has no rule.
4. **Partly.** "One more bloom or turn back" repeats each trip (rule: fur grows in light), but there is no leave-now call, the night ends at dawn by itself, and a turn costs time and trampled blooms, not the cauldron. The whole crew loses only if a wolf is loose at dawn (rule).
5. **Partly.** "You see it on your own arms" (rule) does not say the others can't see your fur (unwritten). The beam watcher's knowledge comes from where they stand, not from a rule.
6. **Partly.** Beam and cloud timing and bloom places change (rule). One mansion, one bloom kind, one threat.
7. **Yes.** The mark rises every night and a failed night ends the run (rule).

### 2. Closest two sheets
- **g03** (stamina bar that fills with afflictions, friends pull up and carry). i04 has: the failed player becomes the hazard and keeps playing; a rising target that ends the run. i04 lacks: voice by distance, a generated mountain with biomes and items, a place to reach.
- **g05** (bodies tied together, no creatures, one mistake hits everyone). i04 has: role states that change through the night, a target. i04 lacks: coupling that is always on. In g05 the chain never lets go; here the pair is coupled only while out in the light.

### The plan by the fifth night, and which feelings survive
Best move for a target-only crew: **turn on purpose and get caught.** A wolf held for 5 seconds comes back "with no fur" (rule). Shade only shrinks fur "slowly" (rule, no number). So the catch is the fastest fur removal in the game. Cost: two holders gain fur at double speed for 5 seconds. From the scene (three quarters to nine tenths in two seconds inside a four-times beam) the plain rate is about 2% a second (scene-derived), so a catch costs about 20% fur on each holder and removes 100% from the wolf. The wolf reaches double speed only "after a minute" (rule), so a wolf caught in its first seconds is slow and its player steers side-on to the catchers on purpose.
Plan: one guide and one picker go out in short trips, a third calls beams from the doorway, the picker becomes a guide at half fur, a guide near full fur walks to a bare patch next to two waiting friends, turns, is caught at once and restarts as a clean picker. Nobody goes out in the last minute.
- "It's up to my shoulders, one more, three steps": **survives.** The guide must stand in light for the picker to see anything.
- "Hold me, I'm nearly gone / if I hold you I'm the one who goes": **dies.** A hold doubles the crew's total fur; walking back earlier or turning-and-being-caught both beat it.
- "BEAM! get out of it": **survives only if beams can be seen coming.** The rule says "without warning"; the scene has Cy seeing one come. These two contradict.
- "MOVE, I can't stop / catch me on the left": **survives as a drill.** The call-out stays; the fear of turning goes, because turning is wanted.

### 3. Is it fun with friends?
**maybe.** It has g05's two strong traits (1 and 2) plus a rising target, but the fear the fantasy is built on ("each of us fears turning") flips in the plan: turning is the reward, and the hold, the design's most personal act, is a move no target-only crew makes.
The rates that decide the verdict are not written, and the plan includes standing in shade waiting.
Between **g05 and g06**.

### 4. Holes
1. **Turning is profitable.** Best move: scheduled turn next to two catchers, as above. Tier A: none shares it.
2. **Rates unwritten.** Fur growth per second, shade shrink per second, beam length, how many blooms a mark needs. If shade shrink is slow, pickers who pass half fur "can't pick any more tonight" (scene) and the crew runs out of pickers without hole 1. Tier A: no.
3. **No way back from too many wolves.** With 4 friends, two wolves leave two humans who can catch one; with 3 friends, two wolves leave one human who can catch nobody, and nothing ends the night before dawn (unwritten). The wolves still play, but for nothing. Tier A: g01's dead players also wait out the day.
4. **"Turns back by itself at the edge of the dark" reads two ways:** the wolf reverses direction, or the wolf becomes human. If human, the wolf costs nothing: steer into the shade. Elsewhere the sheet uses "turn it back" for becoming human.
5. **Doorway watcher is a standing job,** and the cauldron has no carrying rule (where it is, whether dropped blooms are lost: unwritten). Tier A: g01 has the same standing job (ship player).

### 5. What I could not break
The guide and picker pair. A bloom needs a half-furred friend within three steps and clean hands to pick, and both stand in the light for it. No plan separates them, and the picker turning into the next guide rotates the roles without a rule saying "rotate".

### 6. Smallest change (opinion)
A caught wolf comes back at half fur, not at none. Turning then never buys back picking hands, the hold becomes the only way to save a friend's hands, and the "hold me" line returns.

## i01 — Gold Fever

### 0. Novelty
Core act: turn cargo to gold by touch while the gold climbs your own body, and push that gold onto a friend's body part to keep working, before the loaded ship sinks.
The frame is g01's (rising quota, crew-chosen leave, a friend's body worth money as in g09). The act inside the frame is on no ladder sheet.
web: not searched this round.
Mark: **near g01: different because** there are no creatures and no building to search; the danger is the haul itself, on the players' bodies and on the ship's waterline.

### 1. The seven traits
1. **Yes.** Grip for 2 seconds moves one part of gold into a part the friend picks, or the friend pulls away (rule); another's crate takes two carriers (rule); chests take two touchers (rule); a stuck friend needs 4 seconds and two working hands (rule); the rope needs everyone still walking (rule). 4 of 5 listed choices.
2. **Yes.** A golden head makes a statue that hops, is carried to the scale and sold, and comes back next night for a debt (rules). A golden leg sticks in water until pulled out (rule).
3. **No.** No voice rule.
4. **Partly.** The crew casts off when it chooses and a sunk ship loses everything on the scale (rules), so the shared loss is written. But nothing written makes gold beyond the quota worth anything (no shop; "cash" carries with no stated use). Under quota, leaving ends the run, so staying is forced; at quota, staying gains nothing. The call is not a choice.
5. **No.** Gold on bodies and the water are in plain sight; the tide is hidden from everyone alike.
6. **Partly.** Junk mix and tide surges change (rules). One ship, three junk kinds.
7. **Yes.** Quota rises nightly, a miss ends the run (rule). Weakened: surges come "at moments nobody can see coming" (rule), so some sinkings are not the crew's doing.

### 2. Closest two sheets
- **g02** (carry valuables together, a head carried to the drop-off). i01 has: the damage goes onto the players, part by part, and friends hand it to each other by choice; a crew-chosen leave. i01 lacks: creatures, voice heard by creatures and by distance, generated buildings, a shop.
- **g01** (rising quota, leave now or one more load). i01 has: every player busy on the same deck, no player parked at a screen, a failed player who is cargo and not a spectator. i01 lacks: a written reason to overshoot (g01's credits buy tools), hidden information (g01's map player), variety (11 moons, 17 creatures).

### The plan by the fifth night, and which feelings survive
Best move: each friend touches and carries **their own** crates. The rule "light in your own hands, two carriers for anyone else's" makes solo hauling the efficient way, so the carrying itself is uncoupled. Gold is dumped into friends' legs while the hold is dry, crates are stacked at the foot of the stairs and carried up in one burst at the end, the crew casts off the moment the scale reads the quota, and a statue is always sold when short.
The plan cannot avoid the gold: the quota is paid in touches, every touch golds a hand, and gold never leaves the crew, it only moves and creeps (rules). That is why the design holds up better than its neighbours.
- "Take my arm, it's at my neck": **survives.** Forced by the quota.
- "One hand left, I can't take any more of you": **survives.** Free parts run out as the quota rises.
- "Cast off NOW / one more crate": **dies as an argument, survives as a scramble.** See trait 4.
- "Sell Ben / Ben is hopping away": **survives as a joke, not as a choice.** "Sell him or end the run" has one right answer; Ben hopping away is play against the crew with nothing at stake for him.

### 3. Is it fun with friends?
**yes, narrowly.** The target itself forces the funny state (bodies failing part by part, friends bargaining over a leg), as g05's chain does, and the failed player stays on stage as sellable cargo; it adds a rising quota g05 lacks.
It is a narrow yes: the leave-now call is hollow as written, three load-bearing numbers are missing, and with no surprise from outside, night five looks like night two.
Between **g05 and g06**.

### 4. Holes
1. **No reason to load past the quota.** Best move: cast off at the exact quota every night. Tier A: no; g01 and g02 both turn surplus into shop money.
2. **Two rules contradict on where the weight comes from.** Rule 1: the junk is in the ship's hold. Rule 7: every golden thing "brought aboard" sinks the ship. It is already aboard. If the weight counts at the scale, the best move is stack below and carry everything in a last burst; g01 has the same stack-then-haul habit (drop scrap by the entrance).
3. **Solo hauling is best.** Best move: never touch a friend's crate. The two-carrier rule only punishes helping. Tier A: no; g02 makes shared carrying the normal case.
4. **Unseen surges.** A large surge can sink a full scale with no warning and no counter. Best move: none, only a margin. Tier A: g02's guide restarts on a bad creature spawn, a milder form.
5. **Unwritten numbers and cases:** creep speed (fast: everyone is a statue by mid-night whatever they do; slow: gold never matters); gold per touch; which of my parts clears on a transfer; whether a golden hand can still carry; how many carry a statue; what cash is for. An early statue hops for up to nine minutes; g01's dead players wait the same way.

### 5. What I could not break
The gold budget. Gold only grows, a part takes one part's worth, a statue takes none, and the quota sets how much must be made. No plan makes the bodies stay clean, and every transfer starts a creep on the friend who took it.

### 6. Smallest change (opinion)
Write what surplus gold does: gold over the quota counts toward tomorrow's quota. Then "one more crate" is worth something every night and the cast-off argument is real.

## i02 — Hold On

### 0. Novelty
Core act: hang as a chain of bodies from each other's ankles and pass loot up hand to hand while each friend's grip, which only they can see, runs out.
g05's core act is "climb while chained to your friends"; g03 has reaching down to pull a friend up. Here the link is a grip each friend can lose or release, and the chain is the only way to the loot.
web: not searched this round.
Mark: **near g05: different because** the link is a private, draining grip that any friend can let go of, the crew hangs still and works instead of travelling, and there is loot, a bank call and a rising quota.

### 1. The seven traits
1. **Yes.** Every piece taken is weight on every grip above (rule); climbing over a friend drains them double (rule); a shake-off drops all below and lightens all above (rule); a kick swings everyone below for 3 seconds (rule); a catch puts a body on one arm (rule). All five listed choices land on a teammate at once.
2. **Yes.** A falling friend can be caught by the wrist (rule), and one who lands is back at the rim in 30 seconds and has raised the water for everyone (rule). The 30 seconds is a short wait, not the rest of the night.
3. **No.** No voice rule.
4. **Yes.** The sack is safe only once swung onto the rim, 3 seconds one-handed, and it is weight on the top grip until then (rules); if the top goes, the sack and the whole chain go. The call repeats every chain.
5. **Yes.** "Only you see your own grip; the others know it only if you say it" (rule).
6. **Partly.** Niches and ledges are generated and the crab picks a different friend (rules). One well, one creature, treasure that differs only in weight and worth.
7. **Yes.** Quota rises nightly, a miss ends the run, and every fall drowns a row of the richest niches for the rest of the night (rules), so the crew makes its own shortage.

### 2. Closest two sheets
- **g05** (chained bodies, one slip takes all). i02 has: information only the holder has, a choice to let go, a bank call, a target that rises, layouts that change. i02 lacks: everyone moving all the time. A middle friend here mostly holds, talks and passes.
- **g03** (stamina that runs out on the wall, a hand reaching down). i02 has: one resource whose loss is shared instantly, a "one more" call. i02 lacks: voice by distance, biomes and items, a far goal to reach, a campfire rest together.

### The plan by the fifth night, and which feelings survive
Best move: the chain cannot be skipped (niches are reached only from it), so the plan is a tight chain, a fixed code word for grip, the sack banked after every rich piece, and friends below dropped the moment a middle grip is about to go. Honest grip calls are best for a target-only crew, so nobody hides their grip to cheat; what stays is the gamble of "I think I have one more piece in me".
Two unwritten moves would change the plan if allowed: a friend **standing on the rim** takes pieces from the top hanger, so there is no sack weight and no 3-second swing; and **throwing** treasure upward (it appears in the scene and in the other-end list, in no rule), so nothing has to be passed or carried at all.
- "I'm slipping, drop the cup": **survives.** Weight and the crab make the drain hard to predict even with honest calls.
- "Don't let go": **survives.**
- "Got you, you're heavy": **survives while grips are fresh;** a catch late in a chain is a bad move and crews stop making it.
- "Who let go?": **survives weakly.** Private grip lets a shaker say "I slipped", but a target-only crew has no reason to lie.

### 3. Is it fun with friends?
**yes.** It has g05's always-on body coupling and shared falls, and adds what g01 has and g05 lacks: a repeated bank-or-one-more call, information one friend holds, and a rising target; the plan cannot route around any of it.
What holds it below tier A: no voice rule, one well, and the middle of the chain has little to do with their hands.
Between **g04 and g05**.

### 4. Holes
1. **Throwing is unwritten.** Best move if allowed: the bottom friend throws each piece to the rim; no weight ever rides the chain. Tier A: no.
2. **A friend standing on the rim is unwritten.** Best move if allowed: one friend stands, refilled for ever, and banks each piece as it arrives. Tier A: no.
3. **Getting out of a chain is unwritten.** Climbing up is in the other-end list, not in the rules, and has no cost stated. If it costs like climbing down (double drain on tired grips), the cheap exit after banking is everyone lets go: the torrent is a 30-second lift to the rim, and falling stops being feared. The water row per body is the only brake, and the number of rows is unwritten. Tier A: no.
4. **Ledge parties are stranded.** Ledges are four bodies apart and treasure counts only at the rim; a pair resting on a ledge has no written way to send loot or themselves up except hole 1 or the torrent with empty hands. Tier A: no.
5. **Thin hands for the middle and the top:** hold, say a number, pass. Chain length for 6 friends against one top grip is unwritten. Tier A: g01's ship player has the same low-hands job.

### 5. What I could not break
The shake-off with a private grip. A middle friend who is failing must choose between saying so and waiting, or dropping the friends below to save the sack above, and no plan removes that moment, because only that friend knows how close it is.

### 6. Smallest change (opinion)
Write one rule: treasure moves only hand to hand between hanging friends, and can't be thrown or handed to a standing friend. It closes holes 1 and 2 and costs nothing.

## i05 — Moonsick, the wolf we need

### 0. Novelty
Core act: one friend turns into a wolf on purpose to break doors for the crew's loot, loses steering toward their friends the longer they stay turned, and the friends must put the loot down to bring them back.
The frame is g01's (haul to a cart, rising quota, nobody left behind at leaving). The act of a teammate being the needed monster is on no ladder sheet. g06 is the nearest for "one hunter, one friend draws it off".
web: not searched this round.
Mark: **near g01: different because** the only creature is a teammate the crew needs, and the "one more" call is made by that teammate about their own control.

### 1. The seven traits
1. **Yes.** The wolf's pull goes to the nearest friend (rule); large silver in both hands stops a bite, empty hands are needed to hold (rules), so each wolf move makes a carrier pick shield or hands. 4 of 5 listed choices.
2. **Yes.** Staying turned too long keeps the player playing; a bitten friend is down 5 seconds; a second bite makes a second wolf (rules). No spectator state.
3. **No.** No voice rule.
4. **Yes.** Each door and each 20 seconds adds a notch (rule), and a wolf loose at dawn loses the whole night's silver (rule). The call repeats per door and the loss is the crew's. It sits in one player's hands, though.
5. **Partly.** "I see my own notches" (rule), but the pull at 2, 4 and 6 shows the others roughly where the wolf is.
6. **Partly.** Silver rooms and door counts change (rule). One mansion, one loot kind, the same single threat.
7. **Yes.** Quota rises nightly, a miss ends the run (rule).

### 2. Closest two sheets
- **g06** (one hunter in a building, one friend draws it while the rest gather). i05 has: the hunter is a friend who wants to be stopped, a rising target, a failed player who keeps playing. i05 lacks: a voice rule (g06's creature hears the microphone), a threat that is there the whole run, several maps.
- **g01** (haul for a rising quota). i05 has: no dead spectators, a threat with a human mind. i05 lacks: danger for the carriers outside the wolf's short turns, tools and moons, voice by distance, a player with a map.

### The plan by the fifth night, and which feelings survive
Best move: take every beam (more turns means fewer notches per turn), break a set number of doors, then run home to a fixed spot where one friend stands nearest with a large piece as a bumper and two stand beside with empty hands. A fresh wolf can "stop as I like" (rule) and nothing says that ends at 2 or 4 notches (unwritten), so a wolf under 6 walks up and stands still to be held. At 6 the wolf "can no longer turn away" from the nearest friend (rule), which makes it the easiest wolf to trap: it always hits the bumper, is dazed 2 seconds, and is held.
Between turns the crew carries silver through a house with no danger in it. Before the first beam, every door is shut and nobody can do anything.
- "One more door, I've still got it": **survives.** The quota forces more doors per turn, and the notches are the wolf's to judge.
- "He's drifting toward you, get behind the table": **survives partly,** only while carriers are spread out during a turn; in the plan they stand at the catch spot or carry a large piece.
- "Bring me back NOW": **survives when the wolf over-pushes,** but the catch is a drill.
- "Put the silver down and hold him / if I put it down he bites me": **dies.** Roles are set before the wolf comes home: one bumper, two with empty hands.

### 3. Is it fun with friends?
**maybe.** The wolf's seat is good (a real "one more" with a private count and a crew-wide loss), but only one friend sits in it at a time, at most three times a night; everyone else waits for clouds or carries loot through an empty house, and the shield-or-hold dilemma the sheet is named for is planned away.
It sits with tier C: a sound loop with long flat stretches, like g06 without its always-present hunter.
Between **g06 and g07**.

### 4. Holes
1. **Random beams gate all work.** All silver is behind oak doors, only a wolf opens them, and the moon comes three times "whenever the clouds part" (rules). Best move: one friend stands in the hall; the rest wait, from the first second of every night and again after each load. Tier A: no; g01's crew can start hauling at once.
2. **Carriers have no danger outside a turn.** Best move: carry large pieces only while a wolf is out. Tier A: no; g01, g02 and g03 threaten carriers the whole time.
3. **The catch is a drill.** Whether a wolf above 0 notches can stand still is unwritten; if it can, any wolf under 6 is caught for free, and at 6 the bumper routine works. Tier A: no.
4. **No way back, and nothing ends the night.** With 3 friends a wolf at 6 needs three holders and only two exist; the night is lost and runs on to dawn. All-wolves has no ending rule either. Tier A: g01's dead also wait out the day.
5. **Two lines contradict:** "what changes" lists "who starts already bitten", while "what carries" says cash and nothing else. Also "let the beam pass and hope for the third" is not a choice: taking every beam is always better.

### 5. What I could not break
The wolf's own count. Doors add notches, time adds notches, nothing lowers them but friends, and the quota sets how many doors a turn must break; the wolf's player always decides alone how far to go on the crew's behalf.

### 6. Smallest change (opinion)
Let the wolf start whenever the crew wants (drop the three random beams) and leave some silver in open rooms that the hunger-pulled wolf must be walked past. The waiting goes, and carriers are in the wolf's path while they work.
