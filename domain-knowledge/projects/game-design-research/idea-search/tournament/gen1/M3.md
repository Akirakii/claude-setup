# M3 "split one thing" — gen1 results

## Method log
- Steps: listed single things a solo player controls (bodies, a puppet, a crane arm, kite, camera, flashlight, cart, horse, submarine, voice, eyes, map, wallet, battery, pause button, sailing ship, drone, lock, fishing rod, fire hose, train, juggler, pinball, marble run). For each, tried 1–3 unusual splits of its controls, senses or parts across 3–6 players, then checked each split against F3 (does my best move change with what a friend just did?), F5 (does the part count scale 3 to 6?) and N (is this split already a known game?). Kept the 8 strongest with different core rules.
- Raw count: 45 candidates from about 30 things.
- Most common failures: F3 most often. Splitting a thing into alike parts (wheels, rotors, ballast tanks, oars, coin types, shield panels) gives a pooled sum or the same job side by side, and "one sees, one acts" splits make one end only obey. N came next, because the obvious splits are taken: one limb per player (Octodad co-op), stations (Spaceteam style), blink monsters (SCP), catch-and-swing chains (Heave Ho). F5 was third: things with a fixed number of parts (fishing rod, wrecking ball, lock pick, 4-edge camera frame) give fixed seats that don't scale or leave seats idle.
- Good at: high player coupling and visible blame come almost for free. The shared thing shows who moved it (a swinging joint, a cut beam, a cable that jumps to a new user), and splits along a chain (joint after joint, mirror after mirror, sail behind sail) make each move depend on the one just before it.
- Bad at: it gives only the "how we control it" half. The goal and the threat have to be bolted on, so many kept ideas share a "rising threat plus timer" frame (flood, fire, sunset). It also keeps landing on chain shapes, so several kept ideas share that family even though their core rules differ.

## Kept ideas

### M3-1 Long Arm
- Rule (one sentence): Each player turns one joint of a single long rescue arm, and every turn swings everything past that joint.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The arm, a chain of 3–6 joints on a boat that ends in a claw.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): When the base player swings, the tip flies sideways, and the joints after it must turn back to keep the claw on target. The tip player can only close the claw once the joints before it hold still. Middle players bend the arm around chimneys and walls. Joints sag under a load unless held, so nobody can let go. The base gives big reach, the middle routes around things, and the tip aims and grabs.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A flood-rescue crew working one giant salvage arm from a boat, plucking people and crates off rooftops.
- Look (one line): A chunky yellow arm, each segment painted in its owner's color, over gray floodwater.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 it is a short, stiff arm (shoulder, elbow, claw), quick to aim but unable to reach behind things. At 6 it is a long, snaking arm that reaches around chimneys, but every swing at the base whips the tip.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): The water covers the last roof with people still on it. The replay shows the base player swinging while the claw held a survivor and flinging them into the water, with the segment that moved glowing in its owner's color.

### M3-2 Crossed Strings
- Rule (one sentence): Each player pulls one string of a giant puppet, every string is tied to two body parts, and every body part hangs from two players' strings.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The puppet.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): My string lifts the left arm and the head. To raise only the arm, the player who shares the head must let theirs down at the same moment, which drops their other part, which their other partner must then catch. Each string ties to a different pair of parts, so every player has a different job. The wiring is shuffled each show.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Puppeteers on a wobbly bridge above a stage, working one huge marionette in front of a rowdy crowd.
- Look (one line): A wooden puppet, colored strings rising to the rafters, a painted backdrop and tomatoes flying.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there are 3 strings and 3 parts (head and two arms), and the puppet floats and gestures. At 6 there are 6 strings and 6 parts (head, arms, legs, hips), so it can walk, but every step pulls on two other players.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (the wiring is shown on screen and shuffled each show)
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): The crowd calls "bow!" from a short list of acts, with a timer. The puppet's leg kicks up and knocks over the prop. The leg's two strings light up, the one that was being pulled flashes, and that player gets the tomato.

### M3-3 Sunbeam Relay
- Rule (one sentence): One sunbeam bounces from mirror to mirror held by the players, and it burns only where it finally lands.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The beam.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): When the player catching the sun steps or the sun moves, the beam lands on a new spot of the next mirror, so that player must re-aim, which moves it again for the player after. The first player stands in open sun to catch it, the middle players bend it around rocks and trees, and the last one aims it at nests. Creatures crawl toward whoever holds a mirror. Stepping away to dodge breaks the chain unless the others re-route the beam around you.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Desert villagers burning out shadow-creature nests with hand mirrors before dusk.
- Look (one line): A bright gold beam zigzagging across a dim canyon between small figures.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 the beam bounces twice, so only nests near the sun can be reached, but re-aiming is quick. At 6 it bounces five times and reaches deep cave nests, but one wobble at the start sweeps the far end.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): The sun sets with nests left. Everyone saw the beam stop at one player's mirror when they turned to run from a creature.

### M3-4 Borrowed Eyes
- Rule (one sentence): Each player sees only through the eyes of the next player in a ring, never through their own.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The ring of views.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): To walk, I need the player whose eyes I use to look at my path, while they walk blind and need their own partner in the ring to look at them. My best place to look changes the moment the player I see for takes a step. My best place to stand is wherever the player who sees for me can keep me in view. Each player looks for one person and is guided by another.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Cursed explorers whose eyes got swapped, escaping a collapsing temple.
- Look (one line): Each screen shows a shaky view from someone else's head, with a small arrow marking whose head it is.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 the loop is small and everyone stays close, so it is easy to keep each other in view. At 6 the loop spreads across the floor, and the view you need may be coming from across the room.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": Your own surroundings are hidden from you. You uncover them by asking your eyes-holder to look. Nobody hides them on purpose.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): The floor crumbles behind the crew and a player falls into a pit. The replay shows that the player holding their view had turned to look at a falling rock instead.

### M3-5 Stop Buttons
- Rule (one sentence): Each player can freeze one kind of trap for a few seconds, and the whole crew must cross the trap course together.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The trap course.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Freezing the moving platforms holds one still for the jumper but strands the player riding another one. The saw player's best moment depends on where the jumper just landed. The fan player's wind lifts one friend and blows another off a ledge. Every freeze helps someone and hurts someone, and each player owns a different kind of trap, so timing depends on where everyone is right now.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Test crew of a time-stop remote that broke into pieces, one piece per crew member.
- Look (one line): A bright, toy-like course where frozen things turn gray with an outline in their owner's color.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there are three trap kinds (saws, platforms, flame jets), and every freeze matters. At 6 crushers, fans and a rising floor are added, giving more stops and more chances to freeze the wrong thing on a friend.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): The course collapses behind the crew and a player is crushed. The crusher had just unfrozen, outlined in its owner's color, because its owner spent the freeze on the far side.

### M3-6 One Socket
- Rule (one sentence): The crew shares one battery that runs only one gadget at a time, and switching yours on cuts off whoever was using it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The battery.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Each player carries a different gadget (lamp, winch, pump, drill, fan, radio). The pump player's best moment to switch on is right after the winch has lifted someone up the shaft, never during the lift. The lamp player must take the power back before someone jumps in the dark. Every grab for power stops someone else mid-task, so you watch what the current user is doing before you switch on.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A cave-rescue team with one battery pack while the water rises.
- Look (one line): A dark, wet cave lit only by whichever gadget is running, with a glowing cable running from the battery to its current user.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is a lamp, a winch and a pump, with few handovers, and every gadget is always needed. At 6 all six gadgets fight over one battery, and the power changes hands every few seconds.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): The climber on the winch drops back into the water. The screen shows the cable jumping to the pump player, who switched on at that moment.

### M3-7 Rudderless
- Rule (one sentence): The ship has no rudder, so it turns only by the balance of its sails, and each sail steals wind from the sail behind it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The ship.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Front sails swing the bow away from the wind and back sails swing it back. My sail's push changes the moment the player in front of me lets theirs out (I get less wind) or pulls it in (I get more). To turn, the front and back sails must act one after the other, and the middle sails must adjust as the wind behind each change shifts. The front, middle and back sails do different jobs.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A pirate crew whose rudder was shot off, threading a reef before the storm hits.
- Look (one line): A tall ship seen from above, each sail tinted in its owner's color, with an arrow on each sail showing its push.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there are three sails (front, middle, back), and turns swing hard. At 6 there are six sails, giving finer control but a long chain of wind blocking.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): The ship hits a rock. The replay shows the front sail pulled in hard at the wrong moment, with its push arrow swinging the bow onto the reef.

### M3-8 Human Tower
- Rule (one sentence): The crew stands on each other's shoulders as one tower, where only the bottom row can walk and only the top player can grab.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The tower's balance.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): When the bottom steps, everyone above must lean back to keep the weight over the feet. When the top reaches out for a pet, the bottom must step under them. The middle players pass rescued pets down and lean to cancel the others' leans. Each level does a different job, and every move shifts the balance for everyone else.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A circus acrobat troupe rescuing pets from the windows of a burning street.
- Look (one line): Stacked, brightly dressed acrobats against a tall row of house fronts with orange windows.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 the crew is a one-one-one stack, tall and wobbly. At 6 it is a three-two-one pyramid, wider and steadier, but the bottom three must step together.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): The tower tips into the street. The replay shows the middle player leaning out to catch a falling cat while the bottom was mid-step, and that level's lean glows red.
