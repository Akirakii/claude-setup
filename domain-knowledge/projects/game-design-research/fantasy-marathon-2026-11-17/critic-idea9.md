**Verdict on the base: fail.** It is the infection mode of hide-and-seek, which already ships as one of three modes inside the hit the idea cites, and its written choice only exists while there is exactly one hunter.

## The base, against the saved research

- So the idea is a mode of a $5.99 game, and the mode is advised above the target's 3 to 5.
- The hit's hiding is painting your body and posing in plain sight; the seeker looks at you and must spot you (saved research, searched).
- Idea 9 hides in closed furniture: opened is found, so the seeker has no craft and the hider has no gag.
- The verbs report says the big hits "also had a funny moment you could read in a second" (the body paint); the idea names none.
- The report's own limits are not quoted by the idea: "players are the content" is "unproven for a closed friend group"; hiders in MECCHA "act side by side"; "keep the seeker's budget below check every spot, or hiders fall back to one safe spot"; a co-op game's other mind "has to come from teammates".
- Scoreboard: no 1-vs-many game there is a hit for friends only; the friends-only hits are all co-op against the game, and "friends on one side" is the formula's first line (saved research).
- Among Us added a hide mode after its wave and kept falling (saved research); that mode gives the seeker a ping to survivors in the last moments (searched), a rule against the end-of-round standoff that idea 9 lacks.
- HuntNPrey, a small hide-and-seek for up to 8 with a wolf flip, has 5 user reviews two years on (searched).
- No werewolf-mansion infection game came up in two searches, so the skin is free and the rules are a commodity (searched).
- Is the quote "search games need mixed choices" fair: pass for the first hiding spot, fail for the written stay-or-run choice (severe 1).

## Severe
**S1. The written choice exists only while there is exactly one hunter.**
Problem: one hunter can't search and listen at once; two can.
With two, one makes door noise and one stands silent, so a hider who runs on door noise is caught, and "never move" is right for the rest of the night.
Example: Ana's last 86 s at 3 players. The one-hunter phase is about 114 s at 3, 86 s at 4, 55 s at 6 (script).
Nothing outside the wardrobe pulls a human out, so even against one hunter the only reason to move is the gamble itself.
Fix direction: humans need something outside their hiding place, and a second hunter must not be able to cover both jobs.

**S2. The script never tests the choice, so the "ratio" claims do not reach it.**
The counts are right: I got the same 0, 29, 25 and 15 (script).
But hiders never react to a hunter, moving carries no risk, and "moves every 45 s" beat "never moves" in 27 of 27 pairs (script).
So in the toy the choice has one answer: move.
When I add the written risk (a chance to be seen crossing), moving wins below about 10% and staying wins above it: 25% stay against 30%, 18%, 10% at risks of 10%, 20%, 30% (script, 4 friends).
That is one ratio on a number the idea never names; "depends on the numbers" is not a pass.
Two more gifts to the humans in the toy: hunters forget what they searched at the instant hiders move, and hunters never share what they searched.
With shared memory the 25–75% band falls from 15 settings to 9 of 54, and at 6 friends to 2 (script).
Fix direction: model the hall, the sound and a hunter who answers, or claim only "the clock can be tuned".

**S3. The humans never cooperate, and talking or hiding together is harmful.**
Any friend may be the one the moon picks, and a turned friend keeps what they saw and heard (the scene: "Ben knows where he saw Cy go").
If a turned friend knows where the others hid, human wins fall from 36% to 4% at 4 friends and from 26% to 1% at 6 (script).
So the best human plan from second 0 is: hide alone, be seen by nobody, say nothing.
The idea's ❌ says "not cooperative after the first turn"; it is not cooperative before it either, and no rule lets one human help another.
This is Moonsick version 4's move again: fix "one answer" by dropping co-op (postmortem: "version 4 has a reason to lie and no cooperation").
The user decided "cooperative friendslop where failure is fun, with the twist of running from a converted ally"; the idea keeps the twist and drops the first half.
Fix direction: none found inside "everyone hides alone"; a joint human act would be a new game.

**S4. Rule 4 is a way the user already threw out.**
Thrown out: "The werewolf wins small if nobody is human at morning", because "spite isn't natural to being powerful".
Rule 4: "If nobody is human at dawn, the turned win".
The user's need: the behaviour comes "from being a werewolf, not from a score rule"; here the only reason to hunt is the score rule.
The idea says "caught fairly... so no grudge"; the user's words are "it earns enemies for the next game", and the idea gives no evidence either way.
Fix direction: take it to the user as "this is the rejected rule"; do not reword it.

**S5. Six rules the scenes lean on are not written, and each reading has a one-line answer.**
- What a touch needs. If opening the spot is the catch, a found hider decides nothing. If the hider can bolt, speeds are needed.
- Speeds. Equal speed: the last human runs laps round a table until dawn. Faster wolf (Moonsick's is 1.44x): running is never right.
- Whether a hider sees out. If yes, they know when to move. If no, they choose blind.
- What each side hears through walls and floors. Both ends of the core choice act on it. If crouch-walking is silent, listening is dead and moving is free.
- Whether a turned friend looks turned, and whether they may lie on voice ("still human, where are you?").
- Who wins when all are turned. As written, everyone.
Fix direction: write these six, then replay.

**S6. Being caught is never bad for the player.**
All turned means all win, so the last human gives up only a personal number.
A friend who is bored in a wardrobe walks into the first hunter at 0:55, gets the active job, and joins the side that wins more often (the turned nearly always win in 29 of 54 settings, script).
The idea lists this as Zombie Panic's failure ("the infected often starts helping the other side") and does not run it against itself.
"Each friend's own number" also pays a human for giving another human away, since they outlast them.
Fix direction: a caught friend must lose something the pack's win does not give back.

**S7. The cited games cause the feelings by rules this idea does not have.**
- Hiding while a friend searches: MECCHA and Prop Hunt hide in plain sight by disguise. Fail.
- Fear of the friend next to me: in Zombie Panic it comes from a hidden infection among armed teammates. Here it lasts only until the random turn, and its answer is "stand near nobody". Fail.
- Being caught isn't the end: the rule is borrowed as is. Pass.
Fix direction: mark the first two "feeling exists, rule not borrowed".

## Medium
- The random pick hands one friend a personal number of 20 to 120 s by luck (blame from luck), and its timing swings the night: humans win 41% when the turn comes before 0:53 and 60% after 1:27 (script, 4 friends).
- The hunters' work is parallel: "I take upstairs", then doors. That is side-by-side work, the user's complaint about Deathly Reality Show.
- "Carried from a dropped family ✅ none" is wrong: this is Moonsick's stage 2 ("The werewolf has 3 minutes to eat everyone... If time runs out, the humans win") with eating swapped for turning.
- The Discord ❌ is understated: split calls cut the friend group in two for the night, and no call split removes what a turned friend remembers.
- Lag: a touch on a running human is the classic "it hit on my screen" dispute; ✅ holds only if opening the spot is the catch.
- A hider sits still for two to four minutes of a five-minute night, plus the wait before the turn (paper).
- After 20 nights the spot guess stays a guess (pass), and the hunters' route becomes a fixed sweep: 288 s for one hunter, 144 s for two, 96 s for three (script).
- "All hide in one room" loses three humans in 36 s, so it is not a winning plan (pass); it also means humans avoid each other.
- "The first hunter listens all night" loses to hiders who never move (pass), which shows listening only pays if something makes hiders move.
