# Proposal for the critic: Cellar Run

**Fantasy:** A group of friends is shut inside a dark, monster-filled place such as an abandoned shopping centre, and wants to solve its puzzles and get out of the exit without dying.
One-player test: Yes, the game can be played alone, though the game itself recommends four.

**Players:** 1–4, one crew. **A session:** one map played from entrance to exit; exact length of one map (not found); a reviewer says all the maps together fill about one evening.

**Feelings the game is known for**
- "We were being chased by a giant mascot while still trying to work out what the puzzle even wanted."
- "I met you in the corridor and you would not answer me, so I ran."
- "We found the hint an hour before we found the lock it belonged to."

**The rules, one sentence each**
- The crew plays the maps in order, and escaping one map opens the next.
- A map is finished by reaching its exit alive; each locked door opens the way to further doors, so progress follows one fixed path.
- Doors open by collecting the needed items and solving puzzles, with hints placed around the map.
- A hint can show up before the crew can reach the puzzle it belongs to, so the crew has to remember unused hints.
- Puzzle solutions are not always the same between playthroughs, for example the compass directions in a maze are drawn fresh each time.
- Each map has its own creatures, one or more main ones and some minor ones, and each has a weakness that the game hints at.
- One creature is a tall figure that costs sanity if a player keeps looking at it, so the player must turn away.
- One creature copies the look of a player and acts like a friend, but cannot answer over the microphone.
- One creature is a shadow that is beaten by playing music.
- Some ordinary-looking objects are traps that stand up and run when triggered, which costs sanity.
- Some devices on the map check whether the players stay quiet, and at least one creature reacts to loud noise such as a horn.
- Every player has a sanity meter that must be kept up even when no creature is near; a puzzle cube, a chocolate bar and a noodle cup restore it.
- Creatures, traps and other map hazards can kill a player; what happens at zero sanity (not found).
- A dead player can be brought back by a crewmate using a doll item; what a dead player can do while waiting, and what happens when the whole crew is dead (not found).
- Coins picked up on the map buy items in a shop, including items that cannot be found on the map.
- Creatures keep chasing after the crew has found the way out.

**How much there is:** 3 maps per the store text, 4 per one player guide, 2 per one review (the count has changed over time; all hand-made, with some solutions drawn at random); at least 5 named creature kinds across the maps (full count not found); mini-games (count not found); found items include bottles, a horn, a torch, a fuel can, three sanity foods and the revive doll (full count not found); shop items (count not found); hidden collectibles on each map; upgrades (not found).

**Voice:** Voice by distance, with filters that change the voice to fit the surroundings; the copycat creature is caught by its lack of voice, and noise-checking devices punish sound.

**The choices**
- Answer the friend standing silently in the corridor, or back away in case it is the copy?
- Split up to search for items and hints faster, or stay together so a downed player can be revived?
- Use the sanity food now, or hold it for a crewmate who is lower?
- Spend coins in the shop now, or save them (what for: not found)?
- Keep talking to coordinate the puzzle, or go silent near the noise-checking device?

**What the other end decides in the same second**
- Silent friend: the real friend, elsewhere, decides whether to speak up at once to prove who they are.
- Split or stay: each other player decides which way to go.
- Sanity food: nothing.
- Coins: nothing.
- Talk or go silent: the others decide whether to stop talking too.

**What experienced crews do:** A player guide says to ask any crewmate met unexpectedly to speak into the microphone, and to keep away if there is no answer.
The same guide says to stay silent near the devices that check for noise.
A full-completion guide says to keep track of hints not yet used.
Nothing was found on how crews split roles or routes.
