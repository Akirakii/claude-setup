Friendslop field guide

What makes cheap co-op party games ("friendslop") work or fail, what has already been made, and where a furry / animal friendslop game has room.

2026-11-0113 mid-tier hits · 15 failures · 25 animal games · 12 subgenres4 research passes

Sales are mostly third-party estimates (Raijin, SteamSpy, VGChartz) that often undercount: Raijin shows PlateUp! at 422k against 3M official. Named cheap-friendslop flops are rare in the press, so the failure data leans toward pricier games. The mega-hits are covered in the earlier report.

Summary

Seven findings

  1. 1

    Co-op as a category beats single player on average. The typical co-op game sells about 40k copies against about 5k for single player, and even the bottom quarter of co-op sells better (VG Insights 2024 estimate).

  2. 2

    The mid-tier is reachable solo. Mage Arena, Bopl Battle and PICO PARK 2 were each made by one person and sold roughly 0.8–1M copies.

  3. 3

    Measured failures cluster at $20–30 and in live-service matchmaking games, not in the $5–10 friend-group game. The top failure cause is a population that dies after launch (9 of 15 cases).

  4. 4

    Each hit owns the audience briefly. The top game takes about 50% of friendslop players, then falls to about 10% within 3–9 months. The audience buys a new game every couple of months.

  5. 5

    Steam has no “Furry” tag, and the word is dominated by cheap adult games. Every animal million-seller (Party Animals, WEBFISHING, Cult of the Lamb) sold as “cute animals”, never “furry”, and the fandom adopted them anyway.

  6. 6

    There is no anthro friendslop hit yet. Animal co-op alone doesn’t sell: Trash Day (raccoons, 3 weeks old) peaked at 2k players, Frog Sqwad 930, Pawperty Damage 46. The verb has to carry the game.

  7. 7

    Open verbs remain: raising a creature together, moving house, delivery, building, gardening and digging have no friendslop-scale hit.

What has been made · by mechanic, not theme · added 2026-11-02

Friendslop by formula

The subgenres below group games by theme. This section groups them by what makes friends need each other. A theme is skin; two games with different skins can run the same formula.

tie+pressure+laugh(+ optionallayer)
Tie: what forces friends to need each other. This is the grouping axis, and each game has one main tie.
Pressure: what threatens the group (a monster, a clock, gravity, debt).
Laugh: where the funny moment comes from, usually the tie failing.
Layer: a loop on top that works with any tie (push your luck, earn and reinvest).

Five ties

Each tie answers “why do I need you?”. Party PvP doesn’t (friends are targets, not teammates), so it sits outside the model as a neighbour.

Reading

  • The two biggest ties by top game are split knowledge and linked bodies.
  • Too many jobs has the most mid-tier hits (several at 1–6k live) and the most upcoming entries.
  • Hordeline (the user’s direction) is too many jobs + earn and reinvest; it has neither of the two biggest ties yet.
  • LORT (co-op roguelite, stack ridiculous power-ups; 31.5k followers, 877 live on Nov 1) is the nearest released game to a survivors-style build layer.

How to read the numbers. Numbers on released games are live players: checked (Steam, one snapshot, 2026-11-02 21:53 JST, two days after Halloween, which likely lifts horror). Released games without a number weren’t in that snapshot. “f” = Steam followers, checked (Steam, 2026-11-01 scan); grey chips are upcoming. A dashed chip either fails this repo’s friendslop tag definition (Supermarket Together, Barotrauma, Sea of Thieves, Among Us) or wasn’t in the scan, so it isn’t tag-checked. The grouping is an assumption: sorted by Claude from store descriptions, not play. Ties are ranked by their top game on one evening; a fair ranking needs each tie’s full spread of released games (review counts, all-time peaks), and the released list is thin (the scan was mostly upcoming games).

The fandom as buyers

  • Size is unmeasured: estimates range from about 100k to a few million.
  • High spenders at conventions: mean $1,007 a year on cons, $441 on merchandise (Anthrocon sample, skewed by big spenders).
  • Anthrocon had 17,639 attendees in 2024 and is growing.
  • Most furries call themselves gamers; avatar spending is real (Booth sold about $21M of 3D assets in 2023, much of it VRChat avatars).
  • Game spending specifically is not measured.

Positioning

  • Steam has no Furry tag: 1,714 games tagged Animals, 145 Animals + Online Co-op, none a breakout.
  • Searching “furry” on Steam surfaces cheap adult games, which colours the word for buyers.
  • Since July 2025 Steam’s payment rules restrict some adult content, and itch.io deindexed NSFW games. Any adult association is a store and console risk.
  • Mainstream streamers carried Party Animals; “furry” likely makes them hesitate, “cute animal chaos” doesn’t (opinion).

The model that works: market “cute animal chaos with friends” to everyone, and give furry fans what they want without naming it: a deep character creator, many species, colour and pattern editors, expressive emotes. Keep everything safe for work. This is how Party Animals, WEBFISHING and Cult of the Lamb did it.

Where the potential is

Rules for a furry friendslop game

  1. One verb that makes a 10-second clip, chosen from the open list (raise, move, deliver, build, garden, dig). The animal theme is the skin, not the hook.
  2. $5–8, 4–8 players. Every confirmed mid-tier hit launched at $10 or less; 8 is the party norm.
  3. Friend-hosted peer-to-peer from day one (GodotSteam with Steam relay): no matchmaking, no dedicated servers, no live service. Those are where the measured failures cluster.
  4. Fun from 1 player up. Protects reviews, gives lone streamers something to play, and survives empty friend lists.
  5. More than 2 hours of play. Steam refunds games played under 2 hours; a short game saw 21% refunds.
  6. Character expression as the furry draw: species, colours, patterns, emotes. Safe for work.
  7. Market before the mechanics are final: short clips on TikTok, Bluesky, X; furry artists and VTubers; a demo. YAPYAP’s first viral clip came before its mechanics were decided.
  8. Ship a finished game, expect a spike. The audience moves on within months; plan a small content drop, not a treadmill.

Directions to consider

  • Raise a creature together: a proven solo hook (Tamagotchi, Slime Rancher) with no co-op hit; fits a furry cast. Haunted Paws (puppy co-op demo) is an early sign.
  • Moving day: online physics furniture-carrying; the hauling pleasure of R.E.P.O. without horror. Moving Out was couch-only.
  • Build it together: co-op construction where physics collapse is the comedy.

These are starting points from the gap list, not designs. Each needs a prototype and a test clip.

Main risks

  • Solo netcode: “makes everything take 5 times as long”.
  • Animal theme alone doesn’t sell (Trash Day, Frog Sqwad, Pawperty Damage).
  • Hits are copied within weeks, including free Roblox versions.
  • Cheap failures are invisible, so the true odds are worse than the data shows.
  • The word “furry” on the store page invites adult-content associations.
