# Comparing critic, round 5: i04 Moonsick

Read: the brief (r2), ladder.md, g01–g10, sheets/i04.md. Nothing else. Judged strictly: only written rules count.

## 0. Novelty (kept apart from the verdict)

Core act: two friends hold a friend through a 10-second fit; if they are late, that friend becomes a wolf that the player still steers and that the crew must catch from the side.
Not the core act of any ladder sheet. The nearest is g03 (carry a passed-out friend to stop a death timer), different because here the unrescued friend becomes the hazard and keeps playing. The rising mark is g01's rising target, but that is the frame, not the core act.

## 2. Closest two reference sheets

**g03 (Summit Scouts)** and **g05 (Linked Climb)**.
- Like g03: a friend in trouble on a short timer, saved only by teammates' bodies (pull up, carry). Like g05: no creatures, the friends are each other's hazard, very little content.
- Has that they lack: the failed player becomes a threat they steer themselves and that wants to be caught; a rising mark with a run-ending fail (g05 has no fail state); 8-minute rounds.
- Lacks that they have: a voice rule (g03); coupling that acts every second (g05's chain never lets go, here the coupling is off until a beam touches someone); terrain that changes (g03's generated mountain, g05's long tower); a danger the crew cannot decline (the wall must be climbed; here the beam can be stood away from).

## 3. Is it fun with friends? **maybe**

The wolf and the two-holder fit are real, rule-made shouting moments that no ladder sheet has. But they are punishments the crew can decline by standing back, the only thing that forces them is a mark with no numbers, and half the listed feelings do not survive a crew that plays to meet the mark.
Sits between **g05** (tier B) and **g06** (tier C): above g06 because failure keeps everyone playing and the target rises; below g05 because g05's coupling is always on and cannot be avoided.

### The best move for a crew that only wants the mark (worked from the written numbers)

- A beam is 4 steps wide and moves at running speed, so it passes a standing friend in about 1 second, and a fit needs a full 1-second touch. A friend moving across it, or 2 steps sideways from its middle, is touched for less. Whether the second is counted in one go or added up is unwritten.
- Blooms stay open 6 s. A crew that waits 2 s is about 8 steps behind the beam with 4 s of picking left, and a swerve back takes about 2 s to reach them, in full view. So fits are close to optional.
- A night is 480 s. If each half is one beam phase (the split is unwritten): 240/30 = about 8 beams early, 240/11.5 = about 21 late, about 29 beams. How many blooms a beam opens, how fast a bloom is picked, how many can be carried and what the mark is are all unwritten, so nobody can show the safe pick falls short on night 5, or that the mark can be met at all.
- A wolf costs little more than a fit. Held fit: two friends, 5 s. Missed fit: the same two friends hold the wolf for 5 s. The pull is "a little at first", so the wolf's best move is to come at once and pass side-on while it can still steer.
- Holders who arrive late (the scene's "8 seconds") are already standing at the friend's sides when they turn, hands free. "Thrown off" is in the scene, not in the rules. So a late hold just continues as a wolf hold: about 5 s lost, some blooms trampled.

### The plan a crew settles into by the fifth night

1. Spread in a loose triangle, each more than 4 steps from the next (one beam can't touch two) and inside a 5-second run of two others.
2. Pick late, about 8 steps behind the beam; step sideways on any swerve.
3. Bank after every beam, so nobody has blooms to put down when a hold is needed.
4. On a fit: the two nearest hold. If they will be late they hold anyway and keep holding through the turn.
5. On a loose wolf: two friends stand side by side in the open, the wolf comes at once and passes side-on.
6. Once the mark is banked, stop picking and wait for dawn (in the cauldron room if beams can't reach it, which is unwritten).

## 4. Holes of this design alone

1. **No numbers behind the difficulty.** Mark, blooms per beam, pick time, carry limit: all unwritten. Best move: none needed; the claim "the mark rises until pairs who stay back can't reach it" can't be checked, and neither can "the mark is reachable". Tier A shares it? No: g01 states its first target (130) and its three-day cycle.
2. **The danger can be declined.** Best move: pick 8 steps back, sidestep; a moving friend is never under a 4-step beam for a full second. The fun (fit, wolf) is then a penalty the crew avoids, not the job. Tier A shares it? No: g01's crew must enter the building, g03's crew must climb the wall.
3. **A missed fit is nearly free, and the wolf's 20 seconds never arrive.** Best move: late holders keep holding through the turn; a loose wolf comes straight in while it can steer. The rules don't say what the turn does to holders, whether a grab stops the wolf (it "never stops running" yet is "held for 5 seconds"), or whether one grabber slows it. Tier A shares it? No.
4. **Nothing to do after the mark, and no way to leave.** Best move: bank the mark, then wait for dawn. Whether beams or the wolf reach the cauldron room is unwritten. Tier A shares it? No: g01 has the lever, g02 has the truck.
5. **Small crews hit dead states the rules don't end.** At 3 friends, one wolf plus one fit leaves one free friend; nothing can be undone and the night runs on. Also unwritten: whether a friend in a fit counts as "human" at dawn, whether a wolf is pulled toward or bites a friend in a fit or another wolf, and what a beam starting in the last 10 seconds leaves the crew able to do. Tier A shares it? Partly: g01 and g02 lose everything on a full wipe, but there the day or run ends at once.

Rules that pull against each other:
- The "experienced crews" guess (pairs two steps apart) against the 4-step beam: one beam touches both, giving the double fit that needs four holders.
- "The wolf never stops running" against "hold it for 5 seconds".
- "Sees where each beam will start" against "beams cross where everyone can see them": the information is worth only its lead time, which is unwritten.

## 5. What I could not break

- The failed player never waits: fit, wolf and bite all keep them steering and shouting. No ladder sheet does this better.
- When two fits do happen at once, "hold Ben, hold Cy, or split and save neither" has no right answer; the two-holder rule makes it.
- The wolf is a threat with no traitor: the player wants to be caught and the body wants to bite.
- Blooms put down for a hold can be trampled: carrying many makes rescuing costly. (The plan dodges it by banking often, which costs walking time; that trade is real.)
- Low content: one room, one noun.

## 6. Smallest change that would make it more fun with friends (opinion)

Let the crew end the night itself: a bell at the cauldron that calls dawn, and blooms above the mark count toward tomorrow's mark. A wolf or a fit at the bell still fails the run.
That turns the dead wait after the mark into "one more beam, or ring it now" with the whole crew sharing the loss (trait 4), and gives the late, fast beams a reason to be stood under.
It does not fix hole 1: the numbers still have to be written.
