## 1b. Dragon's Hoard, version 2

**Base: fail.** The new core choice, "call out or stay silent", needs a friend who is in danger and cannot see the eye, and the rules cannot produce that friend: whoever the eye can see can see the eye.

| Round 1 severe finding | Fixed? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No clock: standing still wins | No | The smoke only bites "past a couple of minutes", so the 40-second stand-still still works several times per trip. The cave mouth clears the lungs while quiet drains the dragon, so it is a free reset spot. The only real cost is dawn, and dawn has no number. |
| Fire goes to the last loud noise (decoy) | Yes, for the noise decoy | A thrown goblet no longer aims the fire. It opens a new hole: where the eye looks has no rule (severe 3). |
| The core is built on silence | No | The choice is now literally speak or stay silent, priced through the in-game mic. On Discord the call is free. The noise ring and the partner's body already carry the message. |
| Greed has no rule | Yes | Loot past the order pays the debt and buys gear. Lethal Company crews do keep scrap for a later quota and get a bonus for selling over it (searched), so the cited rule is real. The argument it makes is still a sum (medium). |

What each role gains and pays.
- Caller: gains nothing, pays noise. On Discord pays nothing.
- Friend in the open: stands still. Decides nothing.
- Statue: sits out with no job. On Discord the statue is a free watcher next to the head who can talk at no cost.

- "Wait at the cave mouth": wins against the dragon and the smoke at once; only dawn prices it, and dawn has no number. Fail.
- "One burglar is the permanent watcher at the head": works at 3 and 5, where one burglar is spare; he is one seat holding the cue, and inside the smoke he is also the one coughing. Fail.
- "Never be in the open": the file answers it with pieces placed in the open. That part passes, and it makes freezing the only tool left.
- "The call is never worth it / always worth it": the eye opens at half, so every call starts from just past half. A call of fixed size is then always right or always wrong, by the tuning. Fail.

Best plan by night 5: trips shorter than the cough, rest at the mouth, freeze at every ring, never call.
Best plan by night 20: the same with soft shoes.

## 6. Sleepover

**Base: fail.** The fear is ended by any friend standing near, so help is a position and not an act, and a pair is safe forever with nothing against it.

Played scene (paper).
- 3 players: Ana and Ben in the cellar, Cy alone in the attic. Cy says on Discord "I'm nearly full". Ana and Ben walk up together. Nobody is left alone. The file's "Ben leaves Ana" happens only if the crew chooses it to save seconds.
- 3 players, the other tuning: the attic trip is shorter than the fill time. All three run one dare each, alone, and meet in the hall. Nobody needed anybody.
- 6 players: three pairs, each with fear at zero, walk a list of dares. Nothing threatens a pair. The only question is whether the list fits the clock.
- 1 human + bots: a bot that follows the human keeps fear at zero for the whole night. A bot that leaves by rule is learned on night 1.

What each role gains and pays.
- The one alone: gains a dare, pays a meter that fills by itself. Nothing they do while alone changes it.
- The companion: stands within a few steps. Pays nothing, decides nothing.
- The fainted kid: lies there until anyone walks over.

The plans the brief asked me to test.
- "The nearest friend goes": wins. Any friend empties the fear, and the rescuer's own fear is emptied on arrival. Fail.
- "Break the pair whose other kid is calmest": wins whenever a pair must break, and the faint count that decides "calmest" is public. Fail.
- "Everyone runs every dare alone and hides": wins whenever a trip is shorter than the fill time. Whether hiding from the monster works is not written. Fail.
- Does the hidden monster matter to any decision: no. It appears only when nobody is near, and it fades when someone arrives. Fail.

Best plan by night 5: the kid with the fewest faints takes the shortest dare alone, everyone else pairs, a pair moves as one to any scream.
Best plan by night 20: the same sort, with known trip times per room.

Co-op check: 1 coupled fail (the friend's part is a place to stand) · 2 split fail (same role for all; on Discord everyone knows everyone's fear) · 3 talk pass · 4 blame pass ("you left me") · 5 not easy fail (fill time not written) · 6 not solo fail (solo is free below one fill time) · 7 two-way fail · lone survivor pass (can revive a friend or properly lose) · dying on purpose pass (a faint only hurts).

Findings.
- **Severe 1: the Hold the Door drop reason applies; the file's own ❌ is right.** "Only a specific friend relieves it" fails: checks.md names "anyone being able to do it" as a fail. The difference the file offers (going leaves a third friend alone) holds only at 3 and 5, and only if the pair splits instead of walking up together. Example: Ana and Ben go up as a pair. Fix direction: relief must be an act that costs the helper, from one friend who is the only one able.
- **Severe 2: a pair is a safe state with no opposition.** Fear is zero next to a friend and nothing else in the house acts. The play left is walking to dares, and the dares are not written. If they are small tasks, they are the minigames of the user's second family; if they are pickups, they are fetch runs. Example: the 6-player night. Fix direction: something must threaten two kids together.
- **Severe 3: one ratio decides everything, and inside it the answer is a public sort.** If a round trip is shorter than the fill time, solo is free; if longer, solo always faints. Between the two, "calmest goes alone" is the answer, from facts all can see. That is Low Tide's drop reason (one interest, open facts) and the banned "send whoever is least X". Example: the two tunings above. Fix direction: none with a meter everyone can read and shared pay.
- **Severe 4: the monster only one kid sees changes nothing, and its laugh has no audience.** The monster exists only while no friend is near, so no friend watches the flailing. Dead Space 3's reviews praise Carver's visions as story and as fun to play; none describes laughing at a partner, and they are scripted missions for two (searched). Example: Cy swings at air in an empty attic. Fix direction: the unseen thing must appear while friends are watching.
- **Severe 5: the rule is Moonsick's sickness with Moonsick's counter made official.** A meter on one body, ended by friends standing around it, with a lasting penalty after. The user's words on Moonsick: "the game can be countered by having all people around the sick one". Round 1 struck The Glasses' frost for the same thing. Fix direction: none; it is the core rule.
- Medium: the file contradicts itself on hearing. "A kid far away can't be heard" is its answer to the drop reason, and its own scene has Ben hear Cy through the floor. On Discord everyone hears everyone.
- Medium: Evil Dead: The Game is four survivors against a human demon, and a full fear meter pays that human with a possession (searched). The row "all four are co-op crews" is wrong for it.
- Medium: "fainted kids fill faster for the rest of the night" only goes one way. After two faints at 3, the night is a slow sure loss.
- Medium: at 4 and 6 everyone pairs off, so the core choice shows up only when the clock forces a solo.
- Medium: the file says it "leans toward a minigame" between nights. That is a fail on game-or-minigame.
- Medium: near games were under-searched. Slumber Party is a shipped 1 to 4 player co-op horror game about a slumber party; no fear-alone meter was found on its page (searched). The file says none was found with a sleepover.
- Minor: better precedents exist than Dead Space 3. Lethal Company's ghost girl and The Outlast Trials' Skinner Man are each seen only by the affected player in a co-op crew (searched). The file's memory claim on Outlast is confirmed. In Outlast the cure is an item, not a friend.

Carried rules: fail (the sickness family, severe 5; the dares risk the minigame family).
Minigame or game: fail, by the file's own words.
Scope: pass for days, pass for 3 to 4 months. It is the cheapest of the three and the only one that passes lag and runs fully with bots. Costliest part: the dare list, and making a meter read as fear.
Do the file's own ❌ marks drop it? Yes. The Hold the Door ❌ is the base, and my replay confirms it.

## 7. Parade Dragon

**Base: fail.** The head sees the stalls first, steers and sets the pace, so one seat holds every call, and the act the fantasy rests on (bodies linked by physics) is the one the lag check fails.

Played scene (paper).
- 3 players: Ana head, Ben middle, Cy tail. Ana sees the gold cup on the left stall four seconds before Ben's eyehole reaches it. Ana: "cup left, Ben, three, two, one". Ana and Cy hold. Ben grabs. Ben decided nothing.
- 3 players, between stalls: Ben and Cy hold forward and keep the poles up. Cy looks backward at nothing the rules use.
- 6 players: four middles follow a chain they cannot see the front of. Two can grab at once at most. More hands show, so the one suspicion pile fills faster. The tail whips on every turn.
- 1 human + bots: "cup, left, two steps" is a spoken call, and bots do not speak. As head, the human drags bots that follow perfectly. As a middle, the human waits for a bot head to pass a stall.

What each role gains and pays.
- Head: decides route, pace and which stall. Pays nothing extra.
- Grabber: is whoever the stall passes. Gains the item for all.
- Pole holders: press and hold when asked. With shared pay and no private stake they refuse only when an arm meter says so.
- Tail: follows, is thrown about, and sees behind, where nothing happens.

The plans the brief asked me to test.
- "The head decides everything": yes. Fail.
- "Only ever grab in gaps": the crowd cheers again while the dragon dances, so the crew grabs, dances the suspicion down, and grabs again. A fixed line on a pile that drains, which is version 1 of Dragon's Hoard. Fail.
- Is the pole-holding a real cost: no. "Keeps them from grabbing" bites only when two neighbours pass loot in the same second. Tired arms are a second meter with its own fixed line. Fail.

Best plan by night 5: the head calls loot and turns with a countdown, neighbours hold, grab only while the crowd cheers.
Best plan by night 20: the same.

Co-op check: 1 coupled pass · 2 split fail (the head's view holds the middles' view, earlier) · 3 talk pass · 4 blame pass (a sag or a swing is one body) · 5 not easy fail (no numbers) · 6 not solo pass · 7 two-way fail (holders and followers obey) · lone survivor pass (nobody dies; the costume comes off for all) · dying on purpose pass.

Findings.
- **Severe 1: the head is a quarterback.** Caught five times before this run and once in round 1. Example: Ana's countdown. Fix direction: the head must not see the goods; say what each eyehole hides, as a rule.
- **Severe 2: the choice is "the neighbours hold", the plan Hold the Door was dropped for.** The file's drop-reason row says "none found". The holders only hold, and who grabs is whoever is nearest. Example: Ana and Cy press hold on request, every stall. Fix direction: the holder needs something to lose that is their own.
- **Severe 3: the threat is a pile that drains.** Nothing in the crowd searches or answers a habit. The fear of being noticed is the only opposition, and it is a threshold. Example: grab, eight seconds of dancing, grab. Fix direction: a watcher who looks, with a line of sight.
- **Severe 4: the rules are the user's second family.** One shared thing (the dragon's shape and the crowd's mood) is kept up by everyone holding, while stalls arrive like events. "Hands are scarce" is the rule Claude carried from Red Button into Pet the Cat. The user's words for the family: "too simple and easy and requires no cooperation at all". Fix direction: none inside "keep the costume up".
- **Severe 5: the core act fails lag and the bot test, and that is the fantasy.** Three to six bodies linked by physics is the worst case in checks.md. Dragon's Hoard can swap its carry out; this idea cannot swap out being one body. Bots need chain walking, pole holding and a ping to stand in for speech. Fix direction: none that keeps "one stupid body"; take the bill to the user.
- Medium: the tail's backward view feeds no decision.
- Medium: a fair with a route, stalls laid out anew, and a crowd that shows suspicion on its bodies is the most art of the three.
- Medium: nothing gets worse if the dragon just dances; only the parade's end prices it, with no number.
- Minor: the ❌ on "fear of being noticed" is too hard on the idea. Payday 2 and Monaco are co-op crews afraid of being noticed by computer guards (memory). That ❌ alone should not drop it.
- Minor: no shipped game with friends inside one festival dragon was found; Barongsway is a local co-op lion-dance platformer on itch.io (searched). The premise is free.

Carried rules: fail (severe 4).
Minigame or game: pass on paper (list and debt), paid for with a new fair each night.
Scope: fail. The smallest version is not days, because the chain is the game and it has to work over the network first. Costliest part: linked bodies in sync, then bots in the chain.
Do the file's own ❌ marks drop it? The lag ❌ does, because it sits on the core act.
