# Proposal for the critic: Breakables

**Fantasy:** A crew of small robots is sent into abandoned, haunted buildings to haul out fragile valuables for an unseen employer, and wants to fill the money target and get back to the truck alive.
One-player test: The line still works with one player, but heavy items are harder to carry and nobody can bring the player back after a death.

**Players:** 1–6, one crew. **A session:** one level is one building visit followed by one shop stop; a guide treats about six minutes as the point by which the target should be reached; how many levels a sitting holds is (not found).

**Feelings the game is known for** (2–4, each as a line a player says to a friend)
"Slow down, slow down, you're going to smash it on the door frame."
"Stop screaming, it can hear you."
"I'm just a head now, please carry me to the drop-off."
"We had the target and then he dropped the vase."

**The rules, one sentence each** (every rule a player must know; as many as the game really has, 8–14)
Each level is a building with rooms put together at random, holding loose valuables and roaming creatures.
Players lift and carry objects with a physics grab beam; heavy objects need more than one player or more strength.
Every valuable shows a money value, and every bump, drop or hit lowers that value until the item breaks.
A wheeled cart carries many valuables at once and shows the total value inside it.
Each level has one or more drop-off points, each with its own money target.
Valuables placed on a drop-off point are collected when the target is met, and the money beyond the target becomes the crew's spending money.
Every drop-off target on the level must be filled before the crew may leave in the truck.
Creatures hunt by sight or by sound, and voice chat counts as sound.
Crouching makes a player harder to see and quieter; most creatures give up a chase after some distance, a few never do.
Fighting is possible with bought weapons or by throwing objects, but guides treat it as a last resort.
A dead player leaves a head behind; if a teammate carries the head to a drop-off point and that drop-off completes, the player returns with 1 health.
A player can give some of their own health to a teammate.
If every player is dead the run ends and the crew starts again from the first level.
Between levels the crew visits a shop and spends the money on permanent upgrades, weapons, explosives, tools, health and energy for tools.

**How much there is:** four building themes (a manor, a school of magic, a polar station, a museum) with generated layouts; creature kinds about 19 at one point with 10 more added later (exact total not found); about ten kinds of permanent upgrade (exact count not found); weapons, explosives and tools counts (not found).

**Voice:** voice by distance; teammates far away cannot be heard, creatures that hunt by sound react to it, and a dead player cannot talk to the living unless a bought upgrade lets the head act.

**The choices** (3–5 lines a player really faces, "do X, or do Y?")
Carry the big expensive item slowly through narrow rooms, or leave it and take small safe ones?
Load one more room into the cart, or go to the drop-off now with what we have?
Go back for a dead teammate's head, or finish the target without them?
Spend the money on an upgrade for one player, or on healing and a weapon for the crew?
Shout a warning to the others, or stay silent so the creature does not hear?

**What the other end decides in the same second** (per choice, or "nothing")
The second carrier decides how fast to walk and which way to turn the item, without seeing what the first carrier sees.
The players away from the cart decide whether to keep looting or come back to guard the route.
The dead player decides nothing; they watch.
Each other player argues for their own upgrade at the shop.
The players who hear the warning decide to hide, run, or drop what they hold.

**Scene, 4 friends** (5–7 bullets, one real moment the game is known for, second by second)
- Two players carry a large fragile item down a hallway toward the cart, calling out each step.
- A third pushes the half-full cart behind them; the fourth has gone ahead and is out of voice range.
- A creature comes round the corner and one carrier yells.
- The yell pulls the creature toward them, and the other carrier lets go to crouch under a table.
- The item hits the floor and its value drops by a large part.
- The creature kills the player who yelled; their head rolls next to the cart.
- The crouched player waits, then puts the head into the cart and pushes for the drop-off.

**What experienced crews do:** Guides say to treat creatures as obstacles to walk around and not to fight.
Guides say to crouch-walk near creatures and close doors behind you to gain a few seconds.
Guides say to gather heavy items near the drop-off before starting it.
Guides say to spread upgrades across the crew (finding, carrying, distracting, surviving) and not stack them on one player.
One guide's habit is to restart in the first two minutes if the creature spawn is bad, and to reach the target by about minute six.
One guide says to leave tempting items that sit in exposed spots.
**What changes it from round to round:** The room layout, where items lie and where creatures are placed are different each time; every fifth level a global modifier changes how the game works.
**What carries from one round to the next:** Money, bought upgrades, bought weapons and tools carry through the run; all of it is lost when the whole crew dies.
**Can a new crew lose? Does it get harder?** Yes, a full crew death ends the run; later levels have more loot, more creatures and more drop-off points, and inside one level the danger rises after a time that depends on the level.
