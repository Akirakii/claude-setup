# M1 "the grid" (control), gen 1

## Method log
- Steps: read `formula.md` (and `~/.claude/design-anti-patterns.md`, a user rule for any design sketch; it lists failure patterns, not ideas). Built the 8 x 3 grid (rows: handoff, shared object, bodies in the way, split information, shared pot, timer, private goals, something hidden; columns: teammate, world/physics, hidden traitor). Wrote one candidate per cell, plus one spillover that came up while filling a cell. Then self-checked each against F1-F7, G and N from memory (no web) and kept the 8 best with different core rules. No critic agent: that would change the method under test.
- Raw count: 25 (24 cells + 1 spillover).
- Most common failures among raw candidates: N, novelty (a known game has the same rule: Pico Park, Octodad co-op, Among Us, Chained Together, Spaceteam, Keep Talking), mostly in the traitor column and the "bodies" row. Next was F3, mostly in the shared-pot and private-goal rows (pooled bars, sums of pulls, everyone doing the same job). Then F5 (a traitor at 3 players is easy to spot) and F6 (the world hides something but no player has a reason to hide it).
- Good at: coverage and speed. The physics column gave the strongest ideas (tipping raft, crumbling floor, throw-only cargo), because physics makes the challenge with no hand-made content. Forcing every row also pushed out ideas I would not have reached on my own (the balloon, the line of sight rule).
- Bad at: the rows overlap ("split information" and "something hidden" gave the same idea twice). The traitor column mostly gives existing social-deduction games with a new theme. The pot row tends to give pooled bars, which fail F3 by definition. The grid fills cells but doesn't judge quality: about a third of the cells were filler.

## Kept ideas

### M1-1 Tool Line
- Rule (one sentence): The crew crawls through a tunnel one person wide where nobody can pass anybody, and there are fewer tools than players, so each tool must be handed along the line to whoever's spot is failing.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the line of bodies in the tunnel (who is where in the order).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): problems open at any spot along the line (a leak needs a plug, a falling rock needs a prop, a stuck hatch at the front needs a bar, rising water at the back needs the pump). The right tool is often 2–3 bodies away, so the people in between must put down what they hold and pass it. Whether I keep my prop or pass it forward depends on what just broke at my friend's spot. The front player digs, the back player holds the water, the middle players plug and pass.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): miners crawling out of a flooding shaft.
- Look (one line): side-view slice of a cramped tunnel, headlamps, dark water creeping in from the left.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 players, 2 tools, short tunnel; 6 players, 4 tools, longer tunnel. Problems spawn at a rate tied to crew size, and every seat has a spot that can fail.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the leak at Sam's spot opened while Dana held the plug for two extra seconds; the water passed Sam and reached the front. Everyone saw the plug sitting in Dana's hands.

### M1-2 Crumbling Floor
- Rule (one sentence): Every floor tile falls a moment after someone steps off it, and the crew must get a crate that needs several carriers across.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the floor tiles.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): carriers move slowly and need an unbroken path ahead. Fetchers run to the edges for planks (each plank patches one hole), but every step they take eats floor. A fetcher's route depends on where the carriers just turned, and the carriers re-route around tiles a fetcher just burned. The carriers must agree on a direction because they hold one crate.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): movers hauling a piano along a rotting pier at night.
- Look (one line): top-down plank pier over black water, cracks glowing under each footstep.
- At 3 / at 6: carriers are half the crew, rounded up, and the crate gets heavier at larger crews (3 = 2 carriers + 1 fetcher; 6 = 3 carriers + 3 fetchers). Piers are generated.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Kai cut across the carriers' line to grab a plank, the tile dropped behind him, and the piano went into the sea one step later. His cracked footprints were right there.

### M1-3 Tipping Raft
- Rule (one sentence): The raft tips toward wherever the crew is standing, and tipping is the only way to steer it down the river.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the raft.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): each job pulls a person to a spot. Poling off a rock needs someone at that edge, grabbing floating supplies needs someone on the side they float past, and bailing needs someone where water pools on the low side. Every move shifts the tilt, so the others must step to counter it or the raft swings into rocks. Where I should stand depends on where my friend just ran.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): castaways riding a homemade raft down wild rapids.
- Look (one line): top-down log raft in white water, crates bobbing past, a tilt arrow on the deck.
- At 3 / at 6: each person counts for 1/crew-size of the weight, so the tilt range stays the same. At 3 every step swings the raft; at 6 more jobs run at once and bodies crowd the deck. Rivers are generated.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Mia dashed to the right edge for a crate, the raft swung into a rock and split. The whole crew saw her run and the tilt follow her.

### M1-4 Throw-Only Cargo
- Rule (one sentence): The cargo can never be carried, only thrown, and it breaks if it touches the ground.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the cargo (one fragile egg).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the holder can't move and picks whom to throw to. The catcher has to be where it will land. The others run ahead to set up the next catch or shove rolling junk out of the landing spot. Who I throw to depends on where my friends just went, and where I run depends on who just caught it.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a crew moving a dragon egg across a scrapyard full of swinging cranes.
- Look (one line): low-poly scrapyard, rolling barrels, a glowing egg arcing between players.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it's a tight leapfrog (thrower, catcher, runner); at 6, throws are shorter and the yard has more moving junk, so more hops and more clearers. Yards are generated from one physics junk list.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Leo threw to where Ana had stood a second earlier; she had stepped aside to dodge a barrel. The arc landed on empty ground and everyone saw whose throw it was.

### M1-5 Blind Spot
- Rule (one sentence): Each player sees the traps around every teammate but never the traps around themselves.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the trap field (one generated floor).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): everyone fetches parts of a machine scattered across the field, and the parts need two people to lift. To warn a friend you must look at them, so you stop watching your own route. Who I watch depends on who just got close to a trap, and my safe path depends on who is watching me. (Honest note: the jobs are mostly the same for everyone.)
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): treasure hunters in a trapped temple who can see their friends' doom but not their own.
- Look (one line): stone temple tiles, traps glowing red only on other players' screens, a soft beam showing who watches whom.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 each player has two watchers; at 6 there are more eyes but more voices talking over each other, and the field grows.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": the traps near you. They are uncovered only when a friend tells you or you step on one. No player has a reason to hide them; the game hides them.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ben walked onto spikes while Zoe, the only one looking his way, turned to lift her part. The watch beams show she left him.

### M1-6 Sheet and Ball
- Rule (one sentence): Everyone holds one edge of a single sheet, and the ball on it rolls toward whoever holds their edge lowest.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the sheet.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the crew walks the sheet through a fairground of gates and holes. To steer the ball, the players on one side lower their edges while the others lift. When anyone dips, the ball runs to them, so everyone must react to each dip. Semi-co-op: one hidden traitor is shown a single hole and wins if the ball drops into it.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): circus hands carrying the star's giant ball across the fairground; one is a spy from the rival circus.
- Look (one line): bright striped cloth, a huge ball, stalls and open trapdoors.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 the sheet is a triangle and the traitor is very exposed (2 honest players watch 1 edge each); at 6 the traitor hides easily among the dips. Works best from 4.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": who the traitor is. Every edge's height is visible, and the crew votes after each drop. The traitor hides by dipping only when someone else is already low.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the ball rolled off at Ike's edge into the trapdoor. Everyone saw Ike's edge was lowest, and the argument is whether Noor lifted first.

### M1-7 Look At Me
- Rule (one sentence): You can only move while a teammate is looking at you.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the crew's lines of sight.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): each player picks whom to watch and where to go. Watching means facing that friend, not your own path, so moving together takes chains and circles. When a watcher turns away to grab something, the person they were watching freezes on the spot. A night guard walks a simple patrol and catches anyone standing in its light, so a frozen friend in the guard's path is lost.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): museum statues that come alive at night and must escape before dawn.
- Look (one line): marble statues in a dim gallery, soft beams showing who watches whom, a guard's flashlight sweeping.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 a triangle (A watches B, B watches C, C watches A) lets everyone move, but nobody faces where they're going. At 6 there are pairs and chains, more options, and more crowding. Galleries are generated from one tile set.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Rosa turned to grab the exit key, and Tom froze mid-step in the guard's path. The beam from Rosa to Tom vanished the moment she turned.

### M1-8 Balloon Basket
- Rule (one sentence): The balloon floats higher for every person who climbs out of its basket and sinks for every person who climbs back in.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the balloon's height.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): mail bags and stranded people sit on rooftops below. To grab one, someone drops down the rope, which lifts the balloon, so the rope's end rises away from anyone already below. One player at the burner can spend scarce fuel to sink, one holds the rope steady, and the others jump for pickups. When I can jump depends on who is still on the rope.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a balloon crew racing a storm across a rooftop town to pick up mail and stranded people.
- Look (one line): patchwork balloon over crooked rooftops, a swinging rope ladder, storm clouds closing in.
- At 3 / at 6: each person counts for 1/crew-size of the lift. At 3 one jump is a big rise; at 6 rises are finer but more people are off the basket at once. Towns are generated.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ola jumped for a mail bag while Fin was still climbing the rope, the balloon shot up, and Fin's hand slipped. Everyone saw Ola leave the basket.
