# Gameplay verbs and player coupling: a catalog of 30 multiplayer verbs

Current as of 2026-11-09. Player counts are snapshots from trackers and press. They differ by tracker and date, so read them as rough sizes, not exact numbers.

**Tags.** `[source](link)` = found this session in a fetched page or search result. `[read]` = Claude's earlier reading (training data), not re-checked this session. Treat it as likely but not checked. `[opinion]` = Claude's judgment. Every rating is `[opinion]`, backed by the facts listed for that verb.

**Terms used in the ratings (defined before use):**
- **Acts on**: what the verb changes. *Mind* = what another player believes, sees or decides (fooling their eyes counts as mind). *Body* = another player's avatar: position, health, what they hold. *Env* = the world, objects or AI enemies.
- **Hidden info**: the verb works on something one player knows and another does not (Y / some / N).
- **Human counter**: a human on the other side picks a reply to this verb, rather than an AI or the level (Y / N / depends).
- **Content need**: how much authored material (maps, enemies, items, prompts, songs) games built on the verb needed to stay fresh. *Low* = 1–3 maps or modes, or a word list. *Medium* = about 5–15 maps or levels, or dozens of items. *High* = dozens of maps, enemies or characters plus regular updates.
- **Player coupling**: how much one player's action changes another player's result and their next decision. *Low* = side-by-side play. *Medium* = shared win or loss, but separate actions. *High* = each action changes what the other player can or should do, moment to moment.

## Q1. For each verb: example games, content-size facts, and ratings

### Takeaway
Verbs that act on another player's mind or body, where a human picks the counter, needed very little authored content: other players' choices supply the variety. Verbs that act on the environment against AI needed the most content. A few recent "friendslop" hits (R.E.P.O., Lethal Company, Content Warning, PEAK, Chained Together) made environment verbs strongly coupled by sharing one object, one wallet, one rope or one camera. The rating table is under Inferences below. [opinion]

### Cited Findings

#### 1. Hide
- MECCHA CHAMELEON (developer "lemorion_1224", released 2026-06-10, $5.99). Seekers versus hiders. Hiders are white figures who paint their bodies to blend into the stage. Seekers win if they find everyone before time runs out. 2–10 players recommended. Fan-made Steam Workshop maps exist. — [source](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/06/meccha-chameleon-is-a-hide-and-seek-body-painting-game-taking-over-steam/)
- MECCHA CHAMELEON was made in about two months (headline "se hizo en dos meses"; one image caption says one month). It peaked above 240,000 concurrent players within days. To celebrate 5 million sales, the developer planned to add one extra map plus new game options. That suggests a very small map count at launch, but no exact count was found. — [source](https://www.3djuegos.com/pc/noticias/se-hizo-dos-meses-se-lanzo-hace-unos-dias-tiene-240-000-jugadores-asi-ultimo-exito-steam-que-nadie-vio-venir/amp)
- MECCHA CHAMELEON all-time Steam peak was about 340,534 concurrent players on 2026-06-21, and it sold 7 million copies by 2026-06-22. — [source](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/06/meccha-chameleon-is-a-hide-and-seek-body-painting-game-taking-over-steam/). Press headlines report 10 million sales in 16 days — [source](https://www.gosugamers.net/entertainment/news/78702-viral-indie-hit-meccha-chameleon-reaches-10-million-sales-in-just-16-days) — and 20 million sales with about 22k daily players by mid-August 2026 — [source](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/meccha-chameleon-hits-20m-sales-with-22k-daily-players).
- Garry's Mod "Prop Hunt" mode: hiders turn into props on existing community maps. It needs almost no new content. [read]
- Dead by Daylight: survivors hide from the killer, but the game needed a large cast (see Tag/chase). — [source](https://www.gfinityesports.com/misc/dead-by-daylight-best-killers-tier-list-dbd-dowsey/)
- Classic: hide-and-seek and Sardines. Zero content beyond a house. [read]

#### 2. Seek
- MECCHA CHAMELEON seekers (above). Against human hiders, every round differs because the hiders choose new spots. [opinion]
- Phasmophobia (seek and identify a ghost; the counter is AI): 14 maps as of January 2026 — [source](https://gonintendo.com/contents/57161-phasmophobia-content-roadmap-for-2026-revealed); about 27 ghost types per a fan wiki (not official) — [source](https://cf-inc.shapes.inc/fandom/phasmophobia/ghost-types); a 2026 roadmap with map reworks, a new map and a 1.0 release — [source](https://kineticgames.co.uk/news/phasmophobias-roadmap-for-2026).
- Lethal Company: crews search moons for scrap. — [source](https://gamingbolt.com/lethal-company-is-the-latest-indie-game-to-cross-100000-concurrent-players)
- Reuter et al. list "players splitting up while searching for rare materials in Minecraft" as the "Parallelization" pattern: low communication, low timing demands, players work fairly independently. — [source](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/639/639)

#### 3. Bluff / lie
- Liar's Bar (Curve Animation, Ankara; early access 2024-10-02). It has two modes. In *Liar's Dice*, each player rolls six dice, bids, and the next player must raise. In *Liar's Deck*, players play cards matching the table card. Anyone can call a liar. A caught liar drinks poison (dice) or plays Russian roulette with one bullet in six (deck). There are four selectable animal characters. It won the 2024 Steam Award for Most Innovative Gameplay. — [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar%27s_Bar)
- Liar's Bar peaked at about 108k–114k concurrent players (October 2024). Raijin estimates about 2.2M copies sold and about $13.3M gross (third-party estimate). Mid-2026 monthly peaks were about 1,100–1,700, and recent snapshots show about 545–1,197 online. — [source](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/10/21/liars-bar-100k-ccu-steam-curve-animation-turkey-success); [source](https://raijin.gg/app/3097560/Liars_Bar); [source](https://steambase.io/apps/liars-bar)
- Coup, Skull, Liar's Dice, Poker: a deck or a few dice. [read]
- Fibbage (Jackbox): players write fake answers to fool others. The content is a prompt bank. [read]

#### 4. Deduce / accuse

- Among Us Steam peak: 438,524 concurrent players (September 2020). Innersloth reported 3.8 million concurrent players across all platforms in late September 2020. — [source](https://headphonesaddict.com/among-us-statistics/); [source](https://dotesports.com/news/among-us-hits-100-million-downloads-3-8-million-concurrent-players)
- Innersloth was about three people at the time. [read; one search result cited a November 2020 article saying "three-person developer team", not opened]
- Werewolf / Mafia: role cards only. Blood on the Clocktower: a large role script, but a human storyteller does the work. [read]
- Phasmophobia: deduction against the environment (evidence → ghost type). It needs more content than deduction against humans (see Seek). [source](https://gonintendo.com/contents/57161-phasmophobia-content-roadmap-for-2026-revealed)

#### 15. Coordinate without talking

#### 16. Bet (shared money)

#### 17. Carry together
- R.E.P.O. (Semiwork, Sweden; early access 2025-02-26; Unity). Up to 6 players carry valuables to extraction. Damaging them lowers their value. Layouts are randomized and modular. It peaked at 271,571 concurrent players on 2025-03-23 per SteamDB. Update 0.4.0 (2026-05-07) added cosmetics, a redesigned shop, a racing mode and items. It won the Golden Joystick for Best Early Access Game (2025). — [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.P.O.); [source](https://steamdb.info/app/3241660/charts)
- Human: Fall Flat (physics co-op, one-man developer No Brakes Games at its 2016 launch): 55 million copies sold by January 2025. — [source](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/curve-games-human-fall-flat-has-sold-55-million-units); [source](https://www.kitguru.net/gaming/mustafa-mahmoud/human-fall-flat-has-surpassed-25-million-copies-sold)
- Reuter et al. "Concurrency" pattern: operating an object one player cannot move alone (moving a heavy object in Resident Evil 5). It is obligatory, needs medium communication and high timing, and feels artificial if the world gives no reason for it. — [source](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/639/639)
- Moving Out: carry furniture together. [read]

- **The abstract pattern behind the table: where does each round's variety come from?** It comes either from authored content (maps, enemies, items) or from other humans' choices. If a human picks the counter, that human acts as the content generator, and every round differs for free. Verbs with "Human counter = Y" and "Acts on = Mind/Body" cluster at Low content. Verbs with "Human counter = N" and "Acts on = Env" cluster at Medium–High. [opinion]
- **Hidden information multiplies this.** Hiding, bluffing, deducing, betraying, mimicking and split-info signaling all make the other human's guess the game. Even one map stays fresh because who is lying changes every round. [opinion]
- **Environment verbs can still be strongly coupled if the object is shared:** one valuable that breaks (R.E.P.O.), one wallet or quota (Lethal Company), one rope or chain (Chained Together, PEAK), one ship (Sea of Thieves). Then the content stays at Medium and the coupling comes from physics and proximity voice, not from rules. [opinion]
- **Asymmetric versions of human-counter verbs bring back high content.** Dead by Daylight's chase needs about 42 distinct killers. Symmetric tag (Gorilla Tag) did not need them to launch. Asymmetry means one side's kit must be authored. [opinion]
- **Coupling between teammates and coupling between opponents are different things.** Bluff and tag couple you to opponents. Carry, signal and revive couple you to teammates. For a co-op game the second kind matters more. Co-op verbs with High teammate coupling and Low content: signal/describe, coordinate without talking, draw/guess, revive, carry together (Medium content). [opinion]

- No reliable user or player counts for Gartic Phone or skribbl.io (free browser games are not on Steam trackers).
- No official Overcooked 2 level count (sources say about 36 versus about 45) and no official sales figure.
- No 2015–2026 online hit found that is built mainly on **negotiate** or **trade**. Both seem to live mostly in board games and MMOs. Not confirmed absent; my searches did not target it.
- Sales figures from Raijin, SteamSpy, SteamPulse and Alinea are third-party estimates. Lethal Company estimates range from 642k to about 10M depending on date and method.
- Ratings are judgment calls. No published source rates verbs on "content need", so that column cannot be anchored to a study.

## Q2. Which verbs reached hits with very little content, and which needed lots?

### Takeaway
Low-content hits cluster in hide/mimic, bluff, deduce/vote, draw/guess, symmetric tag, and physics carry or push, all made by teams of 1–5 people in weeks to months. High-content games cluster in PvE shooting, asymmetric chase, building, racing and environment deduction, and they need steady updates. But low content got the spike, not the tail: most low-content hits lost about 90–99% of their peak within months. [opinion, backed below]

### Cited Findings
**Low content, big result:**
- MECCHA CHAMELEON (hide/mimic): about 2 months of development, about 340k Steam peak, 7M sold by day 12, reported 20M by mid-August 2026, $5.99. — [source](https://www.3djuegos.com/pc/noticias/se-hizo-dos-meses-se-lanzo-hace-unos-dias-tiene-240-000-jugadores-asi-ultimo-exito-steam-que-nadie-vio-venir/amp); [source](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/06/meccha-chameleon-is-a-hide-and-seek-body-painting-game-taking-over-steam/); [source](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/meccha-chameleon-hits-20m-sales-with-22k-daily-players)
- Liar's Bar (bluff/bet): 2 modes, 4 characters, about 113k peak, about 2.2M estimated sales. — [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar%27s_Bar); [source](https://raijin.gg/app/3097560/Liars_Bar)
- Among Us (deduce/vote/sabotage): 3 maps during the 2020 boom, about 438k Steam peak, 3.8M concurrent across platforms. — [source](https://among-us.fandom.com/wiki/Maps); [source](https://dotesports.com/news/among-us-hits-100-million-downloads-3-8-million-concurrent-players)
- Content Warning (collect plus a shared camera): "mostly developed in a single month", 204k peak, 6.2M free claims in 24 hours. — [source](https://www.yahoo.com/tech/mega-viral-co-op-horror-205435610.html); [source](https://pcgamer.com/games/horror/april-fools-phenom-content-warning-draws-in-more-than-204000-concurrent-players)
- PEAK (climb, rescue, carry): from a game jam, about 10M sold by mid-August 2025 at $7.99. — [source](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/from-game-jam-to-10-million-units-indie-hit-peak-success); [source](https://alineaanalytics.substack.com/p/peak-sold-18-million-copies-last)
- Human: Fall Flat (physics carry and push): one-man developer at launch, 55M sold. — [source](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/curve-games-human-fall-flat-has-sold-55-million-units)
- Gorilla Tag (tag): $100M+ revenue and 10M+ players. The team later grew past 100 people with updates every couple of weeks, so content grew after the hit. — [source](https://roadtovr.com/gorilla-tag-revenue-vr-success-another-axiom/)
- Lethal Company (collect plus a shared quota): solo developer, 100k+ concurrent within weeks. — [source](https://gamingbolt.com/lethal-company-is-the-latest-indie-game-to-cross-100000-concurrent-players)
- Valheim (build) with a 5-person team: an exception that used a procedural world in place of authored content. — [source](https://embed.gamereactor.eu/valheim-was-developed-by-only-five-people/)

**High content needed:**
- Dead by Daylight (asymmetric chase): about 42 killers and about 50 survivors, with new chapters through 2026. — [source](https://www.gfinityesports.com/misc/dead-by-daylight-best-killers-tier-list-dbd-dowsey/)
- Helldivers 2 (shoot PvE): players tired of the same content when the update pace slowed. Big updates and Warbonds drove spikes (e.g. 129k after "Heart of Democracy", 500k after the Xbox launch). — [source](https://game8.co/articles/latest/helldivers-2-escalation-of-freedom-update-doubles-player-count-after-downward-spiral); [source](https://gamingbolt.com/?p=620029)
- Deep Rock Galactic (shoot PvE): a free seasonal content model, 8M+ sold. — [source](https://80.lv/articles/deep-rock-galactic-sales-surpassed-5-5-million-copies/)
- Phasmophobia (seek and deduce against AI): 14 maps, about 27 ghosts (fan count), a yearly roadmap. — [source](https://gonintendo.com/contents/57161-phasmophobia-content-roadmap-for-2026-revealed)
- Mario Kart 8 Deluxe (race): 96 tracks. — [source](https://nintendolife.com/news/2024/02/mario-kart-8-deluxe-zooms-past-60-million-sales)
- Knockout City (throw at enemies): shut down, citing retention and "the amount of content required to sustain an audience". — [source](https://www.pcgamer.com/two-years-after-launch-live-service-dodgeball-game-knockout-city-is-shutting-down)

**Low content did not guarantee a hit (counter-examples):**
- ROUNDS (shoot other players, Landfall, 65+ cards): about 2.3k Steam peak despite about 95% positive reviews. — [source](https://steamdb.info/app/1557740/charts)
- LOCKDOWN Protocol (sabotage/betray): about 9.4k peak. — [source](https://steambase.io/apps/lockdown-protocol)

**Decay after the spike:**
- Liar's Bar: about 113k peak, now about 0.5k–1.2k online. — [source](https://steambase.io/apps/liars-bar)
- Chained Together: about 99% below peak. — [source](https://steambase.io/apps/chained-together)
- MECCHA CHAMELEON: about 340k (June) down to about 21k–25k (August). — [source](https://raijin.gg/app/4704690/MECCHA_CHAMELEON); [source](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/meccha-chameleon-hits-20m-sales-with-22k-daily-players)
- LOCKDOWN Protocol: about 9.4k down to the low hundreds. — [source](https://steambase.io/apps/lockdown-protocol)

### Inferences
- **The premise holds in part.** Verbs whose variety comes from other humans can reach a hit with tiny content. But the verb alone does not explain it. ROUNDS and LOCKDOWN Protocol used low-content human-counter verbs and stayed small. The big ones also had a funny, instantly readable moment for streamers and viewers (a roulette gun, body paint, a ragdoll fall, a proximity-voice scream). [opinion]
- **Low content buys the launch spike; something must hold the tail.** The tail-holders seen here: frequent updates after the hit (Gorilla Tag), player-made content (MECCHA Workshop maps, Trackmania), procedural worlds (Valheim, R.E.P.O. modular layouts), or accepting a short life and a cheap price (Liar's Bar, Chained Together). [opinion]
- **Practical rule for a cheap co-op game:** pick a core verb from the Low-content, High-coupling cluster (hide, mimic, bluff, deduce, vote, signal/describe, draw/guess, revive, symmetric tag or push). Add one shared physical object or shared wallet if the verb is environment-facing (carry, steer, collect). Avoid PvE shooting, asymmetric casts, building and racing unless there is a content plan (procedural or player-made). [opinion]
- **Sabotage and betray are low content but did not clearly produce a recent online hit on their own,** apart from inside Among Us's deduction loop. They may work better as a twist on a deduction verb than as the core. [opinion]

### Gaps
- No data on how long friend groups keep playing low-content games (retention curves per group). Concurrent-player decay is the only proxy found.
- No developer interview found that states MECCHA CHAMELEON's or Liar's Bar's exact content at launch (map and asset counts).
- No source compares how much each hit's success came from streamers versus its verb. The streamer role is reported for Liar's Bar and Gartic Phone but not measured. — [source](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/10/21/liars-bar-100k-ccu-steam-curve-animation-turkey-success); [source](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/what-is-gartic-phone-and-why-do-streamers-love-it-the-multiplayer-drawing-game-explained)

## Q3. Are there published verb taxonomies or interaction typologies to anchor the ratings?

### Takeaway
Yes, for the **coupling** and **co-op interaction** axes: Rocha et al. 2008, El-Nasr et al. 2010, Reuter et al. 2014, Zagal et al. 2006, plus Björk and Holopainen's general pattern library. None of them rates **content need**, so that axis has no published anchor. [opinion, based on sources below]

### Cited Findings
- **Björk & Holopainen, *Patterns in Game Design* (2004/2005):** first presented in 2003. Patterns are grouped into 11 major groups, including "social interaction". Gameplay is defined as player interaction with the game system *and with other players*. — [source](https://www.cp.eng.chula.ac.th/~vishnu/gameResearch/design/game-design-patterns.pdf); [source](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/9284). Their online pattern database held "almost 400 patterns" by 2014. McGee (2007) criticized it for lacking guidance on when to use each pattern. — [source](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/639/639)
- **Rocha, Mascarenhas & Prada (2008), "Game Mechanics for Cooperative Games":** six informal co-op patterns. These include giving players different abilities (complementarity), abilities that enhance each other (synergies), abilities usable only on other players, shared goals, and synergies between goals. — [source](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/639/639). The sixth, "special rules for players of the same team": [read]
- **El-Nasr et al. (CHI 2010), "Understanding and evaluating cooperative games":** added patterns including interacting with the same object, shared puzzles, shared characters, enemies that target separated players, automatic vocalization and limited (shared) resources. They also added *Cooperative Performance Metrics* for spotting co-op behavior in playtests. — [source](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/639/639). Sources conflict on the sample: Reuter et al. say they "analyzed 14 games" while a reading note says 215 games. — [source](https://kksgandhi.github.io/personal_site/Cooperative_Game_Design_Patterns-El-Nasr.html). The metric names (e.g. laughing together, helping, waiting for each other, getting in each other's way): [read]
- **Reuter, Wendel, Göbel & Steinmetz (DiGRA 2014), "Game Design Patterns for Collaborative Player Interactions":** 9 detailed patterns out of 20+ in their technical report. *General:* Concurrency (operate together what one cannot) and Parallelization (split up to speed up). *Gates:* Separation gate and Gathering gate. *Support:* Strengthening (buffs), Resupply (heal or ammo), Protector (prevent harm), Savior (remove a bad state, e.g. revive) and Sacrifice (help others at a cost to yourself). Each pattern is classified on these dimensions: **spatial** (collecting vs separating players; specific vs pervasive location), **temporal** (short, medium or long), **player** (voluntary vs obligatory, plus observed player experience) and **functional** (fixed vs free roles, number of players involved, communication and timing demands rated low/medium/high, genre). — [source](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/view/639/639); tech report listing — [source](https://www.kom.tu-darmstadt.de/publications/RGS14-1)
- **Zagal, Rick & Hsi (2006), "Collaborative games: Lessons learned from board games" (*Simulation & Gaming* 37(1)).** *Lessons:* (1) create tension between individual and team utility; (2) let players act without the team's consent; (3) players must be able to trace payoffs back to their decisions; (4) give players different abilities or responsibilities. *Pitfalls:* (1) one player deciding for the whole team, so give a real reason to collaborate; (2) players must care about the outcome; (3) the experience must differ each time and the challenge must evolve. — [source](https://grdphil.blogspot.com/2010/11/review-collaborative-games-lessons.html); [source](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:simgam:v:37:y:2006:i:1:p:24-40)
- **Bunker, Ryan & Carter (2017):** tested combining closely coupled co-op patterns (complementarity, interacting with the same object, limited resources). Combining them had no effect on player experience, except higher engagement for *same object + limited resources*. — [source](https://auc.edu.au/2017/11/combining-cooperative-design-patterns-to-improve-player-experience/)
- **Emmerich & Masuch (CHI PLAY 2017), "The Impact of Game Patterns on Player Experience and Social Interaction in Co-Located Multiplayer Games":** varied time pressure, control and player dependency in a 2-player puzzle game. Results were not retrieved. Also Emmerich, Masuch & Sykownik on shared-control patterns (ACE 2017). — [source](https://bibtex.github.io/CHI-PLAY-2017-EmmerichM.html); [source](https://www.uni-due.de/informatik/ecg/evaluating-social-interaction)
- **BoardGameGeek mechanism tags** (Hidden Roles, Traitor Game, Negotiation, Trading, Voting, Betting and Bluffing, Communication Limits, Deduction, Take That, Team-Based Game), and Engelstein & Shalev, *Building Blocks of Tabletop Game Design* (2019), a mechanism encyclopedia. [read]

### Inferences
**How the published work maps onto the five rating axes:** [opinion]
- **Acts on** has no direct published axis. The nearest are Rocha's "abilities usable only on other players" (target = teammate's body) and El-Nasr's "interacting with the same object" (target = a shared object).
- **Hidden info** maps onto BGG's Hidden Roles, Deduction, and Betting and Bluffing tags, and onto Keep Talking-style split information. The co-op pattern papers mostly ignore it.
- **Human counter** maps onto the plain competitive vs cooperative split. Zagal's lesson 1 (individual vs team tension) is the bridge for traitor and betray verbs.
- **Player coupling** is the best covered. Reuter's *obligatory vs voluntary* and *communication/timing demand* are the closest published measures. Concurrency (carry together) is obligatory, high timing. Parallelization (gather apart) is voluntary, low on both, which matches the Low coupling rating for solo-style gathering. Bunker et al. found *same object + limited resources* is the pairing that moved engagement, which is the R.E.P.O. and Lethal Company recipe (shared fragile loot plus a shared wallet).
- **Content need** has no published anchor. Zagal's pitfall 3 ("the experience needs to be different each time") is the closest. It names the problem (replay variety) but not the cost of solving it with authored content versus human counters.

The catalog's main claim, that human-counter verbs replace authored content, is therefore Claude's synthesis from game evidence (Q2), not a published result.

### Gaps
- Could not open the full Rocha 2008 paper, the El-Nasr 2010 paper (for full pattern and metric names), Reuter's 20+ pattern tech report (FTP link), or the Emmerich & Masuch results.
- Did not check Manninen's (2003) "interaction forms" typology or Stenros et al. on sociability, which may hold a verb-level typology of player-to-player interaction.
- No published taxonomy of multiplayer *verbs* (as opposed to patterns or mechanisms) was found. Anna Anthropy & Naomi Clark's "verbs" framing (*A Game Design Vocabulary*) exists [read] but is single-player-focused as far as I know.
