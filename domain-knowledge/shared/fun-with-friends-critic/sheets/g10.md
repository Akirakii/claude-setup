# Proposal for the critic: Hot Bags

**Fantasy:** A crew of cat couriers on a dead, poisonous planet finds and cooks food for alien customers, and wants to earn enough each day to keep the last delivery company open.
One-player test: The line still works with one player, but nobody can stay at the base to read orders out, and reviewers call solo play very hard.

**Players:** 1–7, one crew (the store page recommends at least 2). **A session:** one round is one timed day; the length of a day is (not found); how many days a sitting holds is (not found).

**The rules, one sentence each** (every rule a player must know; as many as the game really has, 8–14)
The crew's base is a train that holds an order terminal, a shop, a cash machine, camera monitors, a fridge, a cooking table and a radar of active orders.
Alien customers place food orders with a calorie demand, and the orders are read at the terminal on the train.
Each day has a time limit and a money target set by humans, and the target rises over time.
The map outside is generated fresh each day, with poor visibility and harmful air.
Food and ingredients are found in rubbish bags and containers, or taken from creatures, such as an egg from a nest.
A player holds one item in each hand.
Some food must be brought back to the train and combined at the cooking table before it can be delivered.
Finished food is carried to the customer's delivery point on the map.
Delivering the wrong food makes the customer attack the courier.
Hostile creatures and hazards roam the map, appear suddenly, and often kill in one hit.
Digging in radioactive waste kills the player.
Junk can be sold at the train shop, and the shop sells gear such as batteries and a flashlight.
Tools can be combined into makeshift weapons and traps.
If the whole crew dies, the run is over and a new room must be made from the main menu; whether a dead player can be brought back is (not found).

**The choices** (3–5 lines a player really faces, "do X, or do Y?")
Stay on the train to read orders and watch the cameras, or go out and carry food?
Deliver the food in hand that might be wrong, or walk back to check the order?
Take two orders at once, or finish one before starting the next?
Go back to the train to cook, or look for food that needs no cooking?
Sell the find as junk for gear, or keep it for an order?

**Scene, 4 friends** (5–7 bullets, one real moment the game is known for, second by second)
- One player stays at the terminal and reads two orders over the radio.
- Three players spread out into the haze and open rubbish bags.
- One courier returns to the train with an ingredient in each hand and cooks at the table.
- A second courier, who heard only half of the order, hands a customer the wrong food.
- The customer attacks and the courier runs for the train.
- A large creature is standing on the only path between the map and the train.
- The day timer runs low and the last courier delivers whatever they are holding.

**What experienced crews do:** Players report that a group of four keeps one player on the train to relay order details to the others.
