# Proposal for the critic: Deep Disposal

**Fantasy:** A crew hired by a drug company steers a windowless submarine to sunken labs and removes the bodies and records of failed experiments before the sea police find them.
One-player test: The line still works with one player, but one person must swim between the engine, the wheel and the sonar alone.

**Players:** 1–8, one crew. **A session:** one dive at a site lasts about 7–8 minutes with an alarm at 2 minutes left; a run is a chain of dives with a submarine trip between each, and it lasts until the wanted level is full (dives per sitting not found).

**Feelings the game is known for**
- "I was reading the sonar out loud and nobody was turning the wheel."
- "We heard something outside the hull and we had no window to look."
- "The alarm went and I still had a body in each hand."
- "He died in there, so we went back for him because he was worth money."

**The rules, one sentence each**
- The crew picks a difficulty and a crew size at the start, and the crew size sets which of three submarines it gets.
- The submarine has no view outside, so one player reads a sonar screen, one steers, and one sets engine speed; one player can do several jobs with more effort.
- The sonar shows dive sites, unknown marks and creatures as labelled blips, and a second station lets a player match a blip's wave to learn or change what it is.
- Running the engine fast makes noise that draws the sea police, and hitting a giant sea creature on the way costs credits.
- The submarine has three hearts, and a hit opens a leak that a player fixes with a wrench.
- At a site the crew swims out through an airlock into a generated wreck and has a fixed time before it must leave.
- Each dive asks for a required amount of evidence: body parts, experiments, data screens to smash, and batteries.
- A player carries two items unless they bring a cart, and only items with a price tag are worth anything.
- Air runs down outside, and air and health refill only inside the submarine.
- Body parts brought back go into a shredder on the submarine, which pays credits.
- A dead player cannot return until the dive ends, and their body counts as evidence that must be brought back or the crew is penalised.
- Falling short on evidence, leaving bodies, or overstaying the timer raises a wanted level shown in stars, and police then appear on the map and at sites.
- Six stars ends the run with the police boarding the submarine.
- Credits buy tools and submarine modules, batteries upgrade the submarine to go deeper, and going deeper is the crew's choice and raises the danger and the pay.

**How much there is:** 3 submarine sizes; at least 3 site kinds named (sewers, labs, ruins), all with generated layouts, plus special sites (count not found); creature kinds (count not found), described as small thieves, shooting robots, mutants that chase, one rare stalker that kills in one hit, and giant creatures on the travel map; about 7 tools named (cart, scooter, see-through vision, body-part detector, grappling hook, bigger net, a gadget that flies toward the wreck); submarine modules (count not found); unlockable diving suits (count not found).

**Voice:** Voice is heard by distance, and the crew uses it to pass sonar readings to the pilot; the store page states a feature that copies players' voices, but what uses it was (not found).

**The choices**
- Run the engine fast to reach the site sooner, or slow and silent to stay off the police's attention?
- Stay for one more body after the alarm, or leave on time?
- Go back for a dead friend's body, or leave it and take the penalty?
- Swim back to the submarine for air now, or push on to the next room?
- Stay at this depth, or go deeper for more pay?

**What the other end decides in the same second**
- Fast or silent: the sonar reader decides which heading and speed to call, and the pilot decides whether to follow it.
- One more body: nothing found for a player left in the submarine.
- Dead friend's body: nothing.
- Air now or push on: nothing.
- Deeper or stay: nothing.

**Scene, 4 friends**
- One player calls sonar blips by letter and number while a second turns the wheel and a third holds the throttle.
- A blip closes in, the sonar player shouts to slow down, and the engine goes silent while something is heard outside the hull.
- The submarine reaches the site and all four swim out; two take the cart into the wreck.
- A fast mutant chases one player through a narrow corridor, and that player leads it away from the others.
- The two-minute alarm sounds while the cart is half full and one player is low on air.
- One player dies near the exit, and another drops a body part to drag the dead friend back instead.
- Inside, the crew feeds the parts and the friend's body to the shredder and watches the star count.

**What experienced crews do:** The sonar reader calls turns and speed to the pilot and learns to tell blips apart by their signal without the reference chart; the crew runs the engine silent near police, leaves each site before the timer ends, makes frequent trips back to the submarine for air and health, has one player bait an aggressive creature away from the carriers, and buys snacks from a vending machine to bribe the police and lower the stars.
**What changes it from round to round:** The generated wreck layout, the site kind, what the sonar shows on the way, which creatures appear, and the current wanted level.
**What carries from one round to the next:** Within a run, credits, tools, modules, submarine upgrades and the wanted level carry from dive to dive; after the police end a run, progress restarts from scratch according to one review, another player says upgrades are kept, and unlocked diving suits stay.
**Can a new crew lose? Does it get harder?** Yes, one reviewer's crew was near a full wanted level after two dives; each star makes the police more active, and going deeper raises the danger, but whether the required evidence amount grows by itself was (not found).
