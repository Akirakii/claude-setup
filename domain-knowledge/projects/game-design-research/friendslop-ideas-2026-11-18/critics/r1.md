## i03 Moonsick

### 0. Novelty
Core act: pick flowers in moonlight that grows your fur until you turn into a wolf that can't stop running, which two friends must grab from the side and cure.
On the ladder: no sheet has it. Nearest in shape: g06 (one creature hunts while the crew works) and g03 (a bar fills, then friends must come for you).
Web (2 searches): no shipped game with a turned player who stays on the crew's side, can only steer, and is caught by hand.
Nearest finds: "Wolfsbane" (unreleased; the crew does tasks to turn a cursed player back to human), "Wolfsbane Manor" (planned late 2026; a possessed player hunts the others), "Cursed Deal" (a cursed friend becomes a creature that comes after you).
Mark: **near Wolfsbane (unreleased): different because here the wolf is still a teammate who steers to be caught, and the cure is a physical two-person hold, not a task list.**

### 1. Seven traits
1. **Partly.** Rule: "whatever it hits head-on it bites" and "two friends who grab from the side or behind hold it" make wolf steering and friend position one shared second. But choices 1, 2 and 4 (take the bloom, run out under a thinning cloud, fill the second hand) change nothing for a teammate in that second, and before anyone turns the picking is side-by-side solo work.
2. **Yes.** Rule: "you are the wolf and you play it ... you can only steer". The failed player becomes the busiest player. A bitten friend drops the load and keeps playing with half fur.
3. **No.** No rule.
4. **Partly.** The "one more flower" call at three-quarters fur repeats (rule: moonlight grows fur), and the crew shares the loss (rules: trampled beds, bites, a flask lowers the cauldron). But one player makes the call alone, and there is no "leave now": the night ends at dawn whatever the crew does.
5. **No.** Rule "at half fur you see the rare silver blooms" gives one player sight the others lack, but the one who sees is the one who picks, so nobody else needs it.
6. **Partly.** Rules: clouds open without warning, blooms move, half fur carries over. One mansion, one flower, no creatures, so only timing and placement differ.
7. **Yes.** Rule: "the mark rises every night; a missed mark ends the run".

### 2. Closest two sheets
**g06** (one hunter, crew does the work, someone draws it off) and **g03** (a personal bar fills and the friends must physically come to you).
Has that they lack: the hunter is a friend who is trying to help; the failure state is the most active role in the game; a rising target (g06 has none).
Lacks that they have: g06's microphone rule; g03's pull/boost acts that tie players together in normal play, before anything goes wrong; any generated layout or creature.

### 3. Fun with friends? **Maybe**, top of tier C
The wolf minute is as loud as anything on the ladder, but a crew that wants to win makes it rare and then tame, and the rest of the night is quiet solo picking with a meter.
The plan a crew settles into: pick to just over half fur at the shade's edge, step back, wait for fur to drop; when someone does turn, the wolf steers into a shaded room and laps it until grabbed.
Feelings in that plan: "one more flower" survives once the mark outgrows safe picking; "MOVE, I can't stop" lasts a few seconds until the wolf reaches its lap; "Sorry, I tried to turn" mostly dies, because a wolf who wants to help can avoid everyone.
Sits between **g05 and g06**.

### 4. Holes
1. **The wolf is on the crew's side, so it tames itself.** Best move: steer out of the moonlight (rule: only light makes it grow and break doors) and run a small loop in a shaded hall, passing the holders side-on. No beds trampled, no bites. Tier A: none has this; their dangers are not played by a teammate.
2. **With three players a wolf can't be cured as written.** The hold needs two friends with both hands on; a flask takes a hand; the "other end" line says a holder lets go a hand to pour, which the hold rule says ends the hold. Wolf plus two holders leaves nobody to pour. Tier A: no.
3. **Not curing is cheap.** A wolf left lapping a shaded room costs no flask (a flask lowers the cauldron) and no holders' time; the only price is half fur next night. Best move late in a night: ignore the wolf. That wolf's player then runs circles alone till dawn. Tier A: g01 has the same wait (a dead player watches until the day ends), so the wait itself is not held against it; the cheapness is.
4. **Nothing interacts until someone fails.** With a low mark no one needs to pass half fur, so early nights have no danger at all (no creature, no trap). Tier A: no; g01 and g02 have creatures from the first level.
5. **Waiting in shade for fur to shrink "slowly" is dead time inside the best plan.** Tier A: g03 has the same (rest on a ledge to refill stamina). Not held against it.

### 5. Could not break
The turn itself: a cloud opening "without warning" can fill a careful player's fur, so no plan removes the wolf entirely.
The side-grab: the wolf's steering and the holders' position are one act, and the wolf's own voice ("on your left") is part of the catch.
The rising mark forcing pickers past half fur and deep into the light.

### 6. Smallest change (opinion)
Give the crew a reason to want a wolf: "only a wolf can break into the locked rooms, where the richest blooms are."
Then turning is a plan with a price, not just a mistake, and the catch happens every night.
Second, fix the count: a held wolf stays held for 3 seconds after one hand lets go.

## i01 Piggyback

### 0. Novelty
Core act: carry a sick friend on your back who alone sees the valuable herbs and points the way, while their fever passes into you.
On the ladder: no sheet has it. g03 carries a passed-out friend and g02 carries a head, but neither carried friend is the one who sees.
Web (2 searches): no co-op game found with a rider who sees and a carrier who walks, or with sickness that gives sight.
Nearest in shape: "Backseat Drivers" (one drives, one shouts directions; whether the driver is blind is unknown from the snippet), "Carry the Patient Together" (two carry a stretcher).
Mark: **new** (nearest shape: navigator-and-driver games; different because the seer is a physical load that makes the carrier sick).

### 1. Seven traits
1. **Yes.** Rule: "the rider sees and points with an arm, and the carrier walks and picks with both hands". Four of the five choices land on the other half of the pair at once.
2. **Partly.** Rule: stage 3 "can't walk at all" turns the sickest friend into the rider, which is a job. But the real failures are stage 4 ("a body to be dragged", with nothing written that they can see or do) and stuck in mud (wait for a pull).
3. **No.** No rule.
4. **Partly.** "One more bloom at 25 seconds" repeats (rule: 30 seconds of carrying raises the carrier a stage) and the quota is shared. But no rule destroys the basket, and there is no "leave now": the night ends at dawn.
5. **Yes.** Rule: stage 0 sees nothing, stage 1 red, stage 2 blue, stage 3 silver. The friend who can see the best herbs can't walk to them.
6. **Partly.** Rule: "herbs bloom in new places every night, and the mud moves with them", plus carried stages. One marsh, no creatures.
7. **Yes.** Rule: "a quota that rises every night, and a missed quota ends the run".

### 2. Closest two sheets
**g03** (afflictions eat the bar, food removes them, a downed friend is carried) and **g01** (one player holds what the others can't see and directs them, under a rising quota).
Has that they lack: the seer and the mover are one physical pair, and helping is what makes the helper sick.
Lacks that they have: any sudden danger (g01's creatures, g03's fall); g01's voice rules; g01's loss of the haul on a wipe; generated terrain.

### 3. Fun with friends? **Maybe**, middle of tier C
The pointing pair is real and can't be played around, but every danger runs on a 30-second or 3-minute clock, so there is planning and no panic, and the fever's cost can be eaten away for almost nothing.
The plan a crew settles into: one rider kept at stage 3, backs that set the rider down before 30 seconds, red moss eaten whenever a stage is gained, mud walked round.
Feelings in that plan: "Left! MY left" survives (it is the only way to silver); "you're coughing on my neck" and "we're both down, come and drag us" die, because nobody takes a stage they can undo for one red bundle.
Sits between **g06 and g07**.

### 4. Holes
1. **Eat red, sell silver.** Rule: a bundle lowers fever one stage; red is worth 1, silver 9. Best move: every stage gained by carrying is erased with the cheapest herb, which the stage-1 carrier can see and pick on the way. The fever stops being a price. Tier A: g03 has food that removes hunger, but food there is scarce and also the stamina reserve; not the same hole.
2. **The carry clock.** "Every 30 seconds of carrying" doesn't say whether setting the rider down resets it. If it resets, one carrier puts the rider down for an instant at 29 seconds and never sickens. If it doesn't, the sheet's own "25-second turns" does nothing. Tier A: no.
3. **No rule says how the first fever is caught.** Everyone at stage 0 sees no herbs and has no one to catch it from. The game can't start as written. Tier A: no.
4. **Mud is a trap only for a crew in a hurry** (nothing says it is hidden). Best move: walk round. With hole 1 removing the hurry, the stuck-pair scene doesn't happen. Tier A: g01's mines and turrets are also avoidable by a careful crew with a ship player; shared, not held against it.
5. **The clean basket-carrier's night is walking behind the pair.** Their two listed jobs (pull from mud, drag the fallen) only exist after a failure the plan avoids. Tier A: g01's ship player is also apart from the action but holds the map; not the same.

### 5. Could not break
Silver needs a stage-3 friend and a pair of legs: no solo route to the rich herbs, so the rider-and-carrier pair exists in every plan.
The carrier at stage 1 starting to see red moss everywhere and wanting to stop, while the rider wants home: a real disagreement made by a rule.
Fever carried to the next night, which makes the end-of-night "eat the surplus or bank it" call real.

### 6. Smallest change (opinion)
Make the sight run both ways: "only a friend at stage 0 can see the mud."
Then the sick see the herbs, the clean see the ground, and a carrier who catches stage 1 goes blind to the mud under the rider.
Alongside it, close hole 1: a bundle only cures if it is the herb of your own stage's colour.

## i04 Hold On

### 0. Novelty
Core act: hang as a chain of friends from each other's ankles down a well and pass treasure up before someone's grip gives.
On the ladder: not the core act of any sheet, but near **g05** (bodies joined, one failure takes everyone below) and g03 (stamina that weight eats, friends holding friends).
Web (2 searches): the results are tethered climbers: "Chained Together" (climb chained at the waist), "Tied Up", "Dont Pull Me" (planned 2026), "FLING UP TOGETHER"; a "Heave Ho" link came back with no description.
None found where the players' own grip is the chain and the goal is loot under a quota.
Mark: **near g05: different because the link is a grip that drains with weight (so it breaks by a choice somebody made), the crew goes down for loot, and a quota rises.**

### 1. Seven traits
1. **Yes.** Rules: grip "drains faster the more weight hangs below"; "they and everyone below them fall"; a kick "swings everyone below". Every listed choice lands on another body at once.
2. **Partly.** Rule: the fallen get "30 seconds on the side ladder". The fall is shared and loud, but the fallen are not carried or caught; they climb alone.
3. **No.** No rule.
4. **Yes.** Rule: each piece taken is weight on every friend above, and a fall loses what was held. The bottom friend's "one more piece, or call up" repeats every dip and the whole chain pays.
5. **Partly.** The sheet has friends "say their grip out loud", but no rule says a grip is hidden from the others.
6. **Partly.** Rule: niches refilled each night, water height, the crab's pick. One well, one creature.
7. **Yes.** Rule: "the quota rises every night, and a missed quota ends the run".

### 2. Closest two sheets
**g05** (joined bodies, one mistake drops all) and **g03** (stamina eaten by weight, friends as each other's handholds).
Has that they lack: greed is the thing that breaks the chain (g05's chain never tires; g03 has no haul); a rising quota; a fresh layout of loot each night.
Lacks that they have: g05's size of loss (an hour of height against 30 seconds here); g03's carrying and reviving of the fallen, its voice by distance, its six stages of different ground.

### 3. Fun with friends? **Yes**, bottom of tier B
It has what g05 has, a body-to-body link where one friend's act drops the rest in the same second, and the best plan is itself the shouting: grip numbers called up the chain against a bottom friend who wants one more.
The plan a crew settles into: short dips, lightest friend at the bottom, a called grip number that means "up", light pieces first.
Feelings in that plan: "drop the cup" and "who let go?" survive, because the rising quota forces heavier pieces and deeper chains than the safe number allows; "kick it off my hands" survives as long as the crab comes.
Sits between **g05 and g06**.
This "yes" rests on hole 1 being closed; as written, hole 1 removes the chain.

### 4. Holes
1. **The water and the ladder reach the niches.** Falling costs nothing but 30 seconds, the water "rises through the night", and a side ladder runs from the water to the rim. Nothing written stops a friend from jumping in, taking treasure from the niches at the waterline, and climbing the ladder with it. Best move: swim, never hang. Tier A: no.
2. **Ledges end the long chain** if they are common: grip refills on any ledge and "any ledge can become the new top". Best move: hop ledge to ledge in chains of two. Ledge spacing is not written. Tier A: g03 has the same (crews plan routes around ledges where stamina refills). Not held against it.
3. **The middle links only hold.** Pass or pocket is their one decision, and with no personal score nobody has a reason to pocket. Tier A: no; g05 (tier B) has the same.
4. **A listed choice is empty:** the top friend "lets go on purpose to save the treasure already on the rim", but treasure on the rim is not at risk. Tier A: no.
5. **Cheap falls.** With only 30 seconds and the held pieces lost, a crew can treat falling as the fast way down and the fall stops being feared. Tier A: no; g05's beginner mode has it.

### 5. Could not break
The weight rule: inside the chain there is no way to take treasure without spending a friend's grip, so the bottom friend's greed always costs someone else.
The cascade: a grip that fails takes everyone below, at once, with a named cause.
The crab kick: helping the friend above costs the friend below 3 seconds, a three-body trade no plan removes.

### 6. Smallest change (opinion)
Close hole 1 and turn the fall into a save in one rule: "a hanging friend can catch a falling friend by the wrist as they pass; caught friends hang as new weight; whoever reaches the water is out for 30 seconds and can't take treasure from it."
That gives the failed player a rescue (trait 2) and the catcher a new load on a grip already low.

## i02 Gold Fever

### 0. Novelty
Core act: turn junk to gold by touch while gold climbs your body, and get rid of your gold by gripping a friend so it flows into the part they offer.
On the ladder: no sheet has it. Nearest: g02 (hauling valuables to a scale-like drop-off for a target) and g03 (a body bar that fills until friends must carry you).
Web (2 searches): nothing found. A Midas idle clicker with no co-op; "Cursed Companions" (a curse run by speech); no co-op game with a curse passed by grip or creeping limb by limb.
Mark: **new.**

### 1. Seven traits
1. **Yes.** Rule: "you grip one part of a friend for 2 seconds ... and the friend can pull away". The gripped friend picks the part or escapes in those 2 seconds. Three of five choices land on a friend at once.
2. **Partly.** Rule: a statue "can only hop slowly" and is carried to the scale or the cabin, so it stays in the scene. But rule: "a sold friend sits out the next night", which is a whole round of silent waiting.
3. **No.** No rule.
4. **Partly.** "Touch one more crate" repeats, and more haul floods the hold for everyone. But gold on the scale is never lost, dawn clears every body, and there is no "leave now".
5. **No.** Every body's gold is in plain sight. No rule hides anything.
6. **Partly.** Rule: a different mix of crates, barrels and chests each night. One ship, no creatures.
7. **Yes.** Rule: "the quota rises every night, and a missed quota ends the run".

### 2. Closest two sheets
**g02** (haul valuables to a counted drop-off, two-carrier items, the haul itself is the danger) and **g03** (afflictions take the body bit by bit; friends carry whoever is finished).
Has that they lack: the hazard is handed from friend to friend by choice, part by part; a friend can be sold.
Lacks that they have: g02's creatures, voice heard by them, and loot that loses value when handled badly; g03's shared route and revival at the stage end; any loss of what is already banked.

### 3. Fun with friends? **Maybe**, bottom of tier C
The grip-and-pull-away is funny on a first night, but one plan switches the whole design off: one friend takes everybody's gold, and then nothing in the rules touches the others.
The plan a crew settles into: one friend stands in the cabin as the dump; the toucher walks over after every crate or two and grips them; the carriers never hold gold.
Feelings in that plan: "it's at my neck" and "not my hand, put it in my leg" die (gold never passes a wrist, and the dump needs no limbs); "sell Ben" becomes a routine on short nights and Ben sits out a round; "hopping away from the scale" survives.
Sits between **g08 and g09**.

### 4. Holes
1. **The dump.** All of a friend's gold flows into one part of another (scene: Ana's whole arm becomes Ben's one leg), a statue in the cabin "is safe", and dawn clears everything. Best move: one friend takes it all and stands still all night. Whether a statue can take more gold is not written; if it can, the dump is bottomless. Tier A: no.
2. **The flood only bites a golden leg.** Rule: stuck in water needs "a golden leg". With the dump, no worker has one, so the rising water does nothing. Tier A: no.
3. **Being a statue costs nothing after the fact.** No carry-over, no penalty, safe in the cabin. Best move near dawn: touch until you are a statue. Tier A: no; g01 fines the crew per death.
4. **Selling a friend benches them for a round.** A quarter of a quota for one friend's night is a trade a short crew will take, and the price is paid by a friend doing nothing for ten minutes. Tier A: g01 has the same kind of wait (dead until the day ends), though not a whole round chosen by teammates.
5. **Touching and carrying don't need the same friend.** Nothing ties the toucher to the carrier, so the crew splits into one who touches and others who haul, and the "golden hand ends my carrying" choice never comes up. Tier A: g01 has a similar split (ship player and haulers), where the split itself is the fun because of the map; here the split removes the interaction.

### 5. Could not break
At a high enough quota one dump is not enough (alone, a body is a statue "after a few crates"), so gold has to sit on working bodies and the part-by-part choices come back. How soon is not written.
The statue's own hop against two carriers deciding scale or cabin: a real three-way moment when the crew is short.
Two-carrier chests needing four working hands on a crew that is losing hands.

### 6. Smallest change (opinion)
One rule against the dump: "a grip moves one part's worth of gold, a part that is already gold can't take more, and a statue takes none."
Then gold has to be spread across working bodies, which is the choice the sheet is built on.
Second, give the statue a cost: a friend who is a statue at dawn starts the next night with one golden hand.
