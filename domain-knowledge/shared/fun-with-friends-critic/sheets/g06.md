# Proposal for the critic: Siren Ward

**Fantasy:** A group of urban explorers is locked inside an abandoned building with a creature that hears them, and they want to find the parts for a way out before it finds them.
One-player test: The line still works alone, since one player can search, hide and escape, but nobody is left to draw the creature away.

**Players:** 1–5, one crew. **A session:** one run is one escape attempt on one map; length of a run and how many fit a sitting (not found).

**Feelings the game is known for**
- "I laughed into my mic and it came straight for us."
- "I was under a table holding my breath while it walked past."
- "I had to saw the planks knowing the noise would bring it."

**The rules, one sentence each**
- The crew picks a map and must leave through one of its two exits.
- Each exit needs its own set of items, found around the building and used at the right spots.
- Some steps are small puzzles, such as a safe code, a light sequence or a breaker panel.
- One creature roams the map and kills players it catches.
- The creature hears footsteps and also hears the player's real voice through the microphone.
- Some work is loud, such as sawing planks, repairing a lift, prying with a crowbar or fixing a car, and it draws the creature.
- Each map's creature senses differently: one is blind and hunts by sound, one is slow but sees far, one is very aggressive.
- Which exit the crew works on changes which creature they face on that map (one guide).
- Players hide in cabinets, under tables, in toilet stalls or in shadows, and crouching before reaching cover keeps the creature from knowing the spot.
- A thrown brick or glass stuns the creature, and thrown objects also pull it toward the noise.
- First aid kits heal a hurt player.
- Death is permanent for the run (what a dead player can still do: not found).
- Money and documents lie around the maps; money buys new maps and costumes, documents count toward achievements.
- A difficulty setting changes how easily the creature notices players and which items and doors appear (the list of settings: not found).

**How much there is:** about six hand-made maps (school, hospital, asylum, submarine, military base, snowed-in building; one guide lists seven); one creature per map, varying by exit; two exits per map with roughly three to six key items each; a few counter items (brick, glass, fire extinguisher, first aid kit); costumes; no upgrades found.

**Voice:** The creature reacts to the player's microphone; whether voice between players is by distance (not found).

**The choices**
- Say where the key is out loud, or stay silent and lose the information?
- Do the loud repair now, or wait until the creature is far away?
- Work toward the first exit, or the second one with different items and a different creature?
- Stay together, or send one player to draw the creature while the rest collect items?
- Throw the brick now to stun it, or keep it for a later chase?

**What the other end decides in the same second**
- Speak or stay silent: the listener decides whether to answer, which makes more noise.
- Loud repair: the others decide whether to stand guard, hide, or make noise elsewhere.
- Which exit: nothing.
- Stay or split: the player drawing the creature decides which floor to lead it to and when to double back.
- Brick: nothing.

**Scene, 3 friends**
- Three players creep through a school corridor with two of the three planks needed for a ladder.
- One player whispers that the saw is in the workshop, and the others stop talking.
- At the workshop table one player starts cutting the planks, which is loud.
- The creature's noise comes from the floor below and gets closer.
- A second player runs up the stairs making noise, and the creature follows that player.
- The second player crouches into a toilet stall at the far end and goes quiet.
- The first player finishes the ladder, the third unlocks the emergency door, and they call the second player back only once the creature has moved on.

**What experienced crews do:** In pairs, one player leads the creature to another floor or the attic and keeps it there while the other collects exit items; crews plan a route to run before any loud action, check walls and closed rooms early for the randomly placed key box, and carry bricks and first aid kits.
**What changes it from round to round:** Which exit the crew goes for, the spots of some items (a key box, a safe, loose items in boxes), and the difficulty setting.
**What carries from one round to the next:** Money, unlocked maps, costumes, collected documents and a player level (how the level is gained: not found).
**Can a new crew lose? Does it get harder?** Yes, by every player dying; it gets harder only through the chosen difficulty setting and the choice of map, not by itself over runs (assumed from the guides, no scaling rule found).
