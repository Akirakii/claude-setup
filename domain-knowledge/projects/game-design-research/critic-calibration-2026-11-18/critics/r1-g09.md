# Critic round 1: g09, Dome Salvage

## The problem underneath
- The fantasy does not need a friend: the proposal's own one-player test says one player with the vacuum carries a crew's worth.
- Three of the four feelings are caused by written rules (light charge, one-hit kill, dead players wait).
- All three happen to one player alone; only "we fed him to the thing in the hole" needs a second person.
- The third feeling is boredom, and the rules cause it on purpose: a dead player has nothing to do until the visit ends.

## The scene, second by second
- Dock and walk in the dark: four players do the same search side by side, and nobody holds anything another needs.
- Split: the timer is the only reason, and each player decides it for themselves.
- Morse door: one player meets it alone; where the pattern comes from is not written, so no second player is needed as written.
- Light runs out: what the dark costs is not written, and no friend's act fixes it.
- The robot kills in one hit: no warning, no counter and no friend's act is written, and the weapons are called weak.
- The dead player watches; no job, no way back before the visit ends.
- The body goes to the hole: the first and only act on a friend, and it is the same act as carrying loot.

## Fewest and most players
- One player: the same game with the vacuum, and without the wait after death; what a lone death does to the visit is not written.
- Four players: four solo runs under one timer; whether the calorie target grows with crew size is not written, so three more friends are three more pairs of hands.

## The plans
- Always wins: everyone takes a vacuum light, splits, stays on the easier dome, and leaves before the timer.
- By the fifth session: the same split, a fixed route (entrance, loot, out), and one remembered answer per creature kind.
- A perfect crew's round: no light runs out, nobody is touched, nobody waits, nobody is fed to the hole; none of the four feelings happens.

## Findings
- SEVERE, only this design, and a missing baseline part: no living friend does anything for or with another living friend, so there is no help to price.
- SEVERE, only this design: one-hit kills plus no revive plus no job for the dead put a player out for the rest of a visit, and the feelings list names that wait.
- MEDIUM, only this design, a missing baseline part: every written threat has the same answer (don't get touched), so nothing forces a pair to cover two things at once; creature behaviour is unwritten, so this stays medium.
- MEDIUM, only this design: more players only add carrying hands to one calorie sum (a pooled sum, not a coupling).
- MEDIUM, only this design: a body is calories and the dead rejoin free, so a death near the submarine may be the cheapest food; the body's calorie value is unwritten, which is a fail, not a pass.
- MEDIUM, only this design: a death with no written warning or counter is blame from luck, not from a player's choice.
- MEDIUM, only this design: harder domes pay in gear that is lost on death, where death takes one hit, so the easier dome wins unless armour stops a hit (unwritten).
- The baseline has this too (row "Bought tools lower the need for friends"): the vacuum light.
- The baseline has this too (row "A choice is one sum for the whole crew, with no friend in it"): search on or go back, harder or easier dome, body or loot.
- The baseline has this too (row "The crew settles into fixed roles with a card of answers, and the fear only happens in mistakes"): the fifth-session plan and the perfect round above.
- Not counted, facts not found: whether the calorie target grows, what a missed deadline does, whether voice fades by distance.

## The four baseline parts
- Priced help that happens on good nights: missing; the only act on a friend is carrying their corpse, which helps the calorie count and not the friend.
- A pressure that rises and breaks last night's plan: missing as written; a calorie deadline exists, its growth was not found, and depth is "slightly harder versions"; not counted as a finding.
- Two threats at once: missing; 15 creature kinds are named and no rule makes two of them need different answers at the same moment.
- Something carries over: present; calories toward the deadline, depth reached, and gear until death change the next visit.

## Co-op check
- 1 Coupled: fail; no action fails without another player's.
- 2 Split: fail; players are apart, but no information or role is split between them.
- 3 Talk: fail; the timer makes the crew split, and no rule needs a word said.
- 4 Blame: pass; one player's death is a headless body the crew carries and feeds to the hole.
- 5 Not easy: pass; new crews die in one hit in nearly every dome kind, though the rise is slight.
- 6 Not solo: fail; the proposal says one player with the vacuum is enough.
- 7 Two-way: fail; the proposal's own table says "nothing" for three choices, and the fourth is each player choosing alone.
- Lone survivor: can still finish, as the solo game shows; dying on purpose may pay (the body finding).

## What I could not break
- The one-hit kill causes its feeling plainly, and the result is visible on the body.
- The draining light against a hidden entrance is a real push-or-go-back moment, even though it is a solo one.
- Feeding a dead friend to the hole makes a loss a joke and an input to the goal at once.
- The goal fits in one line: feed the thing in the hole before the deadline.
- Climate, generated layout and depth change a round without new hand-made content.
