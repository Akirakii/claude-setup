# Critic r1 on g01 (Night Clearance)

Judged from the proposal text only; "(not found)" facts were left out of every count.

## The problem underneath
- The fantasy does not need a friend: the proposal's own one-player test says the line still works alone.
- The core job (walk in, pick up scrap, walk out) never fails for lack of a second player; friends only make it safer.
- The four feelings do come from written rules: voice by distance, the dead going silent, the ship map, the rising target.
- Three of the four feelings need a friend's death or near-death, so they are caused by mistakes or ambushes, not by a choice.

## The scene, second by second
- Second 0: the ship player sees a dot and speaks; the inside player only obeys or doubts (one end holds all the facts).
- Next: the warned player walks backwards staring; that end only stands and looks.
- Next: a thing drops on the second player's head; the third hears it faintly, and no written rule lets the third do anything about it.
- Next: the ship player opens a door; the two inside walk through it.
- At the ramp: the blind beast makes silence the answer, so the last beat is nobody acting on anybody.
- Lever: one player decides, then the crew adds up a sum.

## Rounds at the fewest and the most
- 1 player: no map seat, no doors, no radio partner, nobody to hear a scream; three of four feelings cannot happen.
- 1 player: every death is a whole-crew death, so all stored scrap is lost each time.
- 2 players (assumed): a ship seat halves the carriers, so both go in and the map rules go unused.
- 4 players: one sits, three carry side by side, each filling their own four slots; the carrying is parallel work.
- 4 players: after two deaths, two people watch with nothing to do but a vote.

## Always-win plan, fifth session, perfect crew
- Always-win: one on the ship calling everything, pile scrap at the entrance, leave by mid-afternoon, sell on the last day only.
- Fifth session: the proposal's own "experienced crews" line is that plan, with fixed roles and a fixed shopping list.
- Perfect crew: nobody dies, nobody screams, no dot stops, and nobody goes back for one more load.
- The rising target is the one written thing that forces that crew to stay later and break the plan.

## Findings
1. SEVERE, only this design: the fantasy and core job do not need a friend, and one player can play and meet a target (co-op 1 and 6 fail).
2. SEVERE, missing baseline part: no written help between two living friends that costs something at the moment it is given.
   The nearest ones are the ship seat (price paid once, up front), the teleporter (no price written) and the body carry (the friend is already dead).
3. MEDIUM, only this design: the dead only watch and vote; no job, no voice.
4. MEDIUM, only this design: the early-leave vote has no count written, and a dead majority could leave the living behind to die.
5. MEDIUM, only this design: the amounts that decide two choices are unwritten ("a share", "a lower share", "a short time", how fast the target grows), so body-or-scrap and sell-or-hold cannot be checked.
6. The baseline has this too (row 1, tool shows the answer): the map shows crew, creatures and scrap through walls.
7. The baseline has this too (row 3, bought tools lower the need for friends): radio, teleporter, lights.
8. The baseline has this too (row 4, one sum for the crew): one more load, tools or travel, sell or hold; the last has one answer in the proposal's own expert line.
9. The baseline has this too (row 5, one end only stands): the stare at the thing that backs away; walking through an opened door.
10. The baseline has this too (row 6, one friend stays out): the ship player prevents the whole-crew death and the loss of stored scrap.
11. The baseline has this too (row 7, fixed roles, fear only in mistakes): see the fifth-session plan above.
- Baseline row 2 (help free once the load is put down) was not seen in this text.

## Co-op check
1. Coupled: FAIL, carrying works without anyone else.
2. Split: PASS, the map is on the ship, eyes are inside, and anyone can take the seat.
3. Talk: PASS, the midnight clock and moving dots make the ship player speak.
4. Blame: PASS, a death is one body, one stopped dot, one scream, and a fine on the crew.
5. Not easy: PASS, a new crew can miss the first target and the target always rises.
6. Not solo: FAIL, 1 player is a listed size and the line works alone.
7. Two-way: FAIL, three of five choices have "nothing" at the other end and the ship seat knows everything.
- Lone survivor: can still pull the lever, so not stuck (if they reach the ship).
- Dying on purpose: costs credits, so it does not make things easier.

## The four baseline parts
- Priced help on good nights: MISSING, see finding 2.
- Rising pressure that breaks last night's plan: PRESENT, the target rises without limit and pushes crews to paid, more dangerous moons.
- Two threats at once: PRESENT, the thing that needs eyes, the thing from the ceiling, and a sound hunter that punishes the caller.
- Carry-over: PRESENT, credits, tools, upgrades, unsold scrap and death fines change the next landing.

## Could not break
1. The target with no ceiling: every run ends in a loss and no plan stays good.
2. The clock: more creatures by the hour plus the midnight departure give "one more load" a price that grows.
3. The split by position: the map is in one place and radio range limits who hears it.
4. Threats that overlap in one scene, so one caller cannot cover everybody.
5. Carry-over that changes the next day, including the fine for each death.
6. Rounds that differ by moon, weather, layout and creature timing.
