### R103 Cursed Lantern
- Rule (one sentence): One lantern is the only light, and a ghost always hunts whoever holds it; you can throw the lantern to any teammate.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A crew of tomb robbers hauling heavy relics out of a flooded crypt at night.
- Aesthetic (one line): One warm orange circle of light in a blue-black crypt; everything outside it is flat silhouette.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I throw the lantern to Kim. She has about a second to catch it, and catching means dropping the relic she carries in both hands; a miss lands it in the water and it goes out for 5 s. Once she holds it, the ghost turns to her, and she decides whether to light the path for the others or run with it, which leaves them dark.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The floor (pits, broken stairs, which door leads out) shows only inside the light, so the route is found during the round. The ghost gets faster against whoever has held the lantern most this run, so "our fastest player always carries it" stops working by the third crypt. Relics lie in new spots each round, so who should hold the light changes with who is near what.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The lantern.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is one holder and two carriers, and every throw costs a carrier their relic grip. At 6 there are more relics and a faster ghost, the dark is crowded, and the light covers only 2–3 people, so the holder chooses who sees.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": The floor layout, uncovered by bringing the light. Nobody hides it (it is the setting), so there is no reason to hide.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ben throws the lantern to Kim while she is on a plank carrying the crown. She misses, the lantern drops in the water, Joe walks into a pit in the dark, and the ghost takes Kim. Everyone saw Ben's throw.
- Extra rules (2): the ghost's speed against a player grows with their total hold time; a relic needs both hands.
- Lag: the catch window is judged on the catcher's machine.
- Novelty: found Haunt (the lantern reveals the ghost), OBAKEIDORO (a lantern stuns the ghost), Lightscape (teleport to a teammate while chased) and Insanely Twisted Shadow Planet's Lantern Run. None has the ghost hunting the holder plus throwing the light. Source moment: being the bait.

### R115 One Take

- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I swing at Lea. She decides within half a second which way to fall: back over the table, into the window, onto Kai. A good sell scores big; a late sell is a real hit and she's "injured" for the take. Falling onto Kai hands him the next beat.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The camera rolls along a path revealed as it goes, so which side a punch must come from changes every few seconds. The director shouts open goals live ("somebody through the window!", "two hits on the same guy!"), and the team picks who. The director gets bored of repeats ("seen it, give me something new"), so the team's favorite combo stops scoring.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The camera and its frame (drawn on the floor).
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, triangle fights (one holds, one hits, one sells). At 6, two fights in the room and the camera chooses which one it's on, so the other fight must reposition into frame.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): none (the camera frame is shown on the floor).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): The camera swings to Theo's side; his punch at Lea shows a clear gap; "CUT!" The replay shows the gap from the lens and who was out of line.
- Lag note: half-second sell windows; the server judges each beat.
- N [memory, unchecked]: no known co-op game where the victim sells a fake hit for a moving camera. Stuntman (2002) is solo driving.

### R118 Spoon-Fed

- Aesthetic (one line): A giant kitchen seen from mouse height, goblins with puffed cheeks, one bright colour per power.

If he bites, he breathes fire for 5 s wherever he faces: the web ahead, or the hay behind with Sue in it. The wrong snack for the wrong friend wastes it or hurts them: gum under the pepper shelf means a sneeze and noise.

- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Which snacks drop, and where, changes who should eat what. Dares push you to feed the wrong friend, who sees it and decides in the moment to bite or refuse.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): feeding a friend.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, each goblin feeds two friends and every power runs on a short clock. At 6, there are more mouths, more misfeeds and more noise.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide): Dares. Feeding a friend shows you their dare for 2 s. You hide yours because friends won't bite from a known darer.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ana feeds Bo gum because it's her dare, while Bo stands under the pepper shelf. Bo floats up and sneezes, and the giant wakes. Everyone saw the pink gum leave Ana's hand.
