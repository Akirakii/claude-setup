The game in short: 4–8 friends in an enchanted, cartoonish witch mansion. Stage 1 (~2–4 min) is a free-for-all race:
everyone's fur grows toward turning into a werewolf; you shave others with a razor and knock them down with
slapstick-brutal weapons (pan, machete, potion bombs, bear traps, lots of cartoon blood). The first to 100% (or the
furriest when the moon peaks) becomes the werewolf. Stage 2 (3 min, the climax) is asymmetric: the werewolf must eat
everyone; the humans survive together or brew an anti-werewolf potion (wolfsbane + a rat + silver, two players stir)
and throw it. Under discussion: making the race a short build-up and the hunt the main co-op climax ("friendslop").

## Task
Use the `design-research` skill's method (web research, cite sources, tag every claim [source] / [read] /
[opinion]). Find 10–15 similar games across:
- co-op "friendslop" (e.g. Lethal Company, R.E.P.O., Peak, Content Warning, Phasmophobia);
- social deduction / betrayal (Among Us, Deceit, Town of Salem, Goose Goose Duck, Project Winter, Dread Hunger);
- asymmetric 1-vs-many (Dead by Daylight, Secret Neighbor, Evolve, Friday the 13th, Murder Mystery 2, Specimen 8);
- physics party brawlers / party FFA (Gang Beasts, Human Fall Flat, Pummel Party, Fall Guys, Party Animals).
For each: what it is, how it maps to Moonsick, success (peak concurrent players, sales or revenue estimates, Steam
review score, lifespan; cite numbers with sources and dates), and why it worked or failed.
Then: the shared success formula (what the hits have in common, what the failures lacked), and concrete, ranked
recommendations for Moonsick (what to add, cut or change; especially race vs hunt balance, voice chat, streamability,
round length, player count, price), each marked with which game(s) back it.
