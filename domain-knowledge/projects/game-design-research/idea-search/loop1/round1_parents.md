# Parents: earlier ideas whose core lands on a friend (8)

### P1 Bull Relay
- Rule (one sentence): The bull charges the nearest player it can see, so the crew passes it from one to another by stepping out of cover just as the last one ducks behind it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the bulls (one charge rule).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a bull outruns a person in the open, so whoever has its eye can only keep it a few seconds. The next player must step out where the bull will see them the moment the current one ducks, standing so the new charge heads toward the pen. If two step out, the bull takes the nearer one, maybe not the planned one. A charging bull smashes the cover it hits. The ducker picks when; the next player picks where.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): villagers herding bulls that broke loose on market day back into the pen.
- Look (one line): a whitewashed street, fruit carts and stalls, dust clouds, red scarves.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one bull, then two. At 6, two bulls, then four. A new bull breaks loose every 40 s until that cap. With two bulls loose, two players can end up holding both bulls' eyes at once.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (each bull's view cone is shown).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the bull bowled Pia over because Tom ducked behind the cart before she stepped out, and it turned on her at close range. Or Tom stepped out early, and the bull broke away toward him, away from the pen.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the market is generated, and the bulls smash carts and stalls as they charge, so the cover changes all round long.

### P2 Shove Crew
- **Rule (one sentence):** In zero gravity the only way to move is to shove a crewmate, which sends you both drifting apart in opposite directions.
- **Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses):** the crew's own bodies and their momentum: one push rule where a heavier body drifts slower. Extra rule 1: hull rails let you grab and stop, but never push off. Extra rule 2: a patch plate you carry makes you heavier.
- **Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):**
  - To reach a leak I need a friend on the far side of me, and my shove decides where *they* end up.
  - They line themselves up before the shove so their drift lands on a rail or a third player. Either of you can trigger it, and both of you set the line by where you float.
  - The friend I just sent away is now the only anchor someone else can use.
- **Fantasy:** a salvage crew with no jetpacks on a leaking, tumbling station.
- **Look:** chunky suits drifting through a slowly turning wreck, with air hissing from glowing cracks.
- **At 3 / at 6:**
  - At 3, every shove leaves exactly one possible catcher, so it is tight and new players can lose.
  - At 6, relay catches (catch, then shove onward) cross the wreck fast, but more bodies drift off.
  - A lone drifter can throw a plate away to recoil, at the cost of the plate, so nobody gets stuck.
- **Hidden:** none.
- **A failed run:** Mia shoves Tom toward the last leak at the wrong angle. Tom sails out of the open bay with the final plate. Everyone watched his drift line leave from Mia's push.
- **Tail:** the wreck tumbles and leaks open at random spots. Each crossing starts from wherever the last shoves scattered the crew, so the positions are the players' own mess.

### P3 Fling Rope
- **Rule (one sentence):** Nobody can climb on their own; an anchored friend reels you in so hard that you fly up past them, and you must grab a hold at the top of your flight.
- **Hub:** the ropes between players, a single physics rule: a fast reel flings, a slow reel drags. Extra rule: a used hold crumbles a few seconds after you let go.
- **Coupling:**
  - The anchor picks reel speed and timing.
  - The flyer picks the lean and which hold to grab, and shouts which hold they want.
  - Once they grab, the flyer becomes the anchor for the next person. The anchor's flight is already wasted if the flyer is late.
  - Each player is roped to two others, so who can fling whom changes as the crew climbs.
- **Fantasy:** a mountain rescue team climbing a collapsing cliff before the tide comes in.
- **Look:** bright ropes zig-zagging up a dark, crumbling sea cliff.
- **At 3 / at 6:**
  - At 3 it is a chain of three: every fling uses one of only two ropes.
  - At 6 the ropes form a web, giving more routes, but tangles block flings.
- **Hidden:** none.
- **A failed run:** Ana reels too hard and Leo overshoots into an overhang, falls, and drags the chain down. His flight arc showed Ana's reel speed.
- **Tail:** the wall is generated from rock chunks, holds crumble after use, and the order of the rope web changes every run.
- **Novelty:** ~. *Tangled Together* has reeling but both players walk; *Mountain Climbers* has launching.

### P4 Bed Board Tunnel
- Rule (one sentence): The crew crawls an escape tunnel one person wide whose only props are the boards already holding up its roof, so every board moved to a newly cracking spot leaves bare roof over somebody else.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the boards along the tunnel roof (which body is under which board).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): sand starts trickling over Sam. The nearest board is over Dana, two bodies back. Dana must crawl clear before it comes down, but nobody can pass anybody, so the players ahead must shuffle forward too. Then the board goes hand to hand to Sam. The digger at the front needs boards to push forward. The last board at the back comes free only once the last player has crawled past it. The back player decides when to pull, the middle players decide whether to keep or pass, and the front decides when to dig.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): prisoners of war digging out under the fence before dawn roll call, shoring the tunnel with boards from their bunks.
- Look (one line): side view of a sandy tunnel lit by fat lamps, bed boards over crawling bodies, sand trickling down.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, 3 players, 4 boards and a short tunnel. At 6, 6 players, 7 boards and a longer tunnel. Cracks open at a rate tied to crew size, and every seat sits under a board someone else may need.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (a crack trickles sand for 3 seconds before it falls).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the roof falls on Ben two seconds after Ana pulled the board above him to pass it forward. Everyone saw the sand pour where the board had been.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the soil is generated in bands: soft sand cracks often, clay rarely, and roots slow the digger. Cracks open at random spots, so the boards shuffle differently every round.

### P5 Salt Flat Crossing
- Rule (one sentence): Each player is immune to one kind of storm and casts a shelter behind them against it, so the crew survives each incoming storm only by getting the right friend in front.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the storm fronts crossing the plain (one weather rule) and the shelter shadows they cast.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): when a fire front shows on the horizon, the fireproof player runs to face it and the others tuck in behind. When a frost front comes from the other side a few seconds later, the frost-proof player turns the other way and someone must switch shelters. My spot depends on who is covering which side at that instant. Each shelter covers only two people, so somebody always has to choose whom to cover.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): elemental spirits escorting each other across a cursed salt flat to the last oasis.
- Look (one line): blinding white salt flat, coloured storm walls rolling in, and short sharp shadows behind each player.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 there are three storm kinds, and each shelter exactly covers the other two, so being one step late kills. At 6 there are six kinds and storms arrive two at a time, so the crew must split into two huddles facing opposite ways.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Lee freezes. The frost-proof player was standing on the wrong side, and their shadow is visibly pointing away from Lee.

### P6 Toolbox Drift
- Rule (one sentence): In a ship with no power and no gravity, the crew can only change course by throwing and catching one heavy tool chest: whoever throws it drifts backward, and whoever catches it drifts on with it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the tool chest (its flight) and everyone's drift through the hold.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): nobody can steer in open space. To send Kim toward the lifeboat hatch, Leo throws the chest from behind her. He drifts off the other way, so now someone must throw it to him head-on to stop him. A catcher chooses: grab it and ride, or let it fly past to someone beyond. A player touching a wall can push off, so throws are how the crew crosses the open middle. Every throw moves two people in opposite directions, so each throw depends on who is drifting where right now. The thrower picks the target and force; the catcher picks catch or let pass.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a mining-ship crew whose power died, crossing the tumbling cargo hold to the lifeboat before the air runs out.
- Look (one line): a huge dark hold, drifting crates and spinning cargo chains, helmet lights, one orange tool chest.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one chest and a triangle of throws. At 6, two chests in a bigger hold. The chests can meet mid-air, and a player who catches both shoots off fast.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Kim drifts out through the open loading door because Leo threw the chest to her too hard. She caught it and sailed past the last handrail. Everyone saw the throw.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the hold layout, the drifting crates and the spinning debris are generated and run on physics. Every throw changes two drifts at once, so the crew's paths never repeat.

### P7 Flying Trapeze
- Rule (one sentence): The only way across the big top is to let go of your swinging bar and be caught by a friend hanging from another bar, so the flyer's release and the catcher's swing must meet at the same moment.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the row of swinging trapeze bars (their swing timing).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the catcher pumps their swing to reach the meeting point, and the flyer reads that swing and picks the release. After a catch, the catcher swings the flyer on and throws them toward the next bar, whose person must already be in step. Someone on a platform must push an empty bar out at the right time. At 6, flyers cross from both ends, so a catcher chooses whom to catch and the other flyer must wait a swing. Actions: release (flyer), pump and grab (catcher), send the bar (platform player).
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a family flying-trapeze act on its first night in a new town; everyone must swap ends of the tent before the music ends.
- Look (one line): a striped big top seen from the side, bars on long ropes, a net below, spotlights following the flyer.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = two bars and two platforms, and each crossing needs all three (one sends the bar, one catches, one flies). 6 = four bars with traffic both ways. Bar spacing and rope lengths are generated each show.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the flyer drops into the net with a loud bounce and the crowd groans. The release point and the catcher's swing are both on screen, so it's clear who was early or late. The faller climbs the ladder again while the music runs out.

### P8 Cage Eyes
- Rule (one sentence): The troll in the yard is invisible to free players and visible only to players it has caught, who watch from the cage and talk the free players past it, but kicking the can frees everyone and leaves the crew blind again.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the troll (one simple chase rule: walks toward noise, chases whoever it sees).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): free players see the yard and the eggs but not the troll; caged players see only the troll and the players, not the eggs. Runners grab eggs on the cage's calls; caged players throw pebbles to lure the troll away. Every rescue takes away the crew's eyes, so the crew must decide who stays caged, and at the start someone has to get caught on purpose.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): village kids stealing eggs from a troll's yard before dawn.
- Look (one line): moonlit yard, a cage hanging from a pole, big footprints appearing in the mud.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 one caged and two running is the sweet spot, and a second capture forces a blind rescue; at 6 the crew picks its own mix of eyes and runners.
- Hidden: where the troll is; uncovered only by being caught; the troll hides by itself, and the crew hides from it by sneaking.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): dawn comes with 4 of 5 eggs because Ana kicked the can early, freed the only eyes and got caught; or Tom ran left when the cage shouted right.
