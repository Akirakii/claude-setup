# Calibration: the same critic on a shipped hit (isolated agent, 2026-11-17)

Asked by the user: "Proceed", after Claude proposed testing the critic instead of another idea.
Proposal attacked: `proposal-calibration-r1.md`, which is Lethal Company's loop written by Claude from memory under another name ("Night Clearance").
The critic was blind: no web, no other files in this folder, told not to judge resemblance. Same standards files and attack list as the Imps round.
Findings were sorted into two bins: A breaks the choices or feelings themselves; B only breaches the user's limits.
Condensed by Claude from the critic's report; the plays are the critic's, on paper, with its own numbers.

**Verdict on the design alone (bin A): fail, 7 severe. Verdict with the limits (both bins): fail, 5 more.**

## Bin A: the design itself
1. **"Cy went quiet: look or keep hauling" is answered by the van's map.** Ask the friend at the screen where his dot is; nobody goes to look.
2. **The pull-out is free:** drop the piece first, then get pulled. (Partly Claude's wording: the proposal wrote no wind-up or cooldown.)
3. **The bought tools end the need for friends:** radios, the van seat and the pull-out remove the fear the idea runs on. The better the crew earns, the less the idea exists.
4. **"One more load or leave" is one crew sum with no friend in it:** a table by day and quota gap. The critic adds that the quota gap bends the table rather than holding it.
5. **Stare or run: one end stands,** and running isn't an option because the thing is fast when unwatched.
6. **The whole-crew loss can't happen** while one friend never leaves the van. (Partly Claude's wording: nothing says the van can be entered.)
7. **A group with a lookup card:** fixed roles and one row per kind of thing, learned by the second run; in a perfect crew's round nobody is alone, so the fear for a lost friend only happens in mistakes.

## Bin B: the user's limits only
Proximity voice doesn't exist on Discord. A thing that hunts microphones makes speech the price. Fifteen kinds of thing, generated buildings and a shop are far over one developer. Bots can't test the quiet friend, the scream or the argument. Lag on the stare.

## What the critic could not break
- **"Get it off me!"** The hit has real prices in the rules (the hitter drops what they hold, the friend is hurt, the noise calls another thing), and it happens on good nights because the drop isn't the crew's mistake.
- **The rising quota as the plan breaker.** "Out by seven" stops paying once a quota needs three full visits. It is built with the core, not added on.
- **The stare with a second thing present.** One thing needs eyes and another kills when ignored, so a pair has to split its eyes and no single role covers it.
- **A full game, not minigames.** Cash, the quota gap and tools carry across days and change the next visit.
- Laughing at the scream (under in-game voice), blame (one visible body, one visible thing), a lone survivor can still finish.

## What this shows (Claude's reading)
- A hit with millions of players fails this critic on its design alone, for the same reasons the ideas did: one crew sum, a lookup card, an end that stands.
- So "passes the critic on paper" is not a bar any idea is likely to clear, and failing it doesn't show an idea is bad.
- The critic is still useful for two things: the concrete holes (a help that's free at the empty moment), and the list of what it couldn't break.
- Limits of this test: one hit, one critic run, written from Claude's memory; two of the seven findings lean on Claude's wording.
