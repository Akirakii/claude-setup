## 2. Traits used (yes/no questions, most important first)

1. **Does a player's action change what a teammate must do in that same second, for most of the listed choices?**
   Friends shout at each other only when one person's move lands on another; parallel solo play is quiet.
2. **Does a failure turn into a story that keeps the failed player involved (carried, filmed, revived, dragged down together) instead of a silent wait?**
   The laughs come from the fail; a player who only watches leaves the voice call in spirit.
3. **Is voice itself under a rule (fades by distance, heard by creatures, recorded, cut off)?**
   It makes talking a risk and a resource, which produces the half-heard warnings and cut-off screams groups retell.
4. **Is there a repeated "one more, or leave now" call where the whole crew shares the loss?**
   It gives the group something to argue about every round and someone to blame afterwards.
5. **Does one player hold information the others need and cannot see themselves?**
   It forces constant talk and gives a quieter friend a job that matters.
6. **Does every round differ without the crew having to seek new content (generated layout, changing creatures or modifiers)?**
   Groups come back on later nights only if the tenth session can still surprise them.
7. **Does the target rise so that every run ends in a failure the crew caused?**
   A run that ends on a group mistake ends on a story, and a restart is a natural "one more run".

## 4. The two hardest to place

- **g05 Linked Climb.** It scores highest on the first trait (every move pulls on every friend) and should produce the most shouting per minute, yet it has one fixed course, no variation, and falls that cost up to hours. I could not tell from the rules whether groups finish it once and stop, or quit in anger before the top; I placed it mid-table on the guess that it is played hard for a few sittings and not kept.
- **g08 Deep Disposal.** It is two games: the windowless submarine (sonar reader, pilot, throttle) is the best information-split in the set, but the dive, which is where the time and the money are, lists "nothing" for four of five choices. Its rank depends on how much of a session is spent steering, which the sheet does not say.
