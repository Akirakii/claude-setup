## Flag on Moonsick (Claude)
The user threw out "I'm too strong and kill by accident while seeking help" ("why would i be helping others as a werewolf?", "i can just stay still") and "my body kills by itself and I watch" ("not me").
This version is near both: the wolf bites on contact without my choosing, and I want to be caught.
What differs: I can't stay still (the wolf never stops), I steer the whole time (so it is me), and I am not helping others, I am trying not to hit them.
A bite turns a friend instead of killing them, which softens the user's word "kill".

## What kept Moonsick A at "maybe" (Claude's reading across five rounds)
| Version | How turning came | Why the fun died in a careful crew's plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a fur meter in moonlight | the wolf, being a teammate, steered into the shade and lapped safely |
| 2 | fur meter, cloak, flask | the guide stood in the doorway; nobody poured the cure |
| 3 | fur meter, holds, beams | a caught wolf came back clean, so turning on purpose was the best move |
| 4 | fur meter, wolf back at half fur | everyone waits in the shade two thirds of the night |
| 5 | no meter: a beam starts a 10-second fit | stay eight steps behind the beam and no fit ever starts |
One reason ended all five: the crew can keep its distance from the thing that turns them, and a crew that wants to win does.
In the hits the danger comes to the crew (Lethal Company's creatures, PEAK's fog); in every Moonsick here the crew goes to the danger, and only a rising mark pushes it.
By the method's own rule, a reason that ends every branch goes to the user as a limit, not as an idea problem.
What the critic could not break in any version: the turned friend is the busiest player, the catch needs the wolf's own steering and voice, and the see-or-pick split of version 2–4.
