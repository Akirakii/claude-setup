# Critic round 1: g02 (Breakables)

## The problem underneath
The fantasy does not need a friend: the proposal's own one-player test says one robot can still fill the target.
Feeling 1 ("slow down, you'll smash it") is caused by the rules: two carriers, one fragile item, value falls on every bump.
Feeling 2 ("stop screaming") is caused by the rule that voice counts as sound.
Feeling 4 ("he dropped the vase") is caused by the shown value and the drop.
Feeling 3 ("I'm just a head, carry me") is NOT caused as written: the voice rule says the dead cannot talk to the living without a bought upgrade.

## The scene, second by second
The carry holds up: both carriers decide speed and turn every second, and neither sees what the other sees.
The yell is priced twice: it pulls the creature and it costs the item when the partner lets go to hide.
The creature round the corner is not the crew's mistake, so the scene can happen to a careful crew.
After the death the scene goes flat: the crouched player waits, and the head rides in a cart that was going to the drop-off anyway.
The fourth player, out of voice range, is not in the scene at all.

## Fewest and most players
At 1 player: playable and losable, heavy items are skipped or bought out with strength, and there is no revive, so two feelings of four are gone.
At 6 players: nothing written says the target grows with the crew, so six looters may fill it with small items in parallel.
At 6, one pair carries and four loot alone: most seconds are side-by-side work.

## Always-wins, fifth session, perfect crew
Always wins as written: crouch-walk, never fight, close doors, skip exposed items, restart early on a bad spawn.
Fifth session: upgrades spread by role, heavy items gathered near the drop-off first, target by about minute six.
Perfect crew's round: silent crouching past every creature, no drop, no death; lines 2, 3 and 4 never happen, line 1 still does.

## Findings: only this design
M1. Solo clears it, so the friend is a bonus and not a need (co-op point 6 fails).
M2. The dead player has no act and no voice, and with one drop-off waits for the whole level; feeling 3 cannot be said without an upgrade.
M3. The restart on a bad spawn in the first two minutes is free at the first level, so the spawn is rerolled until it is easy.
M4. Two rules are unwritten: whether the target scales with crew size, and what happens to an unrescued dead player at level end; choice 3 cannot be judged without the second.
Small, not counted: fighting is called a last resort, so the shop's weapons are a worse buy than hiding, which is free.
Small, not counted: a lone survivor facing only heavy items and an unfilled target may be stuck; the amount of spare loot is not written.
No finding of this design alone breaks the round, so none is severe.

## Findings: the baseline has this too
S1. The cart total and the item values answer "one more room or go now" (row: a tool shows the answer).
S2. The head rides in the cart on the trip the crew makes anyway, so the revive is close to free (row: a help is free when the load is put down).
S3. The strength upgrade replaces the second carrier, and an upgrade gives the head its voice (row: bought tools lower the need for friends).
S4. "One more room or drop off now" is a sum over cart total, target and time (row: one sum for the whole crew).
S5. The dead player watches, and the receiver of health does nothing (row: one end only stands).
S6. Crouch, walk around, spread upgrades, minute six; one answer covers sight and sound hunters, and the fear lives in mistakes (row: fixed roles with a card of answers).

## The four baseline parts
Priced help: present; the joint carry costs both hands, speed and the item when one hides, and giving health costs the giver's own.
Rising pressure: present; more drop-offs that must all be filled, more creatures, a danger rise inside the level, a modifier every fifth level.
Two threats: present; the item needs hands and calls while the creature punishes sound and wants the carrier hidden, so one pair cannot serve both.
Two threats, limit: among creatures alone, crouching answers both kinds as written.
Carry-over: present; money, upgrades and tools change the next level, and a full death takes all of it.

## Co-op check
1 Coupled: pass; the heavy carry fails when one end lets go.
2 Split: pass; carriers see different things, voice by distance, anyone can take any job.
3 Talk: pass; the carry forces calls and the level's danger rises with time.
4 Blame: pass; the item hits the floor and its shown value falls.
5 Not easy: pass; a full death ends the run and levels grow.
6 Not solo: fail; one player can finish.
7 Two-way: fail; the dead end and the healed end decide nothing.

## Could not break
1. The joint carry of a fragile item: two decisions per second, a visible price, no safe way to hide.
2. Speech as both the carry's tool and the creature's bait.
3. Money over the target becomes spending money, so greed has a reason every level.
4. The revive only lands when a drop-off completes, so the rescue cannot skip the work.
5. A full death sends the crew back to the first level with nothing.
