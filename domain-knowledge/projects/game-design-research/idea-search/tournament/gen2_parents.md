# Parents: the 10 best gen-1 ideas from different families (formula score 16–17 of 18)

### P1 Striking Gang
- Rule (one sentence): The holder moves a glowing bar on the anvil while strikers swing heavy hammers that land one second after they start, and the bar must be beaten to the target shape before it cools, because a blow on cold metal cracks it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the hot bar on the anvil (its shape and its glow).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a striker commits a blow at a spot. The holder sees the swing start and slides or turns the bar so the right part lands under it, or pulls it clear. The other striker must swing on the off-beat (two hammers at once bounce off each other) and aim where the holder is moving the bar. The holder decides when to go back to the forge to reheat, which stops every striker. Holder = place, turn, reheat; strikers = where and when to commit.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a village forge crew rushing to make an anchor before the ship sails.
- Look (one line): a dark smithy, a bar fading from orange to red to grey, a shower of sparks with every blow.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = one holder and two alternating strikers. 6 = a long anchor shank held at each end by two holders who must move it together, and four strikers in two pairs on either side. Target shapes come from a list of ≤ 20, and everyone sees the target drawn on the wall.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the bar cracks with a loud snap right where a striker hit it after it turned grey. Or the bar flies off the anvil because the holder slid it into a falling hammer. Either way, one person is seen at the spot.

### P2 Log Jam
- Rule (one sentence): Players stand on a jam of floating logs above a mill dam and pry the logs loose with poles before the water tops the dam, and every log you push rolls or shifts the logs your friends are standing on.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the floating log pile (one physics pile).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): before I push, I check who stands on that log or its neighbours, and I call it. A player can hook a pole into a log to hold it still, so a friend can cross it or push from it. The key log holding the jam (it bows and creaks) moves only when two players push it from different sides at the same moment. When the jam breaks, everyone runs for the bank over rolling logs, and the order matters. Actions: push, pin a log, cross.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): 1890s river drivers breaking a log jam in the spring flood.
- Look (one line): brown spray, a tangle of pine logs, drivers in red wool with long pike poles.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = a small jam with one key log: one player pins while two push. 6 = a bigger jam with two key logs that must go in order, and more bodies rolling logs under each other. Each round's jam is a generated pile on one river.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (logs under strain bow and creak).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a player is rolled into the water and the logs close over them. The player who pushed that log is standing on the next one with their pole still out. Or the water tops the dam because the two key-log pushers didn't push together.

### P3 Tool Line
- Rule (one sentence): The crew crawls through a tunnel one person wide where nobody can pass anybody, and there are fewer tools than players, so each tool must be handed along the line to whoever's spot is failing.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the line of bodies in the tunnel (who is where in the order).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): problems open at any spot along the line (a leak needs a plug, a falling rock needs a prop, a stuck hatch at the front needs a bar, rising water at the back needs the pump). The right tool is often 2–3 bodies away, so the people in between must put down what they hold and pass it. Whether I keep my prop or pass it forward depends on what just broke at my friend's spot. The front player digs, the back player holds the water, the middle players plug and pass.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): miners crawling out of a flooding shaft.
- Look (one line): side-view slice of a cramped tunnel, headlamps, dark water creeping in from the left.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 players, 2 tools, short tunnel; 6 players, 4 tools, longer tunnel. Problems spawn at a rate tied to crew size, and every seat has a spot that can fail.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the leak at Sam's spot opened while Dana held the plug for two extra seconds; the water passed Sam and reached the front. Everyone saw the plug sitting in Dana's hands.

### P4 Balloon Basket
- Rule (one sentence): The balloon floats higher for every person who climbs out of its basket and sinks for every person who climbs back in.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the balloon's height.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): mail bags and stranded people sit on rooftops below. To grab one, someone drops down the rope, which lifts the balloon, so the rope's end rises away from anyone already below. One player at the burner can spend scarce fuel to sink, one holds the rope steady, and the others jump for pickups. When I can jump depends on who is still on the rope.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a balloon crew racing a storm across a rooftop town to pick up mail and stranded people.
- Look (one line): patchwork balloon over crooked rooftops, a swinging rope ladder, storm clouds closing in.
- At 3 / at 6: each person counts for 1/crew-size of the lift. At 3 one jump is a big rise; at 6 rises are finer but more people are off the basket at once. Towns are generated.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ola jumped for a mail bag while Fin was still climbing the rope, the balloon shot up, and Fin's hand slipped. Everyone saw Ola leave the basket.

### P5 Hump Yard
- Rule (one sentence): Freight cars roll one at a time down a hill into a fan of tracks, and the crew must cut each car loose, throw its switches and ride and brake it, so it meets the cars on its chalked track gently before the shift ends.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the track fan below the hill (its switches and the rolling cars).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the cutter's release timing sets the gap between cars. If two cars bound for different tracks run too close, the switch player can't throw the switch between them and shouts "hold!". Riders walk back up after each car, so the cutter releases only when a rider is ready. A rider brakes according to how far down the track the last car stopped. Cutter = when, switch player = where, riders = how fast.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a night crew in a 1950s railway yard, sorting a long freight train before the morning trains leave.
- Look (one line): a floodlit yard from above, chalk numbers on the car sides, sparks from the brake shoes.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = cutter, one switch player, one rider (the cutter must pace the train to the lone rider's walk). 6 = cutter, two switch players (upper and lower switches), three riders, and a longer train. One yard; the car order and destinations are random each shift.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (each car's track number is chalked on its side).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a boxcar slams into the cars ahead and jumps the rails, spilling crates, and its rider is seen braking late. Or a car runs down the wrong track while the switch player is still pulling the lever, a beat too late.

### P6 Salt Flat Crossing
- Rule (one sentence): Each player is immune to one kind of storm and casts a shelter behind them against it, so the crew survives each incoming storm only by getting the right friend in front.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the storm fronts crossing the plain (one weather rule) and the shelter shadows they cast.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): when a fire front shows on the horizon, the fireproof player runs to face it and the others tuck in behind. When a frost front comes from the other side a few seconds later, the frost-proof player turns the other way and someone must switch shelters. My spot depends on who is covering which side at that instant. Each shelter covers only two people, so somebody always has to choose whom to cover.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): elemental spirits escorting each other across a cursed salt flat to the last oasis.
- Look (one line): blinding white salt flat, coloured storm walls rolling in, and short sharp shadows behind each player.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 there are three storm kinds, and each shelter exactly covers the other two, so being one step late kills. At 6 there are six kinds and storms arrive two at a time, so the crew must split into two huddles facing opposite ways.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Lee freezes. The frost-proof player was standing on the wrong side, and their shadow is visibly pointing away from Lee.

### P7 Cage Eyes
- Rule (one sentence): The troll in the yard is invisible to free players and visible only to players it has caught, who watch from the cage and talk the free players past it, but kicking the can frees everyone and leaves the crew blind again.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the troll (one simple chase rule: walks toward noise, chases whoever it sees).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): free players see the yard and the eggs but not the troll; caged players see only the troll and the players, not the eggs. Runners grab eggs on the cage's calls; caged players throw pebbles to lure the troll away. Every rescue takes away the crew's eyes, so the crew must decide who stays caged, and at the start someone has to get caught on purpose.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): village kids stealing eggs from a troll's yard before dawn.
- Look (one line): moonlit yard, a cage hanging from a pole, big footprints appearing in the mud.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 one caged and two running is the sweet spot, and a second capture forces a blind rescue; at 6 the crew picks its own mix of eyes and runners.
- Hidden: where the troll is; uncovered only by being caught; the troll hides by itself, and the crew hides from it by sneaking.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): dawn comes with 4 of 5 eggs because Ana kicked the can early, freed the only eyes and got caught; or Tom ran left when the cage shouted right.

### P8 Passing Troupe
- Rule (one sentence): Each player has two hands, more clubs join the air every 20 seconds, and you must throw every club you catch on to any friend within two seconds; a club that reaches someone with both hands full hits the floor.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the clubs in the air between players.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a throw takes a second to land, so I throw to whoever will have a free hand when it lands. That depends on what they just caught and who else just threw to them. A player about to be swamped can throw early to free a hand, which pushes the problem onto someone else. A long throw across the ring buys time but is harder to catch. At any moment one player is catching, one picking a target and one clearing a hand early.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a circus passing troupe in the big ring, the act getting harder every chorus.
- Look (one line): a spotlit circus ring, each club leaving a trail in the colour of whoever threw it.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = a triangle starting with 4 clubs. 6 = a ring starting with 7; throws to a neighbour are fast and throws across the ring are slow.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a club bounces in front of a player whose hands are both full. Its colour trail shows who threw it into full hands. A club fumbled by a catcher with a free hand is that catcher's drop.

### P9 Heavy Boots
- Rule (one sentence): In a tumbling zero-gravity station any player can turn heavy, which anchors them in place and pulls every light player toward them.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the pull between players (who is heavy right now).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): to cross a gap, the friend ahead goes heavy and anchors while I go light and drift to them. Then I anchor so they can let go and push off. If two players go heavy at once, the light ones are pulled between them, maybe into spinning debris. So the heavy call depends on who is drifting where right now, and the anchor and the drifter do opposite things at the same moment.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a salvage crew whose gravity boots got wired together, escaping a station that is breaking apart.
- Look (one line): dark station interior, slow-spinning debris, and heavy players glowing with a pull ring.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is a three-person leapfrog where every swap is critical. At 6, pulls from several heavy players add up, so the crew needs a rhythm to stop yanking each other off course.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ana is pulled into a spinning beam because Joe went heavy while she was mid-drift toward Kim. His glow lit up at that exact moment.

### P10 Pull Tower
- Rule (one sentence): The crew stands on a tower of loose stone blocks in rising water and must build a bridge to the next tower using only blocks pulled out of the tower they stand on.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the tower (a physics stack that is both everyone's footing and the only building material).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): blocks are too heavy for one: one player pushes a block out, another must catch it on the far side. Before the push, the others must move their weight to the side that keeps the tower standing, so where I stand depends on which block a friend just started on. Pushers, catchers, counterweights and the bridge layer do different jobs.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): flood survivors hopping from ruin to ruin.
- Look (one line): grey stone stacks in brown floodwater, small figures leaning out as counterweights.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 one pair works while the third balances and lays the bridge; at 6 two pairs work and two balance, and the extra weight makes the tower touchier.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the tower tips when Ken pulls a corner block while Mia stands on that corner; everyone saw both moves.
