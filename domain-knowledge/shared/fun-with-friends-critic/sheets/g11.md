# Proposal for the critic: Spirit Survey

**Fantasy:** A small crew of paid investigators enters a haunted building to work out which kind of ghost lives there, and wants to leave alive with the right answer.
One-player test: Yes, one investigator can take a job alone, with nobody to watch the vehicle's monitors or talk on the radio.

**Players:** 1–4, one crew. **A session:** one job on one building, about 10–30 minutes depending on building size (length is from guides saying the ghost's room alone can take 10+ minutes on big buildings); a sitting holds several jobs.

**Feelings the game is known for**
- "The lights flickered, the front door locked, and we all stopped talking at once."
- "I was sure it was one type, and then it did the one thing that type never does."
- "I was in the vehicle watching the monitors and heard my friend's radio go to static."
- "I asked the ghost a question out loud and it answered."

**The rules, one sentence each**
- Each job puts one ghost, of one randomly picked type, in one building, and the crew's goal is to name the type in a shared notebook before leaving.
- Each ghost type gives three kinds of evidence out of seven, and each kind needs its own tool to detect.
- Each type also has its own behaviour quirks, such as hunting earlier or later than the others.
- The crew arrives in a vehicle parked outside, which holds the equipment, a map of the building, a monitor of each player's sanity and a monitor of ghost activity.
- Every player has a sanity meter that starts at 100% and drains in the dark and during ghost events; lit rooms slow the drain and pills restore part of it.
- When the crew's average sanity drops to about 50% or below, the ghost can start a hunt, and the chance rises as sanity falls; some types use a higher or lower line.
- When a hunt starts the exit doors lock, nearby electronics flicker, and after a grace period of a few seconds (shorter on harder settings) the ghost walks the building and kills any player it reaches.
- During a hunt the ghost is drawn to players who talk into their microphone or carry switched-on electronics near it.
- Hunts last longer in bigger buildings, and on harder settings a hunt is extended when the ghost kills someone.
- A ward object placed near the ghost stops a normal hunt from starting, and a smoke stick blocks new hunts for about 90 seconds or halts a running hunt for about 5 seconds if the ghost is close.
- Each building also holds one cursed object that gives information or sanity at a price, often an immediate hunt with a 1-second grace period that smoke sticks cannot stop.
- The ghost and some tools react to words the players actually say aloud.
- Each job lists optional side objectives, and photos, videos and sound recordings of ghost activity add to the pay.
- The job ends when the surviving players return to the vehicle and close its doors, with a right or wrong answer in the notebook.
- A dead player stays in the session as a spectator who cannot speak to the living, and loses the equipment they brought (from the researcher's own knowledge, not confirmed in the sources fetched).

**How much there is:** about 29–30 ghost types; 7 kinds of evidence; 17 hand-made buildings (9 small, 6 medium, 2 large); 8 starting tools, with more tools and three quality tiers per tool unlocked by level and bought with money; several kinds of cursed object (exact count not found); 5 named difficulty settings plus a custom one (exact count of named settings not confirmed).

**Voice:** Voice by distance inside the building, plus a hand radio that reaches the whole crew; the ghost hears voice during hunts, and some tools and cursed objects need spoken questions.

**The choices**
- Stay in the dark ghost room to get the last piece of evidence, or go back to the light and the vehicle to save sanity?
- Use the cursed object for a quick answer, or avoid it because it can start a hunt right now?
- When the hunt starts, run for a hiding spot, or lead the ghost in circles around furniture?
- Spend the ward object and smoke sticks now to stay safe, or keep them for when sanity is lower?
- Lock in a guess now and leave, or stay longer to rule out the last two types?

**What the other end decides in the same second**
- Stay or go back: a crewmate in the vehicle reads the sanity monitor and decides whether to call the room team out over the radio.
- Cursed object: the others decide whether to stand near an exit or a hiding spot before it is used.
- Hide or circle: everyone else decides whether to stay silent and switch off their electronics, or keep talking and be found.
- Ward and smoke: nothing.
- Guess now or stay: nothing, beyond agreeing or arguing.

**Scene, 4 friends**
- Two players are in the ghost's room placing tools, one is in the hallway with a camera, one sits in the vehicle reading the monitors.
- The player in the vehicle says over the radio that average sanity has passed below half.
- The torches start to flicker and the front door slams and locks.
- All four stop talking, because the ghost hears microphones.
- One room player steps into a closet and shuts it, the other lights a smoke stick as the ghost appears in the doorway.
- The hallway player's radio turns to static, then goes quiet.
- The hunt ends, the doors unlock, and three voices come back on the radio asking who is missing.

**What experienced crews do:** Guides say to find the ghost's room as fast as possible, usually with the temperature tool and the talk-to-the-ghost radio, and then to pick two hiding spots straight away before doing anything else.
On small buildings guides say to lead the ghost in circles around counters and tables rather than hide, unless the ghost is one of the fast types.
On large buildings guides treat sanity pills as a must, because those jobs take much longer.
A crewmate at the vehicle's map guides lost players out over the radio.
**What changes it from round to round:** The ghost type, the ghost's room, the building the crew votes for, the side objectives, which cursed object is present, and on the harder settings how many kinds of evidence the ghost will show (two or fewer instead of three).
**What carries from one round to the next:** Money and experience; levels unlock tools and tool tiers, money buys them, and equipment taken into a job by a player who dies is lost (last part from the researcher's own knowledge).
**Can a new crew lose? Does it get harder?** Yes, the whole crew can die or leave with the wrong type, which pays little; the game does not get harder by itself, the host picks the difficulty, and harder settings give less setup safety, faster sanity loss, shorter grace periods, less evidence and a higher pay multiplier.
