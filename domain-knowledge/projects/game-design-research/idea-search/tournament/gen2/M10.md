# M10 "random crew × material" (gen 2, 2026-11-10)

## Method log

**Steps run**
1. I wrote two lists of 40 before drawing:
   - **Crews:** roofers, change bell ringers, barn raisers, rowing eight with cox, firefighter hose team,
     mountain rescue stretcher team, theatre fly-system stagehands, shepherds with dogs, glassblowing
     team, dragon boat crew, tug-of-war team, sailing crew, bricklayer gang, concrete pour crew, F1 pit
     crew, surgical team, kitchen brigade, log drivers, coal miners, sandbag levee crew, hay harvest
     crew, bucket brigade, castellers (human towers), bobsled team, curling team, cave divers, piano
     movers, window cleaners on a gondola, beekeepers, grape pickers and treaders, trawler crew, lion
     dance team, avalanche probe line, big-top tent raisers, blacksmith with strikers, bomb disposal
     team, orchestra, dockers, rope-bridge builders, bell-founders.
   - **Materials:** wax that hardens as it cools, ice that cracks, wet sand, rope, expanding foam,
     magnets, sucking mud, smoke, honey, glass, snow, chain, oil, dough, cloth in wind, oobleck, clay,
     molten metal, water, a gunpowder trail, elastic, helium balloons, steam, sawdust, gravel, soap
     bubbles, paper, bamboo, mercury, glue, fire, lava, spider silk, salt, cream that churns, rust, ink,
     jelly, marbles, tar.
2. I drew 22 pairs with `random.SystemRandom().sample`, one draw from each list without repeats. I
   kept every pair as drawn: none was picked by hand or re-rolled.
3. I forced one co-op idea per pair. Two pairs gave a second take (1b, 2b). The material's physics had
   to carry both the challenge and the blame.
4. I kept 8 with 8 different core rules, then ran one web search per kept idea.

**Raw count:** 24 candidates from 22 pairs.

**Checks the raw candidates failed most**
- **F3 player coupling:** 5 ✗ and 6 ~.
  - Pooled sums: steam pressure, snow-slab load, the parachute cloth, the silk crane's pull on one crate.
  - Parallel play: the vineyard pickers, the rowers.
  - A quarterback: the cox.
- **N novelty:** 5 ✗.
  - Searched: magnets (Co-opposites Attract, Scrap Pirates).
  - From memory, not searched: carrying a glass object (Carry The Glass; Moving Out), building the
    track ahead of the ride (Unrailed!), curling, the playground parachute.
- **F7 tail:** weak in 3, all where the material was only a skin over a fixed task (tar, cream, rust).

**Swap 1:** I dropped the magnet gondola and put in the next-best raw candidate, the foam bulkhead
(#20). Its search found Grim Repair, Piece of Ship and Barotrauma: co-op games about patching a
sinking ship, but none uses swelling foam that sets and traps. Kept as M10-8, N ~. That was the only
swap.

**What the method was good at**
- **F4 blame and F7 tail.** The material gave both for free: the colour of the ripple, crack, smoke
  stream or foam shows who did it, and the physics made by 3–6 people never repeats.
- **Novelty.** 7 of the 8 kept ideas survived their search on the first try.
- **Clean rules.** Most rules fit in one sentence about the material.

**What the method was bad at**
- **The crew half added little.** It mostly became the fantasy skin, and the material did the design
  work.
- **Shared-material traps.** Materials that one group steers together (rope, cloth, steam, snow) fell
  straight into the pooled-sum failure.
- **Build cost.** Many ideas need a soft-body or fluid simulation (jelly waves, smoke, clay, foam).
  That is a real cost for one developer in 3–4 months, and F1 does not score it.
- **Nothing to hide.** Hidden information almost never came up (F6 is mostly n/a).
- **Small crews.** Some ideas are thin at 3, e.g. chain herding with only one chain.

## Kept ideas

### M10-1 Jelly Kitchen
- Rule (one sentence): Every move a player makes on the jelly floor sends out a visible ripple; ripples
  add up or cancel where they meet, and anything sitting on a high crest tips over.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the jelly floor (one wave field).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The kitchen is a chain: chop → cook → plate → run. Each station has one delicate moment (pouring,
    stacking, carrying a full tray) that needs calm floor under it.
  - A friend's step reaches me as a ripple a second later. I can wait it out, step aside, or stomp in
    time to cancel it (a crest meeting a trough). The cancelling stomp sends its own ripple on to
    someone else.
  - Different jobs, and the chain stops if one station stops.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): cooks in a restaurant built on a giant dessert, getting
  orders out during the dinner rush.
- Look (one line): a glossy pastel jelly floor with rings in each cook's colour spreading from their
  feet.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: chop+cook, plate, run. Each person walks more, so each makes more ripples.
  - At 6: two cooks, two platers, a runner and a dishwasher. The floor gets busy, and cancelling
    ripples becomes part of everyone's job.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a stack of plates tips over. The crest that tipped it
  shows the colour of the cook whose step made it, or whose cancelling stomp came half a beat late.
  Orders time out.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):
  - Ripples from 3–6 people meet in new patterns every service.
  - The orders are drawn at random from a list of ≤ 20 dishes, so the busy stations move around.
- Search: Jelly Express (co-op jelly box balancing) and The Floor is Jelly (single-player). No
  ripple-interference rule found.

### M10-2 Smoke Shove
- Rule (one sentence): The garage fills with smoke that is never destroyed, only pushed: each player's
  fan clears the air around them by blowing the smoke onto whoever is downwind.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the smoke cloud (a fixed amount of smoke in one room).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - Each player has a different pit job: jack, wheel gun, tyre swap, fuel, fire-damage repair. Each
    job needs clear air for a few seconds at its own step.
  - Clearing my spot dumps smoke on a neighbour, so where I aim depends on who just cleared their spot
    and where their smoke went.
  - The jobs come in order: the jack must be up before the wheels and down after them.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a pit crew servicing a race car whose engine is on fire,
  every lap.
- Look (one line): thick grey smoke cut by bright fan lanes, each lane tinted the colour of its owner.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: jack+fuel, wheel gun, tyres.
  - At 6: two wheel guns, two tyre carriers, jack, fuel/repair. More fans push the same smoke, so
    there are more fights over clean air.
- Hidden: what is under the smoke (which nut is loose, which part is burning). It is uncovered by
  fanning it clear. Nobody hides it on purpose; the cost is that your smoke lands on someone.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the jack man drops the car onto a half-fitted wheel
  because his view went grey. The fan lane that pushed the smoke onto him a second earlier shows its
  owner's colour.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):
  - The smoke flow comes from 3–6 fans and never repeats.
  - Each stop draws a random fault from a list of ≤ 20, and the fault moves where the smoke comes from.
- Search: only smoke grenades in shooters and an ash-clearing fan in Volcanoids. Pushing a fixed
  amount of smoke between teammates was not found.

### M10-3 Many-Hands Wheel
- Rule (one sentence): The crew throws one pot on one wheel: one player sets the spin, every other
  player presses the clay at their own height, and clay pushed in at one height swells out at the
  next.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the lump of clay (a fixed amount).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - When I press in, clay bulges into the band of the friend above or below me, and they must thin it
    out or the wall sags and tears.
  - The spinner's speed scales every press: fast means big changes and wobble, slow means small and
    safe but slow.
  - Clay left dry cracks, so someone has to keep wetting it.
  - My next press depends on the bulge a friend just pushed into my band.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): potters racing the kiln to throw one huge vessel to order.
- Look (one line): a big wheel seen side-on, with each player's coloured handprints on their own band
  of clay.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: spinner plus top hands and bottom hands; the hands also wet their own bands.
  - At 6: spinner, waterer and four bands.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the wall tears at band 3 because the band-2 player
  pushed too much clay up while the spinner sped up. The tear starts at a coloured handprint.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each order is a
  random outline (a curve through a few random points), and how the clay flows between bands is
  never the same twice.
- Search: Kiln (Double Fine, 2026) is a multiplayer pottery game, but each player throws their own
  pot. No shared wheel split by height was found (N ~ at worst).

### M10-4 Crack Loop Roof
- Rule (one sentence): Every step on the glass roof grows cracks from the foot (heavier steps grow
  longer ones), and any piece of glass fully enclosed by cracks drops out with whoever is on it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the crack network on the roof.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - Two players carry a stretcher, which is heavy and makes long cracks; they must step together.
  - Scouts walk ahead to pick a route and lay planks, which spread the weight but crack where they
    land.
  - A scout's cracks both show the weak spots and add cracks the stretcher must cross.
  - My safe step depends on the cracks friends just left, and one step can close a loop under someone
    else.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a rescue crew carrying an injured person across the glass
  roof of a burning atrium.
- Look (one line): a blue-white glass roof over an orange glow, every crack line drawn in the colour of
  the foot that made it.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: two carriers and one scout.
  - At 6: two stretchers (four carriers) and two scouts; the stretchers' routes cross each other's
    cracks.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a pane drops under the stretcher. The crack that closed
  the loop flashes in the colour of the scout who stepped there three seconds earlier.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):
  - The cracks grow from wherever this crew stepped, so no roof repeats.
  - The pane grid is generated.
  - The heat below makes cracks grow faster as the run goes on.
- Search: Carry The Glass (carrying a pane) and a Roblox floor that collapses on a scripted event. No
  rule where pieces fall once cracks enclose them was found.

### M10-5 Chain Fence
- Rule (one sentence): The herders are links in one chain fence, each joined to a neighbour by a
  fixed-length chain; sheep can't cross a tight chain but slip over a slack one, and every sheep runs
  from the nearest herder.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the chain fence.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - When I step toward the flock, the sheep flee from me toward my neighbours.
  - My chain goes slack if a neighbour steps toward me, so they must step back out, or I must, before
    sheep pour over.
  - One player is the dog outside the chain. The dog drives stragglers in from behind, and the two end
    herders swing the chain open and shut to let them through.
  - The dog's push and the ends' timing both decide whether sheep get in.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): shepherds with lanterns, getting a panicked flock into the
  pen before a storm.
- Look (one line): a top-down green hillside, a line of lantern-lit herders joined by chain, and a
  white swirl of sheep.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: two herders with one chain, plus the dog. Thin, since it is just a sweeping line.
  - At 6: five herders with four chains, plus the dog.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the flock bursts over a sagging chain. The chain glows,
  and the herder who stepped inward and made it slack is marked.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):
  - Flocking (flee the nearest herder, follow your neighbours) makes every drive different.
  - The hill and pen spots are generated, and the wind changes which way sheep bolt.
- Search: co-op herding games exist (Too Many Sheep, Baaad Trip, Flock!), and Chained Together has
  chained players (from memory, not searched). No chained fence of herders was found (N ~).

### M10-6 Set Glue
- Rule (one sentence): The crew rebuilds a broken statue; each glue joint sets four seconds after it
  is painted and locks the pieces at whatever angle they are held, and nobody can glue a piece they
  are holding.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the statue being rebuilt.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - I hold a piece and line up its edges (they glow when they match) while the statue sways.
  - A friend paints the glue at the moment I'm lined up, and I must stay lined up for four seconds.
  - Meanwhile a third player bracing their own piece makes the whole statue sway.
  - Then the jobs swap, so everyone both holds and paints. The painter waits on the holder's
    alignment, and the holder fights the sway the others make.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): museum restorers rebuilding a giant statue between
  earthquake aftershocks before opening time.
- Look (one line): white marble pieces joined by gold glue lines, each line tinted with its holder's
  colour.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: one holds, one paints, one steadies the base or holds the next piece.
  - At 6: three joints in progress at once on one wobbling statue.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): an arm sets crooked, so the next pieces won't fit and
  the statue tips. The crooked gold line carries the holder's colour, and the bump that moved them is
  seen as it happens.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):
  - The statues are broken by a fracture routine (random cuts through a few base shapes), so the
    pieces differ every round.
  - A crooked joint carries forward and makes the later fits harder.
- Search: Porcelaine (2 players rebuild porcelain) and Stuck Together (one glues crates, one moves the
  crane). The rule "can't glue what you hold, and the glue locks the held angle" was not found
  (N ~).

### M10-7 Salt Cross
- Rule (one sentence): Salt crystal grows only where a brine spray and a heat beam cross, and fresh
  water dissolves any salt it touches.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the salt structure in the shaft.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The brine player and the heat player must aim at the same spot at the same time to grow a step or
    a bridge; each picks their aim and adjusts to the other's.
  - If either sweeps while the other is still on, salt grows in the wrong place and walls off the
    route.
  - The water player cuts away bad growth, but the water splashes, and the crew is standing on salt.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): miners climbing out of a flooding salt mine by growing
  their own staircase.
- Look (one line): a dark shaft with white crystal glowing where an orange beam meets a blue spray.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: one brine, one heat, one water.
  - At 6: two of each, so two growth spots at once, and the two water players can splash each other's
    work.
  - At odd sizes the extra player takes brine or heat.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the ledge under the heat player melts. The splash came
  from the water player's stream, seen in the moment, and the rising water takes the crew.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):
  - Crystals branch off whatever already grew (one simple growth rule), so each staircase is the
    crew's own.
  - The shafts are generated from a few room pieces, and the water rises faster each level.
- Search: Lanterns (2 coloured light rays, co-op) and Magicka's crossing beams (from memory, not
  searched). Growing lasting salt only where two players' streams cross, with a third player who
  dissolves it, was not found (N ~).

### M10-8 Foam Bulkhead
- Rule (one sentence): Foam sprayed into the hull swells to three times its size over four seconds,
  shoving anything soft out of the way, then sets rock-hard around whatever is still inside it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the hull, with its water and its foam.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - A breach's water jet washes soft foam away, so one player blocks the jet with a hatch board while
    another sprays around them.
  - The blocker must pull out just before the foam sets, or be set in. A trapped player is freed only
    when a friend chips them out with a pick.
  - The sprayer's timing depends on when the blocker is braced, and the blocker's pull-out depends on
    how much foam was just sprayed.
  - Swelling foam from one patch shoves friends working on the next one.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): the crew of a sinking paddle steamer patching holes to
  reach the shore.
- Look (one line): a dark wooden hull, yellow foam bulging and crusting over, crew stuck in it up to
  the neck.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: blocker, sprayer, and a chipper who also bails.
  - At 6: two blocker+sprayer pairs, a chipper and a bailer.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the blocker is set into the foam, nobody is free to
  block the next breach, and the hold floods. The foam that trapped them is tinted with the sprayer's
  colour.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):
  - New breaches open where the water pushes hardest.
  - The hard foam already placed changes the hull's weight and trim, so breaches open in new places
    every run, and each hull is the crew's own patchwork.
- Search (swap-in for the magnet idea): co-op ship-patching games exist (Grim Repair, Piece of Ship,
  Barotrauma), but none uses foam that swells, shoves and sets with people inside (N ~).
