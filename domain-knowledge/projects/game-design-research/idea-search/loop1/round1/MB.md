# MB: method B, "the moment friends retell"

## Method log

Steps run:
1. Listed 26 moments friends retell from co-op and party games, from memory. Each is one person doing something to another (the list is in the raw candidates, first field).
2. Named each moment's structure: who acts on whom, what the other can do back, and why it was tense or funny (second field).
3. Built one new game per moment that makes that structure happen every minute, with a different act, fantasy and look from the source (third field). No web search while generating.
4. Checked each raw idea against the gates in order (P first, then the formula, then R), and kept 8 with 8 different core rules.
5. Novelty step: one web search per kept idea on its core rule in plain words. Two kept ideas had a game with the same core, so they were swapped for the next-best raw ideas, and each swap-in got its own search.

Raw count: 26 raw ideas, 2 merged into others, 8 kept.

The gates the raw ideas failed most:
- **R, the plan settles (8 raw ideas).** When the source moment is a simple hand-off (pass, launch, step on, hand over), the new game turns into a fixed relay or rotation by round 5: Hot Ingots, Fuse Crew, Stepping Stones, Last Lift, Buddy Breath.
- **The method's own step 3, the act copies the source (5 raw ideas).** The source act leaked into the new game: launching a friend is still throwing (Cannon Post), head-jumping is still head-jumping, shoving is still shoving, and the air drop is still the Helldivers pod.
- **F5, idle seats (2 raw ideas).** Ghost Crew and Rear Guard park players.
- **Already taken (1 raw idea).** Storm Deck is the rope-tied crew.

Swaps in the novelty step:
- **Trapeze Night, out.** Heave Ho (players are each other's handholds and swing each other) and Men on the Flying Trapeze (2010, two players catch each other on trapezes) share the core. **Sleepwalkers, in** (now MB-7). Its search found no match.
- **Splash Alchemy, out.** Sweet Tooth (itch.io, 3-player co-op) has the same core: you throw potions at teammates and the colors' effects change each round. **Bell Monks, in** (now MB-5). Its search found no match. Heal beams such as the TF2 medic share the "beam on a friend" part (from memory).
- **Changelings, kept.** The search found "Competitive Switcheroo", an Overwatch workshop mode with a teammate position swap. It is competitive, a custom mode, and has no "you inherit my trouble" rule, so I scored it N ~ rather than a swap. Shooter Buddies swaps gear, not places.
- **Mirror Knights, kept as N ~.** Alien Blaster 83, a small indie game, bounces your laser off co-defenders. In mine the friend can be burned and chooses where the beam goes next, so the receiver decides.
- **Bulkheads, kept as N ~.** Grim Repair (a sinking vessel where you close doors and can abandon teammates) is close. Barotrauma is too, from memory. The one-sided hatch changes who decides.

Checked vs assumed:
- Searches checked: one search each, so "no match" means none found, not none exists.
- Assumed: every gate verdict here (my own reading), and that the catch, dodge and swap windows survive online lag (notes are on each idea).

Good:
- Every retold moment already has player acting on player, so gate P comes almost free.
- The moments point at the tense or funny beat, which gives each idea a clear "failed run" with a visible culprit (F4).

Bad:
- The moments came mostly from about 8 games (Lethal Company, Left 4 Dead, Gang Beasts, Peak, Helldivers, Phasmophobia, Overcooked, TF2), so many structures repeat: hand-off, bait, rescue.
- The method gives no pushback, so I bolted it on. 5 of the 8 kept ideas share the same extra-rule shape: the threat goes after the team's habit (the stillest player, the longest holder, the most-used thrower).
- Step 3 ("different act") is easy to break without noticing. 5 raw ideas broke it.

## Kept ideas

### MB-1 Cursed Lantern
- Rule (one sentence): One lantern is the only light, and a ghost always hunts whoever holds it; you can throw the lantern to any teammate.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A crew of tomb robbers hauling heavy relics out of a flooded crypt at night.
- Aesthetic (one line): One warm orange circle of light in a blue-black crypt; everything outside it is flat silhouette.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I throw the lantern to Kim. She has about a second to catch it, and catching means dropping the relic she carries in both hands; a miss lands it in the water and it goes out for 5 s. Once she holds it, the ghost turns to her, and she decides whether to light the path for the others or run with it, which leaves them dark.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The floor (pits, broken stairs, which door leads out) shows only inside the light, so the route is found during the round. The ghost gets faster against whoever has held the lantern most this run, so "our fastest player always carries it" stops working by the third crypt. Relics lie in new spots each round, so who should hold the light changes with who is near what.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The lantern.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is one holder and two carriers, and every throw costs a carrier their relic grip. At 6 there are more relics and a faster ghost, the dark is crowded, and the light covers only 2–3 people, so the holder chooses who sees.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": The floor layout, uncovered by bringing the light. Nobody hides it (it is the setting), so there is no reason to hide.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ben throws the lantern to Kim while she is on a plank carrying the crown. She misses, the lantern drops in the water, Joe walks into a pit in the dark, and the ghost takes Kim. Everyone saw Ben's throw.
- Extra rules (2): the ghost's speed against a player grows with their total hold time; a relic needs both hands.
- Lag: the catch window is judged on the catcher's machine.
- Novelty: found Haunt (the lantern reveals the ghost), OBAKEIDORO (a lantern stuns the ghost), Lightscape (teleport to a teammate while chased) and Insanely Twisted Shadow Planet's Lantern Run. None has the ghost hunting the holder plus throwing the light. Source moment: being the bait.

### MB-2 Ventriloquists
- Rule (one sentence): A blind beast charges at sounds, and any player can throw a sound so it seems to come from where a teammate stands.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Stagehands locked in an old theater overnight with a blind beast made of curtains; they must shut it in the prop cages before dawn.
- Aesthetic (one line): A cardboard theater with painted flats, footlights and a beast of red velvet.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I throw a clatter onto Raf. The beast turns and charges him. He has about 2 seconds to sidestep so it rams the open cage behind him (and he slams the door), or to throw the sound on to Lea. If he freezes, he is mauled and out until someone drags him back.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Cages stand in new spots each round and each one breaks after one use, so the bait route changes every charge. The beast learns the voice of whoever throws most and starts charging the thrower instead, so the "Kai always throws" plan breaks. Real shouting on voice chat above a loud level also draws it, so the call-outs themselves are a risk.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The beast.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is a thrower, a bait and a door-slammer, and the roles turn over every charge. At 6 there are two beasts and more cages; more throwers means each throws less before the beast learns them.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": None. The beast's learned voices show as its ears turning toward those players.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Kai throws the sound onto Lea while she is carrying a cage door. She can't dodge and is mauled. Everyone saw Kai's throw marker.
- Extra rules (2): the beast learns the most frequent thrower; loud mic noise draws it.
- Lag: the charge target is fixed when the sound is thrown; the dodge is judged on the target's machine.
- Novelty: monsters that hunt by sound are common (In Silence, Hushed, PANICORE's noise lures); found no game where you throw a sound onto a teammate. Source moment: a friend's voice cut off by a monster.

### MB-3 Changelings
- Rule (one sentence): Any player can swap places with any teammate they can see, and the friend lands in the swapper's spot with the swapper's speed and trouble.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Thumb-sized fairy thieves stealing sweets from a giant cook's kitchen.
- Aesthetic (one line): Tiny figures in a huge warm kitchen: copper pots as big as houses, flour clouds and candlelight.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I am falling toward the stove and swap with Leo on the shelf. Now Leo is mid-fall above the flames with a second to grab the ladle. Or I hold a cherry and swap with Leo by the mousehole: the cherry is home, and Leo appears where the cook's knife is coming down. Leo can swap on to someone else once his 4 s cooldown is up.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): You keep what you hold when you swap, so swaps are the fast way to move loot, but the cook swats whoever has stood still longest, which kills the "Leo waits at the hole as the anchor" plan. The sweets, the cat and the cook's chore (rolling, chopping, boiling) change each round, so which swap chain is best changes. Every swap is a choice of which friend to put in danger.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The kitchen and its cook.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 each player has only two swap partners, so it is tight and every swap lands on someone known. At 6 there are many choices, more sweets and a second cat.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": None.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ava swaps with Joe as Joe is jumping, and Joe lands in the soup pot. Everyone saw Ava's swap flash.
- Extra rules (2): a 4 s swap cooldown per player; the cook swats the stillest player.
- Lag: the swap needs the server to agree, and both players move at once. This is the riskiest of the 8 for lag.
- Novelty: the closest find is Competitive Switcheroo (an Overwatch workshop mode with a teammate position swap, competitive). Shooter Buddies swaps gear, not places. N ~. Source moment: a Lethal Company teleport yanking a friend at the wrong moment.

### MB-4 Mirror Knights
- Rule (one sentence): The only weapon is a sunbeam that grows stronger each time it bounces off a knight's shield, and a knight who isn't facing the beam when it arrives gets burned.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Knights at dusk holding off shadow beasts with the last of the sunlight.
- Aesthetic (one line): Stained-glass knights, gold beam lines across a blue dusk field, and ink-black beasts.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I bounce the beam to Mia. She has about a second to face me, then picks the next knight or a beast. If she has turned to block a beast, she is burned and drops. If I aimed at her back, the burn is mine to own.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Beasts come from new sides with armor 1–5 (the number of bounces needed), so who stands where and the bounce order change every wave. Beasts lunge at the knight who made the killing bounce, so "Theo always finishes" gets Theo eaten. The beam can only start in the last patch of sun, which drifts as the sun sets.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The sunbeam.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 a 4-armor beast needs a chain like A→B→C→A→beast, which is tight and loud. At 6 there are two sun patches and two beams, and crossing beams burn whoever stands where they cross.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": None.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Theo turns to block a beast instead of facing Mia's beam. He is burned and drops, the chain breaks, and a 4-armor beast reaches the camp. Theo's turn (or Mia's call) was seen by all.
- Extra rules (2): beasts lunge at the finisher; the sun patch drifts.
- Lag: the beam takes about 0.5 s to travel, which hides lag; arrival is judged on the receiver's machine.
- Novelty: Alien Blaster 83 (a small indie game) bounces lasers off co-defenders. Here the friend can be burned and decides the next bounce. N ~. Source moment: accidentally blowing up a friend.

### MB-5 Bell Monks
- Rule (one sentence): A monk is fireproof only while a teammate channels into them, and a monk who is channeling can't move.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Monks in a mountain temple ringing the bells that put a fire dragon back to sleep, running through its flames to reach them.
- Aesthetic (one line): An ink-wash mountain temple, red fire and gold threads between monks.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I channel into Jun so he can run through the fire to a bell. The dragon swings its head to me, and I am rooted. I either keep the thread and take the heat, or break off and Jun burns in the middle of the bridge. Jun shouts which way he is going, and I may switch the thread to Ama if she is closer to the next bell.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The bells light in a new order and in new spots each round, so the best runner (whoever is closest) changes. The dragon breathes at whoever has been channeling longest, so the fixed "Ama channels, Jun runs" pair breaks. A third monk can channel the channeler, so chains form and break mid-round.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The dragon's fire.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is one runner, one channeler, and one who either covers the channeler or runs to a second bell. At 6 there are chains of 2–3 and two runners at once.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": None.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ama breaks off to dodge the dragon's turn, and Jun burns on the bridge. Everyone saw the gold thread vanish.
- Extra rules (1): the dragon targets the longest channeler.
- Lag: the channel state is owned by the channeler's machine.
- Novelty: the search found no match. Heal beams (TF2 medic, from memory; Deadlock's Rescue Beam) share "a beam on a friend". Near "rope-tied" only in look: the thread is not physical and switches freely. N ~. Source moment: the medic pocketing one friend.

### MB-6 Trap Ring
- Rule (one sentence): Each player runs through their own trap room while working the switches of the next player's room, in a ring.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Contestants on a cursed 70s game show, where each contestant's buzzer board runs the next contestant's room.
- Aesthetic (one line): Bright game-show tiles and buzzer lights; your screen shows two panels, your room close up and your friend's room from above.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): Nina yells "red gate!". I click red on her board while dodging the saws that Tom runs for me. If I click wrong, her floor drops. She decides when to step, trusting my click or not.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Rooms are built fresh each round from a short tile list, so what each runner needs comes from the round. A hound walks into whichever room has been safest longest. The ring order (who runs whose room) reshuffles each round.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The ring of switch boards.
- At 3 / at 6: A ring of 3 or 6, odd sizes included. Everyone is always both runner and operator, so no seat is idle.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": The runner sees only nearby tiles, while the operator sees the whole room; it is uncovered by the operator's calls. There is no reason to hide.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Tom clicks the blue floor instead of the red gate, and Nina falls. Everyone saw Tom's click light up on her board.
- Extra rules (2): the hound goes to the safest room; the ring order reshuffles.
- Risk: splitting attention between two panels may be too hard (assumed, untested).
- Novelty: found single-operator trap control (one Steam title; the Lethal Company terminal, from memory), but no ring. Source moment: a friend at the terminal shutting off the turret on you.

### MB-7 Sleepwalkers
- Rule (one sentence): Any player can sing a teammate to sleep; sleepers see the dream-house's hidden holes and the Nightmare but can't move, so awake players must carry them.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Friends stuck in one shared dream, ringing the alarm clocks hidden in a dream-house to wake up.
- Aesthetic (one line): A soft pastel house with floating furniture; a sleeper's view flips to stark black and white, where the holes and the tall Nightmare show.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I sing Pia to sleep; she has 2 s to dodge if it is a bad moment. Now she sees the holes and must tell Max where to carry her, and Max can carry her into one. Anyone can slap her awake, which costs everyone the view.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The holes reshuffle whenever nobody is asleep, so someone must always be out, which means someone must always carry. The Nightmare walks toward whoever has slept longest, so the sleeper must change mid-round. The clocks are in new spots each round.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The dream-house.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3 there is one sleeper, one carrier and one runner to the clocks. At 6 there are two of each.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": The holes and the Nightmare, uncovered by being asleep. There is no reason to hide (the setting hides them).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Max carries Pia straight into a hole she called as "left" (his left or hers). Both fall. Pia's call and Max's step were both heard and seen.
- Extra rules (2): holes reshuffle when nobody sleeps; the Nightmare goes to the longest sleeper.
- Risk: the sleeper sees and the carrier acts, which is close to the Keep Talking split. A "rotate every 30 s" plan card may settle it (R ~).
- Novelty: the search found no match (Dream Eaters.exe and Somnum 2 are dream co-op games with other cores). Source moment: carrying a passed-out friend.

### MB-8 Bulkheads
- Rule (one sentence): Every hatch on the sinking sub can only be opened or shut from its dry side, so you work the doors for the friend running toward you.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Sailors on a sinking submarine racing to the escape pod, each also wanting their own keepsake back from their bunk (semi-co-op: shared escape plus a private score).
- Aesthetic (one line): A side-on cross-section of green-gray steel, red emergency lamps and water rising room by room.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I hold hatch 4 open for Dan while water pours in behind him. I can seal it to save my room or give him two more seconds, and he shouts how far he is. Then he is on the dry side of hatch 5 and holds my life.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Leaks break out in new rooms during the round, so which side is dry, and so who controls which hatch, flips. Each sailor's secret bunk pulls them off the shared route.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The hatches.
- At 3 / at 6: The sub's length scales with the crew. At 3 every hatch decision is one-on-one; at 6 there are queues at hatches and the question of who goes through first.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": Whose bunk is where, uncovered by watching where each sailor runs. You hide it because the crew won't hold a hatch for your detour.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ivy seals hatch 2 with Dan two steps away, and Dan drowns in room 1. Everyone saw Ivy's seal.
- Extra rules (2): leaks spawn during the round; private bunks.
- Novelty: Grim Repair (a sinking vessel where you close doors and can abandon teammates) is close, and Barotrauma too (from memory). The one-sided hatch changes who decides. N ~. Source moment: holding the door as the monster comes.
