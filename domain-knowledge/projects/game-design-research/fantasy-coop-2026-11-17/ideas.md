## 1. Imps

**Fantasy (Claude):** we pick the witch's orchard at night, an imp rides on each of our backs, and **only a friend can see it and slap it off**.
One-player test: with one picker nobody can see or reach the imp, so the line doesn't work alone ✅.
Does one player want and fear the same thing? No.
Premise, searched: *Do You See Sparky?* (a monster the marked player can't see and friends can) was announced in March 2023 and has no release date; its Steam description now reads as 4 against 1 with a traitor. Lethal Company ships the act as one enemy among many (the Snare Flea). **Different because** every friend carries one all night and the slap is the core act.

**Feelings, each about a friend**
| Feeling | Games that cause it between friends | The players are | What forces the moment there | Does this idea have that? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Get it off me, Ben!": I can't fix this and he can | Lethal Company's Snare Flea; Left 4 Dead's Jockey and Smoker | one crew | an enemy that latches onto one player and that only a teammate's hit removes | ✅ the imp is that enemy, with one rule |
| "Sorry, sorry!": I hurt Ana while saving her | Lethal Company (the shovel swing that frees a friend can hit the friend) | one crew | the rescue and the harm are the same swing | ✅ the slap staggers the friend |
| "Is mine big? Tell me": I act on Cy's word about my own back | none shipped (*Do You See Sparky?* is unreleased) | | | ❌ no game behind it |

**The rules, one sentence each (copied from the Snare Flea and the Jockey first, then moved to the orchard)**
1. Every picker starts with one imp on their back, and an imp grows the whole night.
2. A picker can't see or reach their own back; friends can see every back but their own.
3. When the imps on one back are big enough together, they ride the picker: the legs walk to the pond, and the picker loses the basket and ten seconds.
4. A friend's slap knocks every imp off that back; the imps shrink to small and jump to the nearest other back, which is usually the slapper's.
5. The slap staggers the friend, who spills part of their basket.
Job: fill the cart with baskets before dawn.

**The choice:** slap Ben's back now and take his imps onto mine, or keep picking and let them ride him.
**What Ben decides in the same second:** turn his back to me and stop picking, or keep picking because I said they're still small.
**What Cy decides:** step in close so the imps jump to him, because his own is small.

**Scene, 3 friends**
- Ana, Ben and Cy pick three trees. Ben's imp is the size of a cat; he can't know.
- Ana says "Ben, yours is big" and walks over. Ben keeps picking the last branch.
- Ana slaps. Ben spills a third of his basket, and the imp lands on Ana next to her own.
- Two imps together reach the size faster. Cy says "Ana, turn around" and has to choose between his full branch and her legs.
- Ana gave Ben his legs back and paid with her own back; Ben lost fruit to a slap he delayed; Cy holds what Ana needs to know.

**A perfect crew's round:** imps grow whatever the crew does, so slaps and jumps happen all night ✅.
**The plan by night 5:** "Ana and Ben stand together and slap every imp back and forth between their two backs; everyone else picks with a bare back."
Each slap shrinks the imps, so two friends can hold all of them all night.
At 3 that leaves one picker the idea never touches, and at 6 it leaves four, picking side by side, which is the user's own drop reason ("requires no cooperation at all") ❌.
Whether it beats spreading the imps depends on how fast imps on one back grow together, and that number isn't worked out, which the method counts as a fail.

**Checks (author's marks)**

|---|---|
| The fantasy line fails with one player | ✅ |
| Every feeling is about a friend and has a shipped game | ❌ two of three |
| The idea has what forces the moment in those games | ✅ a latched enemy only a friend removes |
| Something gets worse when nobody acts | ✅ imps grow |
| No solo way out (name them) | ✅ only if a tree, a wall or the pond can't scrape an imp off; written as a rule here |
| Both ends decide in the same second | ✅ slapper, slapped and the one who steps in |
| A perfect crew's round still has the core act | ✅ |
| A fixed plan before night 2 still wins night 50 | ❌ two friends who keep every imp between them |

| A dropped family or an earlier drop reason applies | ❌ open: by night 5 it may be "friends keep one shared thing going" with the imps as the thing |
| Low content | ✅ one orchard, one creature, one rule |

**Verdict: fails as written.** One rule to rework (where a knocked-off imp goes), then the ratio.

## 2. Candles

**Fantasy (Claude):** we empty a dark house by candlelight, and **only a friend's flame can relight mine**.
One-player test: one burglar with a dead candle is finished, so the line needs a friend ✅.
Premise, searched: no co-op game with this rule found in one search (TorchR Runners, a co-op platformer with torches that burn down, is unreleased). Sky: Children of the Light has the act between strangers.

**Feelings, each about a friend**
| Feeling | Games that cause it between friends | The players are | What forces the moment there | Does this idea have that? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relief when Ana reaches me in the dark | Left 4 Dead (a pinned survivor is freed only by a teammate); PEAK (the helping hand; "Grab my hand" is the line its reviewer heard most) | one crew | an enemy that picks off whoever is alone; a mountain that drains stamina | ❌ a gust is dealt by the house, with no enemy and no mountain behind it |
| "I'll come back for you, Ben": I spend my own safety on him | PEAK (carrying a passed-out friend pauses their timer and costs your backpack); R.E.P.O. (a dead friend is a head you carry out by hand) | one crew | whatever downs the friend, plus a carry that costs the carrier | ✅ the tilt that relights him costs my wax |
| The warmth of giving Cy my light | Sky: Children of the Light | strangers in a shared world, no pressure | nothing forces it; it is offered | ❌ the relation differs |

**The rules, one sentence each**
1. A candle burns down all night, and its wax is that friend's clock.
2. Running, a gust or a fall puts a candle out; in the dark a friend sees only other flames.
3. A lit friend relights a dark one by touching wicks, and the tilt costs the giver a slice of wax.
Job: carry out what the list names before the last candle is gone.

**The choice:** go back down for Ben, whose candle went out in the cellar, and burn my wax on the way, or keep carrying the clock upstairs while he feels his way toward my light.
**What Ben decides in the same second:** stay where he is and wait, or walk blind toward the nearest flame and risk the stairs.

**A perfect crew's round:** gusts are dealt, so candles still go out and relights still happen ✅.
**The plan by night 5:** "the nearest lit friend goes back", which is the plan Hold the Door was dropped for ❌.
A crew that walks as one tight group relights for free and only loses time, so one ratio (trip time against wax) decides the night, the same shape as Sleepover ❌.

|---|---|

| Every feeling is about a friend and has a shipped game with the same relation | ❌ one of three |
| The idea has what forces the moment in those games | ❌ no enemy that hunts the one who is alone |
| Both ends decide in the same second | ✅ |
| A perfect crew's round still has the core act | ✅ |
| A fixed plan before night 2 still wins night 50 | ❌ nearest goes; stay in one group |
| An earlier drop reason applies | ❌ Hold the Door's and Sleepover's |

| Low content | ✅ one house, no creature |

**Verdict: fails.** Adding the thing that hunts whoever is dark makes it Left 4 Dead's rule, which is an enemy to build.

## 3. Watched

**Fantasy (Claude):** **each of us is only safe while a friend is looking at us.**
One-player test: alone, nobody is ever safe, so the line needs a friend ✅.
Premise, searched: the reverse (a thing that can't move while watched) is shipped many times: SCP-173, Lethal Company's Coil-Head, DON'T LOOK AWAY. No game found where the watched one is a friend.

**Feelings, each about a friend**
| Feeling | Games that cause it between friends | The players are | What forces the moment there | Does this idea have that? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Keep your eyes on it, I'll work": Ana watches so that I can turn my back | Lethal Company's Coil-Head (guides say one crewmate stares while the others loot) | one crew | an enemy with one rule, plus the other monsters and the corridors | ✅ the rule; ❌ nothing else in the house |
| "I looked away": Ben was taken on my watch | SCP-173 in multiplayer (a blink meter forces every watcher to lapse; players call their blinks) | one crew | a clock on the watcher's own eyes | ✅ if eyes tire by rule |
| "Is anyone watching me?" | none found | | | ❌ |

How it fails there: a Lethal Company player on Steam calls the starer's job "pretty boring", sitting with the camera still.

**The rules, one sentence each**
1. A thing in the house creeps toward any friend that no friend is looking at, and takes them when it arrives.
2. A job needs your eyes on it, so a working friend watches nobody.
3. A watcher's eyes tire and close for a moment every few seconds.

**The choice:** keep my eyes on Ben while he works the lock, or look down at my own, which leaves him unwatched.
**What Ben decides in the same second:** keep working under my tiring eyes, or stop and look up at Cy, whom nobody has.

**The plan by night 5:** "two friends face each other with the workers between them."
Both watchers are watched, every worker is watched, and nobody is ever taken, at 3 and at 6 ❌.
The two watchers decide nothing all night, which is the Coil-Head's own documented failure and the check "an end that only stands still fails" ❌.

| Both ends decide in the same second | ❌ the watcher stares |
| A safe state | ❌ two watchers facing each other |
| An earlier drop reason applies | ❌ Sleepover's: help is a place to stand and a direction to face |
| 1 human + bots, Discord, lag | ✅ looking is the price, and a bot can look |

**Verdict: fails.**

## 4. Dolls

**Fantasy (Claude):** **each of us carries a little doll of a friend, and what happens to the doll happens to them.**
One-player test: nobody to hold, nobody holding me ✅.
Premise, searched twice: games where the players are voodoo dolls exist (Voodolls, 2023; Voodoom, unreleased). None found where a player holds a friend's doll.

**Feelings, each about a friend**
| Feeling | Games that cause it between friends | The players are | What forces the moment there | Does this idea have that? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "I'm carrying Ben": his life takes up my hands | R.E.P.O. (a dead friend's head, carried by hand to the extraction point); PEAK (a passed-out friend on your back) | one crew | monsters or the mountain down the friend first; the carry then costs hands or the backpack | ❌ nothing downs anyone here |
| "You dropped me!" | R.E.P.O. (sourced in the first run for dropped loot, not for a dropped friend) | one crew | physics loot and monsters | ❌ not sourced for a friend |
| "Who has my doll?" | none found | | | ❌ |

**Stopped here, before any choice.**
In both games the carry matters because an enemy or level content already hurt the friend.
A doll with nothing after it is a hand that is always full, and the two ways to give it a threat are a creature that goes for dolls (content) or places only a lifted doll can reach (levels), both ruled out by the formula.
The method says a base problem is a fail until fixed.

**Verdict: fails at the research step.** The premise is free, and the cheapest reading of it is a way to move a friend, which needs places to move them to.
