### G201 Diver's Eyes
- Rule (one sentence): The key log holding the jam looks like every other log from above, so someone must dive under the jam to find it, and they can only come back up where friends have levered a gap open.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the floating driftwood jam (one physics pile).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the diver sees the key log from below, is running out of air, and picks a gap to come up through. The players above must lever that gap open, and that rolls the logs their friends are standing on. Once the diver is up and says which log is the key, the levers must shove it from where they stand. The diver picks where; the levers pick how and when.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a fishing village clearing a storm's driftwood jam from the harbour mouth before the fleet comes home in the dark.
- Look (one line): a grey harbour, a tangle of bleached driftwood, a diver's bubbles rising between the logs.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one diver, two levers and one key log. At 6, two key logs and two divers, with four levers splitting between two gaps.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": which log is the key. It is uncovered only by diving under. The jam hides it; no player does.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ola runs out of air under the jam because Pip levered the log next to her gap and rolled it shut. Or the fleet arrives because the levers shoved the log beside the one Ola called.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each jam is a generated physics pile, and the key log is a different log every time.

### G202 Bed Board Tunnel
- Rule (one sentence): The crew crawls an escape tunnel one person wide whose only props are the boards already holding up its roof, so every board moved to a newly cracking spot leaves bare roof over somebody else.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the boards along the tunnel roof (which body is under which board).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): sand starts trickling over Sam. The nearest board is over Dana, two bodies back. Dana must crawl clear before it comes down, but nobody can pass anybody, so the players ahead must shuffle forward too. Then the board goes hand to hand to Sam. The digger at the front needs boards to push forward. The last board at the back comes free only once the last player has crawled past it. The back player decides when to pull, the middle players decide whether to keep or pass, and the front decides when to dig.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): prisoners of war digging out under the fence before dawn roll call, shoring the tunnel with boards from their bunks.
- Look (one line): side view of a sandy tunnel lit by fat lamps, bed boards over crawling bodies, sand trickling down.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, 3 players, 4 boards and a short tunnel. At 6, 6 players, 7 boards and a longer tunnel. Cracks open at a rate tied to crew size, and every seat sits under a board someone else may need.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (a crack trickles sand for 3 seconds before it falls).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the roof falls on Ben two seconds after Ana pulled the board above him to pass it forward. Everyone saw the sand pour where the board had been.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): the soil is generated in bands: soft sand cracks often, clay rarely, and roots slow the digger. Cracks open at random spots, so the boards shuffle differently every round.

### G203 Foam Bulkhead

  shoving anything soft out of the way, then sets rock-hard around whatever is still inside it.

- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - A breach's water jet washes soft foam away, so one player blocks the jet with a hatch board while
    another sprays around them.
  - The blocker must pull out just before the foam sets, or be set in. A trapped player is freed only
    when a friend chips them out with a pick.
  - The sprayer's timing depends on when the blocker is braced, and the blocker's pull-out depends on
    how much foam was just sprayed.
  - Swelling foam from one patch shoves friends working on the next one.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): the crew of a sinking paddle steamer patching holes to
  reach the shore.
- Look (one line): a dark wooden hull, yellow foam bulging and crusting over, crew stuck in it up to
  the neck.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: blocker, sprayer, and a chipper who also bails.
  - At 6: two blocker+sprayer pairs, a chipper and a bailer.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the blocker is set into the foam, nobody is free to
  block the next breach, and the hold floods. The foam that trapped them is tinted with the sprayer's
  colour.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content):
  - New breaches open where the water pushes hardest.
  - The hard foam already placed changes the hull's weight and trim, so breaches open in new places
    every run, and each hull is the crew's own patchwork.
- Search (swap-in for the magnet idea): co-op ship-patching games exist (Grim Repair, Piece of Ship,
  Barotrauma), but none uses foam that swells, shoves and sets with people inside (N ~).

### G204 Threshers

- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the slammers want to slam often because beetles pile up, while the gatherers need the gap open.

- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): beetles spread by one simple rule, grain lands at random, pole layouts vary (1–3 pairs at angles), skewed poles follow physics, and roles rotate.

### G206 Crib the House
- Rule (one sentence): A corner of the house rises one block only when one player heaves its
  jack and a different player slides a block under it at the top of the heave. If the lifter lets
  go first, the corner drops back.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the house, standing on one timber stack per corner.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The lifter picks when to heave and holds for at most ~1.5 s.
  - The slider picks when to shove the block in, and needs a block in hand.
  - Blocks are planks floating past in the flood. A third player hooks them out.
  - Neighbouring corners may differ by at most 2 blocks, so the corner a friend just raised
    decides which corner I must raise next.
  - Lift, slide and hook rotate as players move round the house.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a farm family jacking the farmhouse up on timber
  stacks while the river floods.
- Look (one line): a tilted wooden house on wobbling stacks of timber, with brown water rising.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: a triangle hut. One player lifts, one slides and one hooks planks, and the jobs rotate
    every heave.
  - At 6: a hexagon house. Up to three lift-and-slide pairs work at once. Planks are scarce, so one
    or two players hook, and the flood rises faster.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a corner crashes down, the house tips past 2 blocks
  and slides off its stacks into the water. Blame is visible at that corner:
  - the block lies on the mud beside the stack: the slider missed;
  - the block was still in the slider's hands when the corner fell: the lifter let go early.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each round
  generates the ground under each corner (rock, or mud that sinks a stack one block every N
  seconds), the plank stream (lengths and timing), the house outline for the crew size and the
  flood rate. Which corners sink, and when, is new every round.

### G207 Bell Pour
- Rule (one sentence): One player tilts a crucible of molten bronze into a clay mould whose
  channels branch to several vents, and each vent must be plugged by another player at the instant
  metal reaches its mouth. Too early traps air and the bell cracks there; too late spills metal.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the mould (a tree of channels and its vents), fed by
  the crucible.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The pourer controls the flow rate but cannot see the vents.
  - Each plugger sees only the vents near them and runs between them.
  - A surge from the pourer changes which vent fills next.
  - The pourer slows or speeds up on the pluggers' calls ("two filling at once, slow!").
  - The pourer's job rotates with every bell.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a village foundry crew casting the town's bells
  before the furnace dies.
- Look (one line): a dark shed, glowing orange streams, and sparks spitting from the vents.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: one player pours and two run to cover about 4 vents.
  - At 6: two crucibles pour into one large mould from two gates, and four pluggers cover about
    8 vents. The two metal fronts must meet while still hot.
- Hidden: none. The channels are drawn on the mould, and metal glows through the clay.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a vent overflows beside the plugger who was late.
  Or the bell is rung at the end and cracks at the vent someone plugged early, and the crack shows
  whose vent it was. The clock is cooling metal: if the stream stops for ~2 s, the channels freeze
  (a cold seam the pourer caused), and the crucible cools over ~40 s.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each mould is a
  random branching channel tree from one small generator (vent count, branch lengths, heights), and
  bells grow larger through a run. Round 50 has a channel layout no one has poured before.

### G209 Teeterboard Troupe
- Rule (one sentence): The player on the low end of a teeterboard flies only if they jump in the

- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):

- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a circus troupe trapped in a flooding quarry, ringing

- Look (one line): striped costumes against grey stone, and big splashes.

- Hidden: none.

- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): generated board

### G223 Stage Fight
- Rule (one sentence): In a staged brawl every attack is wound up in plain view toward one body zone, and its target must show the matching block before it lands; a blow that lands is a real injury.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the stage, watched by one simple audience that leaves when the fight goes slow or repeats.
