# critic-baseline

What the critics for co-op game ideas are measured against, and how far to trust them.
The question the user wants answered is "is this idea fun with friends or not" (user); popularity moves with time and trends, so no critic tries to predict it.

## The comparing critic: shipped games as the examples of fun
One isolated judge reads an idea next to ten shipped co-op games and says yes, maybe or no.
The games, their sheets, the ladder and the judge's brief are in the fun-with-friends folder (listed in the shared index).
How it was checked:
- 14 shipped co-op games, from 687,000 Steam reviews down to 139, each written up as a rule sheet under a made-up name.
- A blind judge ranked 10 of them from the rules alone, and its order followed the review counts closely (rank match about 0.94, where 1 is perfect).
- A second blind judge placed the 4 games kept aside into the right tier, 4 of 4.
- The older checklist critic (count the severe problems) scored the same 10 games with a rank match of 0.20, and put two of the three biggest hits below games almost nobody played.
The seven things the judge looks for, most important first:
1. A player's action changes what a teammate must do in that same second.
2. A failure becomes a story that keeps the failed player involved (carried, filmed, revived, dragged along), not a silent wait.
3. Voice is under a rule (fades by distance, heard by creatures, recorded, cut off).
4. A repeated "one more, or leave now" call where the whole crew shares the loss.
5. One player holds information the others need and can't see themselves.
6. Every round differs without the crew seeking new content.
7. The target rises, so every run ends in a failure the crew caused.
How far to trust it:
- The judge may recognise famous games from their rules, and less popular games have thinner sheets; both could explain part of the match.
- A new idea has neither leak, so a "yes" for a new idea is softer than the numbers above.
- A "yes" is a yes on paper; the pass gate is the user playing a small build.

## Date and novelty (reported next to the verdict, never part of it)
In the 14 games, every one that brought a new core act is a hit, and every one that followed an earlier game found few players.
"New" was marked by Claude after seeing the review counts, so it leans toward the result.

## The hole-finding critic: problems a shipped hit has too
A critic that counts severe problems fails Lethal Company on its design alone, so its fail is not a verdict on an idea.
These problems are listed in a round and never fail an idea by themselves:
| Problem | In the hit |
|---|---|
| A choice is answered by a tool that shows the answer | "go and look for the quiet friend" is answered by the map in the van |
| A help is free when the helped friend puts their load down first | the pull-out, after dropping the piece (leans on Claude's wording) |
| Bought tools lower the need for friends | radios, the van seat, the pull-out |
| A choice is one sum for the whole crew, with no friend in it | "one more load or leave" is a table by day and quota gap |
| One end of a choice only stands | the friend who stares at the thing that moves unwatched |
| The worst loss is avoided by one friend who stays out | one friend never leaves the van (leans on Claude's wording) |
| The crew settles into fixed roles with a card of answers, and the fear only happens in mistakes | by the second run: one in the van, pairs, one row per kind of thing |
| One player alone can still finish | true of every one of the ten shipped games checked |
The user's limits stay a fail whatever the hits do (voice by distance, speech as the price, many kinds of creature, generated buildings, a shop, moments bots can't test).
