# M4 random stimulus (de Bono random entry), gen1

## Method log
- Wrote both lists first: 80 nouns (30 objects, 15 places, 16 animals, 19 materials) and 40 physical verbs. Then drew 24 (noun, verb) pairs with `random.SystemRandom().choice` in Python. I did not pick or re-roll any pair.
- Forced one idea from every pair, and a second idea from three pairs where the first was dead. That gave 27 raw candidates. 4 pairs gave nothing usable (3 owl catch, 9 pigeon stack, 11 greenhouse hang, 21 bicycle push). Kept 8 with 8 different core rules.
- Most common fail: F3 player coupling. The verbs push toward one shared physical object, so ideas kept collapsing into a pooled force (kite lines, crew weight), parallel play (flock, leash, dig line) or one player who sees and directs (cow hoist, crank lamp). The next most common fail was N novelty: re-skins of Octodad, Pico Park, Jenga, Among Us and shared-vehicle station games.
- Good at: physical verbs turn straight into rules where physics carries the coupling and the blame, so F1, F4 and F7 come almost for free. Animal nouns gave the best ideas (ant, snail) because they bring a real behavior with them.
- Bad at: every kept idea is body-and-physics co-op. None is semi-co-op, and hidden information appears only once (Thin Floors). The pair rarely suggests a time pressure, so I had to add one by hand each time. Material and place nouns were often dropped after the forcing step. Two pairs drew "broom", which narrowed the spread.

## Kept ideas

### M4-1 Thin Floors
- Rule (one sentence): Each player is stuck alone on one floor of a flooding house, and things move between floors only through the hatch in each ceiling: you knock them up to the player above with a broom, or stomp to shake them down to the player below.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the stack of ceiling hatches that every item passes through. Extra rules: water rises from the bottom, and when your floor floods you climb up and share the floor above.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a knocked item lands only if the player above is at the hatch with free hands. Otherwise it falls back and breaks. A stomp sends my item down but also shakes loose whatever the player below has hanging there. The items needed on the roof are spread over all floors, so every player is both sender and catcher, and when to knock depends on what the neighbor is doing right now.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): neighbors in an old apartment block during a flood, banging on floors and ceilings to save what matters.
- Look (one line): a cutaway dollhouse with one lit room per player, rain on the windows and water creeping up the stairwell.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, three floors with short trips, and every player works both ends. At 6, items pass through more hands. The bottom player floods first and moves up, so the crew squeezes together as the water rises, and nobody's seat goes idle.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": each room is seen only by the player in it: which shelves hold what, and whether the hatch is blocked. Others uncover it by asking over voice. Nobody gains by hiding it.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the clock reaches floor 4, falls back and smashes because the player on 4 was at the window. Or the last item sits on floor 2 when the water arrives, because someone stomped it down by mistake. The blame falls on the player who knocked or stomped at the wrong time, and everyone hears it ("I said WAIT").

### M4-2 Ant Raft
- Rule (one sentence): The crew floats down a flood as one raft made of their own linked bodies. Players in the bottom layer keep it afloat but lose air, and can come up only when someone from the top climbs down into their spot.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the raft, a pile of linked players. Extra rule: at least half the crew must be in the bottom layer, or the raft sinks.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): top players see ahead, push logs away and grab floating food. Bottom players kick to steer but can't see and are running out of air. A swap needs both players at once: if the top player is busy pushing off a log, the one beneath stays down. A top player's best move changes every time a friend below runs low on air.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): fire ants in a flood, holding on to each other until you reach dry ground.
- Look (one line): chunky cartoon ants on muddy brown water, debris tumbling past, bubbles rising from the ones underneath.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, two are on the bottom and one on top, so the lone top player must choose whom to relieve. At 6, three and three, and swaps can be paired or staggered. Odd crews round the bottom layer up.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none. The bubble trail shows how much air each player has left.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a bottom ant runs out of air and lets go, so the raft tips and splits on a log. The blame falls on the top player who stayed up grabbing a leaf instead of swapping in, and everyone saw them up there.

### M4-3 Rubber Band Ring
- Rule (one sentence): The crew is tied in a ring by stretchy bands, so a player who lets go of the ground gets flung by the pull of their two neighbors, and that fling is the only way to cross a gap.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the ring of bands. Extra rule: the ground crumbles behind the crew.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): to send one player across, the two neighbors grip the ground while the flyer walks back to stretch the bands and lets go. The flyer must grip the moment they land, or the stretched bands drag them back into the pit, and then they become an anchor for the next fling. When to grip or let go always depends on what a neighbor just did.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): climbers roped together with bungee cord, crossing a canyon that is falling apart.
- Look (one line): bright suits and glowing colored bands stretched tight over the drop.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, a triangle, where every fling needs both other players. At 6, a hexagon, where two flings can happen at once on opposite sides, but bands link only neighbors, so a far player can't help.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): an anchor lets go to jump early, the stretched band yanks both neighbors off the ledge, and the ring falls in. The blame falls on the one who let go, seen as the first body to leave the ground.

### M4-4 Slime Crate
- Rule (one sentence): Every player leaves a slippery slime trail where they walk, which lets a heavy crate slide but makes feet slip, so the crew must slime the crate's path ahead of it and push it from dry ground.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the ground and its slime trails, one color per player. Extra rules: slime dries after a few seconds, and frost creeps in from the edges.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): slimers walk ahead and so choose where the crate goes. Pushers must stay off slime to get grip, so a slimer who wanders takes away a pusher's footing. Slime dries fast, so slimers must stay just ahead of the pushers' pace, and pushers must keep up with the fresh trail.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): snails moving their house to a new garden before the frost.
- Look (one line): glossy snail shells, a big shell-house crate, glistening colored trails on garden stones.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one slimer and two pushers. At 6, two slimers whose trails can cross and four pushers competing for dry ground. Players switch roles whenever they like.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a pusher slips, the crate stalls on a dried patch, and the frost reaches it. The blame falls on the player whose color shows in the slime under the slipping pusher.

### M4-5 Wax Bridge
- Rule (one sentence): A player swinging on a rope over a cold lake drips hot wax that hardens into stepping stones where it lands, and the walkers can stand only on stones, which sink a few seconds after someone steps on them.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the wax stones on the water.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the swinger can drip only along the arc of their swing, so walkers must route along it. Walkers decide when to jump, so the swinger must choose whose next stone to make first. The swinger reacts to the walkers and the walkers react to the swing, so neither side just follows.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): candle-makers escaping a flooded workshop.
- Look (one line): warm candlelight, steaming drips, pale wax stones bobbing on black water.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, one swinger and two walkers. At 6, two swingers on ropes from different beams, with crossing arcs, and four walkers. Roles swap at each shore.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a walker jumps to where a stone should be, but the swinger dripped it for the other walker, and the walker splashes in. The blame is visible: everyone saw where the drip landed and who jumped.

### M4-6 Blanket Toss
- Rule (one sentence): All players but one hold the edges of a big blanket and toss the flyer up to grab fruit from very tall trees, and the flyer must land back on the blanket.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the blanket. Extra rule: birds take fruit left too long.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the flyer steers in the air, so the holders must run together to be under the landing spot. Each holder's pull tilts the throw toward their side. The flyer picks the targets, and the holders control height and angle. After each catch, the flyer swaps with a holder.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): villagers at a harvest festival in a forest of giant trees.
- Look (one line): a patchwork blanket, huge orange fruit, a flyer tumbling against the sky.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, two holders make a narrow stretcher that tips easily, so every throw needs care. At 6, five holders make a wide blanket that works only if all of them run together.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the flyer comes down at one holder's edge, but that holder ran the wrong way, and the flyer hits the ground. The blame falls on that holder, whose corner is visibly empty.

### M4-7 Sunbeam Relay
- Rule (one sentence): The only light in a cave is a sunbeam from the entrance, and the crew carries it deeper by bouncing it from one player's hand mirror to the next. Anyone outside the light slowly freezes.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the beam. Extra rule: the sun moves, so the angle of the beam coming in keeps changing.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): my mirror angle depends on where the player before me sends the beam and where the player after me is standing. When anyone moves, everyone after them must re-aim. The first player follows the moving sun, the middle players relay, and the last player burns through cobwebs to open the way deeper.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): explorers carrying daylight into a frozen cave.
- Look (one line): one hard gold beam zigzagging through blue-black rock, frost growing on whoever is left in the dark.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, the beam bounces three times and the caves are shallow, with sharp turns. At 6, a long zigzag chain where one slip leaves many players in the dark.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": the cave ahead is dark and is uncovered by swinging the beam forward. Nobody gains by hiding it.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the beam stops at one player's mirror because they turned to look at a noise, and the three players after them freeze. The blame is plain: the beam visibly ends at that mirror.

### M4-8 Rug Ghost
- Rule (one sentence): A ghost hides as a lump under a huge rug and scoots away from any broom pound, so the crew must pound it back and forth between them into a sack held open at the rug's edge before dawn.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the rug. Extra rule: the ghost speeds up as dawn nears.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): my pound sends the lump toward a friend, who must pound just as it arrives to turn it, or it slips under their feet and trips them. The sack holder runs along the rug edge to wherever the lump is heading, and is a different player each round.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): housekeepers of a haunted manor, beating a ghost out of the carpet.
- Look (one line): a giant faded rug, a wobbling lump racing under it, brooms coming down in candlelight.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3, two pounders and the sack holder on a small rug, so every pound must be exact. At 6, five pounders around a big rug who can trap the lump, but who also pound it into each other.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none. The lump shows through the rug.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): one player pounds hard from the wrong side, the lump shoots off the rug's edge and escapes through the wall. Everyone saw that player's pound.
