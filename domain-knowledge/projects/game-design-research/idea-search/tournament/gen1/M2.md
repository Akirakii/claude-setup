# M2 "real-world joint activities" — gen1 (2026-11-10)

## Method log
- Listed 36 real activities where a small crew does different, timed actions and a slip shows (no web search, cooking skipped). For each I wrote its coupling: who does what, what each needs from the others, how a failure shows. Then I turned the coupling into one game rule, taking the fantasy and look from the activity.
- Raw count: 36 candidates. I kept 8 with 8 different core rules: moving in the dark on crossing paths, gaps between cars rolling downhill, blows that land after they're committed, closing a net around a fleeing school, throwing to whoever will have a free hand, pushes that roll a friend's footing, release-and-catch timing, and a swaying stack of bodies.
- The check raw candidates failed most was F3. Many real crews pool one effort (lifting, pulling, paddling: raft, barn, big top, tugs, lifeboat), so it becomes one shared sum. Others are run by one caller (cox, surgeon, crane signaller, tug pilot), so they become a quarterback. Next most often failed was F5: pair activities (crosscut saw, rally co-driver, tandem) don't stretch to 6. Then N: couch carrying, rope teams and volleyball already exist as games.
- Good at: visible blame and fantasy come for free. A real slip is physical and shows on the spot (dropped club, cracked bar, derailed car). Physics or one simple rule (flocking, cooling, sway) supplies the opposition, so content stays low.
- Bad at: real crews are built to remove blame and talk (one leader, rhythm, pooled effort), so the raw ideas lean toward F3 failures. The fantasies are narrow trades. The kept ideas cluster in one family ("physical object plus timing"). Nothing semi-co-op came out, because real crews have no traitor.

## Kept ideas

### M2-1 Blackout Change
- Rule (one sentence): Between scenes the stage goes dark for 20 seconds and each player must move one piece of scenery onto its glowing floor mark; when the lights snap on, the audience sees every piece off its mark and anyone still onstage or hit.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the dark stage floor with its glow-tape marks.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): paths cross in the dark, so I wait or go around based on what others just called out ("stairs coming through centre!"). The fly player lowers a backdrop from the rail without seeing the floor and picks the moment from the deck crew's "clear" calls, while the deck crew must get out from under it. The big wagon needs two pushers who match speed and turn together. Actions: fly a backdrop on a rope, push a wagon, carry a prop, flash the one torch.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): the stage crew of a small theatre on opening night, invisible to the crowd.
- Look (one line): a black stage with glowing tape dots and faint silhouettes, then a hard white light and a row of audience heads.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = three pieces per change (one flown, two carried), few crossings. 6 = six pieces, a two-person wagon, two flown pieces, many crossings. The change time stays 20 s. Pieces come from a list of ≤ 20, and their marks are shuffled each scene on one stage.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": where everyone is in the dark. You find out by calling out, or by one quick torch flash that lights the stage for the crew; the audience also sees the flash, and it costs applause, so the crew keeps the dark.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the lights come up with the sofa a metre off its mark and the lead actor trips on it. The player who pushed the sofa is standing beside it in the light. Three booed scenes end the show.

### M2-2 Hump Yard
- Rule (one sentence): Freight cars roll one at a time down a hill into a fan of tracks, and the crew must cut each car loose, throw its switches and ride and brake it, so it meets the cars on its chalked track gently before the shift ends.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the track fan below the hill (its switches and the rolling cars).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the cutter's release timing sets the gap between cars. If two cars bound for different tracks run too close, the switch player can't throw the switch between them and shouts "hold!". Riders walk back up after each car, so the cutter releases only when a rider is ready. A rider brakes according to how far down the track the last car stopped. Cutter = when, switch player = where, riders = how fast.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a night crew in a 1950s railway yard, sorting a long freight train before the morning trains leave.
- Look (one line): a floodlit yard from above, chalk numbers on the car sides, sparks from the brake shoes.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = cutter, one switch player, one rider (the cutter must pace the train to the lone rider's walk). 6 = cutter, two switch players (upper and lower switches), three riders, and a longer train. One yard; the car order and destinations are random each shift.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (each car's track number is chalked on its side).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a boxcar slams into the cars ahead and jumps the rails, spilling crates, and its rider is seen braking late. Or a car runs down the wrong track while the switch player is still pulling the lever, a beat too late.

### M2-3 Striking Gang
- Rule (one sentence): The holder moves a glowing bar on the anvil while strikers swing heavy hammers that land one second after they start, and the bar must be beaten to the target shape before it cools, because a blow on cold metal cracks it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the hot bar on the anvil (its shape and its glow).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a striker commits a blow at a spot. The holder sees the swing start and slides or turns the bar so the right part lands under it, or pulls it clear. The other striker must swing on the off-beat (two hammers at once bounce off each other) and aim where the holder is moving the bar. The holder decides when to go back to the forge to reheat, which stops every striker. Holder = place, turn, reheat; strikers = where and when to commit.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a village forge crew rushing to make an anchor before the ship sails.
- Look (one line): a dark smithy, a bar fading from orange to red to grey, a shower of sparks with every blow.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = one holder and two alternating strikers. 6 = a long anchor shank held at each end by two holders who must move it together, and four strikers in two pairs on either side. Target shapes come from a list of ≤ 20, and everyone sees the target drawn on the wall.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the bar cracks with a loud snap right where a striker hit it after it turned grey. Or the bar flies off the anvil because the holder slid it into a falling hammer. Either way, one person is seen at the spot.

### M2-4 Purse Seine
- Rule (one sentence): One boat lays a net in a circle around a fleeing fish school while the skiff holds the net's other end, and the winch player pulls the net's bottom shut, which must come after the circle closes and before the school dives out underneath.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the net (a floating ring between the two boats, open at the bottom until it's pulled shut).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the school flees boats and noise by one simple flocking rule. The seine driver chooses how wide and fast to circle. The skiff fights the current and chooses when to swing in to meet the seiner. The winch player watches how deep the school is (how dark the shoal looks) and how close the two boats are, then chooses when to close the bottom. At 4–6, chase boats drive the school toward the net or block its escape, but their noise also makes it dive.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a sardine purse-seine crew at dawn, filling the hold before the boat has to head in.
- Look (one line): a blue sea seen from above, the cork line drawn across it in a ring, a silver flashing shoal.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = seine boat, skiff, winch. 6 = those three plus three chase boats, against bigger and faster schools. One sea; the schools are flocks, not hand-made.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (the shoal's depth shows in how dark it looks).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the shoal pours out of the gap beside the skiff because the skiff swung in late, or dives under because the winch closed too late. The leak happens right beside one person's boat.

### M2-5 Passing Troupe
- Rule (one sentence): Each player has two hands, more clubs join the air every 20 seconds, and you must throw every club you catch on to any friend within two seconds; a club that reaches someone with both hands full hits the floor.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the clubs in the air between players.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a throw takes a second to land, so I throw to whoever will have a free hand when it lands. That depends on what they just caught and who else just threw to them. A player about to be swamped can throw early to free a hand, which pushes the problem onto someone else. A long throw across the ring buys time but is harder to catch. At any moment one player is catching, one picking a target and one clearing a hand early.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a circus passing troupe in the big ring, the act getting harder every chorus.
- Look (one line): a spotlit circus ring, each club leaving a trail in the colour of whoever threw it.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = a triangle starting with 4 clubs. 6 = a ring starting with 7; throws to a neighbour are fast and throws across the ring are slow.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a club bounces in front of a player whose hands are both full. Its colour trail shows who threw it into full hands. A club fumbled by a catcher with a free hand is that catcher's drop.

### M2-6 Log Jam
- Rule (one sentence): Players stand on a jam of floating logs above a mill dam and pry the logs loose with poles before the water tops the dam, and every log you push rolls or shifts the logs your friends are standing on.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the floating log pile (one physics pile).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): before I push, I check who stands on that log or its neighbours, and I call it. A player can hook a pole into a log to hold it still, so a friend can cross it or push from it. The key log holding the jam (it bows and creaks) moves only when two players push it from different sides at the same moment. When the jam breaks, everyone runs for the bank over rolling logs, and the order matters. Actions: push, pin a log, cross.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): 1890s river drivers breaking a log jam in the spring flood.
- Look (one line): brown spray, a tangle of pine logs, drivers in red wool with long pike poles.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = a small jam with one key log: one player pins while two push. 6 = a bigger jam with two key logs that must go in order, and more bodies rolling logs under each other. Each round's jam is a generated pile on one river.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none (logs under strain bow and creak).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a player is rolled into the water and the logs close over them. The player who pushed that log is standing on the next one with their pole still out. Or the water tops the dam because the two key-log pushers didn't push together.

### M2-7 Flying Trapeze
- Rule (one sentence): The only way across the big top is to let go of your swinging bar and be caught by a friend hanging from another bar, so the flyer's release and the catcher's swing must meet at the same moment.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the row of swinging trapeze bars (their swing timing).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the catcher pumps their swing to reach the meeting point, and the flyer reads that swing and picks the release. After a catch, the catcher swings the flyer on and throws them toward the next bar, whose person must already be in step. Someone on a platform must push an empty bar out at the right time. At 6, flyers cross from both ends, so a catcher chooses whom to catch and the other flyer must wait a swing. Actions: release (flyer), pump and grab (catcher), send the bar (platform player).
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a family flying-trapeze act on its first night in a new town; everyone must swap ends of the tent before the music ends.
- Look (one line): a striped big top seen from the side, bars on long ropes, a net below, spotlights following the flyer.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = two bars and two platforms, and each crossing needs all three (one sends the bar, one catches, one flies). 6 = four bars with traffic both ways. Bar spacing and rope lengths are generated each show.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the flyer drops into the net with a loud bounce and the crowd groans. The release point and the catcher's swing are both on screen, so it's clear who was early or late. The faller climbs the ladder again while the music runs out.

### M2-8 Human Tower
- Rule (one sentence): Players climb onto each other's shoulders to build a tower whose top must touch a high flag and come down again; every body sways the tower, so each person below must lean against the sway the climber above just made.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the tower itself (one stack of bodies with shared sway).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the climber picks when and where to step, and the bases lean to cancel it. The middle layer holds still for the climb, then leans once the top is up. Each base's strength drains while it carries weight, so climbers weigh speed against how tired the bases are. Actions: base (lean, hold), middle (climb, then hold and lean), top (climb, touch, come down).
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a town-square human-tower team at a festival (Catalan castells).
- Look (one line): a sunny square, the crowd packed close, coloured sashes, the tower swaying.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = two bases and one top. 6 = three bases, two middles and one top, with higher flags. Flag heights and gusts of crowd-push are generated.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the tower folds to one side, and everyone sees which person gave way first: a base who leaned the wrong way or a climber who stepped too hard.
