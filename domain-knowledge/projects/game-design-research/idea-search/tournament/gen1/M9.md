# M9 "single-player graft" (gen1)

## Method log
- Steps: listed 26 low-content single-player genres or activities (golf, falling blocks, climbing, marble run, sheepdog trial, dodging sunlight, laser mirrors, road driving, and more), then gave each one coupling where each player does a different action and the result is not a pooled sum. Two bases (driving, golf) got a second try, so the list has 28 raw candidates. Then checked each against formula.md and kept 8 with 8 different core rules.
- Raw count: 28 candidates from 26 bases.
- Most common failures: N (novelty): the first coupling that comes to mind is "split one avatar's controls" (car, lander, runner, tilt maze) or "stations on one vehicle" (fishing boat), and both already exist. "Build the path ahead of a chaser" (dominoes, sled line) is the train-track-laying co-op game re-skinned. Next most common was F3: "friends are the terrain" grafts (glider updrafts, tightrope holders, traffic lights) turn the helpers into pressure plates who hold still or obey.
- Good at: F1 and F7. Single-player bases are already one cheap system (physics, a falling-piece rule, a flock), so low content and a long tail come for free, and time pressure usually comes from the base.
- Bad at: meeting "a different action for each player" without an extra list of tools or parts, which adds content and pushes on F2. Making 6 players as busy as 3. Anything hidden: none of the 8 hide anything (F6 is n/a everywhere). Novelty also tends to land at ~ rather than ✓, because the base genre is known.

## Raw candidates
1. Golf: one player putts, the others stand on the green as bumpers, and the ball must touch every bumper before the hole.
2. Stacking blocks: one player stacks, and each other player holds one leg of the tilting table under the tower.
3. Physics climbing: a shaft with no holds, where you climb only on friends who wedge themselves against the walls.
4. Falling blocks: each piece falls through every player's band, and in your band you can only turn it, shift it, or drop it.
5. Rhythm game: your hits become the next player's notes, in a ring.
6. Driving time trial: a long road train where each player steers one trailer and the last player has the only brake.
7. Driving time trial (second try): one player steers, one works the throttle, one brakes the same car (split controls).
8. Fishing: a crew on one boat, where the reeler sets tension, the helmsman sets angle and the gaffer lands the fish. The line snaps when tension plus angle is too high.
9. Marble run: each player carries one track part (ramp, cup, spring, fan) and keeps live marbles moving to the goal cup.
10. Sliding puzzle: each player slides only the row or column they stand on, carrying friends with it, and edge tiles fall off.
11. Bullet dodging: light beams join the players in a ring, and bullets die on beams but end the run if they touch a player.
12. Endless runner: one player moves, one jumps and one ducks the same runner (split controls).
13. Sandcastle: the builder shapes walls, the carrier brings the wet sand that hardens them and the digger cuts channels, and the castle must outlast the tide.
14. Block-pulling tower: each block needs one player pushing and another pulling from the opposite side.
15. Tightrope: a walker crosses a rope whose support points are held by friends standing on crumbling pillars.
16. Gliding: the pilot rises only over updrafts that ground players make by standing still on high spots.
17. Tilt maze: each player turns one hinge of a jointed board.
18. Domino toppling: lay dominoes ahead of the falling front, each player placing only one kind of piece.
19. Laser mirrors: a beam from a moving source must reach a moving target through the optics the players carry, and the beam burns any player it touches.
20. Sheepdog trial: each dog moves the flock its own way (push away, pull in, freeze).
21. Dodging sunlight: the shade you carry falls only on friends, never on you.
22. Juggling: you can't catch a ball you threw, and each player throws only one way (lob, straight, bounce).
23. Auto-walk puzzle: nobody can stop walking, and each player has one tool (stairs, dig, block, umbrella).
24. Sonar cave: your ping lights up the cave only on your friends' screens, never your own.
25. Road crossing: one player crosses, and each other player sets the gaps in one lane of traffic.
26. Line-drawing sled: drawers lay lines ahead with limited ink while the rider leans.
27. Lunar lander: each player fires one thruster.
28. Human cannonball: one player aims the cannon, the flyer steers in the air, and two players run with the net.

## Kept ideas

### M9-1 Drop Relay
- Rule (one sentence): Each falling block passes down through every player's band of the well, and inside your band you can do only your one move (turn it, shift it sideways, or speed it down).
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the well and its stack of landed blocks.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a new block enters the top every beat, so every band always holds a block. Turners can only rotate, shifters can only slide, and the bottom player only chooses when to slam it down. A turner must set up a block the shifter below can still place. A shifter must fix whatever angle the turner left. Neither can do the other's move.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a crew of crane operators stacked floor by floor in a tower, passing crates down a chute into a cargo hold before the ship sails.
- Look (one line): a tall, narrow well split into colored bands, with each player's name glowing in their band.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 the bands are turn, shift, slam. At 6 they are turn, shift, turn, shift, turn, slam: more blocks in flight, and each move can be undone or improved one band later.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the stack tops out after a vertical bar left band 2 turned flat, and the shifter below had nowhere to put it. Everyone saw the turner do it.

### M9-2 Body Ladder
- Rule (one sentence): The shaft walls have no holds, so you can climb only by stepping on friends who wedge themselves across the shaft, and a wedged player's grip runs out faster with every person standing on them.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the shaft and its rising floor (flood water).
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): at every moment some players are wedging (holding still across the walls, grip draining) and others are climbing over them to wedge higher. When to unwedge and climb depends on who is wedged above you right now and how much grip they have left. Wide sections need two players to link arms to span the gap.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): miners climbing out of a flooding chimney shaft after the ladder washed away.
- Look (one line): a dark vertical shaft lit by helmet lamps, rising water below, bodies braced wall to wall.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is a tight leapfrog where every move matters. At 6 the stacks grow tall, the bottom wedger drains fast, and players must hand off quickly.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): one player unwedged to climb while two friends stood on them, and all three dropped into the water. Everyone saw who let go.

### M9-3 Shifting Floor
- Rule (one sentence): Each player can slide only the row or the column they are standing on (rows or columns by their color), which carries every friend on that line along, and the tile pushed off the edge falls away.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the tile floor, which shrinks with every slide and crumbles at the rim over time.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): row players move people sideways and column players move them forward. Reaching the exit needs both. My slide also moves every friend on my line, so after each of their slides I must check who now stands where before I slide.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): thieves escaping a vault whose floor is a giant sliding puzzle over a pit.
- Look (one line): a grid of stone tiles over darkness, with glowing row or column arrows under each player's feet.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is two row players and one column player, a small grid and few moves. At 6 the grid is bigger and three row and three column players crowd each other's lines.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a row player slid their row to reach the exit, and the edge tile under a friend fell into the pit. That one slide is the whole cause, seen live.

### M9-4 Hand-Carried Marble Run
- Rule (one sentence): Marbles drop from a chute at a steady pace, and each player carries one kind of track part (ramp, cup, spring, fan), placing and angling it live to pass the marbles across gaps to the goal cup.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the stream of marbles.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the ramp player tilts, the cup player catches and holds a marble up to 2 seconds, the spring player bounces it up a ledge, and the fan player pushes it sideways in the air. Where I stand depends on where the friend before me just sent the current marble. With several marbles in flight, I also juggle which one to serve.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): toy-shop elves keeping a giant marble machine running by hand after it broke on opening day.
- Look (one line): a wooden toy room with bright glass marbles, each elf hugging an oversized track part.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is ramp, cup and spring over a short course. At 6 all part kinds appear (two ramps), the gaps are longer and more marbles fly at once.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the third marble is lost because the spring player was still setting up for the last marble and the cup player released into empty air. The drop happens right at one person's part.

### M9-5 Three Whistles
- Rule (one sentence): Each sheepdog moves the flock its own way (the barker pushes sheep away, the bell dog draws them in, the stare dog freezes the ones near it), and the flock must cross a cliff path to the pen before dark.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the flock, a crowd of sheep that follow simple steering rules.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): a bark scatters sheep, so the bell dog must gather the strays. A pull drags the flock toward the cliff edge, so the stare dog must freeze the front sheep. Each dog's effect is only local, and the right move always answers what the last dog did to the flock.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): farm dogs bringing the flock down the mountain at dusk with no shepherd.
- Look (one line): a green ridge at sunset, a fluffy white flock flowing like water, and colored rings showing each dog's reach.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 there is one dog of each kind. At 6 there are two of each, with a bigger flock that splits into two groups that must be rejoined.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the barker barked at the cliff bend and five sheep went over the edge. Everyone saw the bark ring and the sheep run.

### M9-6 Shade for Each Other
- Rule (one sentence): Sunlight burns, and the shade you carry falls only on the friends behind you, never on you, so you live in a friend's shade or in a building's shadow.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the sun and the shadows it casts, which shorten and turn as the sun rises.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): each player carries a different shade. The parasol is small and quick, the door is large and slow, and the cloak works only while its carrier stands still. My safe spot is wherever a friend's shade falls right now, and my shade protects whoever stands behind me. The front player hops between building shadows while the line behind follows. When one person moves, the person they shaded starts to burn.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a vampire family racing home through a waking town at dawn, holding parasols and doors over each other.
- Look (one line): pale gold morning light on cobblestones, with long blue shadows the family huddles inside.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is a short chain where everyone shades someone. At 6 it is a long, fragile convoy, and the leader changes at each building.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the door carrier stepped forward too early to reach the next shadow, and the friend behind burned to ash in the open street.

### M9-7 Live Laser Chain
- Rule (one sentence): A beam from a moving source must stay on a moving target by bouncing through the optics the players carry, and the beam burns any player it crosses.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the beam.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): mirror players bend the beam, the lens player restores its strength (it fades over distance), and the splitter player (at 5 and up) splits it to reach two targets. Where I stand and how I angle depends on where the last friend just sent the beam, and my angle can send the beam across a friend's body.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a lighthouse crew bouncing the light across a storm by hand to guide a drifting ship home.
- Look (one line): a dark stormy coast with a single bright beam zigzagging between people holding mirrors.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is two mirrors and a lens with one target. At 6 it is mirrors, a lens and a splitter with two targets, and the beam paths cross more people.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a mirror player turned to dodge a wave, and the beam swept across the lens player, who dropped and lost the target. That one turn is visible.

### M9-8 Road Train
- Rule (one sentence): The truck has one trailer per player, each player steers only their own trailer's wheels, and the last player holds the only brake, on a winding road against the clock.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the truck, a chain of linked trailers.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the front player steers the cab and sets the line. Each trailer player must steer to follow the trailer ahead of them through the bend without scraping. The brake player at the back slows the whole truck before tight bends. A wide line from the front forces everyone behind to correct, and late braking makes every corner harder.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): an outback road-train crew hauling an oversized load down a mountain pass before the road closes.
- Look (one line): a long, dusty, colorful truck snaking along a cliff road, each trailer with its driver's name painted on it.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 it is the cab, one trailer and the brake trailer. At 6 it is a long truck with four middle trailers, where errors grow as they pass down the chain.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): trailer 3 scraped the cliff on a hairpin and jackknifed. The scrape spark and the driver's name are right there.
