# M5 "failure inversion" (gen1, 2026-11-10)

## Method log
- Listed 15 co-op or party games from memory, then named each one's worst check and why: Overcooked 2 (F1: dozens of hand-made kitchens; also some parallel chopping), Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes (F5: built for one defuser, so at 6 the readers pile up with nothing to do; F1: hand-made modules), Pandemic (F3: open info, so one player directs), Hanabi (G: no time pressure and talk banned), The Mind (F3: everyone does the same job of playing a card), Codenames Duet (F3: the clue giver knows the answer and guessers act on it; F1: big word list), Among Us (F3: crewmates do tasks side by side), Lethal Company (F3: the quota is one pooled sum; F1: hand-made monsters), Left 4 Dead (F3: everyone shoots side by side), Spaceteam (F4: one pooled hull bar, so no one knows whose missed order sank it), Human: Fall Flat (G: one good player can carry; F1: hand-made levels), Portal 2 co-op (F5: two seats only; F1: hand-made test rooms), Magic Maze (F4: when the timer runs out, everyone missed a bit; G: talk mostly banned), Werewolf (F5: eliminated players sit idle), Phasmophobia (F1: hand-made ghost types and houses; F3: everyone scans for evidence side by side).
- Inverted each failure into a rule meant to pass that check while keeping the source's best part. Several games gave two inversions. Raw count: 27.
- Most common failures among the raw 27: N (12 stayed too close to the source game or landed on another known game, e.g. one limb each is Octodad co-op, group counting is a known party game). F3 was next (7): inverting a quarterback often just made a smaller one, such as a spotter who tells the shooter where to aim, so one end only obeys. Then F1 (4): inversions of word-list or module games kept the list.
- Good at: F3 and F4. Starting from "whose fault was it?" pushed most candidates toward one visible culprit and different actions per player.
- Bad at: novelty, because the source game anchors the shape. Also F7 tail, since few inversions touched replay value, and talk-banned games, where lifting the ban mostly gave a re-skin.

## Kept ideas

### M5-1 Mess Chain
- Rule (one sentence): Every cleaning turns one kind of mess into the next kind, which only the next player in line can clean, while the first kind keeps spreading.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the grid of floor tiles and the mess on them.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): each player owns one tool and one mess colour. My mess appears exactly where the player before me just cleaned, so where I run depends on their last move. I also choose whether to clean near the next player or far away from them. The last player's cleaning leaves a clean floor.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): night janitors in a haunted manor where slime turns to mud, mud to water, water to soap, and so on.
- Look (one line): top-down tiled rooms with bright colour-coded puddles that spread like ink.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 the chain is short and spread is slow. At 6 the chain is six steps long, so the first mess must be cut fast and the line is easier to jam. The player order is reshuffled each round.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): one colour piles up and floods a room. That colour belongs to one player, so everyone sees whose stage jammed, or whose drops landed somewhere no one could reach.

### M5-2 Shove Crew
- Rule (one sentence): No one can walk; you can only shove a friend away from you, so the crew crosses the board by pushing each other.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the players' bodies on one board.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): where I can go depends entirely on who stands behind me and when they shove. My best shove depends on where the last shove just left each friend. At any moment one player lines up, one shoves, and one is the one being moved.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): clumsy penguins escaping melting ice by shoving each other across it.
- Look (one line): flat ice floes from above, cracks spreading, and round penguins skidding in a line.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 every shove matters and someone always ends up stuck behind. At 6 it is a crowded, chaotic billiards game, with more angles and more people in the way.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a penguin skids into the water. The one who shoved is seen doing it, at the angle they chose.

### M5-3 Door Chain
- Rule (one sentence): Each player places one door, and walking into any door drops you out of the next player's door, so the crew's route is a ring through everyone's doors.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the ring of doors, one per player, in a fixed order.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): where you come out is decided by the next player in the ring, not by you. I place my door for the player before me while I also travel, and my best spot depends on where they just walked and where the goal is.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): thieves with chalk doors, robbing a vault that is filling with water.
- Look (one line): side-on generated tower of ledges, with chalk-drawn doors that glow in each player's colour.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 the ring is short and each person handles a lot. At 6 the ring is long, so routes have more hops and moving one door breaks more routes.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a friend jumps into a door just as the next player moves theirs, and lands in water. The player who moved their door is seen moving it.

### M5-4 Loud Bait
- Rule (one sentence): The horde always runs at whoever made the latest noise, and every useful action makes noise.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): one horde that has a single target at a time.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): when you hammer a repair you pull the horde to you, so I must make a louder noise far away to take it off you. Then someone else must take it off me. Whether to act or wait depends on who just made noise and where the horde is running.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a stranded band fixing their tour bus at night while zombies hunt sound.
- Look (one line): dark generated scrapyard, with a sound ring flashing over whoever is the target.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 one person works and two pass the horde between them. At 6 several repairs run at once and the horde bounces often.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the horde runs through the person doing the repair. The sound ring shows who made the noise that pulled it there.

### M5-5 Living Tower
- Rule (one sentence): Only the player at the bottom of the stack may touch the burning ground, so the crew crosses as a wobbly tower that keeps swapping who is at the bottom.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the stack of players' bodies, held together by physics.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): the bottom player walks and burns, the middle players balance, and the top player reaches for ledges. When the bottom player's burn runs high, someone must climb down to take over at the right moment, and the others shift to keep the tower upright.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): acrobats crossing a lava field after the circus fell into a volcano.
- Look (one line): side-on circus acrobats in bright leotards stacked over orange lava, with the bottom one's soles smoking.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 the tower is short and swaps come often. At 6 the tower is tall and wobbly, and it can tip over to make a bridge across a gap.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the tower topples into the lava. Everyone sees who let go or who climbed down at the wrong moment.

### M5-6 Haunt in the Crew
- Rule (one sentence): One crewmate is secretly the haunt and can wreck things only while no one is looking at them.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): everyone's sight beams, shown to all.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): jobs such as reading a gauge or aiming a valve pull your eyes away, so I must watch whoever you just stopped watching. Watchers and workers keep trading. The haunt asks for jobs that leave them unwatched.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a lighthouse crew keeping the lamp lit through a storm, with one of them possessed.
- Look (one line): one generated lighthouse floor plan with torch beams sweeping and rain on the glass.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 two honest players have to keep watching the third while still doing jobs. At 6 there are more eyes but also more gaps to hide in.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": who the haunt is. Uncovered by watching and by working out who was unwatched when each sabotage hit. The haunt hides to win. A wrong accusation costs time; no one is eliminated.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the lamp goes out. The beam record shows whose look-away left the gap the haunt used.

### M5-7 Unseen Keys
- Rule (one sentence): Each player wears a colour they can't see, doors open only for that colour, and touching another player swaps your colours.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the players' colours, which only others can see.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): I need you to tell me my colour, and the colour I need for the next door may be on someone I must bump. Each bump changes two players at once, so whatever I was told goes stale as soon as I touch someone.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): blindfolded thieves escaping a vault whose doors read the gem pinned on your back.
- Look (one line): top-down generated vault with coloured doors and glowing gems on players' backs.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 there are few colours and every bump matters. At 6 there are more colours to track and more accidental bumps in corridors.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": your own colour. You find it out by asking a friend. No player has a reason to hide it; the game hides it.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): someone walks into the wrong door and sets off the alarm. Everyone heard who called the colour and saw who walked.

### M5-8 Hand-to-Hand Kitchen
- Rule (one sentence): A dish can never be set down, and each player's tool works only on a dish they are holding, so every order is passed hand to hand in recipe order.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the dishes moving between players' hands.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): each player owns different tools. Who I pass to next depends on the recipe, and where I stand depends on who just took the dish before mine. Several dishes are in play at once, so the line keeps reordering itself.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a cooking crew on a ship's galley in a storm, where nothing can be put down.
- Look (one line): one cramped rocking galley, with steaming plates going from hand to hand.
- At 3 / at 6: at 3 each player holds two tools. At 6 there is one tool each and more dishes going at once. The recipe list is 20 or fewer.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a dish is dropped or reaches the wrong tool and spoils. Everyone sees who passed it.
