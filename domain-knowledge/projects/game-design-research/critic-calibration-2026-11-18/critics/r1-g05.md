# Critic round 1: g05, Linked Climb

Judged from the proposal alone, against the baseline and the two anti-pattern notes.
Note on method: the Read call loaded the whole critic-anti-patterns file, last section included; nothing from that section is used here.

## The problem underneath
- The fantasy needs a friend in words only: the rules let one player climb and finish with no chain at all.
- The rules do cause the three listed feelings: the chain makes one late jump pull everyone down, and makes a haul possible.
- All three feelings come from a mistake; none comes from a round played well.

## The scene, second by second
- 0 s: four in a row on a beam, an hour above the last safe landing; the front player counts.
- 1 s: two jump, one is late, one says "not ready"; nothing in the rules stops the count once it is said.
- 2 s: the chain goes tight; from here nobody has a decision left, the physics finishes the scene.
- The only decision in the scene was "jump on the count or not", and the right answer was known before the count: stop.

## Fewest and most players
- 1 player: no chain, no coupling, a plain jumping course; this is the easiest way to play the game.
- 2 players: one chain link, one count, two bodies that can fail.
- 4 players: the same count with four bodies that can fail; each added friend only adds a way to fall.
- In the line setup the front player leads and counts for everyone; the closed loop is the only setup without that seat.

## The plans
- The strategy that always wins: count before every jump, and one "not ready" is a full stop; waiting costs nothing outside the lava mode.
- By the fifth session: the course is fixed and memorised, the order on the chain is fixed, one voice counts, the same anchor points are used every time.
- A perfect crew's round: nobody falls, so nobody hangs, nobody is hauled, and no height is lost.
- What survives in that round: the swing or drag across a gap a plain jump can't cross, which the course forces.

## Findings
1. SEVERE, missing baseline part: no pressure that rises and breaks the plan. The count-and-stop plan works from the first platform to the last in the two main modes; lava is one optional mode at one steady speed.
2. SEVERE, missing baseline part: one threat only (the fall). There are no enemies, and the chain forbids the crew to be in two places, so nothing can ask for two answers at once.
3. Medium, only this design: one player alone plays and finishes (co-op point 6), and experienced crews practise alone first.
4. Medium, only this design: the core coupling is a timed joint jump, a rhythm and not a decision; the proposal's own "other end" line is "jump on the same count or brace".
5. Medium, only this design: nothing changes between climbs; one hand-made course becomes a lookup table (B21).
6. Medium, only this design: the front seat of the line setup holds the count and the route (a quarterback).
7. Medium, only this design: scale runs backwards; more friends make it strictly harder and give nothing a smaller crew lacks (B22), and everyone does the same job side by side (B24).
8. The baseline has this too (row 7, fixed roles and fear only in mistakes): the routine above, and the falls only happen in errors.
9. The baseline has this too (row 5, one end only stands): the hanging player "can do little" while the others choose.
10. The baseline has this too (row 4, one sum for the whole crew): short risky jump or long safe way, and the mode choice, are crew sums with no friend in them.
11. Minor, not counted: the beginner mode's teleport is a safe state the crew opts into; rankings are the only price.
- Not counted, unknown: how the wings work, what lava does on contact, own voice chat.

## The four baseline parts
- Priced help on good nights: present. Hauling a hanging friend risks the hauler's own height, and the forced swing across a gap puts one friend's weight on the others without anyone's mistake.
- Rising pressure that breaks last night's plan: missing. The stake grows with height, which only makes the same plan more careful.
- Two threats at once: missing. One threat, one crew, one place.
- Carry-over: present, thinly. Kept or lost height decides what the next sitting plays; nothing carries from one climb to the next.

## Co-op check
1. Coupled: pass. My jump fails if yours doesn't happen.
2. Split: fail. Everyone sees the same thing and does the same jump; the only role is the front seat.
3. Talk: fail. People talk, but no clock makes them; the talk is a count, and waiting is free.
4. Blame: pass. One late body, seen on the chain as it happens.
5. Not easy: pass. A new crew loses real height, down to the start, and the layers get tougher.
6. Not solo: fail. One player finishes.
7. Two-way: fail. One end hangs, or follows the count, or follows the route.
- Lone survivor: does not apply, nobody dies. Falling on purpose is a real priced choice ("let go to a safer platform"), not an exploit.

## Could not break
1. The blame: one person's failure is physical, visible and instant.
2. The chain as one shared physical thing: the coupling can't be met by standing in a place.
3. Haul or let go: both options cost something, and each wins somewhere.
4. The loss: reachable in one step, and it grows with height without any extra rule.
5. The goal and the first minute: "climb out together" needs no teaching.
