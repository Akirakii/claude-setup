## Patterns
- **R stops the most: 15 of the 17 that reach it, none pass.** About 12 ideas share the core "the threat hunts whoever holds, did or is most X". That rule is fully predictable, so one lookup line beats it ("act from cover", "hand it to whoever is farthest", "rotate"). The things these ideas say keep the plan from settling (random spots, generated maps, shifting hedges) change where to go, not which plan wins (Q2). Only R110 (guard posters built from the crew's own crimes, seen only up close) and R115 (a director who bans repeats, plus a live camera path) make a player need a teammate's news to pick the move.
- **G is next with 10 ✗, all "one end only obeys" or broken as written.** One-way seer splits create a quarterback (R101, R113, R137). Verbs that move a friend's body leave one end with no say: a puck (R109), a partner who only holds (R117), a yank on cue (R119). In R104 the receiver's answer is dominated (always accept). Two fail as written: R106's owl parks on a friend in cover (a free win) and R111's thrower always stays behind (soft-lock).
- **P stops 3 ideas whose friend verb is optional and worse than a solo move:** R102 (a pass only dumps the curse), R133 (an empty-door slam lures the witch for free) and R105 (logs land on a shared stone, not on a friend). The rule as written gives a reason to skip its own interaction.
- **F almost never stops an idea alone** (F5 idle seats in R123 and R125; F2 in R135 with 4 extras). Its ~ cluster on F6 (the setting hides something, but no player has a reason to hide it) and on F2 (3 extra rules).
- **Lag risk in 5 ideas** (R111, R115, R117, R119, R143): all are two-player timing duets with windows of 0.5 s or less. Duplicates: R106 ≈ R121 (swap places, the hunter follows the swap).
