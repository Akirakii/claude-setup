### R102 Cursed Hands
- Rule (one sentence): The crew carries cursed relics to an altar, each relic puts its own curse on whoever holds it, and you pass one by pushing it into a friend's hands, which they can shake off within a second.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Acolytes rushing cursed relics through a haunted abbey to seal them before the midnight bell.
- Aesthetic (one line): Candle-lit cloisters, relics glowing in their curse's colour, the holder's screen blurred, flipped or slowed.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I'm holding the Mirror Skull, so my controls are flipped. I push it into Tess's hands, and she has one second: keep it (she's flipped, I'm free) or shake it off (it drops and cracks; three cracks and it's lost). She decides by where she stands: on the narrow stairs, flipped controls kill her.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): A relic's curse is unknown until someone picks it up (one of: blind, flipped, slow, heavy, mute, giant). Relics appear in random rooms and the altar moves at each bell, so which friend should carry which relic changes every time.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The relics and the altar.
- At 3 / at 6: Relics = crew size − 1, so one person is always free to scout (2 at 3, 5 at 6).
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": The relic's curse, felt only by its holder (their screen and controls). Uncovered by the holder saying it, or by others watching them stumble. No reason to hide.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Tess shakes off the Heavy Bell on the last stairs; it cracks for the third time and is lost. Blame: Tess's shake-off.

### R105 Megalith Haul
- Rule (one sentence): A huge stone slides only over logs, and each log that rolls out behind it must be carried round and laid in front before the stone reaches bare ground.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the stone on its row of rollers.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action): Rope players pull the stone and set its pace. Runners grab the log freed at the back, run it forward and lay it, and the angle they lay it at turns the stone. As a runner I lay my log to fix or continue the turn the last runner made. As a rope player I slow down when no runner is coming with a log. On a downhill the stone rolls by itself, and rope players must switch to the back ropes and hold it, so the jobs flip with the ground. 2 extra rules: rope pull, log angle steers.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a stone-age village moving a standing stone to the henge before the solstice sunrise.
- Look (one line): pale chalk hills at dawn, a grey slab riding on pale logs, hide ropes, people in wool.
- At 3 / at 6: 3 = 1 on the rope, 2 runners, slow stone, short path. 6 = 3 rope, 3 runners, faster stone, longer path. Anyone can drop a rope and become a runner.
- Hidden (what, how it's uncovered, why anyone hides it) or "none": none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the stone's front edge passes the last log and digs in, or tips off a skewed log into a ditch. The gap is where one runner was due, still holding their log, or the rope player who kept pulling; everyone sees it.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): generated ground each haul (slopes, mud where logs sink, boulders and ditches to steer round). Logs split after a few passes, so supply varies. A skewed log turns the stone, so no two hauls take the same line.

### R108 Paint Prison
- **Rule (one sentence):** You splash friends with your paint colour, never yourself: coloured gates only open for that colour, and the guard drone always chases the colour most prisoners wear.
- **Fantasy:** You are prisoners breaking out of a colour-coded prison, where each wing's doors read uniform colour.
- **Aesthetic:** A clean white prison with fat splashes of red, blue and yellow. A drone's searchlight takes the colour it is hunting.
- **Player on player:**
  - Each player gets one paint colour per round: one of each at 3, two of each at 6. I splash Bo red, and he can now pass red gates (help).
  - If red becomes the most worn colour, the drone hunts the red wearers, Bo included (hurt).
  - Within seconds, Bo must use the red gate, get re-splashed by a friend to change the count, or run.
  - Ties go to the last colour splashed.
- **Why the plan can't settle:**
  - The gate order on the route is random per round, from 3 prison wings.
  - The drone hunts whatever colour the team is crowding into, so "everyone go red for the red wing" gets punished.
  - Which friend to repaint depends on the next gate, the current count, and who is nearest the drone.
- **Hub:** the colour count, which aims the drone.
- **At 3 / at 6:** At 3, any one splash makes a majority, so each splash is a big call. At 6, the count needs two or three splashes to flip, and more friends to choose from. Odd sizes are fine because ties have a rule.
- **Hidden:** none.
- **A failed run:** Kai splashes Ana blue for the blue gate, which makes blue the majority. The drone turns blue and catches Ana at the gate. The splash and the count change are on screen. Blame: Kai.
