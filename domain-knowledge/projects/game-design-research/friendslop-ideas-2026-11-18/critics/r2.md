# Critic round 2: five new ideas against the ten-sheet ladder

Read: the brief, `ladder.md`, g01–g10, and the five sheets. No web (task said to skip it). Each design is judged alone against the ladder.
Tags: (rule) = a written rule on the sheet says it; (assumed) = my reading where the sheet is silent.
Strictness used: a trait counts only when a written rule causes it; guess lines, feeling lines and scenes count for nothing.
"Quota crew" below means a crew that only wants to meet the target and takes no risk it doesn't have to.

Shared by all five (said once, applies to each):
- Trait 3 is "no" everywhere: every sheet says "Voice: none in the game", so no rule touches voice. g01, g02, g04 and g06 all have one.
- No shop and no items to buy, so cash above the target has no use except as a cushion for later nights (assumed from "cash toward the rising quota"). A quota crew stops at the target; "one more" is only chosen when the crew is short, which makes it forced, not a gamble. g01, g02 and g04 give the surplus a use.
- One place, no creatures (one crab in i02). Round-to-round change is placement only.

## i05 — Moonsick, the wolf we need

**0. Novelty.** Core act: one friend turns wolf to break doors for the haulers, loses control of the wolf as hunger rises, and friends must drop the loot to hold them and turn them back.
Not the core act of any ladder sheet. The shell (carry loot to a cart, rising quota, crew picks when to leave) is g01's.
web: not searched this round.
Mark: **near g01: different because the danger is a teammate whose control decays, and there are no creatures.**

**1. Seven traits**
1. Same-second effect on a teammate: **yes**. Rules: the wolf's pull toward the nearest friend; empty hands get bitten, silver in hands dazes the wolf; a hold needs two empty-handed friends for 3 seconds.
2. Failure becomes a story: **yes**. Rules: a bite is 5 seconds down; a second bite makes you a second wolf who keeps playing. Nobody waits dead.
3. Voice under a rule: **no**. No rule.
4. One more or leave, shared loss: **partly**. Rules: the wolf's "one more door" raises hunger; the crew picks when to leave. But banked cart silver is only lost if every friend is a wolf at once, so the shared loss is far away.
5. One player holds needed information: **partly**. Rule: "I see my own notches". It is one number, said aloud for free, and the drift shows it anyway.
6. Every round differs: **partly**. Rules: silver rooms move, door counts change, who starts bitten.
7. Rising target, crew-caused end: **yes**. Rule: quota rises every night, a miss ends the run.

**Best move for a quota crew.** Rotate the wolf. Each wolf breaks two doors that sit close together, stops (a wolf under 4 notches can still stop, by rule), is held at 2–3 notches for 3 seconds, and the next friend steps into the beam. The 2-minute lockout is per friend, so four friends cover it. Everyone not holding keeps one small silver piece in hand at all times, since silver makes a bite impossible (rule).
**Plan by the fifth night:** wolf by turns, two doors each, hold early, always carry a piece. At the end of the night the last wolf is simply left behind, because the cost is one bite tomorrow.

**Each feeling in that plan**
- "One more door, I've still got it": weak. At 3 notches the wolf only drifts, and the plan stops there.
- "He's drifting toward you, get behind the table": no. Everyone is holding silver; nobody needs a table.
- "Bring me back NOW, I can't turn away": no. Nobody reaches 6 notches.
- "Put the silver down and hold him / he bites me": no. Holds happen at low notches on a wolf that stands still.
The sheet's own last line says the run ends when "two doors per turn can't pay it", so the scary notches are only visited in the final night or two.

**2. Closest sheets:** g01 and g02.
Has that they lack: the hazard is a friend, the hazard role rotates, hands-full versus hands-free is a real same-second trade (g02 has it only for carrying).
Lacks that they have: creatures that surprise, a voice rule, a real wipe (g01 loses the ship's scrap, g02 restarts), a use for surplus, generated buildings.

**3. Fun with friends? maybe.**
The wolf-pull and the silver-or-hold trade are good rules, but the cheapest play never enters the range where they bite, and nothing random forces a crew into it.
Sits between **g06 and g07**.

**4. Holes**
1. Wolf rotation keeps hunger under 4 all night. Breaking move: the plan above. Tier A sheet with the same hole: none (g01's creatures arrive whether the crew is careful or not).
2. Silver is a full shield. Breaking move: everybody keeps the smallest piece in hand always. Tier A: none.
3. Leaving a wolf behind is nearly free (one bite next night, and a bite is 5 seconds). Breaking move: never go back for the wolf. Tier A: g03 shares it (experienced crews leave the fallen scout for the statue), so not held against it.
4. A hold needs empty hands touching the wolf, and a wolf touching empty hands bites. The sheet doesn't say who wins. Breaking move: none; it is an unwritten rule the whole game stands on.
5. A second wolf starts at 3 notches and can break doors too, so being bitten twice can pay. Breaking move: get one friend bitten twice on purpose when doors are the bottleneck. Tier A: none.

**5. Could not break:** once a wolf is at 4 or more, the hold really does cost the crew its shields; I found no way to hold without dropping silver. Nobody ever sits out.

**6. Smallest change (opinion):** the moonbeam works a fixed few times a night (say three), so each wolf has to break many doors in one turn and the crew meets 4–6 notches every night instead of only at the end.

## i02 — Hold On

**0. Novelty.** Core act: hang as a chain of bodies down a well, each holding the ankles above, and pass treasure up hand to hand while every grip drains with the weight below it.
Ladder: g05 (climb chained together) and g03 (pull and boost each other) are the nearest; neither has the bodies as the rope, private grip, or loot.
web: not searched this round.
Mark: **near g05: different because the chain is the players' own grips, each with a private timer, and it hauls loot down-and-up for a quota instead of climbing a course.**

**1. Seven traits**
1. Same-second effect: **yes**. Rules: grip drains by weight below; a grip that ends drops everyone below; shake-off; a crab kick swings everyone below for 3 seconds; a wrist catch doubles the catcher's drain.
2. Failure becomes a story: **yes**. Rules: a faller can be caught by the wrist and hangs there; otherwise the torrent returns them to the rim in 30 seconds. Short wait, not a dead watch.
3. Voice under a rule: **no**.
4. One more or leave, shared loss: **yes**. Rules: every piece is weight on everyone above; the sack is safe only on the rim; the top friend's fall takes the sack and the whole chain.
5. One player holds needed information: **yes**. Rule: only you see your own grip. Thin (one number, free to say), but everyone above and below needs it and cannot see it.
6. Every round differs: **partly**. Rules: niches, ledges, water height, crab target.
7. Rising target: **yes**.

**Best move for a quota crew.** Bank after every rich piece (the 3-second swing with a light sack), call grip as a countdown, and use shake-off as a routine tool: when the middle is nearly gone, drop the bottom on purpose, because a fall costs only 30 seconds and what is in the hands.
**Plan by the fifth night:** fixed order, counted grips, bank each piece, planned drops. The rising quota plus water covering the low niches first (rule) still forces the longest chain early in the night, and a long chain cannot be made light.

**Each feeling in that plan**
- "I'm slipping, drop the cup": yes. Depth needs bodies and bodies are weight; no plan removes it.
- "Don't let go": yes, but softer, since a fall is 30 seconds.
- "Got you! ...you're heavy": yes, on any fall past a hanging friend.
- "Who let go?": yes. Grip is private and shake-off is a button; planned drops make it a running joke rather than a betrayal.

**2. Closest sheets:** g05 and g03.
Has that they lack: a quota and a shared sack at stake; private grip; a button to drop your friends.
Lacks that they have: g05's fall costs half an hour of height, this one costs 30 seconds; g03 has many afflictions, items, a generated mountain and a fog; in both, every player moves all the time, here the middle links mostly hang.

**3. Fun with friends? yes.**
Its fun comes from the same place as g05's and g03's (one body's mistake lands on the bodies tied to it, by rule, every few seconds), and the cheapest play cannot avoid it because reach is made of people.
The risk is thin agency in the middle of the chain. Sits between **g05 and g06**, at the bottom of "yes".

**4. Holes**
1. Falling is cheap, so letting go is often the right move and "don't let go" loses its weight. Breaking move: planned drops as the way back to the rim. Tier A: g03 has a milder form (statue revives the dead), so partly shared.
2. Banking every piece makes the sack never worth saving. Breaking move: swing after each piece. Tier A: g01 shares it (crews drop scrap by the entrance as they go); not held against it.
3. Middle links have little to do: hold, pass, say a number, shake. Breaking move: none; it is a shortage, not an exploit. Tier A: g01's ship player is a similar low-motion role, but that role is chosen.
4. Movement is unwritten: how friends get down to a ledge, whether climbing over a friend drains anyone, how treasure from below a ledge reaches the rim. The "climb up over them" line is only in the other-end list. Tier A: none.
5. At three players the chain is three bodies, so "deeper is richer" has little room. Breaking move: none. Tier A: none (g01–g03 all work at two).

**5. Could not break:** the link between depth and weight. I found no formation that reaches rich niches without loading the top friend, and no way to see a friend's grip.

**6. Smallest change (opinion):** make a fall cost the crew something shared, for example the water jumps one row of niches each time anyone lands in it. Then letting go is a real loss again.

## i04 — Moonsick

**0. Novelty.** Core act: the friend nearest to turning wolf is the only one who can see the blooms, a clean friend must pick them, and a cloak shades one of them at the other's cost.
Not the core act of any ladder sheet. The split "one sees, the other acts" is g01's ship player and g10's order reader in small.
web: not searched this round.
Mark: **new** (against the ladder only).

**1. Seven traits**
1. Same-second effect: **yes**. Rules: the cloak holder stands in the light with both hands up; the wolf never stops and bites head-on; a hold needs two friends in the light; 3-second pour window.
2. Failure becomes a story: **yes**. Rules: the turned friend plays the wolf, is held and cured. A bitten friend keeps playing with more fur.
3. Voice under a rule: **no**.
4. One more or leave, shared loss: **partly**. "One more bloom" is repeated and a turn costs the crew trampled blooms and bites, but there is no leave call (the night runs to dawn) and what is in the cauldron is never at risk.
5. One player holds needed information: **yes**. Rule: only half fur or more sees blooms, only less than half can pick.
6. Every round differs: **partly**. Rules: clouds, bloom places, who starts at half fur.
7. Rising mark: **yes**.

**Best move for a quota crew.** The guide never walks out. No rule says the guide must stand in the light to see blooms, so the guide stands at the shade edge, steps in and out to stay just over half fur, and calls blooms by landmark. Pickers run out unshaded, pick, and come back before half fur; they rotate while fur shrinks. The cloak is not needed. If someone does turn, the crew stays in the shade (the wolf cannot leave the light, by rule) and does not pour, because a flask lowers the cauldron the mark is measured on.
**Plan by the fifth night:** doorway guide, rotating pickers, no cloak walks, no cure.

**Each feeling in that plan**
- "Come under the cloak, one more past the fountain": no. The guide points from the door.
- "Give me the cloak, you're too far gone": no. The guide's fur is held at the shade edge.
- "MOVE, I can't stop!": rare. A picker caught by a cloud is the only way to turn, and they start under half.
- "Hold him, pour it": no. Holding grows fur and pouring costs the mark; waiting in the shade is cheaper.

**2. Closest sheets:** g01 and g03.
Has that they lack: the seeing role and the danger are the same meter, so the most useful friend is the most dangerous one; the failed player becomes the hazard.
Lacks that they have: anything that hunts the careful (g01's creatures, g03's fog); a leave call; a wipe that loses stored goods; variety.

**3. Fun with friends? maybe.**
The fur rule that splits seeing from picking is the best single rule in the five, but the cheapest play keeps everyone in the doorway and the wolf, the cloak and the cure all fall out of use.
Sits between **g07 and g08**.

**4. Holes**
1. The guide can see from the shade. Breaking move: doorway guide. Tier A: none.
2. The cure works against the target (a flask lowers the cauldron). Breaking move: never pour. Tier A: none.
3. Being a wolf at dawn gives half fur next night, which is exactly the guide's starting state: the penalty is a gift. Breaking move: leave the wolf running. Tier A: none.
4. The wolf cannot enter shade, so the hazard has a wall around it. Breaking move: wait indoors. Tier A: none (g02's creatures follow).
5. Unwritten: what a wolf does when a cloud takes all the light, and what "keeps growing faster" means for something already at full fur. Tier A: none.

**5. Could not break:** the see-or-pick split itself. Nobody can do both, so two friends are always needed per bloom, and clouds are outside the crew's control.

**6. Smallest change (opinion):** blooms show only within a few steps of a furry friend, so the guide has to walk out into the light beside the picker.

## i01 — Gold Fever

**0. Novelty.** Core act: touching junk turns it to gold and the gold climbs your own body part by part; only a friend can take a part's worth into their own body; carry the gold up while the ship sinks under it.
Nearest ladder sheet is g02 (carry valuables together, a broken player is carried as an object).
web: not searched this round.
Mark: **near g02: different because making the loot cripples the maker, and friends are the only place to put the damage.**

**1. Seven traits**
1. Same-second effect: **yes**. Rules: the gripped friend picks which part to give or pulls away; other people's crates and chests need two; a stuck friend needs 4 seconds and two hands; the rope needs everyone walking.
2. Failure becomes a story: **yes**. Rules: a statue hops, is carried to the scale and can be sold, with a debt next night. The stuck-in-water friend does wait, briefly.
3. Voice under a rule: **no**.
4. One more or leave, shared loss: **yes**. Rules: the crew casts off together; if the water reaches the hatch first, everything on the scale is lost. The strongest version of this trait among the five.
5. One player holds needed information: **no**. No rule. Gold on bodies and water height are visible to all.
6. Every round differs: **partly**, weakly. Rule: the mix of crates, barrels and chests. One ship, nothing generated, nothing that moves by itself.
7. Rising quota: **yes**.

**Best move for a quota crew.** Everyone turns and carries their own crates (own gold is light, by rule). Gold is handed off into chests first, since the sheet lists no cost for a golden chest, then into legs while the hold is dry. Water rises only when gold comes aboard, not with time, so the crew counts crates and stops one short of the hatch. Cast off the moment the quota is on the scale. If short by about a statue, always sell: a debt beats the end of the run, so that is not a choice.
**Plan by the fifth night:** solo touch-and-carry, hand-offs in a fixed part order, a known crate count, one friend at the rope.

**Each feeling in that plan**
- "Take my arm, it's at my neck": yes, but on a schedule. Creep is the only clock and hand-offs are compulsory, so they still happen every night.
- "One hand left, I can't take any more of you": yes. Total gold only grows; late in the night bodies really are full.
- "Cast off NOW / one more crate": weak. The sinking is the crew's own arithmetic; nothing surprises them.
- "Sell Ben / Ben is hopping away": no. A statue who wants the quota hops to the scale. The line needs a friend playing against the crew.

**2. Closest sheets:** g02 and g03.
Has that they lack: the crew's bodies are the shared resource being spent, and the comedy of working parts (one hand, a dragging leg, a hopping statue) is caused by rule all night.
Lacks that they have: any uncertainty inside a night (g02's creatures and physics breakage, g03's fog and falls); hidden information; generated places; voice rule.

**3. Fun with friends? maybe.**
The body-part rules and the hand-off give constant physical comedy and need friends by rule, but a night has no surprise in it, so by the fifth night it is a sum the crew already knows.
Sits between **g06 and g07**.

**4. Holes**
1. The ship sinks only as fast as the crew loads it, so it never sinks by accident. Breaking move: count crates. Tier A: none (g01's midnight and g03's fog come on their own).
2. Best play is carrying alone. Breaking move: nobody touches a shared chest unless the mix forces it. Tier A: g01 shares it (hauling is solo); not held against it.
3. Hand-off order is solved (chest, then legs while dry). Breaking move: fixed order. The sheet also doesn't say whether gold moved into a chest goes on creeping from there. Tier A: none.
4. One friend can be the sink: stand at the rope, take everyone's gold, end as a statue with a one-hand penalty. Breaking move: a designated sponge each night. Tier A: none.
5. Selling a statue is never a real choice when short. Breaking move: always sell. Tier A: none.

**5. Could not break:** the need for a friend. Nothing but another body takes gold off you, and gold in the crew only goes up, so every night ends with crippled friends helping each other. The cast-off needing every walker is sound.

**6. Smallest change (opinion):** let the water also come in surges the crew doesn't control (a tide), so the hatch can arrive early and the cast-off call is a gamble again.

## i03 — Piggyback

**0. Novelty.** Core act: the sick ride on a healthy friend's back and point at herbs only they can see, while only the healthy can see the sinking mud.
Not the core act of any ladder sheet. The relay of sight by voice is what g01's ship player, g08's sonar reader and g10's order reader do.
web: not searched this round.
Mark: **new** (against the ladder only).

**1. Seven traits**
1. Same-second effect: **yes**. Rules: the rider points, the carrier walks and picks; carrier and rider sink together; a pull-out takes 4 seconds and both hands.
2. Failure becomes a story: **partly**. Rules: stuck pairs are pulled out, a passed-out friend is dragged. But stage 4 is a silent body until dawn, and stuck is a 4-second pause, not a story.
3. Voice under a rule: **no**.
4. One more or leave, shared loss: **no**. No leave call (the basket is weighed at dawn) and the basket is never at risk. "One more bloom" costs the carrier a stage, which one red bundle removes.
5. One player holds needed information: **yes**. Rules: herbs show by fever stage, mud shows only at stage 0, stage 3 also sees false blooms. Two-way, the strongest trait-5 among the five.
6. Every round differs: **partly**. Rules: herbs and mud move, stages carry over.
7. Rising quota: **yes**.

**Best move for a quota crew.** Keep the carrier clean. A carrier at stage 0 sees the mud themselves (rule), reaches stage 1 only after 30 seconds of carrying, and a red bundle puts them back to 0 (rule); the rider can see red moss and point at it. So a pair is self-sufficient: rider sees herbs, carrier sees ground, a red bundle every 30 seconds of carrying. The clean scout walking point is not needed. Blue reeds need no back at all: a stage-2 friend walks at half speed and sees them.
**Plan by the fifth night:** one stage-3 rider on a carrier who eats red on schedule; the rest walk at stage 2 for blue; the rider eats a silver every few minutes to stay off stage 4.

**Each feeling in that plan**
- "Left. Left! No, MY left": no. The rule says the rider points with an arm, and the carrier is facing the same way; no rule causes the mix-up.
- "STOP, there's mud in front of you two": rare. The carrier sees the mud; it only happens in the seconds between stage 1 and the red bundle.
- "Is that one real?": yes. The false blooms are a rule and no plan removes them; the price is a few carry-seconds.
- "You're coughing on my neck, I can't see the ground": brief, once per 30 seconds of carrying, then cured.

**2. Closest sheets:** g10 and g03.
Has that they lack: both sides are blind to something (g10's relay is one-way), and the body contact of carrying; the false blooms give doubt without any creature.
Lacks that they have: anything that can go badly wrong (the worst event is 4 seconds in mud; g03 has falls and a fog, g10 has one-hit deaths); a leave call; pace. The rider's whole night is an arm.

**3. Fun with friends? maybe.**
The split sight is a real, rule-made reason to talk, but nothing in the marsh is fast, nothing is lost, and the cheapest plan removes the mud from play; what is left is a calm pointing job, close to what the tier C and D relay sheets offer.
Sits between **g08 and g09**, at the bottom of "maybe".

**4. Holes**
1. Red bundles reset the carrier, so the carrier's fever is a small tax, not a threat. Breaking move: eat red every 30 carry-seconds. Tier A: none.
2. Mud costs 4 seconds. Breaking move: ignore it when a puller is near. Tier A: none (g01's mines kill, g03's falls injure).
3. Blue needs no partner. Breaking move: most of the crew walks alone at stage 2; if blue pays the quota, nobody rides. Tier A: g01 shares solo hauling; not held against it.
4. The rider has almost nothing to do, and a stage-4 friend has nothing at all until dawn. Breaking move: none. Tier A: g01's dead watch until the day ends, so partly shared.
5. "The clean friend sees mud only near themselves" is in the other-end list, not in the rules; without it one scout covers everyone. Tier A: none.

**5. Could not break:** silver needs a stage-3 rider and stage 3 cannot walk, so the richest herb always takes two bodies; the false blooms can't be told apart by any written means.

**6. Smallest change (opinion):** put the basket at risk: whoever carries it into mud loses half of it. Then the ground matters to the whole crew and "stop!" is worth shouting.
