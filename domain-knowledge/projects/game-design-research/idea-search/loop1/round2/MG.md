# Round 2, method MG: two-sided information

## Method log
- Listed 15 kinds of information that can be split by position: (1) which floor tiles are weak, (2) where the threat strikes next, (3) which exit is real, (4) what a friend carries, (5) when the next pounce or wave hits, (6) your own danger (only others can see it), (7) the threat itself, seen only in your own light or mirror, (8) goals versus hazards, split by who holds the object, (9) sound versus sight (heard on the ground, seen from the air), (10) near versus far (glare: you can't read what's close to you), (11) above versus below, (12) behind you versus in front of you, (13) a friend's air, health or fuse, (14) what each chute or lock wants, (15) where the threat is listening.
- Built every core act in two halves. The actor's half (aim, direction, who) uses what only the actor sees. The target's half (when, where to land, where to send it next) uses what only the target sees. The target's information shows up mid-act or only where they stand, so there's no time to say it out loud. Each end has to act on it themselves, and that's what keeps both ends deciding by round 5.
- Made the split change every round: layouts and the threat's pick are random each round, and a habit push (the threat drifts to the lanes or landing spots the crew used most) breaks repeated routes.
- Cut an idea when one side could say everything it knows before the act (the other end then just obeys), when the threat follows whoever holds something, when pairs were fixed (odd crews leave someone idle), or when one end only holds still.
- Kept 6 with different core rules. They're ordered by how sure I am that they pass P, then R.

## Raw candidates
1. Glowball: whoever holds the orb sees only the shadow hounds, and only non-holders see the goal rings; a catch flips who sees what. (kept MG-1)
2. Glare ice push-off: two touching players push apart, and each one's facing aims the OTHER; nobody can read the ice near themselves. (kept MG-2)
3. Roof hop: you leave a roof only when a friend throws you; the flier sees rotten tiles from above and picks when to drop, and the thrower hears which window opens next. (kept MG-3)
4. Breath: a shove sends a friend far only if they jump in the same half-second; everyone sees the beast except its target, who alone hears its breath. (kept MG-4)
5. Sun relay: you can't aim sunlight yourself, only flash it onto a friend's mirror; vampires show only in your own mirror, so the receiver aims. (kept MG-5)
6. Borrowed lamps: your lamp lights the floor only for friends, and you see the stalker only in your own beam, where it freezes. (kept MG-6)
7. Swap sight: two players swap places on a double press, and you see strike marks only around others. Cut: the swap moves you into the danger you saw, so there's no reason to accept.
8. Blind crates: you can't read the label on the crate you hold, and only the person at a chute sees its order. Cut: the thrower just obeys "throw it to me".
9. Bomb on the back: you can't see a bomb on your own back; the puller sees the wire, and the wearer hears the tick and spins. Cut: it's a rescue reaction, not a main verb, and the wearer only calls "now".
10. Leap of faith: the jumper feels the ledge going, and the catcher sees which floor below holds. Cut: the catcher only stands still, so one end holds.
11. Sense trade: touch hands to swap your one working sense with a friend's. Cut: "the front player takes sight" settles it.
12. Shield dive: dive in front of a friend, but the shield works only if they duck on the bowstring that only they hear. Cut: same split as Breath, weaker verb.
13. Back to back: two locked-together players each see half the room. Cut: fixed pairs leave someone idle in odd crews.
14. Mine cart switch: the rider sees the track and the switcher sees the cave-in. Cut: it needs many hand-made tracks, and the switcher becomes a caller.
15. Moon masks: unmasked players see the moon color, masked players see monsters, and you put masks on friends. Cut: two lookups, and the receiver just accepts.
16. Leech grooming: the host feels when the leech lets go, and the picker sees what kind it is. Cut: the host only calls "now".
17. Air share in a wreck: you see friends' air gauges, not your own. Cut: the receiver obeys "swim to me".
18. Shadow ride: blink into a friend's shadow; the rider sees behind, and the owner sees ahead and throws the rider out. Cut: by round 5 the rider only waits.
19. Ventriloquist: throw a clatter onto a friend's spot to pull a blind giant there, and only they see the hiding holes near them. Cut: the giant goes wherever the noise is, so it's fully steerable.
20. Echo click: a click lights a friend's surroundings for them only, and the beast hears clicks. Merged into Borrowed lamps.
21. Two-key doors: each player sees which door is real for someone else. Cut: a lookup puzzle with no time pressure; one end obeys.

## Kept ideas

### MG-1 Glowball
- Rule (one sentence): Whoever holds the orb sees the shadow hounds and nothing else, only players without it see the goal rings, and a catch flips who sees what.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Temple novices playing a sacred night ball game, racing to light every ring in the courtyard before dawn while shadow hounds try to snuff the orb.
- Aesthetic (one line): A dark stone courtyard, one warm orb arcing between robed figures, rings of blue fire flickering on and off the flagstones.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): As holder I see the hounds, so I pick a clear lane and throw to one friend. That friend sees the rings (they move and flicker) and picks which ring to stand in, when to call and whether to catch now or let it pass. Catching inside a ring lights it. The moment they catch, they're blind to rings and can see hounds, so now they need me (rings visible again) to call where to throw next.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Rings appear, drift and fade at random. Hounds drift toward the lanes the orb flew most this round, so the favorite pass dies. Who stands where changes on every catch, and both halves of the information swap owners on every catch.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The orb.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, one holder and two callers, so every pass is a real choice between two voices and two lanes. At 6, five callers shout at once and the holder must pick one voice and one lane through more hounds (hound count scales with crew).
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": The rings are hidden from the holder and the hounds from everyone else. You uncover the other half only by passing or catching. Nobody hides on purpose (n/a for a reason to hide).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Leo throws to Mara down a lane he called clear, but he never looked left and a hound snuffs the orb mid-air; dawn breaks with two rings dark. Everyone saw Leo's throw line. On another run, Mara called from a ring that had just faded and lit nothing.
- Note: catching is a same-moment action, so check online lag.

### MG-2 Glare Ice
- Rule (one sentence): Two touching players who both press push apart, each one's facing sets where the OTHER slides, and nobody can read the ice within a few meters of themselves.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): A crew of penguins on a cracking bay, sliding to grab fish and bring them back to the colony rock before the ice breaks up and the orca gets you.
- Aesthetic (one line): Blinding white glare, blue-black water showing through thin patches, a dark orca shadow sliding under the ice.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): When we push off, I aim you and you aim me in the same moment. I can read the ice far from me, which is the path I'm sending you down; you can read the path you're sending me down. Both decide where to send the other, and whether to press now or wait one beat for a better angle. While you slide, I call the thin ice near you that you can't see.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Thin patches are new each round and spread where people land. The orca comes up at a random hole every 10–20 seconds, and its shadow is readable only from a distance. Which friends touch changes every few seconds as people slide in and out.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The push-off.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, a player sliding into a friend can press to push off them mid-slide, so the three keep bouncing in a triangle and nobody waits. Pushing off a rock alone works but is slow. At 6, three pairs at once, plus bumps that break pairs up and form new ones.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": The ice near you is hidden from you; a friend at range sees it and calls it. Nobody hides on purpose (n/a).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Mia aims Joe across a patch she misread, Joe drops through, and the fish is lost. Everyone saw Mia's facing arrow on the push. On another run, Joe pressed late, slid crooked into the orca's hole, and the blame was his.
- Note: the double press is a same-moment action, so check online lag (a 0.3-second window).

### MG-3 Roof Hop
- Rule (one sentence): Every roof is an island you leave only by being thrown by a friend on the same roof; only the flier sees rotten tiles (from above) and picks when to drop, and only players standing on roofs hear which window the watchman is about to open.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Chimney-sweep thieves robbing a sleeping old city across the rooftops, one chimney at a time.
- Aesthetic (one line): Moonlit tin roofs, chimney smoke, yellow windows snapping on below.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I pick your direction and power, steering you away from the window I hear about to open. In the air you see the rot and choose when to drop, either short of the arc or riding it out. Once you land, you're the one who hears windows, and you throw whoever reaches your roof next.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Rot is reshuffled each round and spreads where people land. Windows open at random after a 2-second sound tell. Robbed chimneys go cold, so routes keep moving. Who shares a roof changes after every throw.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The throw.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, one thrower, one flier, and one player on another roof calling windows. A lone player can climb down a drainpipe and up the next roof (slow, and windows can spot them), so nobody is stuck. At 6, several roofs are active at once and throws cross.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": Rotten tiles are seen only from the air, and window tells are heard only on a roof. Nobody hides on purpose (n/a).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Kai throws Lena straight past the bakery window just as it opens, she's spotted, and the watch is raised. Kai's throw arc is on screen. On another run, Lena rides the arc too long, drops onto rot, crashes into a bedroom, and the blame is hers.

### MG-4 Breath
- Rule (one sentence): A shove sends a friend far only if they jump in the same half-second, and the fog wolf is seen by everyone except the one it's stalking, who alone hears its breath speed up before it pounces.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Moor wardens lighting signal cairns across a foggy moor at night while a wolf made of fog hunts you one at a time.
- Aesthetic (one line): Grey fog, orange cairn fires, a wolf shape that's only half there.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I see the wolf circling Tom, so I run to him and shove him away from the side it's coming from. Tom can't see it, but he hears the breath and jumps on the right half-second. A shove without his jump only staggers him, and a jump without my shove is a short hop. The same shove-and-jump is also the only way across wide bog pools, so it's used all round, not only in a rescue.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The wolf picks a random warden for each hunt and comes from any side. Cairns that need lighting (standing still for 5 seconds) come up at random, and the fog moves the bog pools each round. "Always work in pairs" doesn't beat it, because the shove still needs the right direction and the jump the right moment.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The shove-and-jump.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, one lights, one guards and one scouts, and roles change with every hunt. At 6, two wolves, and two rescues can collide.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": The wolf is hidden from its target, and teammates call it. The breath is hidden from everyone else, and the target calls it or simply acts on it. Nobody hides on purpose (n/a).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ana shoves Tom toward the wolf's side instead of away, and he's taken. Her shove arrow and his jump moment both show in the moment. On another run, Tom jumped early and Ana's shove missed him.
- Note: the shove and jump happen at the same moment, so check online lag.

### MG-5 Sun Relay
- Rule (one sentence): You can't aim sunlight yourself, only flash it onto a friend's mirror, and vampires show only in your own mirror, so whoever receives the light aims it, either at a vampire or on to another friend.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Vampire hunters holding a crumbling manor until dusk with nothing but hand mirrors and the sunbeams coming through holes in the roof.
- Aesthetic (one line): Dusty dark hall, hard gold shafts of light, and hunched shapes visible only in a small round mirror on screen.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): Standing in a sunspot, I see which friend has a clear line and how long the cloud gap lasts, so I flash the light onto one friend's mirror. They have 1 second to send it on (or it blinds them), and they see vampires in their own mirror. They decide whether to burn the vampire they see or pass the light to another friend whose area they think is worse. Light grows stronger with each bounce, and big vampires need three bounces.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): Clouds move the sunspots at random. Vampires come in from random sides and show only to whoever's mirror catches them, so the best chain changes every few seconds.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The flash between mirrors.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, short chains, and big vampires force all three into one line. At 6, long chains and several sunspots, and the crew splits into competing chains.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": Vampires are hidden from everyone except the mirror they show in, and the owner calls them or burns them. Nobody hides on purpose (n/a).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Ivo flashes to Sara when she's facing a wall, she's blinded, and the vampire at her back gets her. Ivo chose the target, and the beam is visible to everyone. On another run, Sara passes the light on instead of burning the vampire in her own mirror.
- Weakness: the flasher may sink into "flash whoever yells". The cloud timing and the line of sight are the flasher's own information, but this is the weakest P4 of the six.

### MG-6 Borrowed Lamps
- Rule (one sentence): Your lamp lights the floor only for your friends, never for you, and the stalker shows only in your own beam, where it freezes for up to 4 seconds before vanishing and coming back somewhere at random.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): Cave explorers lost in a pitch-black cave, feeling your way to the surface while something follows you.
- Aesthetic (one line): Total black, cones of lamp light crossing, cracks and pit edges showing only inside someone else's beam.
- Player on player (what my action does to a friend, and what they decide back): I light Sam's feet so Sam can see the pits and choose his steps, while Sam lights mine. I'm also sweeping for the stalker, which only I can see, and only in my beam. Freezing it means Sam walks in the dark, so I decide where my beam goes, and Sam decides where to step and whether to stop and wait for light.
- Why the plan can't settle (what changes round to round because of the players): The cave floor is random each round. The stalker breaks free after 4 seconds and reappears anywhere, so "one player watches the stalker" fails. Who lights whom changes as the crew spreads out.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): The lamp beams.
- At 3 / at 6: At 3, a triangle, where each beam must choose between two friends and the stalker. At 6, more crossing beams and two stalkers.
- Hidden (what, how uncovered, why hide) or "none": The floor is hidden from whoever's walking and shows in friends' beams; the stalker shows only to the beam's owner. Nobody hides on purpose (n/a).
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): Rita swings her beam off Sam to freeze the stalker, Sam steps into a pit in the dark, and everyone saw her beam leave him. On another run, Sam kept walking without light.
- Weakness: the walker may call "light my left" and the lighter just obeys. The lighter's own stalker sweep is the counterweight.
