# Critic round 1: i05 (Moonsick, the wolf we need)

Judged against `critic-brief-r2.md`, the ladder and sheets g01–g10. Only written rules count; the sheet's "(not played; Claude's guess)" lines are treated as guesses.

## 0. Novelty (kept apart from the verdict)

**Core act:** one friend turns into the wolf the crew needs to break doors, loses steering as hunger grows, and the others must drop what they carry to hold him and turn him back, all while hauling silver for a rising quota.

**On the ladder:** no sheet has a teammate as the only threat under a loss-of-control rule. The frame around it (haul loot out of generated rooms to a cart for a quota that rises until the crew misses it) is g01's haul, the same frame g08, g09 and g10 follow.

**Web (2 searches run, of 3 allowed):**
- Midnight Howls (itch.io couch co-op prototype): one player becomes a werewolf at full moon and the others evade until dawn. No holding, no need for the wolf, no loot.
- Wolfsbane (Workbench Entertainment): co-op social deduction where tasks turn a cursed villager back to human. Turning back is there; the crew choosing a wolf as a tool and catching him by hand is not.
- Wolfsbane Manor: 4-player co-op horror where the manor possesses one player, who turns on the others. Not chosen, not needed, not restrained.
- Grain Rot (Aug 2026, co-op extraction): friends can knock each other out of their bodies; no source says a player becomes the monster.
- Found nothing with the same core act. (Searches were two standard queries, so this is "not found", not "does not exist".)

**Mark: near g01: different because** the only danger is a friend the crew turned on purpose, and the crew's defence is to catch him with empty hands. The haul-and-quota shell follows g01, and on the ladder every "g01's haul, with X" sheet sits in C or D.

## 1. The seven traits

| # | Trait | Mark | Written rule that causes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | My action changes a teammate's next second | **Y** | The wolf is pulled toward the nearest friend and bites on touch; a hold needs two friends with empty hands for 3 seconds. So the wolf's path makes a carrier drop, dodge or hold now. Four of the five listed choices have a same-second reply; "who turns tonight" is planning. Weakens under the settled plan (section 3). |
| 2 | Failure becomes a story that keeps the failed player in | **P** | The wolf who loses control stays the centre of the scene and is dragged back by friends: yes. The friend bitten three times is "out until dawn" with no rule for what they do: a silent wait of up to a whole night. |
| 3 | Voice under a rule | **N** | The sheet says none; everyone hears everyone. |
| 4 | Repeated "one more, or leave now" with a shared loss | **P** | "One more door" repeats every door (hunger +1 per door). But the loss is one friend down 5 seconds and silver dropped, not lost. No rule puts banked or carried silver at risk, and no rule lets the crew end the night early. Only when bites run out does the whole crew pay. |
| 5 | One player holds information the others need | **P** | "I see my own notches." But notches are countable by anyone (doors broken plus 20-second ticks), the pull is visible, and on open voice the wolf, who wants to win, just says the number. The rule exists; it hides nothing. |

## 2. The two closest sheets

**g01 (Night Clearance)** and **g05 (Linked Climb)**.

- Has that both lack: the monster is a friend the crew picked, so every bite has a named culprit on voice; the crew's tool and its danger are the same person; needs no creature content at all (g01 needs 17+ creature kinds).
- Lacks that g01 has: voice by distance and a sound-hunting beast; the ship seat that sees what the others can't; a wipe that loses everything stored; a clock (midnight) that forces the leave-now call; tools, moons and weather for variety.
- Lacks that g05 has: a loss that lands on everyone at once and can't be planned around. In g05 one late jump drops all four; here one bite drops one friend for 5 seconds.
- Shares with g05: no enemies, the friends are the whole danger. Shares with g01: haul, generated rooms, rising quota.

## 3. Is it fun with friends? **maybe**

The best move for a crew that wants to win, and what happens to the fun:

**The plan a crew settles into.** Hunger is a ledger, not a threat, because two written rules cancel it for free:
1. A hold costs nothing but 3 seconds from two empty-handed friends, and the cooldown is per friend (2 minutes), so a crew of 4 always has a fresh wolf. Short shifts: break one or two doors, walk to two waiting friends, get held, next friend turns.
2. A bite lowers hunger by 2 and costs the bitten friend 5 seconds; a friend with empty hands drops nothing, and the first two bites of a night have no other cost. So when one more door is wanted, the friend with empty hands and the fewest bites steps into the wolf on purpose. Even at 6 notches the wolf "can no longer turn away", walks into the nearest friend, bites, and is at 4 again, where two friends can hold him. Six notches fixes itself.

The night becomes: "I'm at 4, who has bites left?" "Me, come here." Bite, door, hold, swap. A crew of 4 has six free bites a night (two each for the three non-wolves), and the friend at two bites turns wolf next because the wolf can't be bitten.

**Do the listed feelings still happen in it?**
- "One more door. I've still got it." Partly. The call stays, but it is paid with a scheduled bite, not a gamble.
- "He's drifting toward you, get behind the table." No. Hiding is the wrong move: it keeps hunger high. The right move is to walk into the bite with empty hands. Even a carrier loses only 5 seconds, since dropped silver isn't damaged.
- "Bring me back NOW, I can't turn away from you." Rare. It needs 6 notches, which only happens by mistake, and the bite that follows solves it.
- "You said one more door." Only late in a night, when the friends are at two bites each and the next one puts somebody out. That window is real and is where the design is alive.

**Reason in two lines:** the wolf's path forcing carriers to drop and grab him is a strong same-second link no ladder sheet has, and a friend as the monster is the kind of thing groups shout about. But the written numbers let a crew turn the fear into a bite budget, nothing the crew owns is ever at risk, voice has no rule and one mansion with one loot kind gives little variety, so it has less than the tier A and B sheets have.

**Sits between g05 and g06:** below g05 because g05's shared fall can't be scheduled away, at or a little above g06 because its same-second link is stronger than hide-and-wait.

## 4. Holes of this design alone

1. **A bite is a free hunger reset.** Best move: a friend with empty hands takes the bite on purpose; first two bites per friend per night cost 5 seconds. Removes the loss of control the fantasy is about. Tier A shares it? No. In g01 and g02 nothing a creature does to you helps the crew.
2. **Nothing ends a night.** "About 10 minutes" and "until dawn" appear, but no rule sets a timer. Best move without one: one door, hold, wait out the cooldown, repeat; the quota is met every night until the mansion's total silver is under it. If a timer is meant, it is the rule the whole "two doors per turn can't pay it" claim rests on, and it isn't written. Tier A shares it? No: g01 has midnight, g03 has the fog.
3. **Nothing banked or carried can be lost.** Silver dropped by a bite stays on the floor; silver in the cart is safe. Best move: push every time, since the worst case is a knockdown. Tier A shares it? No: g01 loses stored scrap on a wipe, g02 loses value on every bump.
4. **The out friend waits in silence.** Third bite means out until dawn, with no rule for what they do. Tier A shares it? Yes: g01 (the dead watch until the day ends) and g02 (the dead player "decides nothing; they watch", though the head is carried). Listed, not held against the design.
5. **Six notches can't be held in a crew of 3.** Three holders are needed and a 3-crew has two non-wolves; the only way down is a bite (hole 1 again), so the "three of us later" choice doesn't exist at the low end of the stated player count. Tier A shares it? No.

Smaller, not counted as a hole: the "run for the far wing" choice is never right, since time alone adds notches and nobody can reach the wolf there.

## 5. What I could not break

- The hold needing two friends with empty hands. Every shift pulls two carriers off the haul at a moment the wolf's position picks, so trait 1 survives even in the settled plan.
- The blame. Every bite comes from a friend who was steering, so every loss has a name on voice. No creature design gives that.
- "Who turns tonight": the friend at two bites is safe as the wolf but may be the best carrier. A real swap with no always-right answer.
- The late-night window, when the bites are spent and the quota still needs a deep room. There the fear is real.
- The one-player test: the fantasy line fails alone, as the sheet says.

## 6. Smallest change that would make it more fun with friends (opinion)

Make the bite cost silver or do nothing: **hunger falls by 2 only when the bitten friend was carrying, and the wolf swallows that load (it is gone).** A bite on empty hands knocks the friend down and leaves hunger where it was.

One rule change, and it closes holes 1 and 3 together: feeding on purpose now costs the crew quota, hiding behind the table is right again, "hold now with two or later with three" becomes a real call, and "you said one more door" has a loss everyone shares. Writing the dawn timer as a rule (hole 2) is needed either way.
