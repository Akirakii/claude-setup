# Critic r1 on g03 (Summit Scouts)

## The problem underneath
- The fantasy does not need a friend: the proposal's own one-player test says the line works alone and the game starts with one.
- The friend is needed only by four help verbs (pull, boost, carry, revive), and no rule makes a stage need any of them.
- Feeling 1 (pulled up below the ledge) has a rule behind it.
- Feeling 3 (fog behind, one at the bottom) has a rule behind it.
- Feeling 2 (the poison berry) has no rule: nothing says which food poisons or whether a scout can tell, so it fails as unwritten.

## The scene, second by second
- The climber is "nearly empty" halfway up, then a friend eats, climbs another line, gets above and reaches down.
- No rule says whether hanging still drains the bar, so the scene's timing is not worked out.
- If hanging drains, the climber falls before the friend arrives; if it does not, the wall has a free rest and there was no danger.
- The rescuer climbs the whole wall on one snack, so the answer (eat first) was known before the first climb.
- The fourth scout is the heaviest and hungriest and climbs last with three friends on top and a free pull on offer: the fall is a crew mistake.

## Fewest and most players
- At 1: no pull, boost, carry or revive; passing out is the end of the run; the pursuing figure has no group to measure against; it is a lone stamina sum.
- At 2: when one falls, the other cannot light the campfire while the friend lives, so the cheap move is to wait for the death timer, light alone, and revive at the statue.
- At 4: three bars of spare reach and 28 slots of spread weight make the single wall easy; the pressure left is the fog clock.

## The plans
- The strategy that always wins: stay in one clump, hop ledge to ledge, keep food for extra stamina, never go back down.
- The fifth-session plan: the lightest scout tests the wall, a rope comes down, the heavy one goes last, the fallen are left for the statue (the proposal writes this itself).
- A perfect crew's round: nobody empties the bar, so there is no pull, no fall, no pass-out and no rescue.

## Findings
- SEVERE, only this design: the statue plus "every living player" at the campfire makes leaving a fallen friend the right move, and a dead friend makes the campfire easier to light than a passed-out one.
- So the one help with a real price between two bodies (going down into the fog) is refused by good crews, and dying on purpose helps.
- SEVERE, missing baseline part (two threats): the fog and the pursuing figure are both answered by one move, stay together and keep climbing, and the other creatures have no written rule.
- MEDIUM, only this design: the fantasy and every stage can be done by one scout, so the coupling is optional (points 1 and 6).
- MEDIUM, only this design: the pull has no written price for the puller, so the game's best-known help is free.
- MEDIUM, only this design: the pursuing figure cancels the written choice "climb ahead to test a route", and "far above" has no number, so either the choice or the figure is dead.
- MEDIUM, only this design: the figure's rule does not say what happens when the rest of the group is dead or passed out.
- MEDIUM, only this design: the scene's numbers are not worked out (hanging drain, rope drain, how long the death timer is).
- MEDIUM, only this design: the poison feeling leans on a rule that is not written.
- MEDIUM, only this design: the mountain is the same for 24 hours, so a second run that day is a remembered route.
- MEDIUM, only this design: a passed-out scout cannot move, act or be heard, so that player has no verb until rescue or death.
- The baseline has this too (row 7, fixed roles and fear only in mistakes): the pull, the fall and the rescue only happen after an error.
- The baseline has this too (row 4, one sum with no friend in it): "eat now or save" and "carry more or travel light" are sums on one scout's own bar.
- The baseline has this too (row 5, one end only stands): the boosting scout only crouches and the pulled climber only hangs.
- The baseline has this too (row 3, tools lower the need for friends): ropes and anchors replace the pull and the boost.

## The four baseline parts
- Priced help on good nights: present; handing over food or a healing item costs the giver, and hunger grows with no mistake.
- Rising pressure that breaks the plan: present; the fog, the stage afflictions, and tiers that make items heavier and hunger faster.
- Two threats at once: missing; both written threats push the crew the same way.
- Carry-over: present inside a run only; injury, food and the dead carry from stage to stage; between runs only looks and an optional tier.

## Could not break
- One bar holds every harm, so each affliction reads as lost reach with no extra teaching.
- The campfire that needs everyone, with hunger still growing while the fog is paused, is a regroup point and not a safe state.
- The blame is one body sliding off a wall by its own action, seen as it happens.
- The weight rule makes "who carries what" a real handover between friends.
- The goal and the first verb (climb up) need no teaching.
