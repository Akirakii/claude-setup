# M11 "meet in the instant" (gen 2)

## Method log

**Steps as run**
1. Wrote 40 physical actions: throw, catch, release, pull a rope, push, lift, drop, swing a hammer,
   slide under, jump, duck, cut, tie a knot, pour, plug a hole, pump, brace, aim, fire, open a gate,
   close a gate, turn a crank, kick, roll, stack, balance, climb, hook, whistle/call, light a fuse,
   blow out, stir, shovel, sweep, weigh down, spin, grab, shove off, stamp, snap/break.
2. Drew 24 pairs with `random.SystemRandom().sample(actions, 2)`. Nothing picked by hand, nothing
   re-rolled. A pair could repeat an action (slide under 3 times, plug 3 times, crank 3 times).
3. Built one rule per pair: action A by one player and action B by another must meet in one instant,
   with a physical clock, rotating roles so nobody waits at 3 or 6, and a generator for the arena.
4. Kept 8 with 8 different core rules. Before showing them, checked each against
   `~/.claude/design-anti-patterns.md` (B24 co-location, gate point 7 two-way).

**Raw count:** 24 (one per pair).

**Checks the raw candidates failed most** (my own read, not scored):
- F3 high player coupling, 8 of 24. When the drawn action is a reaction (duck, close a gate,
  balance, brace), that end only obeys. Also pooled weight (P04) and side-by-side work (P24).
  One quarterback (P21).
- F5 crew 3 and 6, 5 of 24. A pair is two roles. Unless a ring or a third job is added, seats sit
  idle at 6 or the last player is stranded (P11).
- N novelty, 2 of 24. P23 is curling. P17 failed the web search (below).
- F7 tail was weak (~) in about 6. Every kept idea gets its tail from a generated layout plus
  physics, not from the players. That is a ~-grade tail, not a strong one.

**Novelty search** (one search per kept idea, after generating):
- M11-1 house cribbing: no match. Nearest are flood games where you stack material (Building & Sea,
  Ice Up!, Squeakers), not two players lifting and cribbing.
- M11-2 bell pour: no match. Nearest are co-op smithing games (Smithworks, The Forge).
- M11-3 stone on rollers: no game match. Nearest are log-balance co-op games (Log Riders,
  Roll Together); NRICH describes the real rollers technique.
- M11-4 hot rivets: no match. Nearest is Dumb Ways to Build (physics building, nail gun).
- M11-5 grapple gondola: no match. Nearest are grapple movement games (Windlands 2, Skyhook).
- **SWAP:** P17 "broadside fuses" (one player aims a cannon while another lights its fuse) matched
  **Hands on Deck** (itch.io): one player aims the cannon and the other fires it, on a ship.
  That is the same core rule, so I dropped it and replaced it with the next best raw candidate,
  P12 "trapper doors" (now M11-8). Its search found no match. Nearest: Cartphus (one player
  steers, one brakes) and Drive Together.
- M11-6 teeterboard troupe: no confirmed match. Possible near match: **The Seesaw Redemption**
  (GMTK 2021 jam), where two dwarves take turns jumping on a seesaw to reach a rope. The snippet
  does not say whether one launches the other. I kept it and rate it N ~ rather than ✗: that game
  has 2 players and a fixed climb, while this one is a chain where each landing launches the next
  player. If the scorer knows that game launches the partner, rate it ✗.
- M11-7 hoist relay: no match. Nearest are Shift 0 (carry objects up floors) and Heave Ho
  (swing and catch).

**What the method was good at**
- Every idea comes with a two-way coupling that is a single moment. Both players decide when, and
  either of them can ruin it, so blame lands on the person standing at the spot (F4 strong).
- The random verbs pushed it away from "steer one shared thing": no kept idea has a pooled bar.
- A physical clock fits naturally (water, cooling metal, tide, wind, crumbling rock).

**What it was bad at**
- Two drawn verbs give two roles. The rotation (ring, leapfrog, or third job) is bolted on every
  time, and at odd crew sizes it needs care.
- Reactive verbs (duck, close, balance) keep making one end obey. A filter "both verbs must be
  something the player starts" would have saved about a third of the draws.
- The themes all came out as historical work crews (builders, foundry, miners). That is one
  family, so method diversity is low.
- Risk, assumed and not checked: for online play, "the same instant" across two machines with
  100–200 ms of ping may feel unfair. Each idea needs a timing window of at least ~250 ms, or the
  server must judge the timing from the catching player's side.
- The tail is only ever a generated layout. None of the kept ideas get round-50 freshness from
  the players themselves.

## Raw candidates

- P01 release + fire: **Grapple gondola.** A basket on grapple lines moves when one player's new
  line bites in the same instant another releases the rear line. Kept (M11-5).
- P02 weigh down + shove off: **Teeterboard troupe.** Landing on a board's high end launches the
  player on the low end if they jump in that instant, in a flooding quarry. Kept (M11-6).
- P03 lift + slide under: **Crib the house.** One player heaves a house corner and another slides
  a block under at the top, against a flood. Kept (M11-1).
- P04 balance + aim: **Harpoon raft.** A harpoon throw counts only when the raft is level, and
  the others level it by where they stand. Fails F3: level is a pooled sum of weight.
- P05 pour + plug a hole: **Bell pour.** One player pours bronze while others plug each vent as
  the metal arrives. Kept (M11-2).
- P06 snap/break + kick: **Log jam.** One player axes the key log as another kicks off to ride
  the freed logs. Fails F5 (one key log per jam, so others wait) and F7 ~.
- P07 catch + turn a crank: **Hoist relay.** Each player cranks a load up to the next floor and
  catches the one from below. Kept (M11-7).
- P08 duck + plug a hole: **Sub leaks.** Plugging one leak moves the steam jet onto a friend, who
  must duck. Fails F3: the ducker only reacts.
- P09 close a gate + climb: **Portcullis climb.** The gate drops as the climber gets through.
  Fails F3 (the climber obeys the gate) and F5 (two roles).
- P10 spin + turn a crank: **Gear clutch.** Two neighbours match spin speeds at the instant the
  clutch engages. F3 ~ (close to matching a sum), F4 ~ (a stripped gear is both players' fault),
  and the fantasy is abstract.
- P11 climb + weigh down: **Counterweight shaft.** One player drops in the top bucket to haul the
  climber up. Fails F5 (the last player has no counterweight) and overlaps P02.
- P12 push + open a gate: **Trapper doors.** A mine-cart rider sets the speed while the others
  leapfrog ahead to open each air door in the instant the cart passes. Kept after the swap (M11-8).
- P13 balance + release: **Crane girder.** The crane player drops a beam onto a support the
  other player is holding level. Fails F3: the balancer only holds.
- P14 slide under + plug a hole: **Hull fothering.** One player slides a sail under the hull while
  another plugs the leak from inside. Fails F5 (two roles) and F4 ~.
- P15 slide under + roll: **Stone on rollers.** One player slides a log under the stone's nose in
  the instant the rope-holder lets it roll, racing the tide. Kept (M11-3).
- P16 sweep + catch: **Chimney sweep.** One sweeps soot and debris down the flue and another
  catches what falls. F3 ~ and fails F7.
- P17 light a fuse + aim: **Broadside fuses.** I aim my cannon and light my neighbour's, the deck
  rolls, and the fuse delays the shot. Was kept, then dropped after the search (Hands on Deck).
- P18 duck + blow out: **Candle ghost.** One player blows out candles as the ghost turns while the
  others duck. Fails F3, and F1 ~ (the ghost is AI).
- P19 close a gate + pull a rope: **Lock haulers.** One player hauls a barge into a lock and
  another closes the gate behind its stern. Fails F3: the gatekeeper obeys.
- P20 throw + swing a hammer: **Hot rivets.** One player throws a glowing rivet into a hole, and
  another's hammer must come down on it in that instant. Kept (M11-4).
- P21 whistle/call + light a fuse: **Firework barge.** The caller on shore calls each launch to
  the beat. Fails F3: the caller is a quarterback.
- P22 turn a crank + kick: **Drawbridge sprint.** One cranks the bridge down so it is level as
  the cart is kicked off the ramp. Fails F5 (two roles) and F7 ~.
- P23 release + sweep: **Stone and brooms.** Fails N: this is curling.
- P24 stack + sweep: **Sandbag wall.** One sweeps the mud off a spot as another sets a bag on it.
  Fails F3 (side-by-side work) and N ~ (flood-defence games exist).

## Kept ideas

### M11-1 Crib the House
- Rule (one sentence): A corner of the house rises one block only when one player heaves its
  jack and a different player slides a block under it at the top of the heave. If the lifter lets
  go first, the corner drops back.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the house, standing on one timber stack per corner.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The lifter picks when to heave and holds for at most ~1.5 s.
  - The slider picks when to shove the block in, and needs a block in hand.
  - Blocks are planks floating past in the flood. A third player hooks them out.
  - Neighbouring corners may differ by at most 2 blocks, so the corner a friend just raised
    decides which corner I must raise next.
  - Lift, slide and hook rotate as players move round the house.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a farm family jacking the farmhouse up on timber
  stacks while the river floods.
- Look (one line): a tilted wooden house on wobbling stacks of timber, with brown water rising.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: a triangle hut. One player lifts, one slides and one hooks planks, and the jobs rotate
    every heave.
  - At 6: a hexagon house. Up to three lift-and-slide pairs work at once. Planks are scarce, so one
    or two players hook, and the flood rises faster.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a corner crashes down, the house tips past 2 blocks
  and slides off its stacks into the water. Blame is visible at that corner:
  - the block lies on the mud beside the stack: the slider missed;
  - the block was still in the slider's hands when the corner fell: the lifter let go early.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each round
  generates the ground under each corner (rock, or mud that sinks a stack one block every N
  seconds), the plank stream (lengths and timing), the house outline for the crew size and the
  flood rate. Which corners sink, and when, is new every round.

### M11-2 Bell Pour
- Rule (one sentence): One player tilts a crucible of molten bronze into a clay mould whose
  channels branch to several vents, and each vent must be plugged by another player at the instant
  metal reaches its mouth. Too early traps air and the bell cracks there; too late spills metal.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the mould (a tree of channels and its vents), fed by
  the crucible.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The pourer controls the flow rate but cannot see the vents.
  - Each plugger sees only the vents near them and runs between them.
  - A surge from the pourer changes which vent fills next.
  - The pourer slows or speeds up on the pluggers' calls ("two filling at once, slow!").
  - The pourer's job rotates with every bell.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a village foundry crew casting the town's bells
  before the furnace dies.
- Look (one line): a dark shed, glowing orange streams, and sparks spitting from the vents.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: one player pours and two run to cover about 4 vents.
  - At 6: two crucibles pour into one large mould from two gates, and four pluggers cover about
    8 vents. The two metal fronts must meet while still hot.
- Hidden: none. The channels are drawn on the mould, and metal glows through the clay.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a vent overflows beside the plugger who was late.
  Or the bell is rung at the end and cracks at the vent someone plugged early, and the crack shows
  whose vent it was. The clock is cooling metal: if the stream stops for ~2 s, the channels freeze
  (a cold seam the pourer caused), and the crucible cools over ~40 s.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each mould is a
  random branching channel tree from one small generator (vent count, branch lengths, heights), and
  bells grow larger through a run. Round 50 has a channel layout no one has poured before.

### M11-3 Stone on Rollers
- Rule (one sentence): A huge stone rides log rollers down a tidal slope. Each log that pops out
  the back must be carried to the front and slid under the nose in the same instant the rope-holder
  lets the stone roll forward onto it.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the stone and its logs.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The catcher grabs the log popping out the back before it rolls into the sea.
  - The slider places it under the nose. The angle they choose steers the stone, which moves
    where the catcher must stand next.
  - The rope-holder decides when to let go and how far to let it roll.
  - If the rope-holder lets go early, the nose digs into the sand or traps the slider's hands. If
    they hold too long, the tide gains.
  - Jobs rotate by one with every log.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): stone-age builders hauling a standing stone across
  the sands to a holy island before the tide closes the crossing.
- Look (one line): a grey stone on wet sand that mirrors the sunset, with the sea creeping in.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: one catches, one slides and one holds the rope, rotating.
  - At 6: a wider stone on two lanes of logs (left and right), with a team of three on each.
    When the lanes get out of step the stone turns, so the two teams must talk.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the nose drops off the front log and buries itself.
  Either the slider standing there was late, or the slack rope shows the rope-holder let go early.
  Or the tide covers the stone.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): a generated flat
  (slope, soft patches, tidal channels to cross, rocks to steer around) and tide speed. Steering
  through it by log angle is new each round.

### M11-4 Hot Rivets
- Rule (one sentence): A rivet sets only if one player throws it, glowing, into a hole in the
  steel frame and another player's hammer comes down on it in the same instant, while it still
  glows (~2 s).
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the steel frame high above the street.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - Players stand in a ring of joints. Each hammers what the previous player throws and throws to
    the next player's joint.
  - The thrower picks the arc and the moment, and the wind bends the throw.
  - The hammer swing takes ~0.6 s, so the hammerer must start it before the rivet lands, on a call.
  - A hammer on an empty hole rings the beam and shakes the frame, which spoils the neighbours'
    next throws.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a 1930s riveting gang on a skyscraper, racing a
  rising wind.
- Look (one line): orange dots arcing against a grey sky, with the city far below.
- At 3 / at 6: a ring of 3 or 6 joints, and everyone both throws and hammers. One player cannot
  throw and swing at once, so the crew has to fall into a rhythm. At 6 there are more rivets in
  the air and more shaking.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a rivet drops to the street. A wide throw is the
  thrower's fault. A rivet left sitting blue in its hole means the hammerer was late. Too many cold
  joints and the frame sways until a beam falls at that joint. The clock is the cooling rivets and
  wind that rises every minute.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): each round
  generates the frame's shape (distances, heights and angles between joints) and the wind's gust
  pattern, and the frame grows taller during a run.

### M11-5 Grapple Gondola
- Rule (one sentence): The gondola hangs from grapple lines the players hold, and it moves only
  when one player's new line bites the cliff ahead in the same instant another player releases the
  rearmost line.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the gondola and its lines.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - Each player owns one line gun and cycles through: fire (front), hold (reel in and out to swing
    the gondola toward the next rock), release (rear), reload, fire.
  - The firer picks the rock (sound or cracked) and the moment. The holders' swinging decides
    when the next anchor spot is in reach.
  - The releaser picks the instant the new line goes tight. Release early and the gondola swings
    down and slams the wall. Release late and the rear line yanks it back and tears out its anchor.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a rescue crew crossing a collapsing canyon in a
  wicker basket.
- Look (one line): a wicker basket with lines like spider legs against red rock.
- At 3 / at 6: one line holds two people, so lines in use = crew ÷ 2, rounded up.
  - At 3: 2 lines held and 1 firer, which makes a clean cycle.
  - At 6: 3 lines held, with one player firing, one releasing and one reloading at every step.
- Hidden: none. The cracks in the rock are visible.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): the basket slams the wall or drops. Either the
  releaser's empty hook is flapping because they let go before the new line bit, or the firer's
  anchor tore out of cracked rock. The clock is the anchors themselves: each holds ~10 s, and its
  cracks widen as it goes.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): generated canyon
  walls (a map of rock quality, overhangs, gap widths and how fast each anchor crumbles).

### M11-6 Teeterboard Troupe
- Rule (one sentence): The player on the low end of a teeterboard flies only if they jump in the
  same instant another player lands on the high end. The lander then sits at the low end waiting
  for the next lander, so the troupe keeps juggling itself while water fills the quarry.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the teeterboards on the quarry ledges.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The lander steers in the air and picks the board. The sitter picks the jump moment: jump early
    and you get a low hop; jump late and you get nothing.
  - Flyers ring gongs on the walls at the top of their arc.
  - With several players in the air, who lands where is a live negotiation.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a circus troupe trapped in a flooding quarry, ringing
  gongs to call for help.
- Look (one line): striped costumes against grey stone, and big splashes.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: 2 boards, with someone always in the air.
  - At 6: 3 or 4 boards, two or three players in the air at once, and possible mid-air crashes.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a flyer falls short into the water. Either the
  sitter jumped early (a visibly low arc) or the lander came down off the end of the board. The
  splashed player swims slowly to a ladder, the chain runs short of players, and the water reaches
  the top board before the gongs are rung. The clock is the rising water, which drowns the lowest
  boards first.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): generated board
  layouts on the ledges (heights and gaps), gong positions and the water rate.

### M11-7 Hoist Relay
- Rule (one sentence): Loads go up the tower floor by floor. Each player cranks a hoist that lifts
  a swinging load to the floor above, where the next player must grab it in the instant it swings
  past the floor's edge.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the tower and its chain of hoists.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - Each player catches from the floor below and cranks to the floor above.
  - The cranker picks speed and stop point; a sudden stop swings the load harder. The catcher
    picks the grab moment and calls for "slower" or "stop".
  - The top player fixes the beam in place.
  - When a floor is done, the bottom player climbs to the top, so jobs rotate.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): carpenters raising a timber bell tower before a storm.
- Look (one line): beams swinging on ropes and a tower of scaffold leaning in the wind.
- At 3 / at 6: there are as many floors as players. At 6 more loads are in the chain at once, and
  a dropped load hits more floors on the way down.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a load slips past the catcher's hands and smashes
  through the floor below. The catcher was standing there; if the cranker overshot, the swing
  shows it. The clock is the wind, which rises through the round and makes every load swing
  further.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): generated load
  lengths (a longer beam swings more slowly), gust patterns and floor heights.

### M11-8 Trapper Doors
- Rule (one sentence): An ore cart runs a mine track through air doors, and each door must be
  pulled open only in the instant the cart passes. Open it early and smoke from a mine fire creeps
  one section closer; open it late and the cart smashes the door.
- Hub (the one shared thing everyone uses): the looping track with its doors.
- Coupling (how my best move depends on what a friend just did; who does which different action):
  - The rider pushes on climbs and brakes on slopes, so the rider sets the speed.
  - Keepers leapfrog ahead to the next unmanned door, and each picks the moment to open their own.
  - If the rider goes faster than the keepers can run, a door is left unmanned ("slow down, I'm
    not at door 4 yet!").
  - The rider swaps with a keeper every lap.
- Fantasy (who you are, what you're doing): a 1900s mine crew hauling out the last carts while a
  fire spreads through the tunnels.
- Look (one line): lamp light, wooden doors on ropes, and grey smoke rolling along the roof.
- At 3 / at 6:
  - At 3: one rider and two keepers leapfrogging.
  - At 6: two carts on the loop, two riders, and four keepers serving both. Two carts at one door
    is the crunch.
- Hidden: none.
- A failed run (what happens, who is blamed): a door is smashed. Either the keeper next to it
  opened late, or it stood empty because the rider outran the keepers (the speed is visible). The
  smoke then flows freely and reaches the shaft before the last cart is out. The clock is the
  smoke front, which advances steadily and faster after every early opening.
- Tail (what makes round 50 different from round 2, with no hand-made content): a generated track
  (slopes, curves, door spacing, branches) and where the fire starts.
